Swears, Oaths and Insults

Swears, Oaths and Insults

Nov 28, 202620 min read

Six peoples and how they curse · the Kharven, the Moto, the Marrowkin, the Accord, the elves and the Zettari · and the words every people shares

Every people swears in its own words first, and in the common tongue’s plain body-swears as well. What a people swears by is what it holds sacred, and what it insults with is what it fears to be. The Sky, the Fourteen (together, or each of them by name), the named Archons, the Veil, the Realmsong, Varūn and the ancestors carry the most weight, and beside them every people swears by its own Wells, its trades and its dead, as each people’s Standing Inventory sets down.

The oldest layer of this page reached the Guild on a strip of ship’s sail. When the wine cog Long Hester opened her belly on the Sowmarsh bar, thirty-eight people spent nine days on a spit of yellow sand with two casks of water and no shared opinion about anything: Kharven sealers, Marrowkin coopers, a pair of Eressean sisters, a Moto retainer in lacquered harness who corrected everyone, and a chancery clerk named Drogo Fulke. By the third day they had stopped fighting over the water and started fighting over whose insults were worse. By the sixth it was a contest with judges, and a Marrowkin cooper was winning it by saying one word three times, very quietly. Fulke took it all down on red sailcloth in fish blood, and lost the top of an ear for asking a sealer to repeat Kiilut. The boats that came for them found thirty-eight people hoarse, sunburned and on excellent terms. The strip is in the Altherion rolls, and these tables are its great-grandchildren.

How to Read the Tables

Strength, four grades:

  • mild: said in front of children, a grunt at a dropped tool.
  • firm: rude; said to a peer’s face it is a rebuke.
  • hard: an insult that has to be answered or swallowed.
  • grave: fighting words, or a curse that some people will not say aloud at all.

Kharven

The clans swear by the Sky and the fire, curse with the cold, and insult by scarcity: bad wood, empty saddles, cracked bowls, short stacks. The Sky is never named beyond the Sky.

Moto

A house that holds the offense is inaccuracy, so its heaviest words are verdicts. The house speaks in the old register: full forms, no contractions, honorifics where the old tongue is used. The Moto never swear by a single Archon, since addressing one alone is Singling, the principal heresy; a Moto who hears a Kharven say “Archons take it” hears nothing wrong, and a Moto who hears “Irath take it” hears heresy. They also do not petition, so there is no Moto “help me.”

The Marrowkin

Marrowkin have no light oath. “Kurme” is the swearing itself, so a Marrowkin who says “I swear” has sworn, and they do not say it for emphasis. Their curses fall on the cask, the angle, the entry and the grade.

The Accord and the Concord

Chartered, tolled and entered people swear by paperwork and the Archons’ days, and insult by weights and measures. The chancery Latin turns up in any educated mouth, and in a temper most of all. The Accord’s calendar gives the Archon days: Auren’s for the dead, Veyra’s for markets, Irath’s for musters, and Uurgath’s, which nobody chooses on purpose.

The Zettari

The Zettari swear in their own tongue. Their oaths need something older than the speaker to hear them (the Stone Witness Oath), their pride is weight and bearing, and their shame is lightness, broken cords and unmarked skin.

Across the Realms

Some words belong to no one people. They come from the faiths, from the Gate, from Stage and Path, from the halls, the round and the dead, and any mouth in the four quarters may use them in its own accent. Pathless and undeclared are one fact in two mouths: an Inquisitor means a soul that walks under no Archon, a practitioner means a Crystal that never named its Path, and the Inquisition holds that the two are the same failing.

The Faiths

The Holy Inquisition

The Ordo files in Latin and speaks in its Tenets. Its insults are classifications: a word from a mask is an entry, and an entry is the first step of a correction (Tenet III: the correction follows “in the same breath, by the same hand”).
