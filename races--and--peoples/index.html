Races & Peoples

Nov 28, 20265 min read

At the Goose Fair at Tarnbridge in 612 IC, a Cask Line cooper, a Varrisak piper in moss-green and an Ironblood drover with nine iron rings in her topknot came to blows over which of them a harvest song had insulted, and by nightfall they had flattened the goose market, the pie row and a good part of the magistrate’s awning, in three languages and under a sky full of feathers. All three were right about the song. This archivist has read the depositions, and that is the whole section in small.

The pattern that runs under every people in this section: one origin, several sincere and mutually incompatible answers to what that origin demanded. Read it as a set of arguments that nobody won, and throw away any family tree that shows decline from a purer ancestor.

“She sang one note. We have spent every Eon since arguing about how it should be sung back.”

The Shape of Every Schism

Every major people in the four quarters was made once, from one source, with one instruction. And in every case the instruction turned out to admit more than one honest reading, and the readings turned out to be irreconcilable, and the irreconcilability was discovered under maximum pressure, with the roof coming down, rather than at a scholars’ disputation over a good dinner.

The useful reading is that no branch here is the degraded version of another. Each is a complete and internally consistent answer to a question the source left genuinely open, and each is living with what its own answer cost.

On Reading These Entries

The codices in this section are partisan, and I would distrust any that claimed otherwise. Each people’s entry is written from a position close enough to that people to make their reasoning legible, which means the same event is described three different ways depending on which page you are standing on. Go in armed.

The Betrayal of the Mountain and the First Rational Act are the same refusal. The Sylvaar Rebellion Schism is a rebellion from one side of the forest and a homecoming from the other. The Sundering of the Deep Halls has a trigger that the surviving branches’ oral records and the High Line archives describe differently, and the difference has never been resolved because neither party has offered to open the question.

Where a document records both accounts, both are reproduced. Where the record contains only one, that is itself a fact about who kept the record.

Tongues. The constructed languages live here rather than under The Magic System, because a tongue belongs to a people before it belongs to a working. The Tongues of the Realms is the framework: what counts as a language against what is only a naming register, the seven conventions binding every grammar, and the family tree. Old Vaross, Eressean and Varrisak, the Tally-Tongue, Accord Latin with its two spoken registers, and Common. Accent sits under Common as a menu for placing a speaker without spelling a single vowel.

Parunic is the exception and stays where it is. It is a script for writing boundaries onto laws, and nobody was ever raised in it, so it lives under The Magic System with the Descent of Parun and the Counted Speech.

Two more are known from outside and have never been written down by anyone who would talk: Drow Shadow-Veil notation, and Mirror-Sign, a full manual language with its own grammar.
