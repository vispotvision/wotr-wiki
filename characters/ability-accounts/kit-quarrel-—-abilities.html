Kit Quarrel — Abilities in full

Kit Quarrel — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20266 min read

Card: Kit Quarrel

Techniques

A PULL AT A TIME · Body art

A PULL AT A TIME is basically incremental strength: the power to pay for any load in small steps and keep every step paid. It is a paymaster’s idea: a big bill can always be paid in small coin, provided the coin already paid stays paid. Kit has built his whole way of fighting on it, and he has no Traits.

Capabilities

What anyone watching sees first is the smoky steel gray held close to his skin, with fine darker lines running through it the way rope runs over a pulley. That is his aura, the visible coat of his own power. The hue and the dry creak of it are nearest Ascensio’s, the Wellspring the scholars call the Ladder of Ascent, whose idea is the ladder: the whole height climbed by steps, with no single step large enough to break you. Kit has not said. Ascensio is a Wild Wellspring, a transformation of matter, and its operation is distillation, the same work passed up again and again by degrees until the whole height is reached; on a Body fighter it comes out plain: the body takes its load in steps. He pulls Aether (mana) up out of the ground he stands on, packs it raw into his Shell, the body-side layer of the soul, as Charge, and lays it on his hands and forearms as a harder coat. About two fifths of what he spends reaches the work, which is his yield. The rest burns in the furnace of his Shell and goes off as warmth in the knuckles and as the dry creak itself.

A heavy crossbow keeps the draw in its bent limbs, and a bow costs the same to bend by any road; the Measurewrights write it as force multiplied by distance. A windlass adds nothing to a man’s strength. Its crank and pulleys let him pay the draw as a small pull over a long way, twenty turns of the handle while the string comes back a hair at a time, and its pawl, the little catch that clicks into each tooth of a ratchet, keeps the string from running back between turns. Kit carries no windlass because his coat is one. The dark lines tighten all at once, like ropes taking up the load over a block, and the string comes back a hand’s breadth, and stops, and holds, because the lines stand on it the way a pawl stands in a tooth; then they run and tighten again, and it comes back another. Each pull is small enough for his joints to bear, and each one stands on the last, so his muscles never carry the whole draw at once and never have to hold it between pulls. Five breaths, and the string is on the nut, the catch in the stock that holds a spanned string, where the bow keeps the work without costing him anything more.

The shot itself is plain skill, with no Wellspring in it; the art has already done its part by putting the work into the bow. A crossbow lets its whole span go in the time a hand falls, the same work in far less time, which is why a bolt goes in to the fletching. He spends it where the body is thinnest: low in the side behind the ribs, where nothing but hide and gut wall covers the belly, or an inch behind the eye and below the ear, where the skull is thin.

Applications

  • Spanning by hand a horn-and-sinew crossbow that no man can draw
  • Keeping the bow spanned at no further cost; he sat a whole night by the fire with it spanned across his lap
  • Putting the whole span into one bolt, in the one place that ends it
  • Letting the price alone do the work, so that nobody has to be shot
  • Drawing the coat to one place and taking a bend slowly enough that nothing breaks, as with a surgeon’s needle straight off the coals

Techniques

  • THE HAND-SPAN - the boot into the stirrup, both bare hands on the string, a hand’s breadth to a pull, and the bow is spanned in five breaths where a windlass takes twenty turns
  • THREE COPPER - he lifts the bow a hair, so the light runs once along the notched stock, says in a flat, unhurried voice what the bolt costs, waits for the thing to stand still, and shoots once
  • THE NEEDLE - the same grip made small: a needle held over the coals in bare fingers until the steel went straw-colored, then bent into a new moon between finger and thumb, slowly, as one might bend a hot grass stalk

Associations

  • Ascensio, the Ladder of Ascent, a Wild Wellspring, is the nearest match to his color and sound; he has not named it himself
  • The Body Path, in the Form of a Shape
  • The crossbow, a big plain bow of horn and sinew with twenty-three notches along its stock, kept and not bonded
  • The Short Measure has only ever seen him do it, never learn it

Limitations

  • The lines hold only what has already been pulled, a step at a time, and they add no strength to any single pull
  • The art makes the pull bearable and does not make it quick: THE HAND-SPAN costs time, five breaths to a bolt, and for those five breaths he is a man standing on a bowstring with both hands full
  • Its failure is the ladder’s own: knock him mid-pull and a step lets go, and everything already paid runs back through his fingers at once, the string first
  • It is one bolt to a span, and he will not pay for a second one
  • About three fifths of what he spends burns in his Shell’s furnace and comes off as warmth and the creak
  • THE HAND-SPAN shows as the boot going into the stirrup and the gray coming up round both hands on the string; afterward, when he opens and shuts his fingers, the gray round the knuckles has thinned, like breath going off a cold glass, and is a moment coming back
  • THREE COPPER shows as the bow lifted a hair and the price said aloud
  • THE NEEDLE shows as the gray brightening at finger and thumb, a little clearer, the lines in the smoke tightening and running, before it sinks back close to the skin; no cost showed, and that it took the heat off his skin and lent the fingers strength is Ned’s guess
  • It is beaten by reaching him in the five breaths it takes, or by knocking him mid-pull so the steps run back
  • Against the shot, do not be the thing standing still: keep moving, keep a cart or a man between you and the bow, or close while he is still paying for the next one
