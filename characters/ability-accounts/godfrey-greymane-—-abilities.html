Godfrey Greymane — Abilities in full

Godfrey Greymane — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 202620 min read

Card: Godfrey Greymane — The Shieldwarden

THE KING’S WALL · Signature art

THE KING’S WALL is basically kinetic force redirection: the power to stand between a blow and the people behind him, and give the blow somewhere to go that is not them. Hit him as hard as you like: he stays where he is, and the ground breaks instead of him. The art is the Greymanes’, and every shield on the Ridgewall knows its first lesson; the name is northern Common, the one the Stark long houses gave to him. His Traits, his techniques and his Release are all that one act done at a different size.

Capabilities

Alone, the act is THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE, and the blow goes into the ground. With a shield it is LOAD PATH. With a king beside him it is THE SWORN WARD, and the king’s blow goes through Godfrey into the ground. With a frightened boy in a chair it is THE FLAT HAND, and what goes to ground is the boy’s shaking. With the Greymane foot beside him it is THE GREY FILE. STONEFAST is the same body made stone to the knee, and the Domain the house is waiting for would be the same law laid over a whole ridge. The idea, the transformed body, the world obeying it.

Petralon, the Foundation Stone, is the lens for nearly all of it, and its idea is the foundation: to become what other things stand on, the load taken, carried and sent down, at the price of slowly settling. Its operation is coagulation, the soul fixed as the foundation, set to bear and never to rise, and it makes his set body one voussoir, one wedge-stone, of an arch whose other end is the world. Masons know the shape of it: a round arch carries a cathedral roof by turning every weight on it into squeeze, each stone pressing on the next down the curve into the footings, and no stone in it is ever asked to stretch. The Measurewrights call the route the weight takes the load path, the squeeze compression, and what a stopped blow carries its momentum. When his feet are planted on ground that is one with the earth beneath it, the weight of a blow, all that has to go somewhere when a moving body is stopped, is not allowed to gather in him as motion. A foundation’s office is to send weight down, so it is handed joint to joint the whole way, out through his heels, and the ground takes it. The ground is very large and he is not, so it is the ground that shows the damage: a ring of cracks, a flagstone sunk an inch, a puff of dust.

It rides a blow in the plainest way there is. He does not win by hitting, and his sheet says so. The real catch lands on the shield and the working goes in with it, so both are true at once: the ash and iron take the dent, and the ground takes the building’s worth of blow that would have gone through the man behind it. Then the short sword comes flat over the rim into whatever the attacker left open by committing, or the ground comes back. Something under half of what he spends reaches the work, which is his yield. The rest burns in the furnace of his Shell and leaves as warmth in his boot soles and knee joints, as the low grinding note, and as the fine grit that drifts down round his feet, which is why a tired Shieldwarden stands in a little pale ring of dust.

Applications

  • Holding a post so that nobody behind him has to
  • Guarding one king
  • Keeping a young king in his chair
  • Anchoring the end of a shield line

Techniques

  • THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE - feet set, and the blow goes into the ground
  • THE SWORN WARD - a blow aimed at the king taken through his own body
  • THE FLAT HAND - a steady hand beside an ally, and the ally’s temper finds it can wait
  • LOAD PATH - the shield squared and every joint in one straight column
  • THE RIDGE GIVES BACK - the ground handing back what it just took
  • THE GREY FILE - shields locked rim to rim, one blow shared down the whole line
  • STONEFAST - the First Release, the body made stone to the knee

Associations

  • Petralon, the Foundation Stone, the Wellspring under the Ridgewall, on which the house has stood for generations
  • Basilithe, the Stone Will, for the one piece that deals in rhythm
  • House Greymane, whose art it is
  • His ground is bedrock and old masonry, and on the Ridgewall’s own stone, where Petralon runs under the floor, he fills faster than anywhere
  • No Domain yet; the house expects that when it comes it will be the ridge

Limitations

  • He cannot take a step while it holds, and everything it spares the people behind him lands on the one body that will not leave the post
  • The law has one condition, and it is the footing: the path has to be unbroken from the point of the blow to the bedrock, and anything that is not one with the ground, loose snow, ice, mud, open water, a deck, a cart, a high stair, breaks it and leaves the load nowhere to go but him
  • A foundation bears whatever presses down on it and was never asked to hold on; masons say the same of stone, which will carry a tower on its back and snap under a tenth of that load hung from it
  • The charge is easy to read for anyone who has been told what to look for: his heels sink, and the dust in the air near him stops drifting and settles straight down
  • The heavy gray of his aura, warm and dark with the ochre of Basilithe only hinted at its edge, thickens toward his boots as if it had weight, with a smell of cut stone, a pressing on the shoulders, and under it the low settling note
  • Against a higher Stage it thins from the ground up: Petralon routes the load and does not make the floor any stronger than the floor is
  • A catch is good to about a building’s worth, the 176 to 275 step on the hidden scale, and a blow well above that breaks the thing he is standing on, after which the path is cut and he is a tall man in the air with a shield
  • A fighter two Stages over him who knows it will hit the floor first and him second
  • It is beaten by never pushing the Shieldwarden: hook the shield and pull, lift him, fight him on ice, or strike him and his king in the same breath
  • Petralon fails by its flaw: a foundation bears whatever presses down on it, and nothing ever asked it to hold on, so the column that holds any push has no strength at all against a pull, and a hooked rim, a lifted belt or a line dragged sideways opens him joint from joint without bending him, with the working still running; the Measurewrights call it tensile exposure
  • Its slow failure is the settling, the price the house accepts for never cracking and the Measurewrights call creep: held too long under too much, he sinks and stiffens without breaking, and comes off a long watch moving like a man twice his age
  • Basilithe fails by its flaw: a soul that holds one note can never change it, even to get out of the way, so nothing that beats against him in another rhythm can shake him, and the one rhythm that sings his own note builds in him with nothing to let it go and rattles him apart; the Measurewrights call it resonant catastrophe
  • The Trait risks he carries: he will not leave a post even when leaving would save it, which is Rigidity, and being made to abandon a ward he was sworn to is his Shear Break condition

Traits

THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE · Primary Trait

THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE is basically immovability: the power to stay exactly where he stands, whatever comes into him. They are his house’s words, become the law of his soul before he could say them.

Capabilities

When his feet are set on solid ground, a load that comes into him does not move him. Petralon hands it down through his bones into the ground, the way an arch carries a cathedral roof into its footings. He goes on standing. The ground under him takes the blow: it cracks in a ring, or settles, or sinks an inch, and a fine dust rises round his boots.

Petralon’s flaw is the pull. A foundation bears whatever presses down on it, and nothing ever asked it to hold on. Push it and it holds; pull it, hook the shield and haul, lift him by the belt, drag him off his line, and it does not give gracefully. It parts. Builders say the same of stone, which carries about ten times in squeeze what it carries in pull, and is why an arch stands for a thousand years and a stone beam cracks across its underside.

Applications

  • Standing under a blow so that the floor cracks in a ring round his boots
  • Keeping a Greymane line of shields from breaking

Associations

  • Petralon is its lens
  • The Body Path; its gates are Fortitude and Fortification on the Body side and Anchoring on the Fate side
  • The house words of the Greymanes

Limitations

  • Its limit is the footing: loose snow, ice, mud, open water, a ship’s deck, a moving cart, a high stair, anything that is not one with the ground beneath, gives the load nowhere to go but him
  • The law routes a load without strengthening the floor: a blow far past what the ground under him can bear breaks the ground, and with the footing gone the path is gone with it
  • It is constant and nearly free while his feet are set
  • Its price is that he cannot move while it holds; a set foot is a planted foot, and lifting it drops the law
  • Its tell is his heels sinking and dust settling round his feet
  • Its counter is written in the Wellspring’s own failure: never push the Shieldwarden; pull him, lift him, or fight him on the ice

THE SWORN WARD · Secondary Trait

THE SWORN WARD is basically damage transfer: the power to take a blow aimed at his king through his own body. It came of the oath his father swore him to on the ridge.

Capabilities

His father swore him on the ridge, the night before he rode south to the Kurenai blade, to carry the secret of Muken’s blood until it could be carried by someone with a better right. Godfrey carried it every morning at the boy’s right hand and said sire and said nothing, and it was the hardest thing he had ever done, and his Crystal kept the shape of it.

It rides the Spirit Axis that formed between him and his king, and the oath, which is why it is the Fate Path’s piece of the art. A bond is a real connection, and Petralon will route a load along it as a builder uses a tie-beam, so the one he is sworn to guard is tied into his own load path like a bay tied to a buttress. Within a few paces, the reach of a long stride, with his feet set, a blow that reaches the king is carried across the bond into Godfrey’s set body and down into the ground. The king keeps a shove. Godfrey keeps the rest, on whatever part of him the path happens to run through, braced or not. It pulls no thread down. The bond was already there.

Applications

  • Taking a blow meant for Muken, so that the king feels only a shove

Associations

  • Petralon is its lens
  • The walked Fate Path: a bond and an oath, landing through the Body; its gate is Oath
  • The Spirit Axis between him and Muken, and his father Wystan’s oath

Limitations

  • He can hold one ward, and the ward is Muken
  • It reaches a long stride, and it needs his feet set
  • It moves weight and nothing else: a blade’s edge still has to be met by something, so he turns the shield or his own shoulder into the line as the load arrives
  • A working that goes past the body to the soul is not a load and does not cross
  • Each catch costs him the full hit, landing on a body that may not be braced for it, and about one and a half percent of his reserve on top
  • Its tell is his eyes going to the ward an instant before any blow lands
  • It is beaten by two blows at once, one to the king and one to the warden, or by a strike to Godfrey first, while he is still turning to the king

THE FLAT HAND · Passive Trait

THE FLAT HAND is basically a calming presence: the power to steady whoever sits beside him. It is the one piece of the art that deals in rhythm and not in weight.

Capabilities

Basilithe, the Stone Will, is one note the soul holds that nobody can retune, and its operation is coagulation, the soul fixed on that note past any retuning, so no rhythm outside him can drive his own. Builders know the danger it answers: a push timed to the rate a thing sways at builds on itself, which is how a marching column can shake a bridge down. The Measurewrights call the rate a natural frequency and the building resonance.

A tall tower in a gale is steadied by a great weight hung near its top and swinging against the tower’s sway, and Godfrey is that weight for whoever sits beside him; the Measurewrights call it a tuned mass damper. A soul that cannot be retuned is a standard for other people, and his Stability spreads his note a little way: within arm’s reach, a frightened or furious ally’s pulse and breath start to fall in with his, and the urge to stand up or lash out comes on slower.

Applications

  • Letting a young king sit through a room full of enemies without moving, because a long gray hand has come down flat on the pine beside his own

Associations

  • Basilithe is its lens
  • The walked Fate Path: presence, the plainest thing that Path gives; its gate is Gravity
  • His Stability, which spreads it

Limitations

  • It never touches, and it never compels; it only steadies, and anyone who decides to stand up stands up
  • It reaches an arm’s length, and it needs the ally’s own willingness
  • It cannot steady Godfrey himself, who has nobody’s hand beside his
  • It costs nothing
  • Its tell is a long gray hand laid flat beside someone, not touching
  • It is beaten by distance: separate them, and the king’s temper is his own again

Techniques

LOAD PATH · Signature Technique, Greymane Shield Art

LOAD PATH is basically shield blocking: the power to send a blow that lands on his shield straight down into the floor. It is the house’s first lesson and his best.

Capabilities

He squares the Greymane shield into the blow, sinks his knees, and sets every joint from the shield-boss down through the forearm, the shoulder, the spine, the hips and the knees to the heel into one straight column, so the blow’s weight goes down the column into the earth, and his body is only the road it travels.

It is the Primary Trait given a tool and a drill. The Trait routes whatever reaches his body, and the technique chooses where the blow reaches him and lines the bones up behind that point, so the road is straight and nothing in it is asked to bend. The shield still takes the dent. What it is spared is being driven back through the arm that holds it.

Applications

  • Sending a blow that would bring down a house into the floor, spent in cracking the stone round his feet

Associations

  • Petralon is its lens
  • The Body Path; its gates are Fortitude and Anchoring
  • THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE, which it gives a tool and a drill
  • The Greymane shield
  • Taught by the house, and mastered

Limitations

  • It needs the footing, as his Primary Trait does
  • A column has one line: a blow that comes from the side of it, or two that come at once, shears it
  • Each catch takes about one and a half percent of his reserve
  • Its tell is the squared shield and the knees going down before the hit
  • Its counter is the one every Greymane is taught to fear, the hook: catch the shield’s rim with an axe-beard or a hand, and pull, and the column that holds any push parts at the one load a foundation was never asked to bear
  • Before the catch, stay off his line and attack the flank; during it, hook and pull; after it, he must reset his feet, and that reset is a beat

THE RIDGE GIVES BACK · Signature Technique

THE RIDGE GIVES BACK is basically attack reversal: the power to hand a blow back to the man who threw it. It is the only strong blow he owns, and every grain of it was the other man’s first.

Capabilities

It is the foundation’s idea turned round: what was laid on it is still there in it. Petralon keeps the last catch’s load in the ground under his feet for a breath or two, the way a bent bow keeps the pull, and then he may let it all go at once in one direction, up and forward, under the attacker’s feet and behind his own shield. The Measurewrights call what the rock holds elastic strain energy.

The ground heaves up under the attacker in a cracked ring, and the shield comes forward with the earth’s own rebound behind it in a bash that can throw a man in plate across a yard. At the most it is a rockfall’s worth.

Applications

  • Throwing a man in plate across a yard
  • Breaking the ground under an attacker’s feet

Associations

  • Petralon is its lens
  • The Body Path, with no gates beyond the Trait’s
  • The catch before it, by THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE or LOAD PATH
  • Drilled

Limitations

  • It can give back only what the ground took; with no catch before it, there is nothing to return
  • It has to leave by the same feet that took it in, so he cannot give back on ground that would not have held the catch
  • The ground holds the load only a breath or two
  • It takes about six percent of his reserve
  • For the instant of the bash his weight is going forward off his heels, which is the one moment in his whole art when a push will move him
  • Its tell is the ring of cracks running out from his boots a heartbeat before he moves
  • It is beaten by giving him nothing to give back: do not strike his shield, strike round it, or wait out the two breaths until the ground lets go of the load by itself

THE GREY FILE · House Art, Greymane

THE GREY FILE is basically a shield wall: the power to share one blow down a whole line of men. It is the art of the Greymane foot.

Capabilities

The Greymane foot lock their shields rim to rim in a long gray line, and Petralon in each of them ties into the next, so a blow on any shield runs along the whole file and down through every set of boots in it. The locked rims are the tie: a rim proud enough to seat against its neighbor carries the squeeze sideways from shield to shield, as the stones of a wall share a weight laid on one of them.

Applications

  • Holding a line that does not break under a blow on one man
  • Holding the end, which is the Shieldwarden’s work, because he is the man the end can be trusted to

Associations

  • Petralon is its lens, in every man of the file
  • The Body Path, each man tied to the next by the house’s drill
  • House Greymane, which teaches it; he has it mastered

Limitations

  • The file is as strong as its weakest pair of feet and as long as its ends
  • It asks the same footing of every pair of boots, and a file drawn up across a frozen ford is so many men on their own
  • Each man pays his own catch and no more
  • Its tell is shields locking rim to rim
  • It is beaten by breaking the file’s end, where the load has only one man to go through

Releases

STONEFAST · First Release

STONEFAST is basically stone armor: the power to become part of the floor, stone to the knee. The folk call it Setting the Stone.

Capabilities

The gray wolf-fur of his mantle stiffens and grays and sets into layered stone-scale over his shoulders like slate on a roof, a skin of ridge-stone climbs from his boots to his knees, his amber-hinted aura flares to the full of what Flourishing allows, and the ground under him groans as Petralon takes him into it. THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE strikes at the top of its base, as far as a soul below Glory can carry it.

As a Body release it changes the body and nothing past it. The stone is his own Shell thickened onto cloth, fur and skin, real slate to the hand and the weight of it too, and while it holds he is part of the floor in fact as well as in law, so the catches that cracked the ground before now only make it groan.

Applications

  • Taking catches that would have cracked the ground
  • Holding off the settling for as long as it lasts

Techniques

  • Called - low in northern Common, never shouted, the way his father called it, for the full rise
  • Silent - weaker: the stone stays at his boots and never reaches the shoulders

Associations

  • Petralon is its lens
  • The Body Path; it is his Stage IV release
  • THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE, which it lifts
  • The Greymane shield, his bonded weapon, carries STONEFAST’s load path

Limitations

  • It lasts about two and a half minutes from full, and ends at a tenth of his reserve
  • When it ends the stone-scale lets go and slides off him as grit, and the settling it was holding off arrives all at once in his knees and back
  • With the shield taken from him, the release holds his body but not the line in front of it
  • Its counter is the same as its Trait’s, and worse: a man set in stone to the knee cannot step off a pull at all
