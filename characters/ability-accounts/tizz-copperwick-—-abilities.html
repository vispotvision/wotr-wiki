Tizz Copperwick — Abilities in full

Tizz Copperwick — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20268 min read

Card: Tizz Copperwick

Techniques

THE LONG ARM, MARK FOUR · Signature art, a Tinker Goblin’s Spliced Runes

THE LONG ARM, MARK FOUR is basically ratcheted strength: the power to win ground one click at a time and never lose any of it. She built a ratchet into her own elbow, and she cut the idea into herself at fifteen with a mirror and a hot needle: a goblin’s temper comes in beats, so build a thing that keeps each beat. She has no Traits of her own; what she has by blood is her people’s.

Capabilities

Tinker Goblins are souls with more pressure in them than the frame can hold, and their people’s answer is the Spliced Rune, a line of marks cut into one limb that gives the pressure a road to run on. Hers begin in a copper socket at the left shoulder, cut close and fine as a page of writing, and run on out of the metal into her own skin as pale blue scars up her neck, so that nobody can say where she ends and the arm begins. Everything the arm does is her Essence sent down those marks, with a ratchet at the elbow deciding what is kept. Essence is spiritual energy: the ground’s raw Aether, which carters call mana, after the Crystal behind her breastbone has set her own color in it.

A ratchet is a toothed wheel with a small iron finger, the pawl, riding on it. The wheel may turn one way, and the moment it tries to turn back the pawl drops behind a tooth and stops it. She drives her Essence down the runes in beats, like a heart, and each beat is a pulse of blue that buys one tooth while the pawl keeps it. Between beats the load rests on the pawl, the arm rests on the load, and she rests. The arm adds no strength; it only keeps her from losing ground. That is how a goblin of three foot five lifts the four-ton outer grille of her gate by the inch and never holds it. The gate helps, because the Copperwicks built it to: the grille hangs against a counterweight a hair under four ton, so all anyone lifts is the hair, about ten stone, and the drag of a dozen chains in their wheels, and the windlass crank is as long as a man’s arm, which trades distance for force the way any lever does.

Anyone watching can read it coming. She sets her boots wide, closes the copper fingers on the work one by one, and winds the brass key in her elbow three full turns with her burned hand, until the pawl sits quivering at the top of its wheel. Then her eyes light, every window at once, bright enough to throw her shadow behind her, and she breathes in long. The breath is the pull: the air round her goes thin and cold and the dust on the floor stirs toward her boots. The whole arm lights from the shoulder down in hard blue lines like the leading of a church window with the sun behind it, and her colors come up close on her like a second coat.

Less than half the pull reaches the crank, and that share is her yield. The rest burns in the arm as heat, the way a rope run fast through a fist burns the fist, and the copper shows it honestly. Hot copper grows a skin of tarnish, thicker the hotter it gets, and light coming back through a skin that thin is colored by how thick it is, as oil is on a puddle, so the copper tempers straw to purple-blue, sliding from the elbow and the knuckles toward her shoulder as the beats go on. With no load on the arm a small beat has nothing to turn, so all of it arrives as heat at the end of the line, which is how the thumb melts wax.

The arm is also her temper’s safety valve, by Ned’s inference: the runes carry her temper out of her as well as into the arm. A Tinker Goblin who loses her temper can go off all at once and take the room along, which scholars call Emotional Detonation and Tollgarth calls a reason to keep buckets by the door. The arm is a place to put it, a crank she can turn it into, one tooth at a time.

Applications

  • Lifting the four-ton outer grille of her own gate, a finger’s breadth of grille to a tooth
  • Melting sealing wax with the copper thumb and signing it in the same touch, since the ball of the thumb is cut flat and engraved
  • Letting her temper out as a spin and not as a blast

Techniques

  • BY THE INCH! - boots planted, the copper hand shut on the work, and the arm turns a tooth at a beat with a pulse of blue down the runes, the pawl keeping every tooth
  • THAT’S A DEED! - one short turn of the key and the copper thumb goes dull red from nail to knuckle; on wax it is the Queen’s seal, and nobody has yet asked what it is on a hand
  • MIND YOUR TEMPER - every window of her eyes goes dark, the key goes back one full turn against the ratchet, the pawl lifts and the wheel runs down; she does not shout this one

Associations

  • The Spliced Runes of the Tinker Goblins, her people’s craft and her own cutting, in the Form of a Make
  • The made left arm itself: black enamel over copper, a saucer-sized ratchet wheel with its iron pawl at the elbow and a brass wind-key standing out of the joint; it is built into her shoulder and is no bonded weapon
  • By her own word the arm is “Rare, if it’s anything”, and it has never been put on a beam
  • The Tollgarth gate, counterweighted by the Copperwicks so that it can be lifted this way
  • Her seal, a candle with a crown on its flame; nobody steals the Queen of Tollgarth’s seal without taking the Queen of Tollgarth’s arm
  • Her colors are the clean hot blue at the root of a solder flame, close to the skin, with a smell of hot metal, burned vitriol and scorched paper
  • The Tinker temper, which is her people’s by blood and is the arm’s fuel
  • Her Stage and Path are not given

Limitations

  • The wheel’s own: it cannot move a thing by less than one tooth or faster than one beat, and it goes one way only until the key is turned back
  • By the wheel’s own law it is slow, a tooth to a breath and three turns of a key first, so it is beaten by being finished before she is wound
  • Lift the pawl and everything it has kept runs back at once
  • Poor ground starves it: at a crowded gate, in a town where every goblin has been pulling at the same hills since sunup, the breath has to run very long before she has her fill
  • Less than half the pull reaches the work, and the rest is heat: after the copper tempers, the black enamel blisters and smokes, her sweat hisses where it drops on her own wrist, she breathes a breath to a tooth, and her colors thin until the coat shows through them
  • The room answers the heat too: the far wall wavers through the blue as a road wavers in summer, the smell of scorched paper and burned vitriol comes off her in a wave, and every little spring bell in the tower rings faintly by itself
  • Copper that has gone blue and enamel that has blistered are an arm run past what it was built for
  • The fuel is her temper, which is the thing she has to mind to see forty; by her own count she has seven years in her only if she minds it, and she says she never does
  • BY THE INCH! shows as blue pulses down the runes and a tooth of the elbow ratchet at each beat
  • THAT’S A DEED! shows as one short turn of the key and blue in the neck scars for no longer than a breath, with her own breath coming short the once, as if she had run up a stair; it is one thumb on a short arm, and the way to beat it is to stay off the end of it
  • MIND YOUR TEMPER has to be reached in time, and a valve can fill faster than a hand can reach the key; no counter is wanted, since everybody in the room is hoping it works
  • When the temper is filling the air goes tight, the smells sharpen, the rune scars light thin blue and the key turns by itself, and all Tollgarth knows the answer: two paces back and a bucket chain
