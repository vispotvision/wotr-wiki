Hayato Amagiri — Abilities in full

Hayato Amagiri — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 202620 min read

Card: Hayato Amagiri

THE INDIGO ROADS

THE INDIGO ROADS (藍の道 · Ai no Michi) is basically path manipulation: the power to own every road he has ever walked, and to decide where it goes. It is his signature art, and his Traits and techniques are this one idea. The eye is the Moto blood’s and the mist is the Amagiri blood’s; in him both were turned to the road, so the north will one day use the one name for all of it.

Capabilities

Every piece is the same act at a different size: know the way, and own it. Like any practitioner he pulls Aether (mana, in the common word) out of the ground he stands on, refines it in the Soul Crystal into Essence (spiritual energy), and drives it out through the layer the working belongs to. His ground is the kind both his Wellsprings favor: a well-traveled road, wet air, moving water, cold. Phreatis, the Waters of Memory, is the keeping lens. Its idea is memory as deep water, and its operation is dissolution: what is lived is taken down and held, and what flows remembers the shape of what it flowed through, the way water that fell as rain reaches a well decades later still carrying the hills it came down. So every road his feet have been on, and every road an Amagiri before him walked and wrote down, lies in him the way a riverbed lies under the river, whole, with its fords and its bends and how long a mule takes on each.

Transference, the Give-and-Take, is the carrying lens. Its idea is that two things bound and in tune can give and take with nothing carried between them, and its operation is distillation, what one holds rising out of it and coming down in the other. Scholars compare it to two bells of one pitch: strike the first and the second rings across the room, and nothing visible has crossed. A road he has walked is exactly such a pair, its two ends joined by his having gone from one to the other, and held in tune by the memory Phreatis keeps of everything between. So on that road the distance between the ends answers to him. He can be at any point of it without the riding, he can say that nothing shall pass a line across it. Both lenses descend from the Titan Sylorin, the river that joins two souls with no body making the trip, which is why the two sit so easily in one man.

The two bloods do the rest. The Amagiri inheritance is mist: on a road, in air that will hold water, the house thins the light round itself until a column reads as weather. Mist is real water, tiny drops hung in air that has cooled to the point where it can hold no more, which the Measurewrights call the dew point. Warm that air and it can carry more water unseen, and the drops go back to vapor, which is all that is happening when the sun burns fog off a valley. The Moto inheritance is the Shingan, the heart-eye, which shows where a thing is carrying its weight. About nine parts in ten of what he spends reach the work, which is his yield. The lost tenth is paid in his Core, and what shows of it comes off him as cold and wet, a fine chill haze, the very mist his blood hides in, so that the harder he works the more weather there is round him.

Applications

  • Knowing the way through any ground he has walked, and how long it takes
  • Going down a walked road faster than he could ride it, with whoever he carries
  • Hiding everyone on a road as weather
  • Shutting a road along one line

Techniques

  • THE ROAD UNDER THE ROAD - the Trait that knows the way
  • RAIN MIST - the Amagiri blood’s cover, on the road
  • NOW I KNOW WHICH WAY - the road once chosen, not turned off
  • AMAGIRI-ASHI - the Step, which goes down the road
  • THE ROAD’S OPEN - the Step held open for others
  • MICHI-KIRI - the Cut, which shuts the road
  • SHINGAN - the Moto eye, which says whether the road will hold a horse today

Associations

  • Phreatis, the Waters of Memory, the keeping lens
  • Transference, the Give-and-Take, a Wellspring of Fate, the carrying lens
  • The Titan Sylorin, from whom both descend
  • The Spirit Path for the read, the keeping and the mist; his Walked Fate Path for the road that answers and every claim on ground; a Studied Body Path for the one stroke of the sword-arm
  • The Amagiri blood, which gives the mist, and the Moto blood, which gives the eye
  • The plain sword, his bonded weapon, and the gilt fox mask

Limitations

  • His power is only as wide as his feet have made it
  • Every piece that rides on mist dies in heat, in a hard dry wind and in strong sun, and is strongest at night, in the cold, beside water
  • When the mist goes he is left standing in the open wherever it ran out, a slight man in a rain-cape with no armor worth the name
  • The charge is small and always the same hand: his finger draws the road first, on a table, in the wet ring of a cup, in dust, in the air, and when the finger stops he has chosen
  • Before he works the gold flecks in his haze turn like moths finding a lamp, the air beads on cold iron, there is a smell of cellar stone and a mineral taste on the tongue, and the sound that was rain on a cape widens until it is rain on a whole forest, though no rain is falling
  • As his reserve falls, at half his breath comes short and the gray haze thins, at a quarter his fingers stop moving entirely, and at a tenth the mist round him lifts all at once and the gold flecks in it go out
  • It is beaten by fighting him on ground he has never walked, by bringing heat or a hard dry wind, by going round the ends of the line, or by sending what crosses without traveling
  • Phreatis fails by its flaw: deep water does not ask what is sinking into it, so it keeps whatever enters, the bad water with the good, and nothing can be taken back out; the Measurewrights call it contamination of the aquifer, and so he cannot forget a road, a count or a morning in the Hall of Verdicts
  • A road that has changed since he walked it comes back to him as it was and betrays him
  • Transference fails by its flaw: the exchange gives toward whoever has less and never asks who is giving, and run backward it carries off the one who opened it, which the Measurewrights call reverse coupling
  • The mist fails as mist does, burned off the ground by heat, torn by wind, thinned by daylight

Traits

He carries two Trait risks. Rigidity: once committed, he will ride a road after it has stopped going anywhere. His Shear Break condition is carrying something down his roads that he swore they would not carry.

THE ROAD UNDER THE ROAD · Primary Trait

THE ROAD UNDER THE ROAD is basically path detection: the power to read any ground for the way through it, and to keep every yard of it for good. It is the Trait that knows the way.

Capabilities

His soul reads ground for passage before it reads anything else: where a column can go, where a mule can carry two sacks in snow and where it must go single file, which herders’ shoulder the smugglers never had a reason to find, which deer track comes down onto the north hill of a house that did not know it had a back door. Everything he has walked stays. Phreatis keeps it the way a riverbed keeps the river, Retention keeps it whole, and Forecast turns it into time, two days, a day and a half, and he is always right.

The keeping is not his alone. Every road every Amagiri walked and wrote down since the house began lies in him through the blood’s memory, and it comes up whole when he reaches for it, the way a riverbed shows when the river runs low. At Dissonance it is more than a map. On a road he has walked, Transference treats the road as his: he can be anywhere along it faster than he could ride it, and so can whatever he carries.

Applications

  • Saying where a column can go and how long it will take
  • Knowing the paces between any two points of a walked road without measuring them
  • Being anywhere along a walked road faster than he could ride it
  • Reading through water as well as the eye: in air thick with steam he feels the shape and weight of a fighter moving through it, the way a fish feels a stone in the current

Associations

  • Phreatis, with Transference
  • The Spirit Path for the read and the keeping, with his Walked Fate Path’s share for the road that answers
  • The Amagiri blood’s memory of the house’s roads
  • His Retention and Forecast

Limitations

  • It reads only ground he has ridden or walked, never a map, however much he trusts it
  • In the Wellspring’s own terms, Transference pools on well-traveled roads and thins to nothing on unmapped ground
  • It reads ground and not men: he can bring twenty riders to any door in the north and has no idea what will be waiting behind it
  • It costs nothing to read, and he cannot stop doing it
  • The finger gives it away: it draws roads on tables, in wet rings, in the air
  • Country he has never walked beats it, and so does a road changed since he last walked it

RAIN MIST (雨霧 · Amagiri) · Inherited Trait

RAIN MIST is basically mist camouflage: the power to make himself and everyone riding with him look like weather. The Amagiri name means rain-mist, and their blood earns it.

Capabilities

On a road at night, in wet or cold air, the Amagiri thin the light round themselves and the people riding with them: a party with him goes gray at the edges and hard to fix in the eye from a wall or a hold, and a sentry looking straight at the track sees weather. It is the oldest thing the line does. The blood gives the mist and a lean toward the wet ground it stands in, and never a Path.

Applications

  • Hiding a party on a road at night from a wall or a hold

Associations

  • The Amagiri blood’s inheritance, with no Wellspring
  • The Spirit Path, the mist laid over what he is and over those riding with him

Limitations

  • It hides from eyes and readings and only from them, and best on the move, in the dark or the wet
  • In a hall, in daylight, standing still, it thins to almost nothing
  • It fails as fog fails: air that is warmed can hold more water unseen, the drops go back to vapor, and the cover is gone from the ground upward with nothing left to gather
  • It will not hold within ten yards of a fighter who walks in furnace heat; there it boils away round him in white rags and leaves him standing in the open like a man caught on a hillside when the mist lifts
  • It costs about two percent of his reserve an hour on the move, less than he recovers
  • To anyone who knows to look, it shows as a patch of road where the mist is thicker than the weather explains
  • Daylight, dogs that smell what they cannot see, a lantern on a pole, and heat beat it
  • When he calls KIRISAME the mist comes off him and goes out over the ground

NOW I KNOW WHICH WAY · Secondary Trait

NOW I KNOW WHICH WAY is basically indomitable will: the power to stay on a road once he has chosen it. It formed in Kiyome Byakuya’s white house the night he admitted he had spent a year mapping the roads out and had only been getting ready to run.

Capabilities

Once he has chosen his road he does not turn off it: fear still reaches him, and his hands still shake, but his feet do not stop. It pulls nothing down and moves nothing outside him. It is the processed half of the Verdict of Six, a fracture made into a feature, his own share of Fate spent the way a kept word is, and it binds only the man who chose.

Applications

  • Riding at the front of six hundred horse with his sword out, though he holds it badly

Associations

  • His Walked Fate Path, with no lens
  • The Verdict of Six, the fracture it was made from

Limitations

  • Its limit is the choosing: until he has picked his road he is the most hesitant man in any room, glancing at the door
  • It costs nothing to choose and everything to keep
  • His restless fingers going still show that he has chosen
  • Put two roads in front of him at once

Techniques

AMAGIRI-ASHI (雨霧足 · the Rain-Mist Step) · Bloodline Art, Amagiri

AMAGIRI-ASHI is basically mist-form travel: the power to go to cold fog and run a road he has walked the way fog runs down a river valley. It is the Step, the art going down the road.

Capabilities

On a road he has walked, at night or in wet air, he goes to mist. The edges first, the haze round him thickening and the man inside it going gray, and then all of him, body and coat and sword and whatever he holds, gone into a fine cold fog that moves along the road the way fog moves along a river valley at dusk, low and quick and following every bend, and comes back together at the far end as a slight young man in an indigo rain-cape, apologizing.

The mist is the blood’s cover and the road does the carrying: Transference hands him from one point of a walked road to the next, bend by bend, and what an onlooker sees is fog moving faster than fog should. His body is never flown through the air as vapor.

Applications

  • Crossing the north in a night
  • Taking passengers: he has carried two Grandmasters with him at once, the three of them gone to cold gray fog along every bend the house had written down
  • Setting a passenger down at a dead run at the far end

Associations

  • The Amagiri blood for the mist, Transference for the carrying, Phreatis for the road known
  • The Spirit Path, with his Walked Fate Path’s share for the road that carries
  • THE ROAD’S OPEN, which is the Step held instead of taken

Limitations

  • Its limits are the mist’s: it needs a road he knows, and it needs air that will hold water
  • It will not hold in heat, in a hard dry wind or in strong sunlight, which burn it off the ground and leave him standing where the mist ran out
  • It costs about a fifth of a percent of his reserve a league, which is why he can cross the north in a night and does not, since a quartermaster who arrives before his message raises questions
  • Before the Step he is a little grayer at the edges than the weather explains, the haze thickening from the hem of the cape upward
  • Heat, wind, daylight and strange ground beat it

THE ROAD’S OPEN · Technique

THE ROAD’S OPEN is basically passage holding: the power to hold the way along a road open like a door, for others to go through. It is the Rain-Mist Step, held instead of taken, and it is his own phrase, said to a king over his shoulder; nobody taught it to him.

Capabilities

He stands at a bend of a walked road with his left hand out behind him, palm open, the way a man holds a door, and the fog of the road stays standing at his back with the way through it kept, for as long as the mist will stand. Anyone he names can step into it and be carried, without waiting on him to go to mist first.

Applications

  • Carrying the people he names down a road without going with them
  • Keeping a way back open

Associations

  • The Amagiri blood, Transference and Phreatis, as the Step
  • The Spirit Path, with his Walked Fate Path’s share
  • AMAGIRI-ASHI, whose price it pays

Limitations

  • Its limits are the mist’s and the road’s: it stands only at the edge of where fog will hold, and only on a road he still has
  • He pays the Step’s price for the stretch he is holding, again every minute it stands
  • He pays with his feet too, because the open end is wherever he is and he cannot leave it
  • The hand gives it away: his left hand held out behind him, palm open, and fog standing at one bend of a road
  • Burn the mist off that bend, or move him off it

MICHI-KIRI (道切り · the Road-Cut) · Signature Technique

MICHI-KIRI is basically passage negation: the power to close a road along the line of one cut, so that whatever is traveling across it arrives and goes no further. It is the Amagiri draw, which his grandfather taught him in a barn with the doors shut.

Capabilities

The name is the old country word for the rope and straw hung across a village road to turn back whatever comes down it at night; the Amagiri kept the custom and lost the rope. He draws the plain sword, the one time he ever draws it, and cuts once across the air in one long flat line, from the left hip, with the whole arm level, at the height of a man’s chest, making almost no sound. The draw is a real cut, a slight man’s whole arm, and it would open a throat if a throat were there. The working goes out along the path the edge travels and for as far past the point as his Radius will carry it, and Transference closes the road along that line.

Whatever is traveling across it, a blade, an arrow, a man running, a charge of horse, a river’s flow, a working’s current, stops traveling. It rides one blow, and the blow strikes nothing. A rider does not hit a wall, because there is no wall, and nothing pushes back on him: his way forward simply ends at the line, the way a river’s ends at a weir, and everything he was carrying in the way of speed stands there with him, pooled.

Sheathing the sword opens the road like a gate, and whatever was pressed against it stumbles forward into the place where it had been, a step late and off balance.

Applications

  • Stopping a charge: cut across a wing of horse at three hundred yards, it has stopped four hundred riders against nothing in the snow
  • Stopping a blade, an arrow, a running man, a river’s flow or a working’s current
  • Holding a thing at the line until he chooses to let the road open

Associations

  • Transference, which closes the road
  • His Walked Fate Path: a claim along one line, carried on a Studied Body’s single stroke
  • The plain sword, his bonded weapon, which carries it in its edge
  • His Radius, which sets how far past the point it reaches
  • His grandfather, who taught it and told him never to use it where anyone could count

Limitations

  • It closes only what travels: heat, light and sound are no travelers with two ends to their journey, only the world’s old habit of passing from one bit of air to the next, so the exchange has nothing to refuse
  • A furnace stopped a hand’s width from a shield still blisters the man behind it, and a furnace on the far side of his line cooks the people behind it as if the line were not there
  • A man standing still on the line is not moved off it
  • The line is as long as one cut of a slight man’s arm and the reach of his Radius, never a wall round a city
  • It costs about five percent of his reserve to draw, and it holds a minute from the draw
  • The line lives in his sword-hand: its near end is where he stands, and if he steps off that end the end moves with him, so a cut that is holding something he cannot afford to let through pins him as surely as it pins the thing
  • The sword out is the tell
  • Go round its ends, or bring something that crosses without traveling
  • A fighter stopped by it can walk along the closed road with his fist against it, feeling for the end the way a man feels along a fence for the gate, toward the slight man standing at it

SHINGAN (心眼 · the heart-eye) · Bloodline Faculty, Moto

SHINGAN, turned on the ground, is basically load perception: the power to see where a slope, a ford or a wall will bear a horse and where it will give. It is the common Moto sight, which shows where a thing is carrying its weight.

Capabilities

In Hayato it turned to the ground early and stayed there, and he reads a slope, a bank, a frozen ford or the mended seam in a wall for where it will bear a horse and where it will give, before he reads the faces in front of it. In him it is the surveyor’s half of the art: the Trait says where a road goes, and the eye says whether it will hold a horse today.

Applications

  • Telling him that a deer track will take a horse
  • Reading a frozen ford, a bank or a mended wall before he trusts it

Associations

  • The Moto blood, with no lens; the blood gives the eye and never a Path
  • It spends his own share and pulls nothing down
  • THE ROAD UNDER THE ROAD, whose other half it is

Limitations

  • It resolves load in ground and stone; it reads men poorly, and the Torvald he read at the Verdict of Six he read with the other five
  • Held open it lasts nearly three hours from a full reserve, paid as a squint that never quite leaves the corners of his eyes
  • It shows as his eyes going to a wall’s mended seam before the people in front of it
  • Soft ground with nothing in it carrying weight gives it nothing: bog, deep snow, open water
