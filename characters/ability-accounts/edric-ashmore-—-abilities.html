Edric Ashmore — Abilities in full

Edric Ashmore — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20269 min read

Card: Edric Ashmore

THE UNTOUCHED WINE

THE UNTOUCHED WINE is basically indomitable will: the power to hold every answer back until the whole thing has been weighed, and then to spend all of himself on the answer. It is his signature art. He won’t say yes until the wine has gone cold, and once he says it nothing on earth turns his horse.

Capabilities

It is one idea, and a grain lord’s: weigh the sack before you put the seal on it, and once the seal is on, the sack is sold. Ashmore has lived for twenty years by owning the bread at tables where other men owned the roads, and a man in that seat who says yes quickly gets eaten. So his soul learned to keep every answer back until the whole of the thing has been looked at, and then to spend all of itself on the answer.

The wine is the weighing, the stone is the answer made fast, and the Line is the answer delivered at the gallop. None of the three is a Wellspring’s work. He has harmonized no lens for them. They are a habit of the soul, a custom of his house and a way of riding, and they take their limits from themselves.

Applications

  • Weighing a thing whole before answering
  • Making the answer fast once it is given
  • Delivering it with everything he has

Techniques

  • THE UNTOUCHED WINE - the weighing, during which nothing moves him
  • THE JUNCTION STONE - the answer made fast, a promise set on stone
  • THE ASHMORE LINE - the answer delivered at the gallop

Associations

  • No Wellspring and no lens: a habit of the soul, a custom of the house and a way of riding
  • The Body Path does the Line
  • The Fate Path carries the stone and the Pressure

Limitations

  • While he weighs, the chance can ride straight past him
  • Once he goes, he cannot turn, even when he should

Traits

THE UNTOUCHED WINE · Primary Trait

THE UNTOUCHED WINE is basically persuasion immunity: the power to be moved by no bribe, threat or shove while he is weighing a thing. He does not move until he has weighed it all, and then he does not stop.

Capabilities

What a watcher sees is a man gone still: the dark eyes on the map and not on the man holding it, the hands empty, the cup at his elbow losing its steam. Under the stillness the russet of his aura draws in close around him, the way a horse gathers before a fence.

A man is rushed into a bad bargain by a handful of ordinary levers: a price that will be gone by morning, a threat, a flattery, a crowd already nodding. Each of them works by getting the body to answer before the head has finished. In him the answer is simply not there to be had yet.

His Dominion (the stat of a soul’s command over what stands around it) sits heaviest of all his Primaries, and while he weighs it is turned inward and holds his own yes and no the way it will later hold six hundred horses, so that a bribe or a threat lands on him like rain on a slate roof. Once he has committed, his horse goes in at full tilt and does not turn.

Applications

  • Reading a map, a price and the man holding them for as long as it takes, and giving nothing away while he does
  • Sitting across a table from Tenrai factors without being hurried
  • Going in whole once the weighing is done

Associations

  • His Dominion, turned inward
  • His own hall, across the table from Tenrai factors
  • The cup of wine at his elbow

Limitations

  • Its limit is time: a man who needs his answer before the wine is poured gets no answer at all
  • A battle that will not wait for the cup to cool goes on without Ashmore in it
  • It costs nothing, because nothing is made or thrown; no reserve leaves him
  • Its tell is the cup going cold
  • To beat it, make him decide fast: put the thing in front of him already moving, with no table, no map and no steward, so that not answering is itself the answer

THE JUNCTION STONE · Secondary Trait

THE JUNCTION STONE is basically oath binding: the power to make a promise with his hand flat on stone that sets like the stone, so that nobody talks, buys or frightens it loose. Ashmore oaths are sworn hand-on-stone at the crossroads below Ashbury, and the stone holds them.

Capabilities

A vow is the small end of Fate, and anybody’s to make: the soul writes the terms down where it cannot pretend later that it did not, and breaking them lands on the breaker at once, by those same terms.

What the stone adds is a place to stand the promise. He walks the Fate Path a little under half his practice, enough to feel a promise as a thing with weight, and when the flat hand goes down he ties the words to one object that will still be there tomorrow, hard and cold and exactly where he left it. After that, every attempt to talk the oath round, buy it back or turn it against him has to shift the stone’s weight as well as his. His Oath (the Sub-Stat that says how well a vow stands up to being cut, twisted or used against its maker) reads as if he were still standing at the crossroads with his palm down.

He swore there to Hayato in the king’s name, and swore again with his hand on the black stone of the dais, as he had on the junction stone.

Applications

  • Swearing to Hayato in the king’s name at the junction stone
  • Swearing again on the black stone of the dais
  • Keeping a promise against talk, coin and fear

Associations

  • The Fate Path, as a binding
  • The junction stone at the crossroads below Ashbury, where his house swears
  • His Oath Sub-Stat

Limitations

  • The limit is the stone’s: it holds one promise at a time cleanly
  • Two set on the same stone grind, each wearing at the terms of the other, until the weaker cracks or he is left keeping both badly
  • It gives him no strength of arm and no step on the hidden scale; it only makes the promise as hard to move as he was while he was weighing it
  • It takes no reserve
  • Its tell is the flat hand
  • To beat it, set two oaths against each other: get a second promise out of him on the same stone that cannot be kept beside the first

Techniques

THE ASHMORE LINE · House Art, Ashmore · Signature

THE ASHMORE LINE is basically cavalry charge command: the power to bring five or six hundred big southern horses in at the gallop in one long ragged line, with his will holding it together and the weight of him rolling out in front. He calls it with two words, said once: “My horse.” Every Ashmore rider is taught the Line; only the lord’s Command and Pressure make it what hit the Skeldmere.

Capabilities

The riders come with lances and long swords out of the Kessen road. The charge is visible from a long way off and is meant to be: the russet coming over the rise, picked so that anyone across five miles of snow can tell whose horse it is, then the pennants dropping level as the lances come down, and the sound of it arriving before the riders do.

A war-horse and an armored man together weigh well over half a ton, and at the gallop the whole of that weight is traveling behind one hand’s breadth of steel at the end of an ash pole tucked hard under the arm, so that the rider is less a man with a spear than the point of a moving wall. That is momentum (weight multiplied by speed), and a standing man has none to answer it with. But the old truth of horse against foot is that the blow itself does a small part of the work. A line of men that stays shoulder to shoulder with its points out will stop horses, because a horse will not run onto a hedge of spikes. A charge wins when the men in front of it look at what is coming and decide, each one separately, to be somewhere else, and then the gaps open and the horses pour through them.

That is where the lord comes in. Command keeps six hundred frightened animals and six hundred frightened riders together and at one pace, ragged as the line looks, so that it arrives all at once and nobody hangs back half a length. Pressure, the plain weight of a Stage V soul that has stopped being polite, rolls out ahead of the hooves on the Fate side of him, where reach and presence live, and gets to the enemy first. Men below his Stage feel their attention bend toward the russet and their stomachs turn before a single lance has touched them, and their own horses feel it sooner than they do. The line that was going to hold becomes a line that was always going to flinch, and the lances find it already open.

Applications

  • Breaking a line of foot before the lances reach it
  • Riding straight at a flank at full tilt
  • The cut down from the saddle with the long swords once the lances are spent, his being the Kessen sword

Associations

  • House Ashmore, which teaches every rider the Line, and where he has it Mastered
  • The Body Path, as a strike, for the Line itself
  • The Fate Path, for the Pressure that goes ahead of it
  • His Command and his Pressure
  • The Kessen sword and the ash lances

Limitations

  • Its limit is ground: it needs a field the horses can take at speed
  • On ice or in broken country it is a line of men on frightened animals
  • It costs the house its horses, and the lord nothing he would call a working
  • The ash goes by the cartload
  • A charge that goes in cannot be called back out
  • Its tell is the russet coming over the rise
  • Pikes, set, beat it, as Thornwall knows: foot who have been drilled to stand, on ground of their choosing, under a captain whose own Stage shrugs off the weight coming ahead of the horses
