Wystan Greymane — Abilities in full

Wystan Greymane — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 202614 min read

Card: Wystan Greymane

KEEPERS OF THE WALL

KEEPERS OF THE WALL is basically impassability: the power to be a wall, a thing that stands between, so that nothing gets past him, not a blow and not a secret, and when he finally moves he only needs to once. The name is northern Common, the house’s own words. It is his signature art, a Trait-art with the Greymane release, and it was the house’s idea before it was his.

Capabilities

A Greymane is a wall, and a wall is a thing that stands between. His children carry the same idea with younger knees. In him it has had sixty-one years to finish, and it has gone past the shield into everything else he does. The body is a wall against a blow. The stillness is a wall against being read. The silence is a wall against a question. The release is the same body made into the thing itself, one piece from boss to heel.

He has said the whole of it once, quietly, to his son, in the gatehouse at the Foot: we stand on the ridge so that nobody behind us has to.

Applications

  • Standing between a blow and the people behind him
  • Giving an opponent nothing to read
  • Keeping what is not his to tell

Techniques

  • KEEPERS OF THE WALL - the Trait of the same name, the body as a wall against a blow
  • STILL, THEN FAST - the stillness, a wall against being read
  • LATER, ULF - the silence, a wall against a question
  • SETTING THE STONE - the release, the same body made into the thing itself

Associations

  • The Body Path, mastered, carries the wall, the stillness and the release; the Fate Path he walks carries the silence, kept the way an oath is kept
  • Fixatio is the lens of the release; no lens is written for the wall and the stillness, which are the house’s raising and his own soul’s habit
  • House Greymane of the Ridgewall
  • The house expects that a Greymane’s Domain, if one of them ever lives to it, will be the ridge

Limitations

  • A wall faces one way and stays where it is: everything he has is paid for in not stepping, not turning and not telling

Traits

KEEPERS OF THE WALL · Primary Trait

KEEPERS OF THE WALL is basically guardianship: the power to hold a line with someone behind it and not be moved back a step. The Greymane soul stands on a wall looking west for something that might come, and his did that before it did anything else.

Capabilities

The body’s part is plain bracing, and any shield-man can be taught it. A blow moves a man by getting his weight outside his feet, and a man keeps his weight inside them by standing staggered and low with the shield seated against the shoulder, so that the push runs through bone into the ground and the ground pushes back exactly as hard, which every shield-man knows in his legs and the Measurewrights write down as their law that every push is met by an equal one.

What undoes a braced man is very seldom the blow. It is the flinch, the small backward start the body makes on its own to get away from what is coming, which lifts the heels and unloads the stance a heartbeat before the hit. This Trait is his soul refusing the flinch. The Shell, the body-side layer of the soul, holds the brace through the instant when a body would break it. While he holds a line with someone behind it, his Anchoring and Fortitude read one step high and nothing that reaches him moves him back.

Applications

  • Holding a line or a post with people behind it
  • Taking a blow without giving a step

Associations

  • The Body Path, with no lens written for it
  • The house’s raising and his own soul’s habit
  • The old sun, the round Greymane shield, seated against his shoulder

Limitations

  • It holds only while there is somebody behind him to hold it for, because that is the only reason his soul has ever accepted for standing still under a blow
  • It faces one way: a wall guards what is behind it and nothing else
  • With nobody behind him the step is gone and he is a strong old man with a good shield
  • A blow from the flank or the rear meets no wall at all
  • It costs nothing, since nothing is made or thrown
  • Its tell is that his eyes go west, or to whatever he has been set to face, before they come to the man in front of him, because he is checking the post before he checks the threat
  • It is beaten by coming at what he is not guarding: go round, split him from the people he is covering, or put a second threat on the far side of them and make him choose

STILL, THEN FAST · Secondary Trait

STILL, THEN FAST is basically counter-striking: the power to stand so still that there is nothing to read, and then make one move quicker than he has any right to. It is the Ridgewall stillness: he has a way of not moving at all and then moving very fast.

Capabilities

Two true things about bodies are doing the work. The first is that a moving fighter is a readable one. Every strike is announced by something smaller that comes before it, a shift of weight, a dip of the shoulder, a breath taken, and a trained eye answers the announcement and not the blow. A man who is truly still announces nothing, so there is no early warning to answer. The second is about choosing. A man who has to pick among several answers is slower than a man who has only one ready, and each choice added costs him time; the scholars who measure such things call the quick kind a simple reaction and the slow kind a choice reaction. Standing there, Wystan has already chosen.

He has one answer loaded, the way a crossbow is spanned and latched: the muscles of hip and shoulder drawn tight against each other and held, the fleece-lined scabbard keeping the blade loose, and nothing left to decide except when. The latch is the stillness, and his soul holds it without a tremor for as long as he likes. While he is still, his Reflex winds up behind it, and the first thing he does out of stillness comes one step quicker than his card. When the other man commits, the latch goes, and the whole wound-up body arrives at once, which is why old Stormfold men who have watched it a dozen times cannot say which hand moved first.

Applications

  • Answering the other man’s first move with the sword along the line he has just opened
  • Showing an opponent nothing to read
  • Striking from a planted body after the release is set

Associations

  • The Body Path, with no lens written for it
  • The Ridgewall sword and its scabbard, whose throat is lined in waxed fleece
  • SETTING THE STONE, which it answers out of

Limitations

  • It is one move, once
  • After the first, he is only an old man of sixty-one with a sword
  • Winding it again takes real stillness, a few breaths of it, which nobody close enough to hit him will give him
  • It takes no reserve
  • Its tell is that he stops moving altogether
  • Never be the first to move: a patient man wastes it
  • A longer weapon can make him come forward out of it
  • A false start he believes spends it as surely as a true one

LATER, ULF · Secondary Trait

LATER, ULF is basically secret keeping: the power to hold a silence that nothing gets out of him. He saw the gold seam in the boy’s eye from a hand’s width away in the yard at the Foot, and his face set like ground taking the frost, and he said “later.”

Capabilities

A secret gets out of an ordinary man through his body long before it gets out through his mouth: the eyes go to the person it concerns, the face answers a question before the tongue can lie, and the wish to be rid of the weight does the rest. LATER, ULF closes those roads. His face sets, his voice goes back to an old soldier’s ordinary voice giving orders about gates and stables and a count, and his eyes do not return to the thing he saw. One word, and the thing he is keeping is as far out of reach as if the ridge stood in front of it.

It is kin to a vow, which is small Fate and anyone’s to make, and his walked Fate is why his holds so cleanly. It pulls no thread and makes nothing happen. It only keeps what he already holds.

Applications

  • Keeping what he saw from his face, his voice and his eyes
  • He swore his own son to the same silence on the ridge, by the woodpile, with a cup going sour in his hand

Associations

  • The Fate Path he walks, kept the way an oath is kept
  • The same habit that keeps the wall: he keeps a silence the way he keeps a wall

Limitations

  • It takes no reserve; what it costs is what keeping costs: he carried the boy’s name south with him and never got to say it
  • A silence cannot defend itself except by staying silent
  • He cannot explain, argue or lie his way round a question, because he does not lie; he can only decline it
  • A declined question tells a sharp listener that there is something there
  • Its tell is the word itself: “Later.”
  • Ask him in front of the one he is keeping it from; he will walk to the window instead, and the walk is an answer of a kind

Releases

SETTING THE STONE · First Release, Greymane

SETTING THE STONE is basically immovability: the power to lock every joint in his body into one post that stands on the ground. It is the Greymane release, called aloud as “Stonefast,” low and level, taught on the Ridgewall as every Greymane is taught it, and his is Mastered.

Capabilities

Like any Body fighter he pulls Aether, mana in the common word, up out of the ground he stands on, packs it raw in his Shell as Charge, and drives it out through the Shell. Fixatio, the Knot, is the lens, and its idea is commitment: what is bound stays bound, because the way back has stopped existing. Its operation is coagulation, the setting of a thing past melting. Some stuffs can be melted and shaped again as often as you like, the way wax can. Others set once: boiled linseed oil dried hard on a shield’s face, lacquer, a raw hide tanned into leather, which will char before they flow, and every Greymane yard reaches for them as the comparison. Laid through his Shell, the house’s lens sets whatever it is laid on the same way, and what it holds stays held because the way back is no longer there to be reached. The Measurewrights say the long strands of a cured stuff have been bridged to one another all through, cross-linked in their word; their account of a shield’s face is good, and they cannot say why a man’s elbow should take the same cure.

A joint is the opposite of a cured thing. It exists to let two bones change their angle, and a body made of joints is a chain: lean on a chain and it folds at every link. That folding is how a blow gets into a man, each joint giving a little, the shield driven back into the arm, the arm into the shoulder, the hips turned, the heels lifted. When he calls the release, Fixatio sets each joint in the line, wrist, elbow, shoulder, spine, hip, knee and ankle, for as long as the feet stay where they are, and the chain becomes a post. A post under a push does one thing: it carries the push along its own length as squeeze, end to end, into whatever it stands on. So the weight of the blow goes down through him into the ground, the ground pushes back, and nothing in between is allowed to fold.

It rides a blow the plain way. The real hit lands on the old sun, on steel over ash, and the shield takes the dent, as sixty years of dents on its face will tell you. The working is what happens behind the shield: the arm that should have been driven back is not, and the man who struck feels his own blow come back up his weapon as if he had hit the gatehouse. At his rung a Body fighter commands matter as an object, what the hand is on, and the object here is himself and the shield on his arm; it is the smallest and oldest kind of command there is.

Applications

  • Taking a blow on the shield without the arm being driven back
  • Sending the striker’s own blow back up his weapon
  • Freeing the sword arm from a planted body, so the one answer of STILL, THEN FAST goes out along whatever line the striker has just opened: the wall first, and then very fast

Associations

  • Fixatio, the Knot, the house’s lens, carried in its drill and never harmonized by him, so it fires at the away rate
  • The Body Path, mastered
  • The old sun, which takes the real hit
  • House Greymane: his son and daughter call the same word, and the Stormfold says theirs are good and his is the one the others are measured against

Limitations

  • It takes about four percent of his reserve to set, some twenty-five settings from a full reserve, and it holds for as long as he does not move his feet
  • A little over three fifths of what he spends reaches the world, which is his yield; the rest burns in the Shell’s furnace, as warmth in the locked joints and as the one long low note people hear from very close
  • The feet are its limit: set, he cannot step
  • The charge is fair warning to anyone who has been told what to look for: the long breath out through his nose, the heels going down, the drifting dust and snow dropping straight to the ground in a ring round his feet, the old sun seeming to take the light, the dull gold edge of his aura ceasing to move and hardening like a bead of resin, a sweet resin smell that turns sharp, and one small sound from somewhere in him like a latch going home
  • Against a higher Stage it thins from the ground up: Fixatio holds the column and does not make the ground under it any stronger than it is, and a blow well over what the footing can bear breaks the footing, after which there is nothing for the post to stand on
  • The cure holds the man, never the footing: the post stands by its weight on the ground and the grip of his heels
  • The counters are the ones the house teaches its children to fear. Pull him, and a column built to be squeezed has nothing to say to a pull
  • Lift him, and there is no ground under the post
  • Or fight him on ice, where the heels have no grip and the whole locked man slides like a dropped statue
  • The lens fails from its own idea’s flaw: commitment cannot tell a true binding from a mistaken one, and a cured thing cannot bend, even to save itself; the Measurewrights call it irreversibility and the trade calls it over-cure
  • A set Greymane cannot ride a blow, roll with it or give an inch to bleed it off, so whatever exceeds the column does not stagger him. It cracks him, at whichever joint was a hair off the line when he called it, because Fixatio sets a wrong closing as firmly as a right one
  • Held too long, the cure lingers after the release has ended, and he comes off a long stand with hips and knees that take half a morning to remember they are joints; at sixty-one that bill is longer every winter
