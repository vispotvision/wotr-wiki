Hollis Draycott — Abilities in full

Hollis Draycott — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20268 min read

Card: Hollis Draycott

WHAT’S YOUR OLDEST

WHAT’S YOUR OLDEST is basically oath attunement: the power to hear what a man has promised before looking at his face, and to be held steady by his own promise. It is a Trait-art named for the greeting of his whole country. Between merchants in Nalūn the first question is what’s your oldest, and the answer is the token on the belt, shown before the name. Hollis Draycott asks that question of everyone he meets without opening his mouth, and he has spent his working life being the answer to it for the Vessant house.

Capabilities

A seal-token is a small disc of bronze or iron that sounds for as long as its contract is sound and goes dead and cold the moment the contract is breached. Oblation, the Wellspring of what is given up, a Fate Wellspring pooled deep under the salt cities, enforces what is sealed there, because a contract is two parties giving something up in front of a witness. The note is that enforcement made audible, kept in the metal for as long as the terms are kept on the road.

A sealed Nalūn contract is an oath with a house name and a Wellspring behind it, and every soul has a layer that holds such things: the Attraction Layer, the fate-side layer, the part that holds bonds, debts and oaths. So a token is a bond that can be heard from outside, and a bond kept long enough takes hold of the keeper. One half of this art is hearing another man’s bond. The other half is being held by his own.

Applications

  • Reading a person by what they are bound to
  • Standing unmoved in a room for as long as the house’s paper is kept

Techniques

  • READS THE BELT - the hearing half: he hears the contract in another man’s token
  • THE OLDEST CONTRACT - the held half: thirty-one years of a sound contract steady him

Associations

  • Oblation, the Wellspring under the salt, keeps the seal’s note in the bronze; he has harmonized no Wellspring of his own
  • The Spirit Path does the reading, in the Form of a Read, and Fate holds the bond, in the Form of a Bind
  • The Vessant house, whose seal and purse do his work
  • His aura is a faint bronze sheen, the color of a well-handled seal-token, with the steady hum of a sound contract under it

Limitations

  • Both halves run on tokens: one reads another man’s, and the other leans on his own
  • None of it is for fighting; he hires people for that
  • Nothing in it costs reserve

Traits

READS THE BELT · Primary Trait

READS THE BELT is basically bond perception: the power to hear in a seal-token’s note what its wearer is bound to. It is how a man of the factor houses reads a room, the way a sailor looks at the sky: before a face, the belt, and the bronze seal-token on it, and the note it hums.

Capabilities

Every factor in Halcrest can hear that a token hums. Draycott hears the contract in it, the way other men hear a tune. He is on the Spirit Path, whose work is what a thing means, and a token’s note means a bond: a house name written in the book, terms, and a term of years. His Core (the center of the soul, where a person’s own identity sits) does the listening, and his trained Perception and Attunement turn the note into what he can say aloud about its wearer. No Aether (mana) is pulled and no Essence (his own spiritual energy) is spent, any more than a listening man spends his ear.

What his ear works with is plain enough to any bell-founder. A disc of metal has its own pitch, set by its size, its thickness and its stiffness, so two tokens are never quite the same note. A ringing thing has a quality as well as a pitch, clean when it is whole and dull or buzzing when something in it has begun to give, which is how a potter raps a jar and knows it is cracked before he can find the crack. And two notes that are close but not the same beat against each other, a slow throb that can be counted; that is how a choir takes its pitch, and it is why the factors of Halcrest tune an ear to the Vessant token. His own bronze has held one note for thirty-one years, and he wears it at his hip as a tuning fork. Every other token in a room is heard against it.

Applications

  • Knowing who holds a man, for how much, since when, and whether the thing is starting to give
  • Telling a token higher or lower, steadier or wavering, sound or failing, old or new
  • Knowing from a silent disc on a belt that something is wrong

Associations

  • The Spirit Path, through the Core, in the Form of a Read
  • Oblation, which keeps the note in the metal
  • His own Vessant token, the tuning fork every other note is heard against
  • He learned token-reading in the Vessant counting rooms, from the agent who wore the token before him

Limitations

  • It reads the token and nothing else: a person with no token gives it nothing to read
  • Without a token to read he falls back on the plain eye, which a working life of belts has left a good deal less practiced than a soldier’s
  • A token tells him what a man has sealed, and never what the man will do with an open hand
  • A stolen token will not hum for the thief, so a silent disc tells him only that something is wrong and not what
  • A junior contract worn in front of an old one says exactly what its wearer wants said
  • It costs nothing, because nothing is made and nothing is thrown, which is also why there is no charge to see beyond the glance
  • Its tell is that glance, quick and open: his eyes go to your belt, then to the belt of whoever is with you, and only then come back up
  • It is beaten by wearing no token, or one that tells him nothing

THE OLDEST CONTRACT · Secondary Trait

THE OLDEST CONTRACT is basically indomitable will, lent by a contract: the power to stay calm, courteous and very hard to shift for as long as he is keeping the Vessant house’s oldest paper. Two agents wore the token before him and wore its rim thin with their thumbs, and when it came to his belt, the bond came with it.

Capabilities

Thirty-one years of a sound contract have bound his Attraction to the Vessant house’s oldest paper, and he has walked the Fate Path a little way, about three parts in ten of his practice, simply by keeping it every day. He works nothing with it and pulls no thread. What it does is hold him, the way ballast holds a ship: a hull with weight set low and fixed in one place rolls and comes back upright, and a man whose soul rests on one settled obligation does the same.

When the contract is threatened, his thumb goes to his token and runs once round the rim, and it is the only part of him that moves. He is checking, though he would not put it so: a token that has been breached goes still, and for thirty-one years the proof that all was well has been a hum under the right thumb.

Applications

  • Taking threats, flattery, a raised voice and a bigger man standing up over him, and coming back level
  • Lying for the house with a steady face

Associations

  • The Fate Path, through the Attraction Layer, in the Form of a Bind
  • The Vessant house’s oldest contract, thirty-one years sound
  • The Vessant token on his belt, Uncommon bronze, its rim worn bright and thin by his thumb and by the thumbs of the two agents before him

Limitations

  • It is loyal to the paper, not the people on the road: twenty-two men dead or missing did not breach the contract, so the token kept its note, and so did he
  • The steadiness is the contract’s, lent to him, and he has not found out what he is with the bronze gone quiet
  • It takes nothing from his reserve; the price is that he is the paper’s man before he is his own, and his breath pays for every lie he tells on its behalf
  • The bond steadies what he wills and has no hold on what his body does by itself: a lie told under strain pulls the breath up out of the belly into the top of the chest, so it comes in high and short, right up under the collarbone, and stops there a moment before it goes out
  • He has good control of his face and none of that, and a surgeon, a singer or anyone who has watched the dying breathe can read it across a room
  • Its tells are the thumb, once round the rim of the token, and the breath high under the collarbone when he lies
  • It is beaten by asking him what the contract has cost and not what it pays, because the paper has no column for that and he has to answer as a man: ask how many came back, not how many died, and watch his collarbone when he answers
