Ulf Stark — Abilities in full

Ulf Stark — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 202613 min read

Card: Ulf Stark

AND THEN WE GIVE SOME MORE

AND THEN WE GIVE SOME MORE is basically charity embodiment: the power to give away everything he’s got, and to always know who can carry it. It is his signature art, and it is one idea that the house had before he did. A Stark gives, and every ability below is that giving in a different part of him.

Capabilities

The cold in the blood is the first of it: a Stark’s body hands its heat down into the ground and keeps none back. The rest is that same habit grown up into a lord. He looks at a man to learn what can be handed to him, he hands it over, and the handing-over is the only time in his day that he is not afraid.

The Winter Road is the house giving its riders to the snow so that the north does not have to give its villages to the Tenrai. He has put the whole art in one sentence himself, and says it to anyone under forty: that’s just the way this life is, lad, we give, and we give, and then we give some more.

Applications

  • Learning what can be handed to a man before handing it over
  • Giving up his brother, his riders, his granaries, his house and his neck
  • Handing the heat of everything near him down into the ground
  • Giving the house’s riders to the snow in place of the north’s villages

Techniques

  • THE MAKING OF A GENERAL - the look that tells him who can carry what
  • WE GIVE - the giving itself, and the steady hand that comes with it
  • STARK RIME - the cold in the blood, a body that keeps no heat back
  • THE WINTER ROAD - the house’s way of war, paid for in riders

Associations

  • The Body Path, Mastered, does the cold, the sword and the road
  • His Walked Fate carries the look and the giving, with a little Studied Spirit in the look
  • The Stark cold, the house’s inheritance and no Wellspring, is the lens for the rime
  • The look and the giving are his own soul’s habits, and the Winter Road is a taught craft of war

Limitations

  • It only goes one way: nothing is promised back
  • The last thing on the list is his neck

Traits

THE MAKING OF A GENERAL · Primary Trait

THE MAKING OF A GENERAL is basically potential detection: the power to take one long look at a man and know what that man can carry and how far. It is the look his signature art runs on, and it is very seldom wrong.

Capabilities

The look is forty winters of picking roads, become a law of his soul. A road is a question about load: how much weight, on what footing, for how many days, before something gives. He learned to answer it for timber bridges and river ice and tired horses, and somewhere in those winters his Crystal (the soul itself, which grows around whatever a person does most) stopped telling the difference between a road and a man. His soul reads a man the way it reads ground.

So he reads a man’s bearing, the way the weight sits, the way he goes quiet before he moves, what a room does when he walks into it, and he comes away knowing what that man will hold up under. Then he says, “Ah. So there he is.” It works best with the man under load, which is why he does not trust a look across a table when he can arrange a road instead.

He has seen two men in his life with what the boy has, and one of them is dead and the other is sitting in this chair. He rode beside Gimbzo for two summers, and knew that weight again on a sixteen-year-old sitting on a stool.

Applications

  • Learning what can be handed to a man before it is handed over
  • Feeling the great ones at ten paces
  • Testing a man by arranging a hard road and counting the wagons that come out

Associations

  • His Walked Fate, landing through a little Studied Spirit as a reading; a small use that opens no gate
  • Forty winters of picking roads
  • Gimbzo, the first weight of that kind he knew

Limitations

  • It reads what a man can carry, not what he will choose
  • Those two summers are the proof: he read what Gimbzo could carry, and he was right, and it told him nothing about what the man would do with it
  • It costs nothing, because nothing is made or thrown: it is a read, and it runs on his eyes and his years
  • Its tell is the long look, the pale eyes going over a man the way they go over a ford, the black iron ring turning in the beard-braid under his thumb, and then the line
  • A man who shows him nothing beats it: stand still, keep your hands quiet, carry nothing in front of him, and never take the road he offers

WE GIVE · Secondary Trait

WE GIVE is basically fear immunity: the power to be beyond the reach of fear for as long as he is giving something away. It formed the day Torvald’s head came back with a note.

Capabilities

He gives: his brother, his riders, his granaries, his house, his neck. While he is giving, fear does not reach him and his hands do not shake. He rode south to the Kurenai blade at an easy walk.

Fear is the body guarding what it still has to lose. It tightens the chest, quickens the heart and sets the fine shake in the fingers, all to make a man hold on harder to the thing in danger. WE GIVE takes away the thing fear is holding on to. When he has decided a thing is given, his soul closes the account on it before the body has heard, and a body with nothing left to guard has no reason to shake.

It is kin to a vow, which is small Fate and anyone’s to make, and his Walked Fate is why his holds so cleanly. He swore the whole house of Stark to a tokenless boy before witnesses and before the Sky, with his maimed hand flat on his chest, and the hand lay still.

Applications

  • Riding south to the Kurenai blade at an easy walk
  • Swearing away the whole house with a still hand
  • Handing over riders, granaries and harness without flinching

Associations

  • His Walked Fate, as a binding
  • Torvald Stark, his brother, whose death formed it
  • The vow, which is small Fate and anyone’s to make

Limitations

  • It is all one way; nobody promises the Starks anything back, not the Sky, not the lines, not the people they give it for
  • It works only on what is his: he cannot give away another man’s life and feel nothing
  • The boys younger than sixteen he lets lie their way into the vanguard are a weight the Trait does not lift
  • It steadies the hand and adds no step
  • It costs everything, and he does not count it
  • Its tell is a sentence and never a gesture: by the time he says “That’s just the way this life is, lad,” the giving is done and his hands are already quiet
  • What beats it is something he would not give, and nobody has found it
  • The nearest anyone came was Wystan Greymane, who talked him into keeping one thing back, the boy’s own name, and Ulf sat on it like a man selling a horse with a cracked hoof under a blanket

STARK RIME · Inherited Trait

STARK RIME is basically heat transferal: the power to move the warmth out of everything near him and send it down through his heels into the ground. It is the cold in the Stark blood at Glory’s clarity, an inheritance of the house and no Wellspring.

Capabilities

It does one thing, the one rule the Stark blood has, which is that it moves heat out and never in. Warmth runs from the warm thing to the cold one, as it always has, and a Stark’s body is a road for it, in at the skin and the hands and out through the soles into the ground, which can take more of it than any man can give. That is why the Starks walk the way they walk. For yards round him timber silvers, snow stiffens and squeaks, his breath stops steaming, and the fur of his wolf-hood goes white. Rime is what the damp in the air does when it meets something that cold: it freezes straight onto the fur and the steel in white needles. It runs harder under feeling.

At rest it spends nothing. To drive it he pulls Aether (mana) up out of the ground he stands on, packs it raw in his Shell (the body-side layer of the soul) as Charge, and sends it out through the Shell. A little over three fifths of what he spends reaches the world, which is his yield, and the rest goes as a deep groan in the frozen ground under his boots. At his rung a Body fighter commands matter as an object, so the rime bites hardest on what his hands are on. That is Marchstone. Steel carries warmth far more readily than flesh does, something like a hundred times by the Measurewrights’ reckoning, so when he sets the cold through the cross the broad double fuller runs white from the hilt to the point in a breath.

That climb is the visible charge, and it rides the blow. The edge does a great sword’s work on harness and bone, and the cold goes in behind it, the warmth of the wound walking out along the blade, up his arms and down through him. Chilled muscle is slow, weak muscle, and wet flesh freezes fast to cold steel, so the man on the end of the cut loses the limb’s strength from the wound outward and finds the blade hard to shake loose.

Applications

  • Setting the cold through Marchstone so it goes in behind the cut
  • Taking the strength out of a wounded limb and freezing the blade fast in it
  • Hardening snow as hard as a floor for yards round him
  • Stiffening a crust for the Winter Road’s horses and setting slush over river ice

Associations

  • The Stark cold, the house’s inheritance, with no Wellspring behind it
  • The Body Path, shaping and striking
  • Marchstone, the Warlord’s great sword, which it bites hardest on
  • The Stark walk, the weight straight down through the heels

Limitations

  • It moves heat and cannot make it
  • On ground already frozen to the bottom there is little left for it to take
  • Against a real fire it is a bucket against a furnace
  • At rest it takes no reserve; driven, a little over three fifths of what he spends reaches the world and the rest is lost into the ground
  • Pushed past what his body can pass along, the cold stops in him on the way down: white fingers first, then a slow, stupid hand on the cross, which a man two fingers short cannot afford
  • Its tell is that his breath stops steaming and the wolf-fur goes white, and anyone facing him can watch the white climb the fuller
  • Heat and calm beat it: a brazier at your back, a warm blade, and nothing said about his brother

Techniques

THE WINTER ROAD · House Art, Stark · Signature

THE WINTER ROAD is basically winter warfare: the power to move riders through the snow while the other side sits by the fire, and to come out of the white from the side nobody was watching. It is a taught school art of the house and every Stark captain rides it; his is the one the north measures against Old Harald Keld’s, and finds second.

Capabilities

It is a craft of war before it is anything else, and the craft is sound on its own. An army in winter is tied to its roads, its roofs and its fires. Its wagons cannot leave the packed track, its men cannot sleep away from a hearth, and every fire it lights can be seen for miles and counted.

A Stark column carries no wagons. It is light, each rider has his own days of food on his back, the rest is waiting in caches laid along scouted routes in autumn, and the men are turned through warm halls in shifts so that no one company stays out long enough for the cold to start taking fingers. They go for the other side’s cook-fires and fodder, never for his walls, and a garrison that has lost its supper two nights running will come out and fight on ground the Starks chose.

The Stark rime makes the cold an ally. Where a Stark lord rides at the head of a column, wet snow stiffens into a crust that bears a horse, and slush over river ice sets hard for the few yards his cold reaches, so the house crosses where a southern scout has written “no crossing.”

Applications

  • Arriving from somewhere nobody was watching
  • Burning cook-fires and fodder until a garrison comes out to fight
  • Crossing rivers and snowfields a southern scout has marked impassable
  • Holding the passes, as the Starks held them against the Zettari for forty years

Associations

  • House Stark, which teaches it to every captain
  • His father, and Old Harald Keld’s example, the only man who ever did it better
  • STARK RIME, which hardens the crust and the river ice
  • The Body Path, for the road

Limitations

  • The thaw stops it: in mud the caches flood, the crust rots, and the tracks show black for anyone to follow
  • In a thaw a light column with no wagons is just a small army a long way from home
  • It takes nothing from his reserve and is paid in riders, not by the lord: frostbite, lost horses, boys who sat down in the snow to rest
  • It has no tell: he has already moved before you knew he was coming
  • A fire, a thaw and a scout who does not sleep beat it
  • A garrison that stays warm, fed and behind its wall cannot be starved out by the weather
  • One waking man on the right ridge undoes a month of marching
