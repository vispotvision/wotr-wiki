Bett Bodkin — Abilities in full

Bett Bodkin — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20265 min read

Card: Bett Bodkin

Techniques

NEEDLEWORK · Signature art, plain yard skill under her own colors

NEEDLEWORK is basically enhanced precision: the power to stab as fast as a blink and stop before the skin. She has no Traits; what she has is twenty years of one stop.

Capabilities

The thrust goes straight from the shoulder to the button at a speed the eye does not catch, and it stops a barleycorn short because she fires every pulling muscle against every throwing one at the last instant. That is the whole art, and it is a real thing a body can do. A fencer’s arm is thrown by one set of muscles and reined by the set across the joint, and when both sets fire together the elbow and shoulder lock stiff as a braced gate, so the arm’s whole speed is eaten inside the arm and never reaches the point. Anybody can do it slowly. Bett does it at full speed, and the stop lands where she chose to the width of a grain of barley.

There is no charge to read, because she sets nothing. The iron hangs point-down by her knee, she stands with her weight so exactly over her feet that neither foot gives her away, and she reads the other fighter’s start, a lifting stroke by the hips or a rake by the hands, and is in through the opening before the stroke is half done. She does it with her colors held down. The soul’s own Essence, which would lie on the body like a coat and make flesh harder than flesh, is kept under the hide, so the candidate in front of her fights her body alone, and the yard sees a pale thin woman with a blunt sword.

Her guard is not a choice. A fighter’s body keeps a guard of its own under all the holding, and it comes up faster than any decision when something touches the place every fencer covers first. Touched at the throat, hers brings the whole pewter coat up at once. Because a reflex has no time to choose a place, it comes up everywhere in the same instant, crown to boots, over the iron and the old buckler too, as thin and as even as wax on a candle. Under the touch it goes hard and very slightly brighter, a thimble over the one place the needle came, and the point finds nothing to bite and slides off. The Measurewrights put it down to pressure, the same push spread over a fingertip’s width pressing less than it would on a pinprick, and they have the how much of it right. The coat comes up cold, with tin and cold water on the air, and every bit of tin in the yard flashes with it for a breath.

Applications

  • Running the yard test: three passes, each ended on a touch or a word
  • Taking a candidate’s measure with two stops placed like a tailor’s shoulder pins, so that she knows the width of them
  • Turning a point off her own throat and past her shoulder

Techniques

  • NEEDLEWORK! - a straight thrust from the shoulder to the button, faster than the eye, stopped a barleycorn short, one on each collarbone a finger in from the shoulder point; she says the name flat, like a sum
  • THIMBLE - touch her throat and the whole pewter coat is up before she has decided anything, hardest where she was touched, and the point skates off it

Associations

  • No Wellspring is in it; what it runs on is twenty years of drill and her own colors
  • The Form is a Strike; her Path is not given
  • She came by it living, twenty years on Wolstan Kemp’s line
  • Her colors are thin bright pewter, new-cast tin-gray with a cold blue light like a winter river, and a winter-morning cold comes off her
  • The rebated yard-iron, its grip cracked from the cross to the pommel by her own clench and bound in red thread
  • The old steel fist-buckler, which is hers and not bonded

Limitations

  • The stop is the dearer half of every thrust, and it is harder than the thrust
  • With her colors in the arm the thrust is far faster and the hand far stronger, and the stop has to eat all of that in the same barleycorn; when it does, the button hangs shaking a barleycorn off the collarbone and the speed goes back down the iron into her fist
  • Lit, the stop is paid by what she grips, and it breaks whatever she is holding before it breaks the terms: it can crack her iron’s grip from the cross to the pommel, and past a wooden grip the next thing in that line is her own hand
  • That failure is the art’s own and has nothing to do with any Wellspring
  • With her colors down NEEDLEWORK! spends nothing; THIMBLE spends a breath of reserve, by reflex
  • NEEDLEWORK! has no tell worth the name: the only warning anybody gets is the word, said flat, and it comes with the thrust
  • THIMBLE has no tell either, being a reflex; afterward, putting her colors back down, the jaw muscle jumps and sets, a flush climbs her throat, her collar sweats dark, and she says one carter’s word
  • THIMBLE turns a point, and it was never shown against weight
  • A touch that asks nothing of her skin has already made her show
  • She does not show her colors on the line until her body does it for her, and she has been made to show once in twenty years, by four feet of reach she had not measured
  • NEEDLEWORK! is beaten by a move with no start to read, such as a yardwand slid from the ell notch to the ferrule, with reach she has not measured
