Larkin Gedge — Abilities in full

Larkin Gedge — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20264 min read

Card: Larkin Gedge

Techniques

HALVES! · Synergia, with Parkin Gedge

HALVES! is basically twin synchronization: the power to feel his brother’s sword as surely as his own hand, so the two of them can cut one wolf from both sides at once without looking, and it has nowhere to duck. Larkin shouts the name and counts each kill twice. Parkin calls it HALF AND HALF and says Halves sounds like a pie.

Capabilities

The thread is the whole art. A man knows where his own hand is with his eyes shut, because the muscles and joints report their place and their load to him without being asked. The scholars call that sense proprioception, the body’s knowledge of itself. The bond lends each twin a share of the other’s. Larkin feels where Parkin’s edge is, how fast it is moving and when it bites, the way two sawyers on one cross-cut saw feel each other down the blade and never need to look up. So they cut blind. A wolf that gets inside one man’s sword has only ducked one edge; with two edges crossing in it from two sides in the same instant, whichever way it goes off the one, it goes into the other.

The brothers are harmonized with Benediction, the Grace, whose law is that a bond freely given is weighed by the world as a real thing. Kit Quarrel’s word for it is “the blessing kind, that keeps no count.”

What anyone watching sees first is the charge. The twins put their shoulder blades together, both coats thicken to the red-gold of a loaf crust as they pull Aether (mana) up off the ground they stand on, and a thread of the same color comes up between their shoulders and pulls taut, with a soft low chord under it, a click as it catches, and a smell of new bread and church incense. About two parts in five of what each brother spends reaches the work, and the rest goes off them as warmth, which is why the ditch smells like an oven mouth.

Applications

  • Cutting blind off his brother’s edge, back to back
  • Cutting high while his brother cuts low through the same body in the same instant, so that it comes apart in two pieces that go on kicking
  • Going in at the head to make a thing look while his brother cuts from behind, “one before and one behind, as is the custom of the house”

Associations

  • Benediction, the Grace, a Wellspring of Fate whose Titan is Florwyn
  • His Path is not given; the Form is a Strike, and it lands through the body
  • Parkin Gedge, his twin and Resonant Pair, with whom he came by it living
  • Two plain hangers off the same smith’s bench

Limitations

  • Benediction has no primary glyph to set its limit, so the limit is the bond’s own measure: the thread is a sword’s reach long and no longer, and past that it snaps and they are two ordinary swordsmen with matching hangers
  • It is paid by the pair: both coats thin at once and they draw one breath together, every time, so neither can spare the other, and a twin who is winded winds his brother
  • It is no working thrown at an enemy and it does nothing to the wolf by itself; it rides two plain sword cuts, and all the damage is steel
  • It is two edges on one target
  • Its named failure is displacement, which the Measurewrights call competitive inhibition: a bond holds only until something one brother holds dearer takes its place, and the thread goes without either of them feeling it, until the blind cut comes and there is no second edge in the wolf; twenty-five years of the oatcake joke have not managed it yet
  • Its tell is the twins going back to back with the red-gold thread taut
  • It is beaten by parting them past a sword’s reach, by coming at one of them from where the other cannot turn, or by giving them more bodies than two edges can cross in
  • No reserve figure has been read for him
