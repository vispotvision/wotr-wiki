Haldan Stainmore — Abilities in full

Haldan Stainmore — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 202625 min read

Card: Haldan Stainmore

ACCOUNT RENDERED

ACCOUNT RENDERED is basically debt enforcement: the power to take what is owed him, on the spot, in the coin it was lent, until the account is square. It is his signature, the one idea his Trait, his named moves and the One he is reaching for each express. In Nalūn an account rendered is the statement a creditor presents for payment, and his is presented aloud.

Capabilities

He was a courier’s son and a road agent before he was a factor, and for six winters on the eastern corridor he read a pack for its weight and a convoy for its lateness to the day. Then he went into the counting room, and for twenty years he has closed accounts to the grain. A soul becomes what it does longest. His has become the closing of an account.

Oblation, the Offering, is the Wellspring under the salt, and its idea is the bargain that gives back exactly what was given up. The salt cities read it as contract: two parties each giving something up before a witness. Every other soul that works it stands on the giving side. Haldan stands on the other, where the same law says that what was lent comes back, never a grain over and never a grain under. Its operation is separation. The owed thing is parted from the one who holds it and goes where the books say it belongs.

He wears the claim on his Attraction Layer the way another Fate soul wears presence, and he spends his own Essence to present it. He reaches for no Thread. An instrument sealed under the Chain Light is already a working, and a debtor in breach is a man Nalūn has already stopped working for, so the instrument does the leaning and Haldan only reads it out. That is why a sealed claim leaves no knot behind it, and why a soul standing on the lowest rung of Fate can do this at all.

Nothing in it strikes. The owed coin leaves the debtor by its own likeliest road, a purse string tied in haste, a ring a size large, a buckle with a worn tongue, and after that it will not stay in a hand it is owed out of. Picked up, it slips. Pocketed, the seam goes. It lies where it fell until Haldan or his clerk takes it up. In a fight the art takes the fight’s tools and leaves the man standing: the sword bought on his note, the armor, the horse, a hired man’s pay. Against a higher Stage the instrument still binds, since a seal holds a great soul as it holds a small one, but the reading is his own, so the coin leaves only where the grip on it has give, one thing to a reading, the loosest first.

His refusal is written into it as a law it cannot break. “I take what was lent. I do not take the borrower.” The art has no hold on a debtor’s body or breath, on his kin or his name, and a debt lent in any such coin is one it cannot collect.

Applications

  • Taking back what is owed the house, on the spot, from anyone in breach of a Stainmore instrument
  • Following lent coin into what it bought, and taking that
  • Holding a pledged thing where it stands, a sword in its scabbard, a gate, a cart, until the account squares
  • Binding an opponent to terms offered and accepted in the room, so that breaking them is a breach he can collect
  • Disarming and unhorsing a man before a fight begins, by buying his debts beforehand
  • Getting back anything taken from him by force, since everything he carries is entered and sealed in the house books, and a taker is a breacher
  • Closing a small man’s whole account in one reading, and a lord’s across days

Techniques

  • TO THE GRAIN - the Trait: every open account in earshot, felt as weight
  • ACCOUNT RENDERED: TERMS OFFERED - terms stated aloud and accepted, sealed by word in the room
  • ACCOUNT RENDERED: DISTRAINT - the owed coin parted from its holder on the spot
  • ACCOUNT RENDERED: THE TRACE - the claim followed out of spent coin into what it bought
  • ACCOUNT RENDERED: HELD AGAINST THE ACCOUNT - a pledged thing set where it stands until the account squares
  • ACCOUNT RENDERED: QUITTANCE - a man’s whole account closed, in one held breath where it fits and across days where it does not, the source of his epithet
  • A LIEN - the One, offered and not yet his: a claim staked on his life

Associations

  • Oblation, the Offering, the Wellspring under the salt, whose law of equivalence is Dazkar’s
  • Fixatio, the Knot, Atla’zon’s, his one held lens, for the Hold alone
  • The Fate Path does the work, as a Claim in Distraint, the Trace and the Quittance, and as a Bind in Terms Offered and the Hold
  • The Factor House of Stainmore and its sealed articles, and the house books, in which everything he carries is entered and stamped
  • The oldest instrument, and its token on the cord at his throat
  • His Class, Surety, the one who stands for another’s debt, gives him the touch-assay: taking any coin, bar or token in his own hand, he knows whether it is short weight or false. What never passes through his hand it cannot judge
  • He reads every account in full Concord Common, in the Ironlink drafting register, clause by clause, and closes on one word: “Due.” for a claim, “Held.” for the Hold, “Quit.” for a Quittance
  • He never says “Called,” the Nalūn word for a First Release. The room says it of him anyway, and the room is wrong, since he has no Release
  • His Pressure has a creditor’s weight: the unwoken feel their knees go and remember, all at once, whatever they owe anyone
  • His aura is Oblation’s warmest teal with an amber edge, laid flat along the lattice of his robe and then across his hands, and it does not flicker

Limitations

  • It works only where a real account stands, and at its full force only where the account is sealed under Parun
  • An unsealed or disputed debt has no working behind it, so collecting one is a true pull from the lowest rung of Fate: about three quarters of his reserve and a nosebleed for a lean that often fails, with a knot due within days. He almost never tries
  • It never touches the debtor: body, breath, blood, labor, kin, name, the clothes he stands in, the day’s food and the tools of his trade are outside every account
  • The debtor must hear the account read or read it in his hand, and nothing lands before the last word
  • The sum must be exact. Named under, he takes only what he named. Named over by a grain, the grain is paid out of him into the debtor’s hand
  • It nets. Any account he or his house owes the debtor is set off first, and if the house owes more, nothing moves and the lean runs the other way
  • Nobody who stands under the mountain settlement’s claim can be collected from while the eastern delivery stands undone, and that claim can be bought in shares
  • He stands at Touched, Fate I, with no grip, and about two parts in three of what he spends reaches the claim
  • He speaks a working into the world, and his soul pours slowly: a purse takes him some eleven seconds of reading and a twentieth of his reserve, and nothing he owns can be hurried
  • At rest on ordinary ground he refills in about ten minutes, faster at Deepvein and on any toll bridge or bargain-stone, slower above the snowline and on water
  • He walks one Path. He cannot coat or reinforce, and against a fighter he loses a straight exchange
  • Anyone watching hears it coming, because he reads the account aloud, unhurried, clause by clause
  • The lamp flames gutter toward the debtor as if something were being taken from beside them, there is a smell of lamp oil and burnt offering and a pull at the sleeves, and the air between the two men hangs in a drawn thread
  • A debtor who wears a seal-token feels it go cold against his skin a breath before the coin moves
  • Paying him ends it, since a payment tendered discharges the account
  • It is countered by never letting him finish the reading: noise, distance, stopped ears, a blow, a hand over his mouth, or a lie he must stop to answer
  • It is countered by being someone he owes, or by carrying a share of the mountain settlement’s claim
  • It is countered by giving him bread and salt at a threshold and seeing him eat it, which makes the giver his guest, and a guest’s goods are beyond his claim until they part company and the next sunrise is up
  • It is countered by holding the thing with nothing to give, sewn in, riveted or worn tight, because the art leans and never forces
  • A prior or senior claim on the same goods takes them first, and so does an instrument sealed under Council authority over his
  • A tokenless man with no instrument to his name gives it nothing to stand on
  • A Fate soul of higher Mastery can contest the claim outright
  • It is weakest on ground where nothing has ever been given up, which Oblation hates
  • Benediction, the gift with no count, is its contrary and the one idea it cannot weigh. A gift he has not paid for stands on his books as an account he cannot read, and while it stands he cannot collect from the giver
  • It fails in Oblation’s own manner. Past some thirty-eight clauses in a day the reading outruns the breath: a breath that is not there in the middle of a clause, the count lost on the clause after, and a wrong figure collected out of him. The Measurewrights call it cavitation and say his lungs read like a pump running dry

Traits

TO THE GRAIN · Primary Trait

TO THE GRAIN is basically debt sensing: the power to feel every open account within earshot, who owes it, to whom, in what coin, and how far overdue. It is his Primary Trait, always on, and ACCOUNT RENDERED starts from it.

Capabilities

It formed on the corridor, where he read packs, mules and men for what each owed the road, and it finished in the counting room. An open account sits on a person’s Attraction Layer as a weight, and he feels that weight in his own hands, the way a salt-taster knows a short bar on the tongue. A sealed account comes exact, to the grain. An unsealed one is a heaviness with no figure in it. A breach is a sour, slack weight, like a cord that has lost its knot. The salt has an old belief that a breaker shows on his face. To Haldan it is simply true.

He cannot put it down. He reads every friend, every lover and every child as a set of balances, and he reads himself the same way.

Applications

  • Knowing to the grain what a man owes before he gives his name
  • Knowing who in a room is in breach, and of whose instrument
  • Reading which of a debtor’s possessions were bought on whose credit, the first step of every Trace
  • Catching a lie about a debt, since the weight does not change when the mouth does

Associations

  • Oblation reads it, on the Attraction Layer
  • Lived, never taught, the road’s habit become the soul’s
  • “What’s your oldest?” is the greeting he never needs to ask

Limitations

  • It reaches earshot, a hall at most, and a man outside it is outside the Trait
  • It is exact only on sealed instruments. An unsealed debt reads as weight with no figure, and a debt the debtor honestly does not know he owes reads very faint
  • It reads balances and nothing else. It cannot tell him what a man intends
  • A gift given without count is a blank where a weight should be, and it unsettles him past bearing
  • It reads nothing on a tokenless man with no instrument to his name
  • It costs nothing at rest. Reading a crowded hall hard takes about a five-hundredth of his reserve a minute, less than he recovers sitting still
  • His eyes go to a man’s purse and belt before his face, and his hands turn palm up a little, as if weighing
  • It is countered by owing nothing, or by standing outside earshot

Techniques

ACCOUNT RENDERED: TERMS OFFERED · Bind

TERMS OFFERED is basically contract creation: the power to seal a bargain by word in the room, so that breaking it makes the other party his debtor in a coin the terms name.

Capabilities

A contract is two parties giving something up before a witness, and where no scribe stands by, Oblation is the witness. Haldan states the terms aloud with each side’s stake named. “Draw before the bell and the blade is mine. Hold until the bell and I pay you forty marks.” A word seals it, or a nod, a hand, a token touched to his. Each stake is set apart from its owner and held against the terms, and he closes with “Dazkar weigh the difference,” and the lamps in the room steady.

A bargain sealed by word binds lightly: for a day or its stated term, and only on what both parties said. It has no Parun behind it and it does not travel. Once it is broken, though, the breaker owes the named coin under a witnessed bargain, and DISTRAINT can collect it. The salt is full of men who accept terms by reflex, because that is how they were raised.

Applications

  • Setting the terms of a duel, a parley or a surrender so that breaking them disarms the breaker
  • Binding a hired man not to strike while terms stand
  • Turning a bribe, refused or accepted, into a claim

Associations

  • Oblation, as witness
  • The Fate Path, as a Bind
  • The salt’s own custom of closing by word before the scribe comes

Limitations

  • Nothing binds without a real acceptance. Silence binds nothing, and veterans in Nalūn know never to answer a factor in a fight
  • Both sides must give something real. Terms in which he risks nothing are no bargain, and Oblation does not witness them
  • He is bound as hard as they are. If he breaks his own terms he owes the stake, and it is collected out of him
  • It cannot name the other party’s body, labor, kin or freedom as a stake. “Your life on it” is a phrase Oblation hears and does not seal
  • Terms sealed by word lapse at their term or at the next sunrise, whichever comes first
  • Sealing costs about a fortieth of his reserve and five or six seconds of speech, and keeping the terms costs nothing. From full he could seal forty bargains, and he recovers one in the time it takes to pour tea
  • It leaves no knot
  • He states the terms in full and waits for the answer, and that waiting is the tell
  • It is countered by saying nothing at all

ACCOUNT RENDERED: DISTRAINT · Claim

DISTRAINT is basically repossession: the power to part the owed coin from its holder on the spot, so that it falls from him and will not stay in his hand.

Capabilities

He reads the account: creditor, debtor, sum, coin, date, term. Then one word. “Due.” The coin of the debt leaves the debtor by its own likeliest road and lies where it falls. Silver lent comes back as silver, grain as grain, and a horse lent comes back as that horse. If the debtor stoops for it, it slips his fingers. If he carries it off, the seam gives.

The same reading guards Haldan’s own person. Everything he carries is entered and stamped in the house books under the house’s sealed articles, so a man who takes a thing of his by force is in breach of a sealed instrument from that moment, and the thing comes back to him the way any owed coin does.

Applications

  • A purse, a ring, a pay-chest, a sword, a horse from under its rider
  • Getting back a weapon struck from his hand or a purse cut from his belt, in the same exchange
  • Stripping a hired man of the pay drawn on a Stainmore bill before he earns it

Associations

  • Oblation, by separation
  • The Fate Path, as a Claim
  • TO THE GRAIN, which gives him the figure before he opens his mouth

Limitations

  • A plain reading is six clauses at the least, and one sealed account to a reading
  • It takes only the coin of the debt. Silver owed does not take a sword unless the sword was bought with that silver, and that is THE TRACE
  • A thing held with nothing to give stays where it is
  • Against a higher Stage it takes one thing per reading, the loosest first
  • The debtor’s person and his privileged goods are outside it
  • A purse or a ring costs about a twentieth of his reserve and some eleven seconds of reading, so twenty from full
  • A sword, a piece of armor or a horse costs a tenth and some twenty-two seconds, so ten from full
  • A cartload costs a fifth and three quarters of a minute, so five from full
  • A sealed account leaves no knot
  • The reading gives it away, and then the debtor’s token going cold, the pull at the sleeves and the lamp flames guttering toward him
  • It is countered by paying, by stopping the reading, or by a grip with no give in it

ACCOUNT RENDERED: THE TRACE · Claim

THE TRACE is basically value tracking: the power to follow lent coin out of a man’s hands into whatever it bought, and take that instead.

Capabilities

Equivalence follows the value and cares nothing for the shape. What the silver became is still the silver. TO THE GRAIN tells him which things in a man’s kit were bought on Stainmore credit, and THE TRACE reads the line of exchange aloud, a clause for each trade: lent at Halcrest, paid to the armorer at Rimward on the second of May, in exchange for this hauberk. The separation is carried across each exchange in turn, the drawn thread of air runs from the debtor’s purse to the thing, and at “Due.” the bought thing parts from him as coin would.

In a fight it is the move men fear. A man armed and horsed on Stainmore credit is carrying arms and riding a horse that were never his.

Applications

  • Taking the sword bought with the house’s silver, the armor, the horse, the roof bought on a note
  • Reading aloud to a room exactly what a man’s finery cost and whose money paid for it

Associations

  • Oblation, by separation carried across each trade
  • The Fate Path, as a Claim
  • TO THE GRAIN finds the line, and DISTRAINT takes the thing at the end of it

Limitations

  • The line dies at a buyer in good faith who gave value and did not know: a soldier paid his wages in that silver, a merchant who sold honestly and was paid
  • It dies at anything eaten, drunk or spent and gone, and at a debt discharged
  • From a mixed purse he takes only his proportion, to the grain
  • Past three trades the line is too thin to read
  • Coin changed by a working instead of a trade, melted and remade by an alchemist or transmuted, is no longer the same coin, and the line loses it. Transmutatio, change with no loss, is its contrary
  • It costs what DISTRAINT costs for the thing taken, and about a tenth of his reserve more for each trade the coin passed through. A sword three trades off is two fifths of his reserve and a minute and a half of reading, twice from full
  • A long trace is a long tell
  • It is countered by anything that breaks the line, or by stopping a reading that takes this long

ACCOUNT RENDERED: HELD AGAINST THE ACCOUNT · Bind

HELD AGAINST THE ACCOUNT is basically lien enforcement: the power to set a pledged thing where it stands, so that it cannot be moved, opened, drawn or used until the account is square.

Capabilities

This one move runs on his second lens. Fixatio, the Knot, holds the idea of commitment: what is bound stays bound, because the way back has stopped existing. Its operation is coagulation, and salt is the matter that fixes, the virtue of the very stone his city is cut from. He lays it on security for a debt. The account is read, he says “Held.”, and the pledged thing sets where it is. A sword pledged or bought on his note stays in its scabbard. A cart does not roll. A gate pledged against a city’s loan stays barred, and a seal stays unbroken.

It holds against everyone, the debtor and Haldan alike, and when the account squares it lets go without being told. A lord who borrowed against his castle has mortgaged its gate to whoever holds the note.

Applications

  • Keeping a debtor’s weapon sheathed through a fight
  • Barring a pledged gate or door in a siege or a flight
  • Stopping goods from leaving a hall or a quay

Associations

  • Fixatio, the Knot, Atla’zon’s, at home on every Path and so worked by a Fate soul at no loss
  • The Fate Path, as a Bind
  • Laid only on what Oblation’s books already name as pledged or owed

Limitations

  • It takes only on a thing pledged as security for a sealed debt, or owed under one
  • It holds the thing and never the person. A man can drop a held sword and pick up another
  • A Hold placed on the wrong thing stays placed. He cannot lift it early, and Oblation books the wrong Hold as a debt he owes. This is Fixatio’s own failure, the way back gone
  • Fixatio thins on open water, at high altitude and on a moving deck. On a boat, in a ford or high on a pass the Hold slips and will not set, and the eastern road runs through passes
  • A hand-sized thing costs about a tenth of his reserve and some twenty-two seconds to set. A gate or a cart costs a fifth and three quarters of a minute
  • Keeping it costs about a fiftieth of his reserve a minute. That is a fifth of what he recovers at rest, so a still man can keep a Hold for days. A man who is also working drains, and with no rest at all one Hold would empty him in under an hour
  • A sealed pledge leaves no knot
  • A smell of sweet resin gives it away, the thing going sticky and then rigid to the touch, and a bead of amber light hardening at the join, the scabbard’s mouth or the gate’s bar
  • It is countered by squaring the account, by fighting him on water or on a pass, or by making him work until he cannot keep it

ACCOUNT RENDERED: QUITTANCE · Claim, the signature

QUITTANCE is basically total debt collection: the power to close a man’s whole account, every note, every trace and every pledge, and leave him quit.

Capabilities

The old word under quittance is quiet. A man who is quit is at rest, and nothing rests that was not paid. Before Haldan meets a man he means to break, he buys that man’s paper: every Stainmore note, and every note he can get from the other houses at discount. Then he reads it in one held breath, every clause in order, and closes on “Quit.” Everything the man holds in the coins of the debts read, traced or pledged, leaves him in the same moment. Purse, rings, sword, armor, horse, banner. A small man’s paper fits in one breath. A lord’s is read across days, one breath a day, and the account stands open between.

A quittance is a discharge. When the last of the account is read the man owes Stainmore nothing, ever again, and the account is closed for good. If what he held fell short of what he owed, the shortfall is discharged with the rest and the house bears it, and Haldan pays the house the difference out of his own pocket and tells no one.

That is where the epithet comes from. When Stainmore has finished with a man, the man is quit, and the name makes it sound like a mercy.

Applications

  • Unarming, unhorsing and unseating a man in one reading, before a fight, a duel or a council
  • Breaking a lord whose venture ran on Stainmore credit, at the source, as a campaign of daily readings and never as one stroke

Associations

  • Oblation, by separation, all at once
  • The Fate Path, as a Claim
  • DISTRAINT, THE TRACE and the Hold, each of which it gathers into one reading

Limitations

  • It takes only what is owed, only in its coins and only what can be traced, and the debtor’s person is untouched
  • One reading closes only what one held breath carries. He can hold one for about a minute and a half, and in that time his soul pours about two fifths of his reserve
  • A small man’s whole account is about a quarter of his reserve and a little under a minute of reading, and it fits
  • A lord’s whole account does not fit. He closes it across days, one Quittance a day, each reading closing what one breath carries, and the account stands open between them. The discharge comes only when the last of it is read, and until then the lord knows what is coming and has days to pay, sell on or run
  • Never more than once in a day
  • It cannot take what has passed to the innocent, soldiers’ wages or goods sold honestly, so it strips the master and never his men
  • After it he has no further claim on that man in any coin, for life. A quittance spent early is a weapon gone
  • It is the longest reading anyone hears him make. The whole room goes still as each item is named, and every lamp gutters at once
  • The held breath is the opening. Interrupt the reading or make him draw breath and it fails, with the reserve and the breath already paid
  • Self-deceit hollows it. Turned on an account he tells himself coin can close, when it was never lent in coin, it comes back empty and costs him the breath anyway
  • It fails as the whole art fails, at full size: held too long, the breath he does not have goes missing in the middle of the reading, and the figure he misreads after it is collected out of him

Releases

A LIEN · the One

Offered, not yet his. A LIEN is basically claim imposition: the power to lay his claim on the ground around him, so that everything owed inside it answers to his note. Fate has one Release, the One, and in Nalūn it takes the salt’s word for a Second, a lien, because it counts as one; the Domain it would open is called a foreclosure.

Capabilities

He stands at Glory, and the One opens a Stage above him. Refraction comes first, by its own Catalyst, and only on that Stage can the One be met, under contest, by a claim staked on his life and kept. He must name aloud a claim over something outside himself, with his life the forfeit, against someone who can take both, and hold it unyielded until the contest ends.

Oblation’s price has to be truly the giver’s own, and a claim yielded at the last hour to save his life was never staked, so the One fails.

Opened, it would make the ground do his reading for him. Inside the lien every account owed is presented at once, with no clause spoken, and every breacher within it finds the coin of his debt leaving him. Nothing pledged or owed leaves that ground unpaid. Dust lies flat from his feet to the lien’s edge, flames stand straight, every lamp gutters toward whoever owes, and people step back to give it room before anyone tells them to. Where the contest ends with the claim still standing, the stillness stays on the ground for a day, and animals will not cross it.

Applications

  • Presenting every account on a stretch of ground at once, without a reading
  • Keeping pledged and owed goods from leaving that ground
  • Opening and powering a Domain, as a Second Release does

Associations

  • Oblation, on the Fate Path, the claim grown from a voice to a place
  • He would call it in the drafting register, the claim read as an instrument

Limitations

  • It is not his. He must first cross to Refraction, and then meet the One there, in person
  • It is staked on his life. A claim yielded is a claim never staked, and he knows it
  • It governs only what is owed. A man who owes nothing walks through it untouched
  • It is a claim on ground, weakest on raw wild ground where nothing was ever given up and strongest on toll roads, bridges and old bargain-stones
  • Called aloud it rises in full. Held silent it is weaker, and the choice is his
  • It is countered by a Fate soul of higher Mastery, who can contest the claim outright, by a debtor with no standing, by ground where nothing was ever given up, and by anything that makes him yield the claim before the contest ends
