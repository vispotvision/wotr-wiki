Amabel Breakspear — Abilities in full

Amabel Breakspear — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20267 min read

Card: Amabel Breakspear

Techniques

THE LONG TAB · Signature art

THE LONG TAB is basically kinetic charging: the power to put the work of a swing on account and pay it all out later in one blow. It is her whole kit, a cooper’s daughter’s idea, with one call for running the bill up and one for paying it. She has no Traits and has called no Release.

Capabilities

Every swing she does not land is a debt, and that is the whole idea. Her Wellspring is Tarturon, the Fire of Reckoning, whose idea is reckoning: a power that rises with what the soul believes it owes and that nothing can smother once it is lit. Its operation is calcination, the soul burning on what it owes down to the last grain. An unlanded swing is a debt she has just written against herself, so the fire holds that swing in the wood of the haft for exactly as long as it stays owed, the way a crossbow’s nut holds a spanned string, by Ned’s reckoning. Each unpaid swing burns a notch of fox-orange light across the ash, white at its heart, the size and shape of a notch on a tally stick, and sparks run up inside the steel of the langets. What the fire reads in her to burn so hot is her own business.

The haft is her father’s hoop-ash, a springy wood a cooper bends for a living. Bend a stave and it wants to come back, and whatever bent it waits in the wood until it is let go. The haft visibly bows under the account, a long shallow curve between her hands. The Measurewrights will tell you the work a stave keeps grows as the square of the bend, so one bent twice as far holds four times as much; their figure is good, and their law has no column for a debt.

She pulls Aether (mana, the raw power in the ground and air of a place) up out of the road she stands on and packs it raw into the Shell, the body-side layer of the soul, as Charge; Tarturon’s lens shapes it there, and she drives it out into her arms and the haft. Tarturon is a Fate Wellspring, at home on bonds and debts, and she is a Body fighter who works on matter and force with her hands, so it is dearer and rougher for her. About half of every pull reaches the haft, which is her yield. The other half burns in the furnace of her Shell and comes off her as heat, which is why her face goes scarlet and the air round her feels like the open door of a furnace with no fire in it.

Running the tab, she throws a full swing, the whole weight of her going round behind it and the plate turning with her, and stops it dead in the air a forearm short, arms locked. Banked or finished is her choice swing by swing, and the tab only counts the swings she chose not to land. Settling up, the ninth bar is the windup itself: the axe drawn back over the right shoulder, the bit behind her head and the shoe out in front, hips wound round like a rope on a windlass, the ninth notch lighting slowly up through the ash below the eighth while she holds it. Then she calls it and lets go. The air bends hot and the dust lifts before the bit arrives, because nine swings are coming due in one blink and the road gives way ahead of the edge the way a crowd parts ahead of a bailiff. The real blow lands first, the edge and her whole wound-up body doing what a long axe does, and the eight swings waiting in the ash arrive behind the same edge in the same instant, so the cut does not slow where flesh and bone ought to slow it. The bars go dark together when the bill is paid.

Applications

  • Banking the swings she chooses not to land, up to nine
  • Killing what is worth killing between banked swings, with the spiked shoe and the bit, and taking jaws on the pauldron
  • Paying the whole tab into one cleave; paid in full, it halved an alpha dog from neck to belly, split the near thill of the cart under it and cracked the road a yard each way

Techniques

  • RUNNING TAB! - the swing put on account: a full swing stopped dead a forearm short, and a bar of fox-orange light burns across the ash where it went
  • SETTLE UP! - the bill paid in one stroke: the ninth bar lights in the windup and every banked swing is let go in one cleave

Associations

  • Tarturon, the Fire of Reckoning, a Fate Wellspring worked away from its home, holds each banked swing for as long as it stays owed
  • The Body Path, through the Shell, in the Form of a Strike
  • ARREARS, the long axe, named with the clerk’s word for a debt that has fallen behind; it is not bonded and carries the tab because her colors are run out into it from shoe to bit
  • The haft is her father’s hoop-ash, cut the year she left his cooper’s yard
  • Her colors are a hot fox-orange, red at the edges and pale gold at the heart, with a smell of smith’s scale and burned salt and a dry heat with no fire

Limitations

  • Nine on the haft is full, because nine swings is as far as that length of hoop-ash will bow and still give the swings back
  • At ten the haft burns and her with it: the fire that holds the tab is fed by the tab and nothing in it knows how to stop, so the whole account pays out at once into the ash and the two arms holding it
  • A debt is only real while it is unpaid, so every bar is a blow that did not land, and whatever she swung at is still alive and coming back at her from another side a breath later
  • Half of every pull burns in her Shell’s furnace as heat; in Kit Quarrel’s words it is “not an axe-woman’s spring by nature, so it charges her double”
  • While she is banking she is slower on every turn, her forearms shake, her colors redden and draw in, and her face goes scarlet
  • Paying the bill empties her: afterward her colors are gone out, she is sick in the road, her forearms are blistered red from wrist to elbow, her hands shake too much to write without steadying, and her face is gray-white with red spots
  • The haft pays the interest, in her own words: it still steams wrapped in a wet cloth, and the bars are left scorched black across the grain, one under the other
  • Anyone watching can read it coming: her colors come up off her like a fire through straw and run out along the axe, a banked swing stops a forearm short with a notch of light, the windup goes back over the right shoulder with the hips wound, and she calls both calls at the top of her voice
  • RUNNING TAB! is beaten by not waiting for the bill: press her while the bars are lit and she is slow
  • SETTLE UP! is beaten by coming straight into the ninth windup, which opens her from chin to knees; she is slow to wind, by Ned’s read, and cannot move until the swing is spent
