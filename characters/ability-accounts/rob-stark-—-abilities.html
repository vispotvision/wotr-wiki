Rob Stark — Abilities in full

Rob Stark — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 202610 min read

Card: Rob Stark

HE’S ALL OF OURS

HE’S ALL OF OURS is basically guardianship: the power to be standing in front of whoever he has claimed as his. It is a Trait-art, one idea, and he had it before he had anything else, because his soul stood guard before it did anything else.

Capabilities

Two winters in the Lanternless he stood where the Sarge could not see behind himself, and fetched him for Ulf, and locked the door and stood outside it, and when Ulf asked if the boy was one of his, Rob said he was all of theirs. Everything he has is that one sentence said again in a different part of him. The claim puts his body in front of his people, the quiet keeps his pain from frightening them, the cold nails his boots to the ground in front of them, and the Wall is all three done at once over somebody who has fallen.

Applications

  • Putting his body in front of his people
  • Keeping his pain from frightening them
  • Nailing his boots to the ground in front of them

Techniques

  • HE’S ALL OF OURS - the Primary Trait: the claim, and the step across that needs no choosing
  • DIDN’T SCREAM - the Secondary Trait: pain that reaches him late and is shown only when he chooses
  • STARK RIME - the Inherited Trait: the Stark cold, which sets his boots to the ground
  • THE WALL OF ONE! - the Signature Technique: the body planted over whoever is down, feet frozen to one line

Associations

  • The Body Path, in the Form of a Shape
  • The Stark cold, the house’s inheritance and no Wellspring, is the lens the art works through
  • The Sarge, the Sarge’s sister and a boy who could not hold his own spear, the ones the company knew he had claimed
  • His aura is the Stark cold, a gray with frost at its edges that whitens his beard when he is angry

Limitations

  • He has one body, and it cannot be in front of two people who are standing apart
  • Whatever was meant for his people lands on him

Traits

HE’S ALL OF OURS · Primary Trait

HE’S ALL OF OURS is basically protective instinct: the power to be between his people and a blow before he has thought about it. Claiming is a thing he decides, slowly, and he does not undecide it.

Capabilities

While someone he has claimed is in reach, his Fortitude and Reflex read a step high on the hidden scale and his body is between them and the blow before he has thought about it. That last part is the real trick, and it is an ordinary one made into law. A man who has to choose what to do about a spear takes a good deal longer than a man who flinches, because the flinch never goes up to the part of the head that weighs things; it goes in at the eye and straight out at the legs. His Shell (the body-side layer of the soul) has taken the choosing out of it: for his own people the step across is a flinch.

The quarter of his practice he has spent studying Fate opens no gate and gives him one small use, which is this: a claim is a bond, and he knows without looking where the ones he has claimed are standing.

Applications

  • Stepping in front of a blow meant for one of his
  • Being a step harder to put down and a step quicker while one of his is in reach
  • Knowing without looking where his people are standing

Associations

  • His Shell, which has made the step a flinch
  • Fate, studied for a quarter of his practice, which gives the sense of where his people are and nothing more
  • Two winters a pace behind the Sarge, where standing over someone was learned

Limitations

  • Reach: he can guard only what he can step across, one long stride of a big man
  • He cannot be in front of two people who are standing apart
  • It takes nothing from his reserve, because nothing is being made or thrown; whatever was meant for them lands on him
  • Its tell is where he stands: a pace behind and to the left of the one he has claimed, on the side a right-handed man cannot see or cover
  • It is beaten by getting past whoever he is guarding first, or by making him pick: go for two of his at once and he has one body

DIDN’T SCREAM · Secondary Trait

DIDN’T SCREAM is basically pain suppression: the power to feel a wound late and choose when to show it. The cub bit him above the knee on the Vessant road and he did not scream; Yukazuri opened the rotten bite on a wagon tailboard with spirit and a knife, and he bit a strap and did not make a sound.

Capabilities

A hard fight does a little of this for anyone. Fear and effort make the body dull its own hurts for a while, which the surgeons call hypoalgesia, less pain from the same wound, and it is why a soldier finds the cut after the fighting. In Rob the soul holds that door shut on purpose and for longer, so that on the road the leg was bleeding into his boot and he had not yet noticed. A bite to the bone gets a groan through shut teeth and another swing of the axe.

Applications

  • Keeping on hitting through a wound and noticing the blood later
  • Keeping his pain from frightening the people behind him

Associations

  • Yukazuri Moto, the little surgeon, who opened the bite and later closed the cut on his leg
  • HE’S ALL OF OURS, since it holds only while he has somebody to stand in front of

Limitations

  • It holds the voice, not the body: the leg still goes bad, the lung still fills, and a wound he has not felt is a wound he has not bound
  • It needs him braced for it: fighting, it holds; lying still and safe with nobody to stand in front of, it lets go
  • At the Foot, when she closed the cut on his leg, he screamed at her and cursed her and then slept, and he never forgave himself the scream
  • It costs nothing
  • Its tell is the jaw: teeth shut on a strap, or on nothing, and a long grinding groan where another man would yell
  • Nobody found a counter to it in a fight, and he kept it to the end

STARK RIME · Inherited Trait

STARK RIME is basically cold manipulation: the power to move heat out of himself and down into the ground. The cold in the Stark blood is an inheritance of the house and no Wellspring, and in Rob it runs plain and strong.

Capabilities

It does one thing, the one rule the Stark blood has, which is that it moves heat out and never in. In him it shows as frost in his beard when he is angry, and the heat going down through his heels into the ground. When he sets it on purpose he pulls Aether (mana) up out of the ground he is standing on, packs it raw in his Shell as Charge, and sets the rime down through his legs. Two parts in five of what he spends reaches the world, which is his yield, and the rest is paid in the body as the hard breathing through his nose that is the only sound he makes.

What the rime does is the oldest thing frost does to a fence post. Wet ground that freezes turns from mud to something near mortar, and whatever is standing in it when it freezes is set there.

Applications

  • Freezing the damp under his soles and in the cracks between the stones to the leather of his boots
  • Sending the heat he pours out through his heels down into the ground

Associations

  • The Stark blood, the house’s inheritance
  • THE WALL OF ONE!, the stance it sets

Limitations

  • Heat always runs from the warm thing to the cold one, and the cold cannot make any
  • On ground already frozen iron-hard there is no wet left for it to set and it grips less
  • Against a real fire it is a bucket against a furnace
  • Three parts in five of what he spends on it are lost
  • Its tell is frost in his beard
  • It is beaten by heat, and by calm: a Stark who is not angry is only a cold man

Techniques

THE WALL OF ONE! · Signature Technique

THE WALL OF ONE! is basically immovability: the power to plant himself over someone who has fallen so that nothing gets through him while he stands. It is the third move of HE’S ALL OF OURS, and it was the last thing he did.

Capabilities

He steps across, not aside: the big body planted over whoever is down, axe up, limewood shield up. The visible charge is the step itself and what the ground does under it, as he sets the rime down through his legs.

A man who is knocked over is nearly always a man whose feet went first. With his feet set, a blow on the shield has nowhere to push him: it goes down through the arm, the hips and the long bones of the legs into ground that will not give, and the heat he is pouring out through his heels goes down the same road. A shield wall works because each man is braced by the men at his shoulders; he has nobody at his shoulders, so he is braced by the ground.

Applications

  • Holding one line over a fallen man
  • Taking a blow on the shield without giving a step

Associations

  • HE’S ALL OF OURS, DIDN’T SCREAM and STARK RIME, all three done at once
  • The bearded axe and the limewood shield, which are what he fights with when someone is down behind him
  • He came by it living
  • The boy with the red ears, whom he stepped across

Limitations

  • A wall faces one way: his feet are frozen to the line he chose, and it holds one line against one blade at a time
  • To turn he has to break his own frost first
  • Setting the rime and holding the stance takes about five percent of his reserve, and it holds as long as he does
  • Its tells are the step across, his breath no longer steaming, his beard going white, and frost running out from his boot soles across the snow or the cobbles in a pale ring a stride wide
  • It is beaten by more than one blade, or by one from the side he is not facing: low, under the right arm, where the mail gaps at the armpit and the shield is not
