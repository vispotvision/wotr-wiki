Mark Stark — Abilities in full

Mark Stark — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20269 min read

Card: Mark Stark

KEPT HOLD

KEPT HOLD is basically sheer tenacity: the power to keep hold, whatever has hold of him. It is a Trait-art, one idea, and he got it on the Vessant road, where a cub bit through his shield and took his forearm to the bone, raked his belly and his face and his good arm, and he kept hold of the short sword the whole time.

Capabilities

His soul learned one thing first: whatever has him, the hand does not open. Everything else he has is that one lesson said again in a different part of him. The hand holds the sword, the talking holds the fear, the knees hold the ground under the thing that is killing him, and the cold holds the blade in the wound.

Applications

  • Holding the sword, the fear, the ground under the thing that is killing him, and the blade in the wound, each with a different part of him

Techniques

  • KEPT HOLD - the Primary Trait: the hand that will not open
  • THAT WAS THE SMALL ONE - the Secondary Trait: the talking that keeps fear out of his hands
  • STARK RIME - the Inherited Trait: the Stark cold, which holds the blade in the wound
  • UNDER AND HOLD ON! - the Signature Technique: on his knees under the thing, the blade in to the hilt, held until it dies

Associations

  • The Body Path, in the Form of a Strike
  • The Stark cold, the house’s inheritance and no Wellspring, is the lens the art works through
  • The Vessant road and the cub, where it was learned
  • His aura is the Stark cold, thin and bright, a pale gray with a coppery shimmer at its edge like frost on a rust-red leaf

Limitations

  • Hurt, he screams, cries and complains the whole time, and the hand stays shut

Traits

KEPT HOLD · Primary Trait

KEPT HOLD is basically unbreakable grip: the power to keep a weapon in his hand against anything short of breaking the hand. It is the first thing his soul learned, and it gives the art its name.

Capabilities

A hand closes by muscles that sit in the forearm and pull the fingers shut through cords running over the wrist, and an ordinary frightened hand opens because pain and shock tell those muscles to let go. KEPT HOLD is his soul refusing to pass that order along. The muscles stay drawn tight, the way a cramp stays, for as long as there is a weapon in the hand, and no wrench, twist or blow on the blade gets it out of his fingers.

While he holds on, his Fortitude reads a step high on the hidden scale: the whole body takes its cue from the hand and is harder to put down.

Applications

  • Keeping the sword through a wrench, a twist or a blow on the blade
  • Being a step harder to put down for as long as he grips

Associations

  • His Shell, the body-side layer of the soul, which keeps it as a law without being asked
  • The short sword he held on the Vessant road, and his brother’s long sword, on which it has already set his knuckles

Limitations

  • It holds the sword, not the man: he can be knocked down holding it, dragged holding it, shaken like a rat holding it
  • A thing a step or two stronger than he is simply throws man and sword together
  • It costs nothing, because nothing is being made or thrown
  • Its tell is the knuckles of the sword hand, which go white on the hilt and stay white
  • Its counter follows from how a hand is built: take the hand, not the blade
  • Break the fingers, cut the cords in the forearm, or fold the wrist hard down toward the palm, which slackens those cords so that even a locked fist has nothing left to pull with

THAT WAS THE SMALL ONE · Secondary Trait

THAT WAS THE SMALL ONE is basically fear suppression: the power to keep fear out of his hands for as long as he keeps talking. He talks when he is frightened, a steady stream of it under his breath, and the talking keeps his feet under him.

Capabilities

A head can only attend to so much at once, and a mouth that is busy making words uses up the part of it that would otherwise be rehearsing what the teeth are about to do. A man talking also has to breathe out long and even to do it, and a long even breath slows the heart and steadies the fingers. So while he mutters, fear does not reach his hands.

That is why his brother’s “Aye” mattered. Rob said it once and the muttering stopped with his feet still under him, because Rob was there to answer for him.

Applications

  • Giving ground on fast feet, talking, with steady hands
  • Holding off the shakes, the scream and the tears until the fight is done

Associations

  • His brother Rob, who answered it

Limitations

  • The fear is held off, never spent: the moment the words stop, everything he has been holding off arrives at once, the shakes, the scream, the tears
  • It takes nothing from his reserve
  • Its tell is the steady stream of words under his breath
  • It is beaten by making him stop: wind him, strike the throat or the jaw, or say the one thing he cannot answer
  • With Rob dead there is nobody to say “Aye”, and the grief has done what no enemy managed and made him quiet

STARK RIME · Inherited Trait

STARK RIME is basically cold manipulation: the power to move heat out of a thing and down into the ground. The cold in the Stark blood is an inheritance of the house and no Wellspring, and in Mark it runs thin.

Capabilities

It does one thing, the one rule the Stark blood has, which is that it moves heat out and never in. In him it shows as frost on the fur of his collar when he is angry or afraid, and the heat going down through his heels into the ground. When he sets it on purpose he pulls Aether (mana) up out of the ground he is kneeling on, packs it raw in his Shell as Charge, and sets the rime through the sword arm. About a third of what he spends reaches the world, which is his yield, and the rest is paid in the body as a shudder through the shoulders.

It rides the blow, and from there the world’s own old habits do the rest. Warmth always runs from the warm thing to the cold one, and steel carries it far more readily than flesh does, something like a hundred times by the Measurewrights’ reckoning, so a cold blade buried to the hilt is a road, and the warmth of the wound walks out along it, up his arm, down through him and into the ground. Two things follow. Wet flesh freezes fast to cold metal, the way a tongue sticks to a pump handle in January, so the blade cannot be thrashed loose. And cold muscle is slow, weak muscle, so the thing on the end of the sword loses its strength from the wound outward while he keeps his.

Applications

  • Freezing a buried blade fast in the wound
  • Taking the strength out of whatever is on the end of the sword, from the wound outward

Associations

  • The Stark blood, the house’s inheritance
  • The sword, which is the road the heat leaves by
  • UNDER AND HOLD ON!, the blow it rides

Limitations

  • The cold moves heat and cannot make any, so on ground already frozen hard it has little to take
  • Against a real fire it is a bucket against a furnace
  • About two thirds of what he spends on it is lost
  • Its tells are frost on the fur at his collar, frost creeping white up the fuller from the wound, and his breath no longer steaming
  • It is beaten by heat, and by calm: a Stark who is not angry or afraid is only a cold man

Techniques

UNDER AND HOLD ON! · Signature Technique

UNDER AND HOLD ON! is basically a pinning strike: the power to get underneath a thing and hold a blade in it until it dies. It is the third move of KEPT HOLD, and the Lanternless still call it the Pin.

Capabilities

The visible charge is the drop. He stops giving ground, the muttering quickens, and he goes to his knees inside the reach of whatever is on him, where its weight is over him and its jaws or its arms have already gone past. The blade is driven up behind the foreleg or under the arm, where a beast’s hide is thinnest and a man’s mail gaps, in to the hilt, and he leans on the pommel with all his weight, his face a hand’s width from its eyes. Then he holds it there, sobbing if he has to, while it dies.

All of KEPT HOLD is in it: the hand holds the sword, the talking holds the fear, the knees hold the ground under the thing that is killing him, and the cold, set through the sword arm, holds the blade in the wound.

Applications

  • Killing a beast from behind the foreleg
  • Killing a man through the gap in his mail under the arm
  • He did it on the road one-handed, with the other arm hanging open to the bone and the cub’s claws working at his chest

Associations

  • KEPT HOLD, THAT WAS THE SMALL ONE and STARK RIME, all used at once
  • Rob, roaring at him to hold it and not let go, which is where the call comes from
  • He learned it by living through it: nobody taught him this
  • The short sword, now a long dagger, that he held behind the cub’s foreleg to the hilt

Limitations

  • He has to be under the thing to do it, and being under the thing is how he got his arm torn open
  • Setting the rime through the arm and holding takes about five percent of his reserve
  • Its tell is that he stops giving ground, the muttering quickens, and he goes to his knees inside the reach
  • It is beaten by not letting him get underneath: keep him at the long end of a spear, stay off him once he is down, or be something with no belly to get under
