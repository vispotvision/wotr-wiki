Parkin Gedge — Abilities in full

Parkin Gedge — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 20264 min read

Card: Parkin Gedge

Techniques

HALF AND HALF · Synergia, with Larkin Gedge

HALF AND HALF is basically twin synchronization: the power to feel his brother’s sword as surely as his own hand, so the two of them can cut one target from both sides without looking. His brother cuts high, he cuts low, and neither of them looks. Larkin shouts it as HALVES! and is told that Halves sounds like a pie.

Capabilities

A man knows where his own hand is with his eyes shut, because muscle and joint report their place and their load to him unasked. The scholars call that sense proprioception, the body’s knowledge of itself. The bond lends each twin a share of the other’s. Parkin feels where Larkin’s edge is, how fast it travels and the instant it bites or misses, the way two sawyers on one cross-cut saw feel each other down the blade, so he never has to turn his head.

The brothers are harmonized with Benediction, the Grace, whose law is that a bond freely given is weighed by the world as a real thing. Kit Quarrel’s word for it is “the blessing kind, that keeps no count,” which is a fine joke on the one man in the company who counts everything. On the body, Benediction also lends the one blessed a real steadiness in the hands and the footing, which is why a man cutting blind in a dry ditch at dusk does not flinch or shorten the stroke.

His half is the low line, and the low line is the one that kills. A four-footed beast gets out from under a sword by dropping its head and shoulders and driving in beneath the cut, where any one man’s blade has already gone past; that is exactly where Parkin’s edge is already traveling from the other side, flat and low, under the chest and behind the forelegs. Whichever way the wolf goes off the one edge, it goes onto the other.

The charge is there for anyone to read. The twins set their shoulder blades together, both coats thicken to the red-gold of a loaf crust as they pull Aether (mana) up off the ground under their boots, and a thread of the same color rises between their shoulders and catches taut, with a soft low chord under it and a smell of new bread and church incense. About two parts in five of what each brother spends reaches the work, and the rest leaves them as warmth.

Applications

  • Cutting blind off his brother’s edge, back to back
  • Taking a wolf apart in two pieces, high and low in the same instant
  • Finishing from behind while his brother goes in at the head to make the thing look

Techniques

  • HALF AND HALF - back to back with the thread pulled bright as wire, Larkin cuts high at the head and Parkin, turning behind him blind, cuts low under the chest of the same wolf in the same instant; or Larkin goes in at the head to make it look and Parkin, behind it, brings one short cut down across the back of the neck to the spine; Parkin counts it as one wolf either way

Associations

  • Benediction, the Grace, a Wellspring of Fate whose Titan is Florwyn
  • His Path is not given; the Form is a Strike, and it lands through the body
  • Larkin Gedge, his twin and Resonant Pair, with whom he came by it living
  • Two plain hangers off the same smith’s bench

Limitations

  • Benediction carries no primary glyph, so the limit is the bond’s own measure: the thread is a sword’s reach long, and past that it snaps and they are two men with matching hangers
  • It is charged to both: both coats thin at once and the pair draw one breath together, every time, so neither brother can carry the other, and a winded twin winds his twin
  • It throws nothing at an enemy and does nothing to the wolf by itself; it rides two plain sword cuts, and every wound is steel
  • It is two edges on one target
  • Its named failure is competitive inhibition, which is displacement: the bond holds until something one brother holds dearer takes its place, and it goes silently, unfelt, until the blind cut comes and there is no second edge in the wolf
  • Its tell is the twins back to back with the thread taut
  • It is beaten by parting them past a sword’s reach, by coming at one from where the other cannot turn, or by sending more bodies than two edges can cross in
  • No reserve figure has been read for him
