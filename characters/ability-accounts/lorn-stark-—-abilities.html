Lorn Stark — Abilities in full

Lorn Stark — Abilities in full

Nov 28, 202619 min read

Card: Lorn Stark — The Sword

WHERE IT ENDS

WHERE IT ENDS is basically boundary manipulation: the power to decide where a thing ends, and to hold that line with cold. He scratches a line in the snow, swears to hold it, and whatever crosses it freezes. It is his signature art, and his Traits, his techniques and his Release are that one idea done at a different size.

Capabilities

House Stark defines where things end and begin. The blood decides where the warmth ends. The scratch in the snow decides where the road ends. The oath decides where he ends, which is nowhere short of the thing he swore to. The walk keeps him on his own side of all three. The Release is the same border drawn all the way round him and held until it breaks him.

The cold is the house’s inheritance and no Wellspring, so it has no Wellspring’s idea, no glyph and no Titan behind it. It has one rule of its own, which is that the Stark blood moves heat out and never in, and that rule is the house’s old decision worn in the body: the blood decides where the warmth ends, and it ends at a Stark. A house needs a fire and an icehouse needs a deep hole, and a Stark’s body is the hole. The air, the timber, the snow and the men inside a few paces of him give up their warmth to him, it passes down through his trunk and legs without stopping to warm him, and it leaves through his heels into the earth, which is big enough to take the heat of a whole army and not notice. The Measurewrights who have been allowed near one call it a heat pump made of a man, a thing that carries warmth from where it is to where it is wanted and pays for the carrying; they can say to a degree how much it carries, and not one word about why the blood does it.

When he puts the cold somewhere on purpose, into a blade or along a line, he drives it out through the Shell, the body-side layer of the soul, down the arm and into the steel. The small walked Fate in him does the one other thing it knows: his Attraction Layer, the soul’s own share of Fate, says on which side of a mark a thing stands.

Applications

  • Deciding where the warmth ends, where the road ends and where he ends
  • Putting the cold into a blade or along a line on purpose
  • Holding a border, an oath or a man past the point where another would let go

Techniques

  • STARK RIME - the blood, which decides where the warmth ends
  • A LINE IN THE SNOW - the scratch, which decides where the road ends
  • BY THE SKY THAT COVERS US - the oath, which decides where he ends
  • THE BREAKING COLD - the cold sent through a bind into another man’s steel
  • THE STARK WALK - the walk, which keeps him on his own side of all three
  • DOWNHILL TO THE FIRE - the cold leaning toward a great heat
  • FROSTFALL - the same border drawn all the way round him

Associations

  • The Stark cold, an inheritance older than the Moto and older than the Starks’ own records, which works outside the Wellsprings and which the Starks do not discuss with outsiders
  • The Body Path, mastered, carries the cold, the blade and the feet; the Fate Path he walks carries the line and the oath
  • House Stark
  • The Stark sword, his bonded weapon, a plain northern longsword with a wolf’s head cut into the pommel
  • His father’s axe rides across his back and has never been bonded to anyone but Ulf
  • He has no Domain yet

Limitations

  • He cannot let go: what he has sworn to hold he holds past sense, after holding it has stopped making sense, which is Rigidity
  • Breaking an oath sworn by the sky is his Shear Break condition
  • When FROSTFALL falls, all the cold he was holding comes home into him at once
  • A little under half of what he spends reaches the world, which is his yield, and the share that does not is paid in the body, showing as the shake in his shoulders and the left side of his jaw
  • It is beaten by heat and by keeping him calm, by going round the ends of the line, by carrying bronze, copper or a warm blade, by making him hurry, or by setting two of his oaths against each other

Traits

STARK RIME · Inherited Trait

STARK RIME is basically heat absorption: the power to draw the warmth out of everything near him and send it into the ground. It is the cold in the Stark blood, and it never stops.

Capabilities

Heat leaves everything near him the way it leaves a house with the door open in winter. The air within a few paces of him drops, and the heat goes down through his body and out through his heels into the ground, which is why the Starks walk the way they walk. What anyone can see is the frost. The water that is always in the air settles on anything cold enough as white rime, so timber silvers near him, the bear-fur on his shoulders goes pale at the tips, snow freezes hard and squeaks under his boots, and his own breath, which should stand white in front of his face like every other man’s, has had the water taken out of it before it can fog.

It runs harder under feeling. When his jaw tightens on the left side, the frost spreads.

Applications

  • Chilling the air, the timber, the snow and the men within a few paces of him
  • Feeding the cold that his line, his blade and his Release all use

Associations

  • The Stark cold, the house’s inheritance and no Wellspring
  • The Body Path
  • The Stark walk, which keeps the heels on the ground where the heat goes out

Limitations

  • It moves heat and cannot make it: on frozen ground or in deep winter it has little to take, and a man already as cold as the snow gives it nothing
  • Against a great heat it is a bucket against a fire
  • The heat has to have somewhere to go: when the ground under his heels is itself inside the fire, he is only carrying heat from the air into the earth, and it comes straight back
  • Its named failure is the full sink: nowhere left to put the warmth, and the frost stops where it stands
  • It costs nothing he notices; he has never been warm in his life and does not miss it
  • Its tell is that his breath stops steaming and his beard whitens
  • Heat beats it: a hot room, a furnace, a fire-worker
  • Keeping him steady beats it, since a Stark who is not angry is only a cold man
  • A far stronger presence does the same work as a fire, driving his frost back toward his own boots before a blow is struck

A LINE IN THE SNOW · Primary Trait

A LINE IN THE SNOW is basically barrier generation: the power to scratch a line in the ground and make it a border of cold. House Stark defines where things end and begin, and his soul did that before he ever drew a sword.

Capabilities

He draws the point of his blade through the ground, snow or earth or a stone floor. Once it is drawn, his Attraction Layer takes the scratch as a border and holds it, and the Stark rime gathers along the mark into a low white wall of cold, a hand high and as long as the stroke.

The wall is low because it does not need to be tall. Cold air is heavy and lies along the ground like water in a ditch, and what crosses the line toward what he guards wades into it feet first: the leather stiffens, the wet on the sole freezes to the rime, the fingers on a hilt lose their feeling, and a chest that takes a breath of it closes for a moment the way it does when a man is thrown into a winter river. Nothing in it stops a body. It makes the first step over the line the worst footing and the slowest hand that man will have all day, and Lorn is standing on the far side with a sword.

Applications

  • Guarding a person or a stretch of road behind a line
  • Taking the footing, the fingers and the breath of whoever crosses toward what he guards
  • Running twice as long under FROSTFALL, or closing into a ring

Associations

  • The Fate Path he walks, which claims the border, with the Body’s cold laid along it
  • The Stark cold
  • The sword, whose point draws it

Limitations

  • It takes about two percent of his reserve to draw, and a little to hold
  • He must stand on his side of it: the claim is that this ground is his and that ground is not, and a man who steps over his own border has unsaid it, so it is only a scratch in the snow
  • Its length is one stroke of the arm and a step, a few yards, never a wall round a city
  • It needs a whole breath to set, half to draw and the rest to plant his feet and bring the point down
  • Against a fighter who can cross eighty paces inside that breath it is never drawn at all
  • Its tell is the point of the sword dragging through the ground
  • It is beaten by going round the ends, or by coming at him from the side the line does not face

BY THE SKY THAT COVERS US · Secondary Trait

BY THE SKY THAT COVERS US is basically oath empowerment: the power to swear a thing and become unable to let it go. His father took him onto the wall-walk in the dark and told him all of it in twelve sentences, made him swear by the sky that covers us to carry it until it could be carried by someone with a better right, and then rode south to the Kurenai blade. Lorn carried it.

Capabilities

When he swears by the sky, with his right fist pressed once, hard, to his breastbone, the terms are written where his own soul can read them, no witness is needed, and the oath sets in him like frost in a crack. The oath gives him no strength. It takes a question away. A frightened man’s arm opens because some part of him is still deciding whether to hold on, and pain and fear are the arguments for letting go. Once Lorn has sworn, that is settled, and the arguments have nobody to speak to. So while he is keeping it, fear does not reach him, pain arrives late, as news, and the arm that holds what he swore to hold does not open.

On the frozen road he swore it with no hand free for the fist, both arms locked round a king with two broken ribs who was trying to throw him: by the sky that covers us, by his father’s death and his honor. The arms did not open, and the oath is still owed.

Applications

  • Shutting fear out and making pain arrive late
  • Holding what he swore to hold with an arm that does not open
  • Carrying what his father gave him until someone with a better right can take it

Associations

  • The Fate Path he walks: a vow is small Fate and spends his own share
  • No lens; his Oath prices it
  • His father, who swore him to the first one
  • His oath to the red chair and to the man in it

Limitations

  • It takes no reserve: nothing to swear, and everything to keep
  • The price shows on him at once: he goes stiller and plainer, and he can no more do the thing he swore against than put his hand through a wall
  • It holds what he said and nothing else, and he chooses his words like a man choosing footing on ice
  • It breaks by its own terms the moment he breaks it
  • Its tell is the right fist pressed once, hard, to the breastbone
  • The counter is the oldest one in the north: set two of his oaths against each other, so that keeping one breaks the other, and he stops between them like a cart with a wheel in each rut

Techniques

THE BREAKING COLD · Signature Technique

THE BREAKING COLD is basically brittleness inducement: the power to chill another man’s steel through a bind until the next hard blow shatters it. It is his signature technique, drilled, and learned by living it.

Capabilities

The working is only the Stark cold sent through a bind; what breaks the blade is an old habit of iron that every northern smith knows by hand. Iron and ordinary steel are tough when warm and brittle when cold enough, and the change comes all at once, inside a few degrees, at a point the Measurewrights call the ductile-to-brittle transition. Above it a struck blade bends, rings and springs back, because the metal can still give a little inside itself. Below it the metal cannot give, the whole blow goes into the first flaw it finds, and the flaw runs, the way a horseshoe left out in the hard frost will snap where in summer it would only have bent.

He needs the bind for it, steel laid on steel, because cold has to travel through metal and metal carries it a great deal faster than air does. The rime goes down his blade and into the other man’s, frost-flowers open across the enemy’s steel from the place the two blades touch, and for the next few breaths that sword, axe-head or stretch of mail is glass waiting for a hammer. It rides a blow twice. The bind sets it and the cut that follows breaks it, so the same exchange holds an ordinary sword stroke and a ruined weapon.

Applications

  • Breaking an enemy’s sword or axe-head
  • Making a stretch of mail brittle before the cut lands on it

Associations

  • The Stark cold
  • The Body Path
  • His own sword, one of the few nickel-rich steels that never go brittle in the cold, which is why it cost what it cost

Limitations

  • It takes about five percent of his reserve
  • It works only on iron and ordinary steel; bronze, copper and the few fine steels that never go brittle in the cold take no harm from it at all
  • The frost has to cross the bind before the blades part
  • Its failure is plain: the wrong metal, or a bind broken before the frost has crossed, and the cold spreads into the air for nothing with a twentieth of his reserve gone after it
  • Its tell is frost-flowers blooming on the other man’s blade
  • Before the fight, carry bronze, copper or a warm blade
  • During it, disengage and let the steel warm before striking with it again; a blade that survived its chill is as good as it was
  • A swordsman who will not bind with him, who slips, voids and cuts at the hands, never gives it a road

THE STARK WALK · House Art, Stark

THE STARK WALK is basically stance mastery: the power to stand and move so that nothing can tip him. There is no working in it and it is the oldest thing he has; he has trained it every day since he was six.

Capabilities

Heavy, even, unhurried, the weight going straight down through both heels, the knees soft, the hands still at the sides, and nothing ever lifted that does not have to be. It is how the Starks cross their own halls and how they cross a battlefield, and it is a fighting stance. A man standing that way has his weight low and between his feet, so a shoulder, a hooked ankle or a thrown elbow has nothing to tip.

It is also the road the cold leaves by. The heels are where the heat goes out, and a Stark who keeps both of them on the ground keeps the door open, which is why the house teaches the walk before it teaches the sword.

Applications

  • Keeping his line against a shoulder, a trip or a thrown elbow
  • Drawing the sword without changing his feet
  • Holding a man who is fighting to be let go
  • Keeping the heels down so the cold has its road out

Associations

  • House Stark, which teaches it, and mastered in him
  • The Body Path, with no lens and no working
  • STARK RIME, which leaves by the heels

Limitations

  • It takes no reserve
  • Anyone can see it: heavy, even, unhurried, weight down through the heels
  • It is beaten by making him hurry, because a Stark who runs has stopped walking it and is only a cold man with a sword

DOWNHILL TO THE FIRE · Technique

DOWNHILL TO THE FIRE is basically heat detection: the power to feel where a great heat is standing before anyone has seen it. He had no word for it and his father had not left him one.

Capabilities

Heat runs toward cold, and he is the coldest thing on any road, so a great heat anywhere near him pulls at the whole of his cold from one side. He feels it as a lean, the way a man wading feels which way the river is going. A furnace of a man standing behind a tree at eighty paces drags his frost toward the tree, and the rime on the side of his beard that faces it melts as fast as it forms and runs.

Applications

  • Knowing where a fire-worker or a great heat is standing, out of sight
  • Feeling such a heat from a quarter mile and placing it at eighty to ninety paces

Associations

  • The Stark cold, used as a sense
  • The Body Path
  • Held, and learned by living it

Limitations

  • It takes no reserve
  • It tells him where a fire is and nothing else: not who, not how strong, not whether it means harm
  • He does not read people and cannot sense a soul, and with his Gnosis as empty as it is, the body is the only instrument he has
  • It needs a heat far above the day’s, and one source; a camp of cookfires or a smithy drowns it
  • Its tell is the lopsided beard, where the rime will not form on the side that faces the heat, and the frost round his boots creeping one way
  • It is beaten by standing among other fires, or by being no warmer than the wood

Releases

FROSTFALL · First Release

FROSTFALL is basically a frost form: the power to run the Stark cold at flood through his own body and hold it there. It is his First Release, open from Stage IV, which the north calls the White Sky, and he calls it low: “the sky covers us.”

Capabilities

The same road runs at flood. Heat is pulled in from every side and driven down through him and out by the heels at the full of the rime’s strength, far faster than the blood does it by itself. The road is his own body, and a road carrying that much gets colder than living flesh should ever be. For as long as the Release lasts the Charge packed in his Shell holds that cold out in his skin, his father’s mail and his blade and keeps it off his heart. So his breath stops steaming, his beard and the fur of his collar go white with rime, a skin of clear ice runs over his father’s mail, and the snow in a ring round him turns to silver and creaks like a lake taking weight. A LINE IN THE SNOW runs twice as long.

The sword makes a second shape of it. With the point driven into the ground between his boots, both hands on the pommel and his head down, the line he would have drawn closes into a ring. The frost runs outward ten paces across, over whoever is lying or standing inside it, and the wet in the air above it falls as ice as fine as flour.

Applications

  • Striking with STARK RIME at the top of its Grade
  • Doubling the length of his line
  • Closing the line into a ring ten paces across to shelter whoever is inside
  • Keeping the air inside the ring only as hot as a summer noon inside a great fire’s field

Techniques

  • The ring - the point driven into the ground between his boots, both hands on the pommel, and the line closes round him ten paces across
  • Called silent - the rime stays on his hands and blade and goes no further

Associations

  • The Stark cold
  • The Body Path
  • The Stark sword, his bonded weapon, which carries FROSTFALL’s rime to its edge
  • His father’s mail, which the ice runs over

Limitations

  • It lasts about three minutes from a full reserve
  • Called silent it is weaker: the rime stays on his hands and blade
  • Inside a great fire’s field the ground is inside the fire, the heat has nowhere to go, and all the ring buys is minutes
  • It ends the way a held breath ends: the holding stops, everything he was keeping at arm’s length comes into him at once, a man who has never been warm in his life is cold for the first time, and he shakes and needs a fire
  • Heat beats it, as it beats the Trait
  • So does its own end: when it falls, the ground round him is ice
