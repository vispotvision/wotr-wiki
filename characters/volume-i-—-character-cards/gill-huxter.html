Gill Huxter

Gill Huxter

Nov 28, 20265 min read

Gill Huxterhuckster (peddler of ribbons, pins and gossip) on the great bridge at Kingsbridge · Human · Accord, Midmarch commons · as of the Tollgarth post, mid-April 715 IC
EpithetArch-an-Hour Gill · what the bridge calls her, after the reeve’s man who moves her on one arch every hour, so that by dusk she has crossed the river twice without meaning to; she hates it, answers to it, and has never once let anybody else finish the joke
ConceptA fast-mouthed ribbon girl with her thumb on the scale who would sooner be caught than let off.
Stat lineUnwoken · no Stage, no rank (Seed)
Level 7 · unwoken · unclassed; the unwoken are offered none
no Path walked, and an unwalked Path reads nothing · Lenses: none
Combat grammarnone
Aura and soundnone; an unwoken soul shows no coat. What reaches a crowd ahead of her is the cry, three times over and rising, and red and yellow silk jumping on four brass hooks · sound: the snap of the steelyard hooked back on its strap
How she movesquick and sideways through a press, the tray riding level on its strap while everything under it hurries, skirts hitched at the hip and pattens loud on wet stone; she hooks a length down, takes the coin and pockets it in one movement without looking at it, and her right thumb lives on the steelyard’s pan. She does not fight.
Stacknone; no reserve to spend and nothing to layer
Kita painted lime-wood ribbon tray on a neck strap with her own small steelyard clipped to it, and the piece people talk about first: her thumb on the steelyard’s pan
Voice”Ribbons, ribbons, ribbons, red for a rose and yellow for a fool, ribbons for your bride and your bridle and your bracket boy…” · speaker type: a bridge huckster, fast and mocking, the last word always hers

Realm / Factionthe great bridge at Kingsbridge; on no hall’s roll and holding no token, so she has no fixed pitch and the reeve’s man moves her on one arch every hour, six years now. Now in Tollgarth, up the Harl road on the wool carts by Ned’s guess
Wanta fixed pitch on the bridge, a stall that doesn’t move: her own name on the roll, her own coin, her own tray, nobody standing her anywhere (said aloud); fixed pitches are let on a sealed bill to pack-roll members only
Refusalshe will not be pitied, nor paid in kind (“I’ll not be paid in kind, Chapman, nor pitied in it neither”); she will not be stood on Ned’s pitch; she will not be let off: she would sooner the beam and the post than a short weight made good by someone else, because called is square
Lie in playher thumb sits on the steelyard’s pan and she says it is resting; she had said so for six years. At the Tollgarth post the steelyard said an ounce and the skein was about four fifths of one, and she knows Ned saw (“You saw”).
Talentshe can whistle a cart-horse to a stop through the gap in her teeth, and has, on a bet
Classnone; unwoken, and no class is offered there
