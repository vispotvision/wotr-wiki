READS THE BELTPrimary———Before a face, the belt: he hears the note of the bronze seal-token on it the way other men hear a tune, sound or failing, old or new, and knows what its wearer is bound to.The whole of its mechanism: it reads the token, and a person with no token gives it nothing to read. Then he has to fall back on the plain eye, which a working life of belts has left a good deal less practiced than a soldier’s.The quick open glance: his eyes go to your belt, then to the belt of whoever is with you, and only then come back up (quickly and openly, before your face).Nothing, because nothing is made and nothing is thrown.Wear no token, or one that says nothing (a token that tells him nothing).No reserve
THE OLDEST CONTRACTSecondary———While he is keeping the Vessant house’s oldest paper he is calm, courteous and very hard to shift, and the thumb that runs once round the rim of his token is the only part of him that moves.It is loyal to the paper, not the people on the road. Twenty-two men dead or missing did not breach the contract, so the token kept its note, and so did he. The steadiness is the contract’s, lent to him, and he has not found out what he is with the bronze gone quiet.The thumb, once round the rim of the token; and when he lies, the breath high under the collarbone.Nothing from the reserve to keep; the price is that he is the paper’s man before he is his own, and his breath pays for every lie he tells on its behalf.Ask him what the contract has cost rather than what it pays, because the paper has no column for that and he has to answer as a man. Ask how many came back, not how many died, and watch his collarbone when he answers.the Vessant contract: thirty-one years sound

Physical Description

FeatureDescription
FaceHe is younger than the job makes him sound, with a long, even face, a straight nose and a level mouth.
HairHis hair is brown, worn thick and untidy, pushed back and falling forward again across his brow.
EyesHis eyes are a clear green and steady, and they go to your belt first.
MarksInk stains the second finger of his right hand, the scribe’s mark, though he has none of the scribe’s stoop.
Dress and the tokenHe wears a long dark traveling coat with a high collar, cut close and lined with something soft at the throat, the snow still on its shoulders when he comes in from the street, and oiled boots; on his belt, where a fighting man would wear a knife, a bronze Vessant seal-token the size of a large coin on a short chain, humming faintly for as long as the contract holds.
