The Practitioner's Field Manual — The Forge

The Practitioner's Field Manual — The Forge

Nov 17, 202637 min read

Being a working register of what each kind of work can and cannot do, and the Forge by which Abilities and Techniques are built at speed

Craft Companion, the Seventh Guide

Governs how magic is written. Does not govern what magic is.

“Ask what he is permitted to do with the power he has, and what it takes out of him when he does. The size of it comes last.”

Oswin Crumb, baker of Karrowgate, Stage III (third on the soul’s sixteen-step ladder), had a heat trade worth two copper an oven. He lit ovens. He had lit every oven on Dyers’ Lane for twenty-three years with the same small, smoky orange push from the same two fingers, and on the morning the bailiff’s men came about the arrears, three of them, in good boots, with his daughter’s name on their paper, he put those two fingers against the first man’s breastbone and did the same thing, with the same spoonful of Charge (raw Aether, packed in the Shell and never refined), all at once.

The shutters of Dyers’ Lane left their hinges in order, flour hung in the street like a snowfall going sideways, and a tray of rolls was found on the church roof. The bailiff’s men lay in the road with their eyebrows on and their boots shining and not one burn among them, and every one of them had drowned in his own lungs. Crumb himself never heard another word from anybody. A Measurewright (a sworn measurer of the Guild) walked in from the Assize before the dust had settled, ate one of the surviving rolls, and wrote on a slate, in a hand that got larger as she went: Same fuel. Same Charge. Entirely different corpse.

That slate is the Delivery Rate Law, below, and this manual is what the Guild’s compilers did about it. A word to whoever opens it: everything here is about how a working gets onto the page and earns its place there. I have kept the arguments in, because they are better than the conclusions.

The Two Layers

Nothing in the Continuum (the lawful fabric of reality) became more complicated. A second layer accumulated on top of the first, the two were carried as one thing, and the second layer is accounting.

LayerWhat it is
The Generative LayerConcept → Affinity → Ability → Technique, seen through a Wellspring (the lens the soul has tempered on) and going out by a Path (the layer the Crystal leans on) in a [[The Magic System/Reference and Craft/Forms
The Accounting LayerLevel, Band, Stage, Grade, Tier of Standing, Class (the name a soul is offered at its thresholds), Aether, Essence and Thread Class (the three ladders, each setting how much power survives the trip through its own layer), EU (Essence Units, the measure of a reserve), yield (η, the share of spent power that does its work, as a distillation has its yield). This settles what a practitioner can actually do and who wins. It answers how much. Load it before writing, never abandon it while writing, and put its numbers on the page only through a mouth, an instrument, a document, a private count or the bearer’s own window; its names may stand in any sentence

Both layers stay open. The generative layer decides what happens; the accounting layer decides whether it could have.

Every outcome should trace to a specific row, and the working notes should say which. What the reader receives is behavior and body, and the window right after it. What the numbers do is make sure the behavior was earned.

The window. The Soul Crystal is the soul and the Continuum measures it, so a window is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in their own tongue, unseen by anyone else. Write them often and in detail, fights included, for damage, skill gains and reserve. Each sits on the page as its own text block, following the blow it reports, and the prose around it stays body first. It shows Grades, percentages, bars and levels. The climb and the purse. Drafts and blessings can supply a Catalyst, and Stages can simply be bought, so the wealthy are stronger because they are wealthy. The great houses hoard the top Drafts; the poor rise by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings. A bought Stage opens the door and no more: a Release must still be found and mastered by fighting, and sects teach what money cannot buy, Releases, Domains, techniques, skills and the rites for their Wellspring. When a character’s Stage is higher than their fighting, that gap is a scene, and usually a funny one until it is not.

Reading anyone else. A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward. Everything else is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide or fake it, which gives every fighter on the page a reason to lie about their numbers and every opponent a reason to test them.

The Rule for Invention Under Fire

Abilities are character architecture: a root idea the Crystal (the Soul Crystal, the soul itself, which works magic) has internalized over years. Cozbi is Antithesis. Krothar is Binding. Sodoku is Ruin.

These are never invented inside a scene. If a fight appears to require a new Ability, the fight is wrong, or the scene stops and the Ability is pitched properly.

Techniques are moves: a specific repeatable application within an Ability already established, a named thing done with a skill. These may be invented at speed, mid-scene, without stopping, provided they are built through the Forge and logged afterward.

Invent freely downward, never upward. A new Technique under a known Ability is craft. A new Ability under a known character is a decision about the whole person wearing a technique’s coat.

The Forge

Before the first step, the concept. A kit starts as one wild idea you can explain in a breath, soup, debt, doors, boredom, anything, so long as the character can push it somewhere terrifying. Character first, then the concept, and only then the lens, the Wellspring and the glyph that explain it and the Path it goes out by; the Sixty and the Glyphs stay fixed and the idea finds its place among them. A major character starts with three to five named moves and adds one when a fight or scene earns it. A fighter may invent and name a new move in the middle of a fight, and it stands from then on. The Forge below takes that idea and makes it hold.

Seven steps at the desk. Steps one through four are structural and cannot be skipped. Five through seven are what separate a technique from a special effect.

StepWhat it demands
1 · The ideaName it from what the idea actually does in the soul and the world, not from the feeling it evokes. A grief-working that rots a body works on flesh. A grief-working that removes the memory of a face works on mind. Name the bench operation it performs (calcination, dissolution, separation, conjunction, fermentation, distillation, coagulation or sublimation) and the flaw it cannot hold. This step is idea-first and it will offend intuition roughly a third of the time. Follow it anyway
2 · VerbOne verb from that idea’s verb table. A technique that uses three verbs is three techniques badly stapled together
3 · RatePush or shatter. This single decision determines what the corpse looks like and is the most underused instrument available
4 · LimitationState what it structurally cannot do before stating what it does. A power invented before its constraint is a power looking for a constraint to justify it, and it reads bolted-on every time
5 · CostFrom the Wheel, deliberately, checked against the character’s existing category; a big art’s price comes out of its own idea, on top of reserve and body tells. A cast where everything costs blood is a cast with one idea
6 · CounterplayOne sentence naming what answers it: its contrary idea, a soul further refined in its own idea, ground that holds another idea, or a refusal of its claim. The answer may be out of reach, rare or none at all; the flaw its idea gives it stays
7 · StampThe Codex line: glyph pair, primary Wellspring (the lens it comes through, in the Measurewrights’ account the type of Aether it pulls on), its kind and Titan, the Operation, the Category and the Stage; then Path, Form and Gates. The Measurewrights’ phenomenon may follow, labeled as theirs. Thirty seconds. Never left unlinked

The three-line version

For use mid-scene, at speed, when a named character needs to do something in the next paragraph.

VERB. RATE. PRICE.

He does one operation from his idea, at a stated speed, and it takes something the reader can see out of him.

The stamp and the counterplay can be filled in at the end of the pass without damaging the prose. The price cannot be added retroactively, because a cost the reader never watched get exacted did not happen.

The gate the Forge does not list, because it stands outside the steps. A Signature Technique requires a matching Trait, and no amount of Stage, instruction or expenditure substitutes for one. The Trait is a structural prerequisite rather than an advantage: it is the law the working applies, and without it the technique has nothing to apply.

Which is the test for whether a signature has been invented honestly. Name the Trait it comes out of. If there isn’t one, what has been built is a good technique belonging to nobody in particular.

The Road It Leaves By

The stamp asks for a Path, and no other line on it changes the page so much.

A blow’s character comes from Wellspring and Path together. Every soul has the three layers, and a Path is the one its Crystal has learned to lean on, so ask which one a working goes out through before writing a word of it. A Wellspring is the lens a soul bases its tempering on, and it shapes what each road works, the idea staying put and the target changing: Pyreveil on the Body shapes raw Aether in the Shell and is heat living in the tissue, burning matter; on the Spirit it shapes Essence in the Core and burns what a soul means and holds, a grief, a grudge, an attachment, and leaves the coat unscorched; and on Fate it is a claim that burns its way through, and what it burns is chances. A Wellspring’s kind names its home and never locks it. At home the lens can reach double after years of mastery, and away from home it costs more and reaches half again at most. A Spirit Wellspring gives no fire on any road. Write what the person can bear, know, keep or let go.

PathWrite it asThe limits a fight must honor
BodyThe literal understanding and manipulation of physical reality. Low down it closes and hits. High up, by recognition and then by law, its reach runs as far as there is matter to read: armor too heavy to lift, a blade rusting in the handNeeds existing substance, has to close until very high, pays in recoil through bones and Crystal, and has no purchase on a soul
SpiritEverything to do with spiritual reality: souls, spirits, the dead, what a thing means. Authored in the Core and thrown at range, past the Shell, where it lands on souls only: a soul knocked out of true, a dread laid on a mind, a Core woundedNeeds a soul or a meaning to work on, has no purchase on matter except through its own Shell at its own Aether Class, has a fragile body, and dies out of position
FateDay to day, Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds. Three roads spend three things, and the pull step of a working names its own road.

The shared tells at half, a quarter and a tenth stand on every road, and these go on top of them. An art that throws fire, force or matter at range on a Spirit line alone is written wrong. Make it a Body-and-Spirit art, with the Body half doing the matter through the Shell, or let its Spirit half work on the souls in the blast, and keep its story, name, numbers and cost. Someone who walks both roads carries the split on the card. The routes are set out on Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell, and the tables of tiring on The Three Paths.

Fate is never casual. A pull takes extreme effort at every Stage, it cannot be reasoned out or drilled in, and almost nobody can do it twice in a day, so a character who does has been written wrong or is about to pay for it. Moving a whole field is a life’s masterwork or a bloodline’s art, and the Works of Hataraki exist because nobody grips it bare. Every pull, landed or failed, leaves a knot, so the price of a pull is written twice: the share of the reserve now, and the knot on the ledger, due within days for a lean and within the arc for a removed prerequisite (The Tell and the Knot).

No road is the better one. Body beats Spirit by getting there, and Spirit beats Body by going past the Shell. Body beats Fate with force and soundness, and Fate beats Body through what closing depends on. Spirit beats Fate by reaching the hand on the thread, and Fate beats Spirit because Spirit survives by being right. Stage, ground and preparation decide which of these holds on the day, and a kit that reads as simply the superior road is a kit with no counterplay. The Three Paths has the full account.

The Delivery Rate Law

It generalizes across every kind of matter a working can touch as one rule and resolves the recurring problem of every escalation reading as simply larger. The rate is the worker’s to choose and the idea’s to carry; what the blow then does to bodies and walls is the world’s old habit, and the middle column gives the Measurewrights’ names for each.

Every working has a delivery rate. Rate decides whether the effect pushes or shatters. Magnitude does not.

The same reserve (Charge on the Body road, Essence on the Spirit), the same Wellspring, the same practitioner, delivered slow or delivered instantaneously, produce two entirely different injuries. This is the correct way to escalate a character without inflating a number, and it is available at every Stage from Murmuring upward.

The road pays first. Before the wheel is turned, the Path has already named a cost. A Body working is paid in the body, which is the wheel’s physical toll and arrives as heat. A Spirit working is paid in the self, in color and memory, which is why Memory sits so well on that road. A Fate pull is paid in a knot, a debt that falls due later on the puller or on what is hers. The wheel’s category goes on top of the road’s bill and never in place of it.

The Vow Clause

Lives in Votia; sealed as Claustra.

A practitioner may bind a technique under a stated condition, sworn into the Lattice or into their own Crystal. The narrower the condition and the heavier the forfeiture, the greater the output.

“Against those who deserve it” buys nothing. “Only against the Zettari, and if I break it my Crystal shatters” is a vow.

Why it works. The Core adopts the restriction as a standing law, and Tempering Coherence measures the alignment between a practitioner’s stated law and their lived behavior. Coherence is the structural prerequisite for Threshold eligibility. A vow kept therefore buys Coherence with conduct instead of expenditure, which makes it the cheapest advancement mechanism in the world and the one that clusters among people who cannot afford Drafts. A vow is small Fate, a person binding their own probability, and anyone can make one: spoken, it shows in the window with the bargain and its terms, and what it buys counts toward the two-Grade cap. See Vows and Oaths.

Why it kills. A broken vow lands at once by its own terms and needs no witness. Repeated denial of the truth behind it makes a Shear Break. The Core has been running a law the bearer has now denied, and the law does not negotiate its own withdrawal. The orders treat a lapsed vow as a medical emergency and get to the disciplinary question later, if there is one.

The disclosure variant is the useful one. Stating a technique’s true mechanism aloud, in the target’s hearing, before use, surrenders every advantage of surprise and increases output accordingly.

This is the best structural justification for a villain’s monologue ever devised and the Continuum should take it.

What the clause buys is an answer to power creep. A character becomes dramatically stronger by narrowing rather than escalating.

A Stage XIV fighter has no use for a larger number. What would change him is a vow the reader can watch him keep.

The Two Tests Before Presenting

The Collapse Test · If two techniques under one Ability share a Limitation, a Cost, and a Counterplay, they are one technique with two names. Collapse them.

The Name Test · Does the name tell you something true about who is using it? It may be the idea itself, direct and unadorned, Ruin is Ruin, Gravity is Gravity, or it may be one big shouted phrase, or a signature’s title with its moves under it (Art: Move), in the fighter’s own tongue with the meaning landing in the same beat. Anyone may reach for a word like World, God, Fate or Infinite: the room laughs until it works. Goofy names can be truly lethal on anyone whose card fits. A name that could belong to any practitioner in the Continuum has failed.

What Each Kind of Work Cannot Do

What each kind of work governs, and, more usefully, what it categorically cannot do. Every line below is a habit the world settled in the Physical Epochs: a working changes the worker, and the world gives way as far as that soul is truly changed, and none of these habits is ever overturned. The lens changes how a working feels and what it charges, and it does not lift them. The wording is the Measurewrights’, who are right about how much.

The Working Trades, for Whoever Writes Them

The workbench behind The Working Trades, which keeps the story and the trades themselves in the hall’s voice.

The Design Problem

A note from the compilers, standing outside the hall’s own voice, on where the tradition was taken from. It stays here because every lever below hangs off it.

The casting model as it stands is correct and complete. It is also, in its center of gravity, Eastern.

The five-step pipeline (Intent, Pattern, Essence Donation, Aether Formation, Attraction Anchoring; see The Disciplines) runs inside one practitioner, in real time, in a single continuous act of will. The most legible unit is the Technique: a named, repeatable thing a person does with their body and their reserve, at a stated cost. Every load-bearing question the system asks is a question about one soul’s interior.

That is the cultivation shape. It is the core of the system and it stays the core, and it is not what Tolkien, Martin, Clarke or Le Guin are doing; the trades below build their made things around it.

What the Western tradition does instead

Tolkien has made things where other books have spells. The power lives in objects made well by people who were what they were: the Rings, the Phial, mithril, lembas, the rope that unties itself. Galadriel’s correction to Sam is the whole thesis: mortals use one word for the Enemy’s deceits and for the craft of the Eldar, and the Elves do not have a word that separates art from enchantment at all. Nobody in Middle-earth casts. They make, and the making holds.

Martin has an economy of denial where other books have a system. Rare, foreign, unreliable, morally contaminating. Its price is paid in blood and in life, directly, with no reserve to spend first. The maesters have an institutional interest in insisting the subject is superstition, which is itself a plot engine, because they are wrong and they are also not entirely wrong.

Clarke gives the sharpest structural gift, and the trade is already most of the way to it. A scholarly tradition with a bibliography, a lost golden age, live controversies, and a hard social distinction between the theoretical magician who reads and would never dream of performing, and the practical magician who performs and is therefore vulgar.

The academies and research halls under the Guild Accord are four-fifths of the way to being that institution and do not yet know it.

And the deepest one: the trade’s own glyph system is already Western Renaissance occultism.

The Parun (the language of law every working is written in) lattice is Agrippa’s kamea method out of the Three Books, II.22, unaltered. That is the operative tradition of European ceremonial magic, lifted whole.

Which means the Four Glyph Roles are already the Solomonic operation, in order, and nobody has noticed.

Glyph RoleSolomonic operationWhat it does
AuthorizationThe conjuration and the citation of authorityNames by what right the operator acts
BoundaryThe circle and the triangle of artFixes where the working may reach
DirectionThe constraintCompels a specific outcome
SealingThe license to departCloses the operation and releases the power

The four roles are the boundary operation, in order, and the order is not decorative.

A glyph creates nothing. A Wellspring (the governing idea, alchemical and spiritual at once, that transforms how a soul uses Aether, and in the Measurewrights’ account one of sixty types of Aether with its own physical law) was already at work. Authorization says by what right a limit is set, Boundary sets it, Direction states which way the law is to run across it, and Sealing takes the limit back down.

Which is why Sealing is the role that kills people. Authorization, Boundary and Direction failing produce a working that goes wrong while the operator is still in the room. Sealing failing produces a working that does not stop, because the law goes on obeying itself after the operator’s attention has left the room. Every burned workshop in the Accord’s incident record is a Sealing failure and roughly a third of them are recorded as something else.

Nobody needs to import the Western frame into the trade. It only needs digging out.

The Five Levers

I · Let the guilds feud over rank. Real-time casting sits at the center by default, and the crowd adores it. In the halls, the made object and the prepared substance sit above the live caster, and the man the crowd came to see is told so at the door. Nothing in the system backs either side. That is a feud, and feuds are free, and they change everything about how the world reads.

II · Contest the word. Nobody calls it magic. Each trade carries an Accord name in liturgical Latin and a plain name in the common tongue, and which one a character reaches for places them by class before they have finished the sentence. This is the Norman and Anglo-Saxon doublet English actually runs on, and it is Tolkien’s method exactly.

III · Make the golden age real, lost, and officially denied. The best inscriptions in the world are all old. Some of them cannot be read. The Accord’s published position is that this is not the case.

IV · Make the filing political. The Forms, and every older sorting of magic before them, are an Accord publication before they are a law of the world. Documents serve the people who publish them. Somebody’s trade was reclassified as Mechanica on grounds that were political before they were metaphysical.

V · Move cost from the ledger to the page. EU (Essence Units, the measure of a reserve), Flux Density (how densely power packs into the Crystal) and yield (η, the share of spent power that does its work) remain the compilers’ business and unchanged, and even a person’s own window (the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, unseen by anyone else) shows only Grades, percentages, bars and levels. What a reader sees is the Cost Wheel: the years, the memory, the sanction, the debt, the man who knows what you bought.

Prose cues, trade by trade

The speaking. The speaker pays inside the same scene, visibly. Nosebleed, tremor, the hand that will not close, the four days of sleeping badly afterward. Somebody in the room reacts to the fact that a working was spoken aloud in company, because in some rooms that is an obscenity. Nobody polite says casting.

The cutting. Chisels, drift, tooling marks, the grain of the stone and where it fought the cut. Somebody’s hands. The apprentice who holds the light. A wall with three hands on it, two of them dead. Weather on an inscription. Somebody scraping a rune off a doorframe with a knife, badly, because the man who cut it is now an enemy. This is where the awe and the old grief get their material, and where the Tolkien register lives without a word of it needing to be stated.

Standing rule for prose: the wall is older than the war. Any standing inscription on the page should carry a date, an author, or a visible repair, and the repair should be worse than the original.

The pouring. Price, always. Weight and volume. What the vessel is made of and why that matters. Sediment. A seal, intact or broken. Somebody checking a seal. An excise stamp. The difference between what the label says and what is in the bottle, and the person who knows the difference and says nothing.

The singing, reference points. Finrod and Sauron singing at each other until one of them falls. Lúthien before the throne. The monastic office sung eight times a day for six hundred years by men who were replaced one at a time.

Which column a character speaks from is characterization before it is information. Bodily speaking is not a separate art and should never be written as one.

What This Changes on the Page

The default working in a quiet scene should be a cut or a bottle, or some petty art done by hand for a fee. A wall that holds. A bottle that was bought. A lamp-warden arguing about what you owe her. The speaking owns the fight: it is what a fighter shouts when the blades are out, and what anyone reaches for when out of time or out of options.

Every named practitioner has skills, and usually one trade they are known for, and that is characterization. Sodoku speaks because Sodoku fights. Somebody in Marrowhold cuts. Somebody in the Amber Cities pours and keeps two sets of books.

Cost is visible outside the working. The Engraver’s hands. The Drafter’s ledger. The speaker who is still shaking at supper.

The ranking is contested in dialogue. A character who calls the pouring mere cookery is expressing a live guild position and is entitled to be wrong loudly.

Elegy attaches to one named thing that is gone. An array under a particular gate that four generations have failed to reproduce, and the last man who could read its third layer, who died in the winter.

Settled Positions

QuestionSettled position
I · Law V settled as a gateLaw V, the Law of Material Anchor, is a Stage gate rather than a universal. Below Stage VII, Refraction, a working requires voice, hand, ink or blood: an external anchor for the boundary. At Refraction the practitioner can hold the Crystal as an object of study, and the Glyph Roles compress into an internal chain that needs no material anchor at all. This is why internalized speaking reads as a mark of rank rather than a technique, and why a man who casts silently in a room has told everyone present his Stage without saying a number
II · Law III struckLaw III is struck from the register. It states that progression follows the fourteen Wellsprings. There are sixteen Stages and sixty Wellsprings and the two were never the same axis. It survives in-world as an error with a history: the fourteen it counted were the Archons, and a copyist’s conflation four hundred years old propagated into three generations of teaching texts before anyone checked. Older practitioners still teach it. They are wrong and they are extremely difficult to correct
III · The singingIt stands on its own terms, the medium-home of Harmonia, and answers to no guild
IV · Whose trade is bestA feud, and the feud varies by culture, downstream of material conditions rather than taste. The Concord baseline ranks cutting first. The Marrowkin rank it first and hardest, since a hold is a permanent substrate and everything they own is inscribed. Kharven ranks pouring first: nothing in a camp that moves is permanent, a Draft travels in a belt, and the standing runework in the death-houses is imported work that no living Kharven hand can read, a fact the seat does not raise and the Accord does not stop mentioning. The speaking ranks highest in Kharven of anywhere in the Concord’s reach, because a soldier is not embarrassed by an art that works now
V · The lost golden ageThe Accord is mistaken, and has become invested in the mistake, which produces a worse institution than lying would. The recovery program is real, funded, sincere and structurally incapable of succeeding, because what was lost was a substrate condition that no longer obtains, and no quantity of technique gets it back. Four generations of honest men have failed at it and each has been promoted for the attempt. Somebody in the research halls has worked this out. They have not published
VI · Scope of the doubletThe wider scope. Every trade term in the four quarters carries an Accord register name and a common-tongue name, and which one a character reaches for places them before they finish the sentence
VII · Standing InventoryEntered for Kharven. Household object: the doorframe, and whether it has been cut. Oath: by the hand that cut it. Proverb, fixed wording: “A cut wall keeps a promise longer than the man who made it.” Contempt terms carry over intact, a mouth, a cutter, a potboy, and potboy is the one that starts fights, because Kharven pours

The Blank Card

Copy, fill, log. Fields marked † are mandatory for anything that will later resolve a conflict.

Name · Ability / Root Idea · Path · Skill It Rides · Trigger † · Function † · Verb · Rate · Mechanism † · Operation † · Numerical Effect · Target Response · Limitation † · Weakness · Cost † · Vow · Counterplay † · Codex Stamp † · First Display † · Window Line · Sensory Description

Cross-reference. This card is the workbook form and carries more than any single scene will use. Mechanism is prose-legal and belongs on the page: the idea and its operation, what it does to the worker’s soul and how that reaches the world, the glyph’s limit, and the fault that falls out of the idea’s flaw. What stays in the workbook is the form itself, the Codex stamp, and the arithmetic.

An entry the reader sees runs: the name in caps with its native name and kind; a hook a child could repeat; the named moves, a line each; How it really works, the whole account by the lens (the idea, what it does to the soul, how that reaches the world, the glyph’s limit), with any science only as a comparison or a Measurewright’s line; one short Cost line and one short Counter line; and a Numbers block at the bottom for the exact figures. Effect, Cost, Limit and Counter all stay required, the Counter naming whatever answers it, out of reach or none included, plus one line of what nobody knows.
