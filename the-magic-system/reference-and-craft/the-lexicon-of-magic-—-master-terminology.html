The Lexicon of Magic — Master Terminology

The Lexicon of Magic — Master Terminology

Nov 28, 202645 min read

People have died of this page. In the spring of a bad year at Altherion, a surgeon’s bill for a dead wool merchant named a Rupture, and two Measurewrights (the Guild’s sworn measurers) fell out over which one: Dame Ysolde Crane held that the bill meant a Rupture of the Shell, Magister Lowell Tench that it meant a Rupture Event, and the widow, who only wanted to know whether she owed the surgeon nine gold or nothing, made the error of asking them in the Guild’s reading room. It began with citations. It went on to brass assay forks, then to lecterns, with Crane’s aura up in arterial red and Tench’s in ochre and the whole gallery paying two copper a head to lean over the rail, and it ended when a hurled lectern met the oak table and brought the Statutes shelf down on both of them.

They are still called the Duel of the Two Ruptures, and the dent is still in the table. Both of them lived, each with one collarbone fewer than they had started with, and the treaty they signed from adjoining beds is the entry you will find below under Clouding · Rupture. I would ask you to read the rest of this page with that in mind. Nobody here is being fussy.

The central terminology hub for the Continuum. Every magical term on these shelves is listed here or on a page linked from here.

The Planes, Aether and Essence, the Soul Crystal, Ability and Technique, Progression and Output, and Traits, Refinement and the Seat live on The Core Vocabulary and are not repeated here. That page runs Sections I to VII by its own count, and this one takes up its own count from Section VI.

Section XV is the one to read before writing anything for a lay reader. It sorts every term on this page into what may appear on such a page and what is workbook only.

Sections XVI to XIX are the newest ink on the shelf: the sheet and the climb, monsters and dungeons, guilds and items, Fate and blessings. Where an older entry reads otherwise, those govern.

VI · Form, lens and discipline

The Wellspring is the what. The Form is the how. Every art states a Path, a Lens, a Form and its Gates, and the card of the person holding it states where they got it. The long account, with Big Maud in it, is Forms.

ShapeBodyTell matter, force or heat what to do, by hand or at reach.
ReadSpiritFind out what is there, what it was and where it leans.

A home is a qualifier and never a lock, as a Wellspring’s kind is.

Seam Art · A combat art whose Form crosses two Paths, a Brew fought with for Body and Spirit or a Song for Spirit and Fate, worked at the Veil’s seams where the planes meet. Rare, unstable and feared, because it sits on no one plane and so nothing on one plane counters it cleanly. Ordinary Draft-making and chanting are trades and skills and are no Seam Arts; the name is kept for an art actually worked at the seam. The long account is on Forms.

Source · How a person came by an art: Lived, Taught, Inherited, Bought or Blessed. It goes on the card, since the same art can be any of the five in different hands.

Method · A clerk’s older sorting word, with a suffix that still names things. Manteia -mancy (Read). Kinesia -kinesis (Shape). Turgia -turgy (Rite, Make). Corporis -kinesis of the body (Strike). Scriptia -graphy (Write), the word on the door of everyone who cuts a rune, a ward or a tag. Alchemia -genesis, -synthesis (Brew). Harmonia -phonia, -chord (Sing), the chant being its commonest tool. Conjunctia, compound roots: one working on two or more lenses at once, and not the alchemical Conjunction, which weds two prepared laws in a vessel. Synergia, named by working: several practitioners combining, on bonds that have to be real. The table is on Forms.

Discipline · The clerk’s name for one Affinity and one Method. Pyromancy, Ferrokinesis, Geoturgy. The Affinity is the single face of a lens’s idea a person works (fire within Pyreveil), and it gives the root.

Magister · Registered Natural · Unregistered Natural · The Bureau’s three words for how a practitioner came by what they can do: an academy and its records, another road and a monitor, another road and a refusal. See The Gate.

VII · Glyphica and the Parunic syntax

Parun The language of law. Older than any surviving kingdom and not fully cataloged by anyone. Glyphica is its syntax put to work, the writing every Form borrows when it needs a sentence: spoken in a called working, cut in a rune or a tag.

What a glyph does A glyph creates nothing and carries no power. The Wellspring’s law was already running and continues after the working ends. A glyph limits what a Wellspring’s idea is doing, at the layer the working leaves through: a physical limit on the Shell, what a thing is taken to be on the Core, a term or a prerequisite on the Attraction Layer. The working is what the law does once the limit has moved. Everything a working produces goes on keeping every habit the world has ever kept, including the ones nobody at the bench considered.

Glyph A single unit of Parunic syntax. Explicit (carved, drawn, spoken) or implicit (internalized as a chain the practitioner no longer consciously assembles).

Wordform Chain A sequence of glyphs assembled into a complete sentence the Continuum can read.

The Four Glyph Roles Every structured working must satisfy all four, whether spoken once or carved into granite for a thousand years.

RoleWhat it establishesWhat a bad one does once the sentence has fired
AuthorizationWhich Wellsprings are invoked, and that the practitioner is the lawful channel.Forged or coerced, Mechanica by definition. The Continuum treats it as debt.
BoundaryWhere and how long the effect is permitted to operate.A Boundary failure: a transformation that spreads as far as the broken limit lets it, while the operator is still there to see it.
DirectionVector and focus. The editorial layer of the sentence.Draft Corruption.
SealingThat the sentence is finished and closed against contamination. Relies on Fixatio chains.An open wound in the local Aether field, which the Continuum will try to close on its own terms.

A role left wholly empty does nothing: no seal, no working, no cost. Only a sentence that has fired can hurt anyone. These four are the Warrant, the Ground, the Vector and the Seal of the spoken grammar, and the Master Glyph Index has the whole account of what a fault does and how long a working lasts.

Fixatio chain · The glyph sequence that fixes and closes a working. The workhorse of every seal, ward and permanent inscription in the Continuum.

Inscription · Glyphica rendered as a durable cut rather than a performance. Write is the Form that lives by it, and people who want to charge more for it have a dozen grander words.

Rune · tag · chant · Three things people mean by holding a working, and three skills anyone with a woken Crystal can level by doing them, with a guild title to say how far the hall trusts you at it. A rune is the sentence cut into material, where it goes on working after the cutter has gone home; a standing runed working is an array. A tag is a sentence prepared on something small, fueled per tag and fired only by a practitioner. A chant is sung, or held in the attention like a held note, and it stops when the speaker does; it sets its condition in resonance and is no Parun, and its words carry a sentence only when Glyphica is present. No school owns any of the three. Several have broken each other’s windows on the question.

Draft Corruption · Law-fragments left when the Continuum partially executes a sentence with a structural error before the error propagates. It is the Direction role’s own failure mode, and an error in any role can produce it: Wellspring processes started and never completed, which keep trying to finish themselves in whatever they contact. Unlike a Boundary failure, which spreads, or a Sealing failure, which never ends, the working itself stops; the fragment it left does not.

Internalized chain · Glyphica compressed below the threshold of conscious assembly at high Temperance. A practitioner with sloppy internalized chains still casts, and what comes out is something slightly different from what they intended, every time, and the error accumulates.

VIII · Alchemia, Drafts and the Work

Aether + Essence + Vessel + Glyphica = Reaction

Essence Draft · A working stabilized into a substance. The product of Alchemia, and a skill anyone can level. A Draft can supply a Catalyst, which is why Stages are for sale and why the great houses hoard the top of the shelf (see Catalyst and Bought Stage, Section XVI).

Core stock · Salt · The fractions of a render, named for the layer each feeds. Core stock is Essence and feeds the Core; a Spirit worker’s fight Draft is made of it, arrives unstamped, and leaves the stamping crash. A Salt is raw Aether in a fraction that packs straight into the Shell; it is the Body fighter’s fight Draft, goes down like swallowing a burn, and leaves a crash of cramps and fever. Shaved Layer, the third fraction, is the black-market Fate reagent.

Vessel · The container in which a Draft is bound. Its material sets what the Draft can survive and how long it keeps, by the material’s virtue as much as its Grade: stone remembers, salt fixes, gold will not corrupt, and quicksilver will not keep a shape, so each holds some workings well and fails others in its own way.

Crucible · The working vessel of active transformation, as distinct from the vessel a finished Draft is stored in.

The Great Work · The seven-stage curriculum of Alchemia. A practitioner’s progress through it is a matter of record in the alchemists’ halls of the Accord and a matter of pride outside them. A lived breakthrough passes the Great Work in its own Path’s terms, through the four colors the benches name nigredo, albedo, citrinitas and rubedo: the soul broken down, purified, woken and fixed.

Calcination · Dissolution · Separation · Conjunction · Fermentation · Distillation · Coagulation · Sublimation · The eight classical operations of the bench, and canon: each Wellspring names its own, and it stands as the Operation on a working’s Codex line (glyphs / Wellspring / kind / Titan / Operation / Form / Stage). Conjunction is the bench operation, where fusion behaves differently enough from the rest to need separate treatment; Conjunctia is a clerk’s word for one practitioner’s working on two lenses at once.

IX · Domains, fields and realms

Domain A defined space in which the practitioner has altered what local law is willing to do cheaply. Persists as long as the Attraction Layer keeps recognizing the claim, which is why a Domain fails all at once rather than fraying. A seed may flicker once from a person’s own Trait at the Threshold into Stage VII, with no Release behind it; a Second Release, or a Fate soul’s One, later lets a seed be called and held, for minutes, and it collapses if attention breaks. From Stage VIII it is a true Domain, grown by living and called aloud. Anyone at Stage VIII can have one, their own concept made real; the Stage grants it and no number ever does. Fields clash by Stage first, a field two Stages up overwriting the lower, and otherwise Dominion holds the ground and a read can flip it. Full account in Domains.

Aspect Sovereignty A Domain built on a single governing principle held by one soul.

Nexus A point where multiple aligned fields hold together durably. The founding condition of a Major Shared Realm.

Major Shared Realm A Domain that has outlived the practitioners who founded it and become a place.

Silence Realm A region in which lawful transformation cannot be authorized at all. Animatria constructs fail completely inside one, since their continuity depends on a connection back to source that Harmonic Silence severs.

Harmonic Silence The severing condition itself, as distinct from the region it produces.

Hollowing The progressive loss of a region’s capacity to sustain lawful working. Closing one is Concordia’s most difficult application and is measured in years. Farmers call it thinning; it is not a Veil-thin place, where the planes press close, nor Aether-thin ground, which was never rich, nor the hollowing of a Core, which is what a Spirit worker’s lost share is paid as (Section XVI, under Essence).

Realm Rot The trade’s name for a region’s ruin under Mechanica: unsealed boundaries summed across the ground. Unlike a Hollowing, by Statute XXVI nothing closes it.

Aetheric Density The concentration of Aether in a given volume, the scholar’s term for what a fighter calls rich ground and thin ground. It sets the local output ceiling, and because a practitioner pulls Aether from the environment around them, rich ground fills them faster and thin ground starves them. It fills every reserve on every road, since every Path draws Aether and nobody works a grain of it they have not first pulled inside. This is why ground is fought over and not only people.

Echo Depth How thick a place is with memory: graveyards, temples, old homes where a great deal has been felt and kept. It cheapens Spirit workings, and a Spirit worker reads it before she reads anything else in a room.

Thread Weight How much has been decided on a piece of ground: battlefields, coronation halls, crossroads, wherever great things were settled. It cheapens Fate pulls. With Aetheric Density and Echo Depth it makes the three measures of ground, one to a plane, and where a contest comes down to the ground, the measure read is the one for the fighter’s own Path. See The Three Grounds.

Core (common speech) · A site where a Wellspring’s Aether pools. Most Cores lie in dungeons, and the rest are said to lie in vast open territories. A worked Core is a dungeon someone cleared and now holds: it recharges, it has keepers, and its keepers can close it. Scholars write Wellspring site to keep it apart from the Essence Core, the inner layer of the Soul Crystal, and from a monster’s core (Section XVII), and the rest of the world says core and trusts you to work out which from what the speaker is pointing at.

Salted ground · bled ground · Two ways of making ground thin on purpose. Salted ground has been worked, usually by hired hedge-workers (the paid hands of the petty arts), so that Aether will not pool on it. Bled ground has been pulled empty by a long fight or a greedy crew and fills back slowly.

X · Summoning and the Called

Vocatia Invocation by recognition. Never domination. The entity answers because it recognizes the caller as lawfully compatible. It exists before and after the call; a presence built from the caster’s own Core is Animatria.

Compact A standing agreement between practitioner and called entity, with terms both sides can state.

Tether The Attraction-side connection sustaining a called presence. Cutting it is the standard counter and the standard atrocity, depending on what was on the other end.

Effigy A constructed vessel shaped to receive or imitate a presence larger than the practitioner could lawfully call.

Usurpation A Vocatia working addressed to an unwilling higher being. Reads as Usurpation at the level the Continuum evaluates intention, regardless of correct surface syntax.

Eidolon An Animatria construct (Animatria: shaping one’s own Core into a presence that acts apart). A detached fragment of the caster’s own Core given temporary independent operation, and not a called thing at all, though summoners count it among their summons and the distinction is invisible from ten paces.

XI · Failure, damage and pathology

The most-used vocabulary on these shelves, and the least standardized. Everything here describes a way a working, a Crystal or a region goes wrong.

Crystal Fracture Event A Soul Crystal splitting under trauma or overload. Produces erratic casting and identity leak.

Dead Set A Threshold melt that cools with no adequate Trait seed to nucleate around. The Stage does not advance, Clarity and Compression fall, and a Trait is commonly lost outright. In the halls, a seat that never took. Slag in every other register. Three names, one failure, and Dead Set is the name on the record; said of a district rather than a man, slag means Residue.

Rough seat A melt cooled around Trait seeds that contradicted one another. The lattice keeps the argument permanently. Rough-seated practitioners are unpredictable rather than weak. The halls cap a rough seat at Tier Four on certification, regardless of demonstrated capability, and that says nothing about the fighting token their Stage gives them, which is another table; see The Standing and the Title.

Shear Break A Trait collapsing, inverting, or turning on its bearer after the bearer has repeatedly denied the truth that formed it. What a broken vow comes to when the denial goes on; the breach itself lands at once by the vow’s own terms.

The Rigidity Effect The soul’s reduced capacity to contradict a law it carries, scaling with how absolute that law is. Power and inflexibility are the same purchase.

Residual Strain Pressure that builds when Level runs past the top of a soul’s rank group with no Catalyst met for the next Stage (over 100 below Expert, 200 below Master, 300 below Grandmaster, 400 below Archmaster). Shows in the room, degrades gain, raises Overchannel and Backlash risk, and does not stop building. It can break an unwoken seal, which is why late wakings happen.

Overgrown A Crystal holding a Stage its Level cannot yet hold, bought or met early. Power spills, lamps lean, Mastery reads low and no Release comes until Level catches up.

Sealing failure The most lethal Glyphica error and the least often recorded as one. Authorization, Boundary or Direction failing produce the failures in the role table, each of which shows itself while the operator is still in the room. Sealing failing produces a working that does not stop, because the law goes on obeying itself after the operator has left the room.

Shard What a fractured Core sheds. Retains fragments of the identity it came from.

Echo Fracture The Harmonia failure state, produced by mismatched Resonant Tone.

Aetheric Saturation Power released by workings flooding an area faster than it disperses, past ten thousand EU per cubic meter. Produces Fracture Events in everyone present, including whoever caused it. Not Ambient Saturation, the surveyors’ name for ordinary ground.

Essence Starvation Operation below ten percent reserve, kept under its old name on every road. Reduced stat expression and Trait flickering for everyone, and then the road’s own bill: a Body fighter’s Shell begins drawing the Charge out of the flesh itself, and the skin splits in fine lines; a Spirit worker’s Core frays, names first and then memory; a Fate fighter’s own name sits wrong in other mouths.

Flagging How a practitioner tires, which tells the road. The shared tells stand for everyone: a short breath and a dimmed aura at half, tremor and thinning color at a quarter, a flickering aura and stuttering Traits at a tenth. On top of them a Body fighter runs hot and cracks, a Spirit worker runs hollow and cold, and a Fate fighter runs unlucky. The table is on The Three Paths.

Soul wound What a Spirit strike leaves once it is past the Shell: a soul knocked out of true, a dread laid on a mind, a cut Core. It is a status line on the window like any wound, and it shows as lost color, a dulled sense or a memory gone thin. A shallow one closes in days. A deep one takes seasons under a mender, and left untreated past a season, or struck twice in the same place, it sets for life. A healed one leaves a pale place in the Core that reads on Gnosis Analysis. Soul-menders are a priced trade. See Soul Wounds and the Menders.

Clouding · Rupture The two Aether Shell failure states, gradual and sudden. Written Shell Rupture wherever it could be mistaken for a Rupture Event.

Rupture Event A Conjunction’s failure: two incompatible laws contesting one substrate without adequate mediation. No relation to Shell Rupture, nor to a rupture in the Veil, which is a channel struck between planes.

Obsession Force Attraction Force curdled into fixation. Sustains corrupted technique through refusal rather than harmony, at escalating soul-cost. With Attraction Force it is one of the two forces the Plane of Fate controls (Section XIX), so a fixation is a thing a Fate Art can get its hands on.

Mechanica debt What the Continuum records against a working performed without lawful Authorization. Statute XXVI, Mechanica Irreversibility, exists because this is the one damage the Continuum does not forget.

The Withering Epoch-scale. Generates dread and problems. Resolves nothing.

XII · Pressure and the read

Field vocabulary. What practitioners use on each other before anyone has cast anything. Aura coat, Pressure and sensing are the three most famous arts in the world: children play at them, every culture has its own name for each, and every culture is sure the others do them badly.

Pressure · What a higher Stage does to the room simply by being in it. One Stage up bends attention. Two produces sweat and nausea. Three or more drops people involuntarily. A gap of a whole Crystal look changes local weather. On the unwoken it lands by rank: dread from an Initiate or Apprentice, weight in the room from a Journeyman, knees bending from an Expert. Felt in the room first, in the lamp flames and the animals, then in the body, and only then does the window report it. It is seen in the practitioner’s own aura, in their own color, burning brighter as they push. It comes from a fighting Stage and nothing else, so a Grandmaster merchant with no Stage to speak of has no Pressure at all, whatever his title does to the prices. The trade says the weight everywhere, and each people has its own word besides: the Moto atsu, the press, which they use of a lord’s dignity as well, so that his weight and his worth are one argument; the Mahuo nureum, a pressing down; the lineage halls wēiyā, majesty that presses; a Marrowkin calls it the load and a Kharven the low sky.

Sensing · The trained art of feeling another’s reserve, Stage and intent before they move. Moto kehai, the sign of someone there. Mahuo nunchi, the same word they use for reading a room, and they will tell you that is the point. The halls’ shénshí, the spirit’s knowing. A Marrowkin sounds you, as he sounds a rock face for a bad seam. In the street, having the nose. Measurewrights hold it is one art and have been unable to get any two peoples to agree on how to teach it.

Aura coat · The visible skin of a practitioner’s own color over reinforcement. The lens tempered on longest sets the base color, the other Wellsprings add texture and sound, and a class or big Trait adds a shape. The road sets how it sits: a Body coat is raw Aether hugging the skin like heat-haze, a Spirit coat hangs a half-step off the body, guards the soul more than the hide and sets the shadows falling wrong, and a Fate fighter’s barely shows, though dust lies still and coins land face up near them. The Stage sets how clear it is: smoke low down, clearing as the Stage rises, a held shape from the middle Stages, and light bending around it at the top. Color says what and clarity says how high; The Color of Essence has the whole picture. Moto matoi, Mahuo gabot, the halls’ hùtǐ, and the rest; see Reinforcement.

Suppression · The skill of holding Pressure in. Tiring to maintain. Dropping it deliberately is a threat and is understood as one.

The read · Identifying what the other practitioner is doing, in the two or three exchanges before it kills you. Taught techniques are documented and counterable by anyone who studied. Self-derived techniques must be read live.

Reading a person · A stronger Gnosis Perception (the faculty that takes in another soul) reads a weaker Resilience Ward (the faculty that keeps it out), a full step against it on the hidden scale. Everything else is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide or fake what you are estimating. Your own window never shows you anyone else’s numbers.

Stage gap · The distance between two practitioners on the sixteen-Stage ladder. The single most consequential fact in any encounter and the first thing anyone competent establishes. One Stage can be crossed with a read; two only with a vow or a breakthrough; three or more shows in the room first and ends the fight the way a rope ends, unless something breaks.

XIII · Institutional vocabulary

The Guild Accord A coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations: adventurers’ guilds, merchant guilds, craft guilds, alchemists, healers, couriers and more. Halls sign on for shared rank, enforceable contracts and passage, and crowns tolerate it and fear it. Its heartland is the Sealed Charter of the Concord. Its register of techniques is out of date and everyone knows it.

The register The Accord’s official catalog of known techniques and disciplines. Absence from the register is not evidence that a thing does not exist.

Forbidden A discipline the Accord has named and prohibited. Chronokinesis beyond Stage X, Chronoturgy entirely. A Forbidden thing has a procedure for handling you.

Unnamed A practice with no register entry at all. Worse than Forbidden, because there is no procedure, and the absence of one has to be improvised by whoever finds you.

Standing A person’s recognized position in their guild, spoken as one of the nine titles (Section XVIII), which is not the same as their Stage and is frequently mistaken for it by people who should know better.

Statute A numbered article of the Codex of Absolute Magical Law. Cited by number in argument and by name only when someone is being theatrical.

XV · What goes on the page

The section to read before writing anything. It sorts everything on this page into its channels.

Mechanism belongs on the page. How a working works, what idea it works and by what operation, why it costs what it costs, and what fault falls out of that idea’s flaw, spectacle first and the full account right after, so that nobody is ever left guessing. A fully explained technique that costs nothing is worse than an unexplained one, and an unexplained one that costs everything is worse than both.

Numbers belong in a mouth or a window. Figures are diagnostic: a Stage and its rank, a Level, a Mastery rung, a yield (η), a count of EU, a Sub-Stat’s value, an item’s Grade letter. They appear when a character speaks them, when an instrument reports them, when a document records them, when somebody is privately counting, or when the bearer’s own window reports them, in a text block of its own right after the blow it describes. They do not appear in narration on its own authority, and the prose stays body first. The system’s words without their figures, Aether, Density, Saturation, Coherence, may stand in narration beside the effect they name.

Effects always appear as behavior and body. A high Ardency Penetration is first the point going through plate that should have turned it, and only after that, if at all, a name. A Stage gap goes unstated, and shows as the man who cannot lift his arm.

ChannelWhat it carries
PageThe idea, its operation and the mechanism by the lens, and the system words that name it (a science word only as a comparison or in a Measurewright’s mouth), what the body and the blade did, the tell, the fault, injury by named structure, what the character cannot do next, what a room smells like afterward
A windowThe bearer’s own numbers, ranks, percentages, bars and Level: damage, reserve, skill gains in ranks, Mastery, a class’s name, a blessing’s line, a Release or Domain with what it changed. EU, AU/s and yield stay out of it and live in the notes
A mouth, an instrument, a document, a private countStage and rank, Level, Mastery, an item’s or a beast’s Grade, Class, yield, EU, AU/s, Sub-Stat names, Path, Form, Wellspring assignments
Workbook onlyThe Design Chain as a form, the Codex assignment line, stat ledgers, the compiler’s own arithmetic

The explaining voices. The point-of-view character’s own read, capped at their competence and frequently wrong, with a confident wrong answer set right on the page before the scene moves on. The knowledgeable second. The opponent, using contempt as instruction. The wielder, bragging. The document at a scene’s threshold. Any of them may explain in any engagement, and narration explains the rest in the POV’s own words, a trained POV in the terms of the art and an untrained one in plainer, more wondering ones.

XVI · The sheet and the climb

What a person can read off their own soul, and what moves it. Tench and Crane would have fought over every line of this, and their pupils have.

Window · The soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in their own tongue, unseen by anyone else. The Soul Crystal is the soul and the Continuum measures it, so the report was always there to be read, and scholars argue about who built it. A window sits on the page as a text block, often and in detail, fights included, for damage, skill gains and reserve. It shows numbers, ranks, percentages, bars and Level, and it follows the blow it reports:

[Reserve: 41%  ████░░░░░░]
[Left forearm: cracked. Grip down.]
[Skill risen: Knife Work, Journeyman]

The sheet · What a normal look at your own window shows: the Eight Primaries as totals, with no letter on them, since letters belong to things. A Sub-Stat appears when it is used or when it moves, and the full set remains there to study. Race and lineage show on the sheet and shape base stats and what comes easily. A blessing has a line of its own (Section XIX), and a real bond with a crew earns one too, with the crew’s bars beside your own.

Stat points · Life spends most of them by what a person does, and a few are theirs to place at each Stage.

Level · The life, counted, 1 to 500. Moves by anything hard and real: danger overcome above all, so killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills and crews argue over who got the kill, but a flood night held or fifty years at a trade move it too. It holds a Stage open (see Catalyst) and places Mastery under its Stage’s ceiling.

Reserve · The store of power a practitioner carries, counted in EU and shown on the window as a percentage and a bar. One capacity and one number, filled by the lead Path: a Body-led soul holds most of it as Charge and a Spirit-led soul as Essence, and someone who walks both carries the split on the card (Charge 70% · Essence 30%). Set by Level, capped by Stage, refilled by the pull.

Charge · Raw Aether, unrefined, packed in the Aether Shell: what the Body Path holds and spends. The Wellspring’s lens shapes it there and the Shell drives it out, so a Body fighter’s fire is Aether burning in the Wellspring’s shape, with none of the fighter in it. Its lost share is heat and its price is the body: Shell strain, torn fiber, recoil. Spending Charge is spending the world, and the body pays for the handling. Charge once held is never refined into Essence, and a sealed Shell holds none.

Essence (as spent) · Aether refined in the Crystal and stamped with identity, spiritual energy in the plainer books: what the Spirit Path holds in the Core and spends through it. Its lost share is paid as a hollowing in the Core and never as heat, and spending it is spending yourself. Thrown, it lands on souls only, since the Shell is the only door into matter. Essence never goes back to raw.

Thread · Fate’s substance, what a Fate user grips and spends. Fate is the plane, and Thread is the strand of it pulled down through the Attraction Layer for one working. It is never held in reserve. It is paid for from the soul’s own share, and every pull leaves a knot (Section XIX).

Aether Manipulation · Essence Manipulation · Thread Manipulation · The common and fighter names for the three styles: working raw Aether through the Shell, spending Essence through the Core, pulling Thread through the Layer. They are literally true. A Measurewright still grumbles, chiefly at students who hear Aether Manipulation and conclude they can skip the body’s bill. See The Three Paths.

Release · A practitioner’s Trait let off its safety limit on purpose, in a shape their Crystal has learned to hold, on a clock. The lead Path sets how many: Body three (First from Stage IV, Second from VII, Third from IX), Spirit two (from IV and VII), Fate one, the One, from VII, counting as its Second; a Walked road flavors them and brings none of its own. Each has its own Catalyst and is found and mastered by living it. Far up, each road reaches a Realm by its own door, late, through the Body’s Third, the Spirit’s Second grown whole or Fate’s One; the doors are still in study. See Releases and Domains by Path. See Release Forms.

Path · The layer of the Crystal a soul has learned to lean on, and so the direction advancement actually took: Body (the Aether Shell, in the Physical World), Spirit (the Essence Core, on the Soul Plane) or Fate (the Attraction Layer, on the Plane of Fate). Assessed afterward and recorded whether the person agrees or not. It gates how far a stat may rise, and each spends its own thing: Body the Charge, Spirit Essence, Fate Thread. A Path stands as Mastered (its gates are no obstacle at the person’s Stage), Walked (three parts in ten of their practice or more) or Studied (knowledge and small uses, open to anyone, opening no gates). No Path is better: each has plain limits, and each can beat each. See The Three Paths.

Continuum · The lawful fabric of reality, which measures a soul and permits as much as its architecture will bear. It grants no power.

Skill · A thing a person can do, risen by doing it and shown on the sheet as one of the nine ranks, the same words as a Stage’s rank: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. A trade has no cap; a combat skill never ranks above its owner’s own Stage rank. Older books wrote skills in letters, and later on six craft ranks, Novice to Legend. Drafts, runes, tags and chants are skills, like knife work or haggling. Anyone can level a skill hands alone can do, pouring Drafts included; runes, tags and chants want a woken Crystal.

Technique · A named thing done with a skill. A technique shows no level. It jumps when its skill gains a rank and a fight pushes it past its old limit, gaining one of power, reach, speed or a lower cost, and takes a bigger name, and the old name stays in use.

Trait (its depth) · Traits stay what they have always been, the Four Reflections and their laws. A Trait carries no level: it works at its owner’s Mastery of the lead road, and at its base below Glory, and Release and Domain are the same Trait escalating. Older books and windows gave Traits levels of their own.

Class · A name, never a number, that the soul offers for how the person has lived, first at Glory with Mastery I; Stages I to V read no class. It evolves each time the lead Mastery rises, into forms some share with many and some hold alone, each evolution giving one move that fits it and a lean on where life spends the stat points. The Path is the road a soul’s growth leans along, and the class is what the road has made of the walker. The one wild concept stays the person’s own, and no class hands it out.

Mastery · How well a soul works its own road’s substance, one rung per Path, I to V and Ω: Body IV · Spirit III · Fate II. Never tracked: the Stage sets the ceiling (unread below Glory; I at Glory, II at Refraction, III at Transcendence and Invocation, IV at Realization and Dissonance, V from Emanation to Zenith, Ω at Revelation and Apex) and Level places the soul under it (I from Level 115, II from 150, III from 215, IV from 260, V from 340), so a bought Stage raises the ceiling and not the rung. From Glory it sets yield on one table for all three roads, a read rung overriding the field average for strangers and a card’s own figure governing its holder. Matter reads Body, a soul or a meaning reads Spirit, a condition reads Fate. A Walked second Path reads a rung behind until mastered, a Studied one two behind and capped at II. Fate climbs with Level like the others, but without a grip (a bloodline’s Work or a blessing) it stops at II, and its price is the knot; an Epoch bloodline’s instrument reads rung I on its road from Stage III, the one thing read below Glory. Any rung may be held Inverted, a feared and wrong way of working it.

The folk names · Each road keeps its own words for the Mastery rungs, from the old ladders, still said in the halls and never the rung’s official name. Body: I Muridic, II Harmonic, III Resonant, IV Luminous, V Radiant. Spirit: I Listener, II Answerer, III Confessor, IV Shepherd, V Ferryman. Fate: I Touched, II Drawn, III Spinner, IV Knotwright, V Weaver. The older books counted three separate ladders, the Aether, Essence and Thread Classes, from Ø to Ω, with a lateral sixth rung on each (Voidic, Sin-Eater, Severer) that is now the Inverted mark, and wrote the line Aether IV · Essence II · Thread Ø.

The Crystal’s look · How the stone looks at its Stage, a jeweler’s reading and no track of its own: Seed (the unwoken), Clouded (Initiate to Adept), Faceted (the Experts, V–VII), Fire (the Masters, VIII–X), Flawless (the Grandmasters, XI–XII), Starred (the Archmasters, XIII–XIV), Absolute (the Paragons, XV–XVI). Older books called it the Soul Crystal Tier and gave the Refraction stone a look of its own, Clear; older still, Dormant, Awakened, Harmonic, Resonant, Radiant, Sovereign and the Crystallized Soul.

Rank · How everyone says a Stage: I Initiate, II Apprentice, III Journeyman, IV Adept, V–VII Expert, VIII–X Master, XI–XII Grandmaster, XIII–XIV Archmaster, XV–XVI Paragon. The same nine words are the Accord’s titles, and the two get mixed up. Older books grouped the Stages in five Bands (Mortal Foundation, Awakened, Sovereign, Mythic, Absolute) and kept a Tier of Standing beside them.

Tempering · The Soul Crystal is literally the soul. Tempering melts it down and rebuilds each layer, refines the Essence and makes it stronger, and the body gains Traits.

Wellspring · An alchemical and a spiritual transformation and concept. It can be seen as a source of power to pull from, and it is more the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether: a lens on magic already potent. It shapes what each Path works (raw Aether in the Shell for Body, Essence in the Core for Spirit, the claim on the Layer for Fate), the idea staying the same and the target changing, starting small and reaching double only with years of mastery on its home Path, and half again at most away from it. Its kind (Wild, Spirit, Fate or Medium) names that home and is never a lock, and a Spirit Wellspring gives no fire on any Path: it shows in what a person can bear, know, keep or let go. It is got at a Core in a dungeon, from a school, from a Draft or blessing, or by birth. Every number on the Wellspring pages stands, and the physics with them as the Measurewrights’ account, often right about how much and never about why.

Catalyst · The one deed a Stage asks, met in the lead Path’s terms, before the Crystal will pass a Threshold: it comes by a crisis, a long seclusion, a tribulation. Drafts and blessings can supply one. A Stage opens only when its Catalyst is met and Level can hold it: II at Level 5, III at 15, IV at 30, V at 101, VI at 115, VII at 130, VIII at 201, IX at 215, X at 230, XI at 301, XII at 320, XIII at 401, XIV at 420, XV at 500. Stage II’s Catalyst is the first lens, a Wellspring’s raw Aether touched under pressure.

Bought Stage · A Stage reached with a purse. Stages can simply be bought, the wealthy are stronger because they are wealthy, and the great houses hoard the top Drafts, while the poor rise by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings. A bought Stage opens the door and no more: it stands Overgrown until Level catches up, it has no Release until one is found and mastered by living its own Catalyst, and sects teach what money cannot buy, Releases, Domains, techniques, skills and the rites for their Wellspring. A purse can buy the Catalyst for any Stage; at Zenith a Hollow Throne must still recognize the buyer, so money buys the attempt and not the seat.

Timer bar · What a called Release or Domain puts in the window, which fills with its name and what changed while the bar drains. The boost cap of two steps on the hidden scale stands, and only outside help goes past it.

XVII · Monsters, cores and dungeons

Monster A beast that gains Levels by killing, each other included, and changes form at its own thresholds. Monsters share the Stage and Level scale with people, read by Gnosis, with question marks for a weak reader, and lean on one Path. They are known and bountied by Grade, E up to EX, like commissions (the old T1 to T9 Threat Tiers, T4 being a Grade B beast), and a famous one earns a name, an epithet and a bounty. They move and live across the whole Inner World, and beasts more widely still; the Scourge of Hell is the one area known for extremely dangerous ones. Monster parts make gear.

Monster core In a way the monster’s Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster, and read from the beast’s own Stage and Level on the coil, with its Grade as the shorthand for danger and price. It is the lawful Crystal Draw of The Standing Index, Provenance Grade IV, “draw” being the bench’s old word for a lot taken off its source.

Rendering What an alchemist does to a core to make Essence Drafts used for tempering. They carry Creature-Sourced potency, the strongest grade of source, and with it Identity Contamination: what the core was cut from bleeds into the drinker’s convictions.

Going raw Swallowing a core unrendered. It gives everything at once, and most die or come out wrong. Lucky commoners holding monster cores are the great threat to every house in the realm, which is a sentence the houses have read.

Cut Crystal A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner, which makes the strongest Draft of all. It is the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable, and it is why Grade IV has never balanced.

Dungeon What grows down in floors around a Core, with something guarding the heart. Kill the guardian and clear the place, or leave it and it grows. A dungeon is alive and learning: it moves galleries and breeds what kills crews best, and scholars argue whether it thinks. See Dungeons.

Well The delver’s word for the ground itself, thick enough to rewrite what lives in it. A delver is anyone who goes down for pay, and a crew is the delvers who went down with you and want a share of the kill.

XVIII · Guilds, titles and items

The nine titles · Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. The same nine in every guild, earned by commissions and grading trust in that trade: a merchant may deal up to it, a craftsman make up to it, an adventurer take commissions up to the Grade of bounty the Accord allows it. Fighting guilds also ask the title’s own rank in Stage (a Journeyman’s token wants Stage III); trade titles ask none, so a Grandmaster merchant may not be able to throw a punch. The same nine words are a fighter’s rank.

Token · The proof of a title. A forged one is a great crime, and an old and popular one.

Grade (of an item, a missile, a beast or a bounty) · Power or danger, on the Measurewrights’ letters, which no person wears. A smith’s title caps the Grade she may make.

Rarity Scarcity: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary, Mythic, Unique. A merchant’s title caps the Rarity he may sell.

The stamps Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous and Proscribed: the old ratings, surviving as the Accord’s stamps.

Bonded gear Gear that grows with its owner. All other gear stays what it is.

Appraisal The skill of reading an item, done by eye or with an assay instrument. A good forger beats a weak appraiser, and everyone at the market has a story about it.

Master Work A piece that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named. Each area has its own.

XIX · Fate and blessings

Plane of Fate · The control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force and the workings of reality itself: manipulating probabilities to work outside what reality already knows, to control the fate or predestiny of something. All spirits and all physical things have fates. It can attach to oaths and is more tied to blessings. “Plane” is a courtesy: Physical and Spiritual Reality are the two lower realities, and Fate is everything that flows, every possibility, idea and concept, anything that can happen in them, above them as the law and construction of the world. Most if not all of the Archons are Sovereign across all three planes, this one included; some are deemed greater than others, and Uurgath and Urion are unparalleled in Fate.

Fate Path · The hardest road there is. Its walkers fight day to day with Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds. A real pull of a thread moves what a blow, a wall or a march depends on, so the result arrives without having been forced: rare, ruinously expensive, and often a failure. Landed or failed, it leaves a knot. It moves only what already has a way of happening, has a visible charge and a tell, and is contested by Dominion’s Fate Sub-Stat against the target’s Resilience Persistence and Continuity. Moving a whole field is a life’s masterwork at high Stage or a bloodline’s art. Being the hardest road makes it no better, and it loses a straight exchange.

Fate Arts · Rare, mostly by bloodline, and considered incredibly overpowered. Hataraki and a few other bloodlines hold them, some high-Stage masters reach them late along the Fate Path, and most people touch Fate only from the outside, through a blessing or a Master Work Draft. Hataraki can take back or remove a blessing, which makes its people unusually polite company.

Blessing · A grant of power out of a person’s natural reach, even in Level. It need not go through Fate, but most do. Higher spirits, Archons, Epochs and people of higher Temperance give them, because they are spiritually stronger, and a great giver spends almost nothing. Lesser spirits and high-Stage people give most of the world’s blessings; Epochs and Archons act rarely, and it shakes the world. A blessing shows on the sheet as its own line and can lie, because the giver wrote the line.

Knot · What every pull on a thread leaves, landed or failed: a debt the world collects later, as an ordinary accident, on the puller or on what is hers. Its size follows the pull’s distance from likely. It falls due within days for a lean on the odds and within the arc of events for a removed prerequisite. A Work of Hataraki lowers the chance of failure and never the price or the knot. A kept vow leaves none, because a vow spends the soul’s own share and pulls nothing. See The Tell and the Knot.

The tell (of a pull) · The charge of a Fate working: a stillness, a held breath, the room settling, and to a trained sense the odds leaning like a thread drawn taut. The warning runs a breath for a lean, several heartbeats for a removed prerequisite and longer for a field, and reaching the puller inside it is the counter.

Knot-cutter A worked piece, usually worn, that can take one knot’s debt onto itself, and breaks doing it. Cordwrights make them, every one reading at least Fate IV, they go by private treaty, and half of those sold are fakes. Whole, it multiplies Dominion and Resilience Continuity like the Fate road’s other gear, the oath-rings and luck-tokens.

Curse Often a blessing with terms you did not read, or a giver who does not care what you want. Nobody can tell at first, and some are both.

Vow Small Fate, a person binding their own probability, open to anyone. Spoken, it shows in the window with the bargain and its terms. Breaking it lands at once by its own terms and needs no witness, and repeated denial of the truth behind it makes a Shear Break, Section XI. On Munahi, the twenty-ninth of February, the law sleeps and Fate does not: no instrument binds, and a vow spoken on it still holds. See Vows and Oaths.

Fate Essence Draft A Master Work of the alchemists: extremely hard to make, and very coveted.
