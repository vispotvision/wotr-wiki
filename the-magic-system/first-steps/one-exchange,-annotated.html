One Exchange, Annotated

One Exchange, Annotated

Nov 28, 202626 min read

On the last evening of the Brazen Lantern’s ranking bouts, half of Lantern Street paid a copper a head to sit on the school’s wall and howl, and the bookie Sukey Threadgold, who had sworn before witnesses to eat her slate if the Hare ever beat the Bellows, was seen next morning at the fish stalls gnawing politely at one corner of it. I make every first-year clerk in the Archive copy this bout out by hand. It takes about as long to read as it took to happen, if you are slow, and everything a newcomer needs is in it: what a fight looks like from the wall, what a fighter’s own soul was telling her while it happened, and, marked with numbers, what was deciding it underneath.

A single exchange from a school ranking bout, with the system shown working underneath it, start to finish. For the measures used here, see The Measures at a Glance.

The fighters. Garrick Vane, the Bellows, because you can hear him coming, is at Stage VI, Glory (a Stage is one of the sixteen steps of the soul’s remaking; Glory is the sixth). He is tempered on the Wellspring Exuroth (the Trial Flame, the trial by fire that proves what a thing is made of; a Wellspring is one of the sixty governing ideas that transform how a soul uses Aether, a lens on magic already potent). At Glory his soul offered him a class, and he took Hammerhand, which surprised no one. Nell Ashby, the Hare, a name meant as an insult, is one Stage below him at V, Splintering, and is harmonized to Judicium (the Wellspring of Truth, which tells a thing apart from what it claims to be, sharpens the eye for flaws, and in a Body fighter’s fist drives into the flaw it found as a spike of arc-blue current). At Stage V she has no class yet. Both of them are on the Body Path (the road that leans on the Shell, the Crystal’s outer skin, works raw Aether packed there unrefined, which fighters call the Charge, and has to close before it can hit), and both of those Wellsprings are Wild ones, at home in the physical world, so here the lens and the road agree. One Stage is a gap a fighter can cross with a good read of the enemy, and only with one. The Lantern’s house style, spottable at fifty paces, is to put everything in the right hand and make sure the neighbors hear about it. Garrick is the finest thing the school has ever produced by that method. Four minutes into the bout, he has been chasing her round the yard.

The windows. A window is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in the owner’s own tongue, and nobody else can see it. This copy is told from Nell’s side, so her windows stand in the text, each one after the thing it reports, exactly as she dictated them to the Archive for the price of a beer. Garrick dictated his the following week, for two, and you will find them in the annotations. Neither of them could see the other’s at the time, and that is most of the bout.

The exchange

The yard of the Brazen Lantern school had been swept for the ranking bouts, and four minutes of Garrick Vane had unswept it. Black scorch-rings pocked the flagstones wherever his misses had landed, each one edged in straw and plum like a blade pulled from the fire, and the whole yard reeked of quench oil and hot slate. Up on the wall two hundred people, most of them disciples in Lantern yellow, were singing the one about the hare and the hot pan, which has nine verses and no merit. The sky behind them had gone the deep apricot of the end of a good day. Along the colonnade the lamp-warden’s forty bronze lamps were burning, and every flame in the row canted toward him, a line of small bows, and Nell Ashby kept her eyes on his feet because they kept wanting to climb to his face. [1]

[Pressure: Garrick Vane, one Stage over. Attention bends.]

She had spent the bout a hand’s breadth outside the maai (the distance at which a blow can land) of that right arm, slipping, giving ground, letting him barrel after her round the well-head while the wall jeered her for a hare. Let them jeer. Every rush cost him dear and cost her almost nothing, and now she could hear the bill in his breath, short and whistling, and staying short between rushes. The cherry-red that had roared off his shoulders like a bonfire in the first minute had sunk and thinned, as if someone had cupped a hand round the lamp, and in its place a heat-haze stood thick on his skin, so that the colonnade swam behind his shoulders. [2]

[Reserve: 87% ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░]

“You going to run all night, Ashby?” He spat, and the spittle struck the flag and jumped to steam. “Elder’s bored.”

“Elder’s watching you sweat.”

On the steps, Elder Hossack took the pipe from his teeth and gave one short snort through his nose.

Then Garrick gathered himself, and the gathering showed, as it had shown every day of his life. He sucked air through his nose until his chest stood out, and the Aether of the yard came to him with the breath, pulled up out of the flagstones and the warm evening and packed into his Shell as it came, raw, with no refining in it and nothing of Garrick but the will. Exuroth’s lens took hold of it there, and the Shell was so rough that she felt its furnace burning on her face from three paces. The red drained off his left flank in one sullen pulse and poured down his right arm, and the arm began to change the way a bar changes in the forge, cherry at the shoulder, straw at the elbow, plum across the wrist, and along the knuckles a thin hard edge of temper blue. His left side stood bare from armpit to belt, sallow in the lamplight, goose-fleshed, shining with sweat. [3]

Behind her ears a fine, quick ticking started up, and hot copper came into her nose. Her eyes ached from the back. Two thin lines of color, one crimson and one green, lay down across his naked ribs, as straight as a surveyor’s cords and visible to nobody in that yard but her. He’s put it all in the right. He’ll throw it straight, because he’s already loaded the back hip and a hook wants it square. When it misses, his weight rolls over the front foot, and for one breath those ribs are naked.

[Perception B, in use. Flaw found: left flank.]

“Furnace Hammer!” [4]

The wall roared it back at him, stamping, Ham-mer! Ham-mer!, and the song fell to pieces.

He drove off his rear foot. The heel came up and the knee turned in behind it, his hips slung round with every pound of him stacked on top, the shoulder came through last, and the right fist left his chin and came dead straight for her breastbone along a yard of empty air, with a bellows-roar behind it.

A good third of what he had put into that arm never reached the fist. It came off him as noise and glare and a wall of heat that dried her eyes in their sockets, and the air in front of his knuckles rippled like a road at high summer. Forty lamp flames bent flat. Under the roar ran the art’s own voice, the sound a bar makes going into the trough, a long hiss ending on one small bright note, and her back teeth took it up, hot and then cold, an ache that climbed and then set hard. [5]

She had gone on the name, a beat before the fist left. She pushed off her lead foot to his left, low, knees bent, her head dropping inside the line of the arm, so quick that the wall saw two of her for an instant, a blue wraith still standing where she had been. The knuckles went by her left ear close enough to crisp the hair, and the heat took her sleeve off at the shoulder in a curl of black thread. The fist went on past her with nothing in the way and met the stone coping of the well-head.

[Evaded. Reflex B, in use.]
[Hit: left ear and shoulder scorched. Vitality 98%]

The coping was limestone, cold, wet from a hundred years of buckets. The fist put a forge’s heat into a patch of it the size of a shield-boss in the time it took to touch, and the skin of the stone swelled against the cold stone underneath it, and the two of them tore apart. A slab as long as her forearm jumped off the rim. Shards spat across the yard and went hissing into the black water, a column of steam stood up out of the well as high as the colonnade roof, and round the broken edge the temper colors crawled from plum to blue as it cooled. “THAT’S YOUR BREASTBONE, Hare!” somebody screamed from the wall, with real affection. It was exactly that. Exuroth is the trial by fire, and what that fist touched it put to the ordeal, plunging it into a forge’s heat to find out what it was made of; cold wet limestone is made of nothing that lives through an ordeal, and it broke the way the world has always let a hot thing break a cold one, the outside changing size before the inside has heard the news. Steel quenched too fast cracks the same way. Measurewrights call it thermal shock and smiths call it a ruined morning, and Garrick had just shown two hundred people what it would have done to the front of her chest.

His arm stood out to its full length, smoking. His hips had gone over with the weight of the blow and his whole body hung out over his front foot, left elbow trailing, and he could not haul them back in the beat she needed. She stood inside the arm at his left side, on dry flagstone, near enough to smell the burned hair on his wrist. [6]

She took one breath down to the belt, and the evening came in thin and dry and tasting of copper, Aether off the stones, packed into her Shell unrefined and bent to Judicium’s shape there in the time it takes to blink. She gathered her own coat into her right fist. It ran down her arm arc-blue and spitting, with filaments of brighter blue crawling through the darker field where Splintering had cracked her, and threads of color fringed it, crimson, green, violet. The nearest lamps went blue at the tips. The hairs on Garrick’s bare ribs stood up and leaned toward her knuckles, and the skin of her own arm prickled as if it had been rolled in nettles and then grew warm, the share of the spend that burned in her own Shell, a hand held near a stove beside his bonfire. The rest of her went to bare skin. [7]

“Arc Nail!”

“She’s got a nail!” a child shrieked from the wall, delighted, and that was the name of the thing explained for anyone who had not seen one: a single spike of current, driven into one place.

She jammed her rear heel against the lip of a flagstone, screwed her hip into it, and let the shoulder come after the hip, and the fist went a foot and a half in a flat straight line, all brawn and hip, along the crimson thread. Garrick saw it. His eyes came round and he clamped his left elbow down, and the elbow arrived a hand too high and one beat too late, and her first two knuckles went in under it, into his lower left ribs, with her whole weight behind them and the flagstone behind that.

Two ribs staved in under her hand, a wet green-stick crunch that jarred up to her shoulder, and in the same instant the Nail went home, on a dry quick ticking that climbed to one high bright note. A crack like a carter’s whip went out across the yard and all forty flames jumped together. The current found his sweat-wet skin wide open and went through it, and every sinew of his flank fired at once and stayed fired. A muscle knows one thing: a nerve tells it to clench with a small pulse of current. Flood it with a great one and every fiber clenches together, and none of them can let go until the flood stops. Physicians call it tetany. The sheet of muscle over his ribs locked like a board. The breathing muscle under them locked with it, and the breath stopped in his chest with his mouth still open to curse her. A red fern the size of her palm stood up on his skin around the knuckle marks, where the current had branched and burned. Under the broken ribs, where she could not see but the green thread could, something soft had begun to bleed. His right foot skidded a yard and left a smear on the stone, and that was the last of the force going out of him into the ground. He folded over her fist, one knee juddering down onto the flagstone, his mouth agape on no air at all. [8]

[ARC NAIL: landed, left flank, uncoated.]

Nell stepped back two paces to his blind side, clear of the right hand. Her knuckles had split white to the bone, and blood ran down between her fingers and pattered on the flag. Her arm hung numb to the elbow, fizzing with a thin crawl of sound she could hear inside the bone, and somewhere behind her breastbone a single note rang, a tapped glass, a warning she knew precisely. [9]

[Hit: right knuckles split to the bone, arm numb to the elbow. Vitality 91%]
[Reserve: 80% ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░]
[Crystal: strain warning. Two layers held.]

For one breath the whole wall had nothing to say. Then, quite small and clear, the voice of Sukey Threadgold: “Well, shit.”

Garrick dragged in one wet, rattling breath, then a second, and lifted a palm off the stone.

Elder Hossack knocked his pipe out on the step in a little shower of sparks. “Ashby,” he said, “you’ll want a healer. So will he. Ninth place to the hare.”

[Level: 128 to 129]
[Skill: Reading a Fighter, Adept to Expert]
[Skill: Brawling, Apprentice to Journeyman]

The wall came down off itself like a landslide.

The annotations

The Archive’s clerks write a bout down in a fixed order, and you have just read it. Every blow is told whole: the set of the body, what the other fighter read, what met what, what it did to the flesh, the sound and the blood, the reaction, and where both bodies stand after (a miss skips the flesh and the blood and tells what it struck instead). Every big working runs in its own order, woven through the blow that carries it: the charge anyone can see, the pull by the fighter’s own road, the aura, what the room does, what the senses get, the mechanism, the effect and the cost. A window follows the blow it reports and never stands in for it, so the body comes first and the numbers after. We are a dull profession, and this is why you can still read a fight sixty years after everyone in it is dead.

[1] Pressure, before anyone moves. Pressure (the weight a stronger soul lays on a room) is felt in the room first, then in bodies. One Stage up bends attention: the flames lean toward Garrick, and Nell’s eyes keep drifting to his face. She pays nothing to bear it; it only costs her focus, and she spends that on watching his feet. Her window reports it after the room has already done it, and it names what it took. The line carries no number: a window shows numbers of its owner’s own, ranks, percentages, bars and Level, and the EU, AU/s and yield (η) behind them stay with the coil and the Archive’s notes.

[2] The cost, read off his body. Garrick’s registered card (the record the assay house keeps on him; see The Core Vocabulary) prices his spread coat (power from the reserve driven through the whole body, lifting it within the step of the Measurewrights’ scale its figures stand in) at ten minutes from full: begun on a full reserve (for a Body fighter, the Charge: raw Aether packed in the Shell), it holds ten minutes before the reserve falls to a tenth. His single strikes cost a twentieth of his reserve each.

That puts him on the first rung of the tell ladder (the body’s fixed signs of a falling reserve): at half, the breath shortens and stays short, and the aura dims. His road adds its own sign on top. A Body fighter flags hot, the heat-haze thickening on the skin at half, where a Spirit worker’s colors would have gone flat and a Fate worker’s small luck would have begun to curdle, so anyone on the wall could have named Garrick’s Path from the shimmer alone. Nell, who spent most of the bout dodging at her passive base (her stats as they sit in the body, costing nothing), is still above 85%. Making him chase was the plan: outlasting is a counter, and she is running a cheaper clock.

Nell cannot see that line. A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward and prints what it found, and her Perception, 190, only draws level with his Ward, the two standing in the same step of the Measurewrights’ hidden scale, so her soul tells her nothing about his numbers. She gets the same answer the old way, off his breath and his color, and says it aloud as an estimate. People can hide or fake that sort of thing, and Garrick has never in his life thought to.

[3] Reinforcement focus, and the gap it left.

  • Intention: win the bout in one blow.
  • Focus: he gathers the whole coat into his right forearm and fist. A focused coat lifts one place a full step on the Measurewrights’ hidden scale (the thresholds under every stat’s number, the same ones the Grade letters mark on gear and beasts, though no person is ever given the letter) and drops everywhere else to the passive base.
  • The gap: his left flank is bare. His Fortitude (how well bone and joint take a blow) stands there at its sheet value, 240, with no coat over it.
  • Why she can see it: his Feint (the skill of hiding a movement, its noise and its aura spill) is 130. Her Perception (sensing threat and reading what is coming) is 190, a full step above it on the scale. One full step up wins a contest outright, so he cannot hide where his coat went from her. Judicium sharpens the same eye. The ticking, the copper and the colored lines are that Wellspring’s own signature, which a trained reader learns to know like a neighbor’s cough, and the price of a read this size is the ache behind her eyes.
  • Her thought: she reasons from what she has seen of him today, not from his registered card. Fighters think a fight through at length, and the best of them are right.

[4] The call. Shouting a technique’s name as it fires buys a surge within its step for the beat the shout takes. It is a layer on the stack (see [7]) and it is also the loudest tell in the fight. FURNACE HAMMER’s own card gives its counter: the call costs a beat, the coat visibly drains from the off side as it gathers, and the hips commit so far that they cannot turn back for a beat. Everyone in Lantern Street knows all three, and he calls it anyway, because a Lantern fighter who wins quietly has wasted the evening. His sheet shows no level for FURNACE HAMMER, since techniques carry none. It began as Hot Right Hand and took the bigger name when it jumped, and he still uses the old one on small days.

[5] Effect, and the furnace.

  • Effect: a blow’s character comes from Wellspring and Path. Exuroth’s idea is the trial by fire, its operation calcination, and on the Body Path the ordeal lives in the striking hand: the lens shapes the Charge into heat as the fist travels, so on contact it delivers a blow and an ordeal of fire together, and whatever it touches is put to the trial of what it is made of. The stone that fails the trial breaks by the world’s old habit, a hot skin tearing off a cold core, the way a quenched blade cracks if it is cooled too fast; the Measurewrights call that thermal shock, and they are right about how hard it hits.
  • Signature: the temper colors climbing his arm, the long hiss with the small bright note at its end, the quench oil and the aching teeth belong to Exuroth, and any Exuroth working carries that smell and that ache. The hiss belongs to the technique alone, and it comes back every time he fires it, so that by the third time the crowd on the wall is hissing along with him.
  • Stack: focused coat (one step) plus the call (a surge inside the step). His Flux (the force a working carries), 352, strikes a full step higher in that fist, past the 400 that Glory caps him at. Two layers at once raise the risk of a Crystal Fracture Event (a crack in the Soul Crystal, the soul itself, which holds power and sends it out), and he is inside the hard cap of two steps over the sustainable figure on his card, so the blow may pass his Stage’s ceiling lawfully for as long as it lasts.
  • The furnace: Garrick is Body I, Muridic as the smiths say (the first rung of Mastery on his road: his Shell, the Crystal’s outer skin, passes power only roughly), which sets his yield (the share of each spend that arrives as effect, as a distillation has its yield) at 0.62. Thirty-eight of every hundred he spends burn in his Shell and leave him as heat, roar and light. Heat is where the lost share goes on the Body road, because the Charge leaves by the Shell, and the Shell is a furnace that never goes cold; only what burns there is heat: a Spirit worker’s lost share is paid as a hollowing in the Core and warms nobody, and a Fate worker’s stays behind as a knot. Waste is loud and hot; mastery is quiet. Her drying eyes and the scorched yard are his yield, made visible. His Class, Hammerhand, sits beside that reading and is no part of it: it gave him the skill of throwing everything down one arm and has leaned his growth that way ever since, with his Shell on the first rung of Mastery, as high as Glory lets anyone stand.

  • The contest she refuses: his focused fist against her Fortitude is two full steps: one-sided. Taking it on a guard would end her. Nothing she owns wins that exchange, so she makes sure it never happens.

[6] The counter. Her Reflex (what moves the body before the mind has finished) is 230. His Celerity (how fast the body travels) is 210. The same step: inside one step, skill, ground and the read decide. She leaves the speed contest tied and moves on the call, a beat before the fist leaves, because the beat was the counter his card offered. The afterimage is what the eye keeps when a body moves faster than it can follow. The spalled coping shows what the blow would have done to her sternum.

[7] Her stack, and her own gap.

  • Focus: she gathers her coat into the right fist. Her Flux, 250, is lifted one full step, past 275, in that fist alone. Everything else on her drops to the passive base. For the length of one punch, she is as open as he was.
  • Stack: focused coat plus the ARC NAIL call. Two layers, inside her cap (two steps over her sustainable figure), and the same fracture risk his carried. Her color shows her Stage: a Splintering aura is fracture-streaked, hairlines of the Wellspring’s true color flaring through a darker field.
  • Timing: the call costs her a beat too. She spends it in the beat his hips cannot use.

[8] What decided it.

  • The stat in use: her blow tests her Flux against his Fortitude. Her Flux, focused, stands a full step above his Fortitude, uncoated, at 240. One full step up wins the exchange outright, and it did. Penetration (the stat for piercing wards and coats) never came into it, because she struck where there was no coat to pierce.
  • Why the lower Stage won: the Stage gap set the Pressure and his higher ceilings, and he never got to use them. The read chose which stats met. She refused the contest she would lose by two steps (his fist against her chest), drew the one she would tie (speed), and forced the one she would win by one (her fist against his bare ribs).
  • The three layers of the working (Aether, Wellspring, Charge): the yard sits on ordinary ground at ordinary density, a long way from any Core, in dry dusk air, and his skin was sweat-wet, all of which suited the Nail. Neither fighter got anything from the ground, though a Measurewright in the Lantern Street taverns argued until closing that the well lifted the yard a hair and the bout should be read again. The lens comes first. Judicium’s idea is truth told apart, its operation separation: her read had already parted Garrick’s guard from what it claimed to be and found the flaw, and the lens shaped her Charge into a spike that goes where the truth parts and drives in there as current. What the current did next is the body’s old habit, and physicians have its name: a current through muscle makes it contract and hold (tetany), so his flank and the breathing muscles under it locked. Dry skin fights a current and wet skin lets it in, which is why she wanted him sweating and why four minutes of chasing was worth more to her than the punch. The glyph she wrote the working in sets where its law stops, and it held the current to his flank, which is why Garrick Vane is alive to keep his left side covered. What she spent was Charge, raw Aether she had drawn off the yard and packed in her own Shell without refining it, as every Body fighter does: the will and the aim were hers and the stuff was the world’s. Judicium’s lens shaped it in the Shell, and it went out through that same Shell, unread (no Mastery is read below Glory, Stage VI), at her Stage’s own figure, about 0.64, so rather more than a third of the spend burned in her Shell and came off her forearm as warmth and spitting light. Had she been a Spirit worker the same Nail would have been Essence spent through the Core, the loss would have been a hollowing with no heat in it, and it would have landed on Garrick’s soul and left his ribs alone. None of that reached her window, which said only that the blow landed.
  • The wound: two lower left ribs broken, with the spleen beneath them. A healer is needed tonight, not tomorrow. Garrick’s window got there first:
[Hit: two left ribs broken, flank locked, no breath. Vitality 58%]
[Reserve: 39% ▓▓▓░░░░░░░]

His report names the ribs and leaves out the spleen, because a window reports what the soul has felt so far and a soul is no physician. Windows have killed men who believed them.

[9] Her price.

  • Reserve: ARC NAIL and a second of focused coat cost her about 7%. She ends near 80%, with no rung of the tell ladder reached. He ends near 39%.
  • The body: split knuckles, and a numb, fizzing arm where her own current ran back up it. That is the Body road’s bill, start to finish: she spent the world’s Aether and her body paid for the handling, in heat, torn skin and recoil. Nothing of herself went out with the blow, so she will remember this evening whole, which a Spirit worker, whose reserve is her own substance, can never quite take for granted.
  • The Crystal: the ringing glass is the warning a stacked Crystal gives. A Splintering Crystal is already a cracked one, and two layers on it is the gamble veterans take. If it had cracked, she would carry the fracture until rest, alchemy or her next Stage healed it. The window’s strain line is the same warning in words, and she had already heard the glass.
  • The gains: Level moves by danger overcome. A fighter a Stage above her, with real blood on the flagstones, moved hers by one, where a year of drill in that same yard had moved it by none. Killing him would have moved it further, and on a crew somebody would now be arguing over who got the kill. Skills rise with use and show on the nine ranks, so the two things she did tonight went up a rank each, Brawling as far as a Journeyman’s and her reading of a fighter to Expert, as high as an Expert’s fighting skills may go. Her Trait, Look Twice, carries no level of its own: below Glory, where no Mastery is read, it works at its base and will deepen as her life does, and ARC NAIL, being a technique, shows nothing at all until the day it earns a bigger name.
  • Afterwards: she won across a Stage with a read, at a visible price, and the whole school watched how. By morning the minstrels had it that she broke four ribs and he wept. Next month Garrick will keep his left side covered, and she will need something new.
