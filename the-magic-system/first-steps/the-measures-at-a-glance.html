The Measures at a Glance

The Measures at a Glance

Nov 17, 202624 min read

Ottilie Crake had run the slate at the Harvest Brackets in Thornby Cross for nineteen years when she chalked up the Expert Bout at evens, because both fighters carried the same silver token and she had a headache. One of them was a Stage V cooper’s son in a borrowed green sash. The other had been lit for six years and had come into the double sight the previous winter (Stage VII, in the Accord’s numbering), a fact he had mentioned to nobody. When they walked out, every banner on the field leaned over toward the older man, the crowd’s noise dropped to a mutter, and the cooper’s son, sweating like a cheese, looked at his opponent, looked at his boots, and was sick on them. Nobody had thrown a punch. By sundown a Measurewright’s apprentice with a very good memory for tables owned a brewery that had been Ottilie’s, and Ottilie, a fair woman, had the whole of this page chalked on the side of her new, smaller premises, where you may read it for the price of a beer.

Learn the numbers. Some of them decide the fight, some of them decide the rent, and the two most expensive mistakes in the trade are confusing one for the other and trusting a token. Ottilie’s chalk has been added to since by people with worse handwriting, because the world now asks you to read a dungeon crew’s sheet, a sword’s stamp and a monster’s bounty as well as a bout.

Every number a practitioner, a monster or a piece of gear is read by, on one page, and what your own soul shows you of them. For the whole system in brief, see How Magic Works.

A practitioner is measured on the Soul Crystal (the soul itself, which holds power and sends it out). Their strengths are counted on sixty-four Sub-Stats (single measured abilities, such as Flux for the force a working carries or Fortitude for how well bone takes a blow), grouped under eight Primaries (Vitality, Ardency, Gnosis, Dexterity, Harmonics, Resilience, Tempering, Dominion). Their power is spent from a reserve (the one store a soul carries, counted in EU, Essence Units, which the guild reckons at a megajoule each, whatever fills it). That reserve fills as they draw Aether (mana) in from the ground and air around them. A Body fighter packs it raw in the Shell and holds it as the Charge, and a Spirit worker refines it in the Crystal into Essence (spiritual energy), which is why a fighter’s numbers and a fighter’s ground get argued over in the same breath. The measures below are the ways the Archives, the Accord and the street describe all that.

Essence is the soul’s own substance, and Charge is the world’s. The Crystal has three layers and every soul has all three: the Aether Shell meets the Physical World, the Essence Core holds identity on the Soul Plane, and the Attraction Layer holds a soul’s own share of fate on the Plane of Fate. Aether refined in the Crystal takes the stamp of whoever refined it, the Core is the center of all identity, and the Crystal is the soul, so a Spirit worker’s reserve is the person, and spending it is spending yourself: what is lost on the way is paid as a hollowing in the Core. A Body fighter’s reserve is raw Aether that was never refined, so spending it is spending the world, the lost share burns off as heat in the Shell’s furnace, and the body pays for the handling. A Fate worker holds no Thread at all: each pull is paid for out of the soul’s own share and leaves a knot, a debt the world collects later. Fighters call the three Aether Manipulation, Essence Manipulation and Thread Manipulation. Ottilie’s chalk prices all of it by the guild’s megajoule regardless.

The owner sees most of it for free. A window (the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in the owner’s own tongue, unseen by anyone else) shows Grades, percentages, bars and levels. EU, AU/s and yield (η) never appear in one: those belong to the coil, the Measurewrights and the notes. A normal look gives the Eight Primaries with Grades, and a Sub-Stat appears when it is used or when it moves. Everyone else’s numbers take a stronger Gnosis Perception against a weaker Resilience Ward, or an instrument, and anything short of that is an estimate said aloud, which people can hide or fake.

Each stands as Mastered (its gates are no obstacle at the person’s Stage), Walked (three parts in ten of their practice or more) or Studied (knowledge and small uses, open to anyone, opening no gates). A card gives each road a Path Stage, the highest Stage whose gates it has opened: Body VII Mastered · Fate VI Walked 35%.The owner’s window, on the top line. Assessors, off the gated Sub-Stats. Yield (η)The share of each spend that arrives as intended effect, as a distillation has its yield; the Measurewrights’ older word is efficiency. The rest is lost on the road it left by: as heat through the Shell, the furnace burning, with sound, light and bleed, as a hollowing through the Core, as a knot on the Layer.From Glory the class ladder of the layer in use sets it: Aether Class through the Shell, Essence Class through the Core, Thread Class for a pull. The Tier of Standing table gives the figure for the unclassed at Stages I to V (about 0.30 at Initiate, 0.60 to 0.70 by Splintering) and a field average above them, rising past 1.0 at Archmaster, where the room itself adds to the working. A read rung overrides that average, and a card’s own figure governs its holder.Measurewrights and diagnostic talk. Everyone else says loud or quiet.How hard a fighter hits, how far a reserve goes and how loud they are. Delivered force is spend multiplied by η, and the rate spent at full output (AU/s) is Flux Density times η, so the yield counts twice on purpose: once for how much the layer in use can move and once for what survives the trip. At full output a higher yield empties the reserve faster; for the same delivered effect it lasts longer. A low yield runs dry first on any given blow and announces every working; a master’s work arrives almost silent.η 0.62: of every 100 EU spent, 62 strike home and 38 roar off him as heat.
SkillOne thing a person does and has got better at by doing it: swordwork, haggling, brewing Drafts, cutting runes, Appraisal.The Grade letters, F upward. Rises with use. Anyone can level a skill hands alone can do, pouring Drafts included; cutting runes, firing tags, chanting and speaking a working want a woken Crystal.The owner’s window. Teachers, by eye.How well the thing gets done, and what named techniques can be built on it. Techniques themselves show no level: they take a bigger name when they jump.Brawling B: a good right hand, and a window line the day it moves.
Trait levelHow far a Trait (a standing law in the soul) has been refined. The laws of Traits stay as filed; this is how much of the law the soul can use.A number on each Trait, with no stated ceiling. Rises with use and grows with overall Level and skill.The owner’s window. Rivals guess.How much the Trait does and how reliably, inside its own law.The Forge Holds, Level 34.
Threat TierHow dangerous a job or a monster is, on the Commission scale (a Commission being a sanctioned Accord job).T1 to T9, from a Minor Threat to a Realm Hazard, a Cataclysmic Entity and an Apocalyptic Beast. A monster also has a Level and Grades on the same scale as a person, read by Gnosis, with question marks for a weak reader. A famous one earns a name, an epithet and a bounty.The Accord’s bills, every tavern, the crews.Who may lawfully take the job, by title, and how much of your crew comes home. T is also the shorthand for price, and most beasts line up with it, but a monster’s stone and parts are read from its own Grade and Level on the coil.T6, under a Gold Sigil, three hundred silver marks on every door in the hundred.
Item GradeHow much power a piece of gear carries, on the fighters’ letters.The Grade letters. A smith’s title caps the Grade she may make. Bonded gear grows with its owner; other gear stays what it is.Smiths, the Accord’s stamps, an Appraisal skill, an assay instrument.What it does when it hits or is hit. An enhanced weapon performs no higher than the Grade of whoever put the power into it.A B-Grade hammer in an A-Grade hand.
Gear multiplierWhat a worked piece (runed, Draft-tempered or made of monster parts) does to its wearer: it multiplies one Sub-Stat’s value, and the Primary and its Grade are read again from there.Plain gear has none. Worked gear shows them on the window, with a Shell draw (light, steady or heavy) paid from reserve to keep it lit. A bonded piece’s multipliers grow with the bearer, and a relic reads as question marks. All gear together multiplies any one Sub-Stat by ×1.5 at most, a cap of its own beside the two-Grade boost cap. Above the wearer’s Stage a piece works at half its multiplier or fights them.The owner’s window. Smiths and appraisers, who price by it.How far a kit lifts the person inside it, and what it costs them to wear: heavy plate may read Fortitude ×1.3 and Celerity ×0.85. Item RarityHow scarce a piece of gear is, which is a separate question from how strong.Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary, Mythic, Unique. A merchant’s title caps the Rarity he may sell. The old words (Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous, Proscribed) survive as the Accord’s stamps. A Master Work (a piece that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, named) is always Unique or Mythic.Merchants, appraisers, and thieves. A good forger beats a weak appraiser.The price, who may sell it, and who will kill you for it.Very Rare: you will be followed home.

How they connect

Level and Stage climb on separate keys. Level comes from danger overcome and fills the reserve; Stage comes from a Catalyst and raises the ceilings, and a Catalyst can come out of a crisis or out of a purse. The Band is where the two are bolted together: past each hundred Levels, the counter stops until the Stage catches up. The Stage caps every Sub-Stat, the Sub-Stat’s number gives its Grade, and the Grade of whichever Sub-Stat a moment calls on decides that moment. Average a Primary’s Sub-Stats and you have the Grade the register prints. The Tier of Standing is a guild’s word for how far it trusts you, in a fighting guild its word for the Stage, and it carries no weight in a fight. Power leaves through a layer, so the class ladder of that layer sets the yield (before the ladders read at Glory, the Tier of Standing table gives it), and the yield sets how much of the reserve arrives as force and how much is lost on the way: burned off as heat and noise in the Shell’s furnace, paid as hollowing through the Core, left as a knot on the Layer. Skills and Trait levels sit beside all this and rise by use, and they decide what a practitioner can do with the numbers the ladder gave them. A bought Stage shows that gap plainly: high ceilings, a thin skill column, and no Release at all. Drawing Aether in fills the reserve, as fast as the ground allows. Spending runs it down, written as time from full (how long a working holds, begun on a full reserve, before the reserve falls to a tenth and the practitioner starves). The Crystal’s tier says what all of this has built; its state says what today has done to it. Gear runs on its own two ratings, Grade for what it can do and Rarity for who wants it, with a worked piece’s multipliers on top, and monsters run on the same Levels and Grades as the people hunting them, with a Threat Tier on top.

Example: one practitioner, read on every measure

Garrick Vane, a senior disciple of a city fighting school, the Brazen Lantern, known from Lantern Street to the river as the Bellows, pays a silver mark at an assay house and stands in the coil. The clerk writes his First Name, the instrument line: Exuroth, Stage VI Glory, Level 160, Grade A, Aether I Muridic, Expert, η .62.

No EU, no yield and no Flux until he uses it. Those Primary letters are means, and Stat Points are few: a man at Level 160 has spent about 4,200 of them across all sixty-four Sub-Stats, half on Ardency, so nearly everything he has never had reason to use sits low, and only the few entries his life has leaned on reach the letters in the table above. The coil knows what his window leaves out, his window knows what the coil leaves out, and a Measurewright will explain over a long dinner why neither is the true account.

The coil’s line is accurate and, by itself, useless. What Garrick is in a fight lives in the gap between his Flux and his Fortitude, and in the roar every working makes: an A-Grade fist on a B-Grade body, loud enough to be timed.
