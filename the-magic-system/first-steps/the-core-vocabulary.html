The Core Vocabulary

The Core Vocabulary

Nov 28, 202646 min read

Bartle the Sponge, a wharf-fighter of the Oxmarket Stairs, stood on a herring barrel for eleven nights one autumn and took all comers at a copper a swing. He banked every blow, held it in his Crystal with his whole body lit up like a feast-day and a hum coming off him you could feel in the planks, and then he bled it off into the river with a great wink at the crowd, and the river steamed. Eleven nights and four hundred swings. On the twelfth night the Dagnall brothers, two tall quiet men from the rope-walk, paid their coppers and hit him in turn, left and right, faster than he could bleed, while the whole wharf counted aloud and got to learn what a Soul Crystal sounds like past two and a quarter times its rating.

Every gull on the wharf went up at once. Bartle lived, a good deal quieter, and has not been able to hold a bank since. The Accord’s inquest ran three days, because no two people in the room would use the words “bank,” “overfill” and “rating” for the same thing, and the fishwives of Oxmarket, having heard that the man had been overfilled, entered evidence about the pies. A clerk of the Archive was sent away to write down what the words mean so that this could never happen again. Here is what she wrote. It has happened every year since.

This page covers Sections I to VII: the Planes, Aether and Essence, the Soul Crystal, Ability and Technique, Progression and Output, Traits, Refinement and the Seat, and the Sheet and the Climb.

A Glossary of the Continuum’s Core Vocabulary

This glossary covers the universal system vocabulary of the Continuum: the planes reality operates across, the Soul Crystal and what moves through it, how phenomena become named disciplines, and how a practitioner’s output is measured. It does not cover bloodline-exclusive terminology or the full stat and sub-stat framework, which live in their own codices.

I. The Three Planes

Every act of magic crosses all three simultaneously. Remove one, and the working fails. The first two are the lower realities, and the third is a plane by courtesy.

Stones fall, fire eats wood, steel cuts, because those habits were settled in the Physical Epochs. Magic changes the worker, and the world gives way as far as that soul is truly changed, overturning none of the old habits. II. Aether, Essence & Attraction

Aether is the ocean. Essence is your water. The ocean is infinite. Your water is yours.

Aether · Mana, to a foreigner. The world’s own substance, the universal medium through which everything exists: what remains of Omnara undivided, which is why it can carry any idea. Infinite and impersonal, and only a body can pull it. Whatever a practitioner works is drawn in from the environment around them first, and no art, crime or legend steers it in the world unpulled. After that the road decides: on the Body Path it is packed raw in the Shell as the Charge and driven out through the Shell unrefined, and on the Spirit Path it is refined in the Soul Crystal into Essence, given identity, and spent through the Core. The old books say it comes in sixty types, which are the Wellsprings (see Section III).

Aether, Essence and Thread Manipulation · The common and fighter names for the three styles of working, one to a Path, and each is literally true. Aether Manipulation is the Body Path’s: raw Aether worked unrefined through the Shell. Essence Manipulation is the Spirit Path’s: Essence spent through the Core. Thread Manipulation is the Fate Path’s: a thread pulled down through the Attraction Layer. Measurewrights grumble at all three, chiefly at students who hear the first and conclude they can skip the body’s bill. See The Three Paths.

Aetheric Density · The concentration of Aether present in a given volume, read in EU per cubic meter. Sets the ceiling on the sustained output and refill a practitioner can get in that location, with bursts spent from what they carried in, which is why battles are fought over ground as well as over people. Rich ground fills a practitioner faster and thin ground starves them; the street says fat ground and lean ground. The ceiling is literal: no working can be pushed into ground that will not carry it, and ground wins that argument. The Measurewrights reckon one EU at one megajoule, which by their arithmetic makes a density in EU/m³ an energy density and so a pressure, the same figure in megapascals; it is their conversion, and it prices the ceiling correctly without ever saying why ground refuses. A site yields at most a thousandth of its density per second, so the ceiling on sustained output is that rate across the volume a practitioner’s field reaches. The four conditions are laddered in numbers in The Sixty Wellsprings.

Aetheric Residue · Traces left behind by a working. A permanent, if often subtle, change to the location where the working occurred. Always readable by someone patient enough. Residue has a loud phase and a quiet one, and the loud phase has its own name: Ambient Resonance is Residue while it is still ringing. The loud phase fades within hours. The quiet residue thins after it on a schedule set by local density, toward a floor it never passes, and the floor is what the patient reader finds.

Aetheric Saturation · A danger state in which the power released by workings floods an area faster than it can disperse. Produces Crystal Fracture Events in everyone exposed, including the person who caused it. The threshold is a density and not a total: ten thousand EU per cubic meter left above the ground’s own density, which the Measurewrights call the stress at which a crystal lattice fails, and which is why a Nexus or a Core is dense past that figure and fractures nobody by standing there. It is reached by leaving that much in each cubic meter of a region, or by sustaining more than a hundred AU/s through each square meter of the region’s boundary. The threshold grows with the surface while output does not. Confinement saturates. Spending does not. A kilometer of open ground swallows what a hall would not.

Ambient Resonance · Residue while it is still ringing, and therefore one quantity with two names. It is read in the same EU per cubic meter as any density, because it is the excess a working left behind on its way out of the ground. It falls by a hundredfold in about seventy-five minutes and by a millionfold in about four hours. That is how long ground remembers a working, and how long it remembers a battle. Resonance leaves every ceiling where it was and raises a floor. Every reach that depends on being heard above the ground, which is addressing, authority and anchoring, falls as the ground fills, for the reason a voice is harder to hear in a crowded room. The Measurewrights’ rule of thumb puts it at the square root of the total local density, and it has never yet been wrong about how far. Twenty practitioners working hard over a two-hundred-meter front leave ground on which a Harmonist hears to about two fifths of his ordinary range until it quiets.

Attraction Force · The law of recognition governing the Plane of Fate. Allows names, vows, lineages, Domains, and outcomes to be lawfully acknowledged inside reality. Clean techniques are sustained by the Continuum’s recognition of the practitioner’s intent. With its inversion, Obsession Force, it is what the Plane of Fate controls, and a true Fate Art works both.

Charge · Raw Aether packed unrefined in the Aether Shell: what the Body Path holds and spends. The Wellspring’s lens shapes it there and the Shell drives it out, so a Body fighter’s fire is Aether burning in the Wellspring’s shape, with the fighter’s will behind it and none of the fighter in it. The share that fails to arrive burns in the Shell, which is the furnace that never goes cold, and comes off the skin as heat; the price is the body’s: Shell strain, torn fiber, recoil. Spending Charge is spending the world. Charge once held is never refined into Essence, a sealed Shell holds none, and a fight Draft that refills it is a Salt, which goes down like a burn and leaves cramps and fever. Below a tenth of the reserve the Shell starts drawing it out of the flesh, and the skin splits in fine lines.

Echo Depth · How thick a place lies with memory: graveyards, temples, old homes. Deep ground cheapens Spirit workings, as rich ground fills a reserve. Where ground breaks a tie between two fighters, it is read on the measure that suits each one’s Path. See The Three Grounds.

Essence · Spiritual energy, in plain words, and the soul’s own substance: Aether pulled into the soul, refined and circulated through the Soul Crystal, and stamped with the practitioner’s identity. It is what the Spirit Path holds and spends, through the Core. The Essence Core is the center of all identity and the Crystal is the soul, so a practitioner holding a full reserve of it is holding themselves and a practitioner spending it is spending themselves. The share that fails to arrive is paid in the Core as a hollowing, and never as heat. Essence never goes back to raw. Below a tenth of the reserve the Core frays, names first and then memory. A fight Draft of Core stock arrives unstamped, and the crash that follows is the stamping. A blow’s character comes from Wellspring and Path.

Essence Quality · The coherence of a practitioner’s Essence imprint.

Essence Signature · The unique trace a practitioner’s Essence leaves, readable through Gnosis-based perception. You cannot forge one and you cannot fully hide one, only muddy it.

Knot · The debt a pull leaves. Every pull on a thread leaves one, landed or failed, and the world collects later, in kind and as an ordinary accident: a strap snaps on the puller next week, a friend’s sound plan fails. Its size follows how far the pull stood from likely. It falls due within days for a lean on the odds, and before the business it was part of is finished for a prerequisite removed. A Work lowers the chance of failure and never the knot. A vow kept leaves none, because a vow spends the soul’s own share and pulls nothing. A knot-cutter, a worked piece of the Fate road, can take one knot’s debt onto itself and break. See The Tell and the Knot.

Thread · Fate’s substance: what a Fate worker grips and spends, and what Fate Mastery measures the grip on. Fate is the plane, and Thread is what is taken hold of in it. It is never held. A thread is pulled down through the Attraction Layer for the one working and paid for from the soul’s own share, and the share that fails to arrive becomes the knot.

Thread Weight · How much has been decided on a piece of ground: battlefields, coronation halls, crossroads. Heavy ground cheapens Fate pulls, as Echo Depth cheapens Spirit workings, while Aetheric Density fills every reserve alike, since every Path draws Aether.

Obsession Force · The corrupted inversion of Attraction Force. When a practitioner cannot release judgment, lineage, memory, or revelation, recognition curdles into fixation, sustaining corrupted techniques through refusal rather than harmony, at escalating soul-cost.

III. The Soul Crystal

The convergence point where all three planes meet inside a single being.

Soul Crystal · The soul, literally. Composed of three layers whose combined state determines everything a practitioner can perceive, become, or do. Tempering melts it down and rebuilds each layer, refines the Essence it holds and makes it stronger, and the body gains Traits. The Continuum measures it, and it reports to its owner in windows (Section VII).

LayerFunctionFailure state
Essence CoreThe Soul-Plane layer. The center of all identity: holds identity, memory, and Traits.Fracture into Shards
Aether ShellThe Material-Plane layer, meeting the Physical World. Converts inner truth into outward force, holds a Body fighter’s Charge, and is the only door into matter for anyone.Clouding or Rupture
Attraction LayerThe Fate-Plane layer, a soul’s own share of fate. Holds bonds, oaths, Domains, and Wellspring harmonizations.Obsession Force

Every soul has all three layers, the unwoken included, and a Path (Section VII) is the one its Crystal has learned to lean on: the Body Path on the Shell, the Spirit Path on the Core, the Fate Path on the Attraction Layer.

Harmonization · Tuning the Crystal to a Wellspring, said once because the trade says it four ways. The lock happens at the Shell, where Aether enters. The tie is held on the Attraction Layer. The lens does its shaping in the lead layer, on whatever that layer works: raw Aether in the Shell for the Body Path, Essence in the Core for the Spirit Path, the claim on the Layer for the Fate Path. A sealed Shell therefore cannot harmonize at all. A soul holds one home lens to Adept and may harmonize one more safely at each rank from Expert up, so a Master holds three; a Clouded soul that reaches early risks tearing.

The Aether Index · A sum scholars of a certain age still score a Shell by: Lattice Conductivity (how easily power moves through it), Resonant Purity (how closely its output matches the Core’s intention) and Continuum Retention (how long its coherence endures after strain). It is a fine way to pass a winter, since nothing on any sheet has ever read it.

The Crystal’s look · How the stone looks at its Stage, a jeweler’s reading and no track of its own: Seed (the unwoken), Clouded (Initiate to Adept, Stages I–IV), Faceted (the Experts, V–VII), Fire (the Masters, VIII–X), Flawless (the Grandmasters, XI–XII), Starred (the Archmasters, XIII–XIV), Absolute (the Paragons, XV–XVI). Older books called it the Soul Crystal Tier and gave a Refraction stone a look of its own, Clear, which the jewelers have dropped; older still they used Dormant, Awakened, Harmonic, Resonant, Radiant, Sovereign and the Crystallized Soul.

Aura · The soul’s color as other people see it. The Wellspring a soul has tempered on longest sets the hue, so color says what; the Stage sets the clarity, so clarity says how high. It is clouded and smoky low down, clears as the Stage rises, keeps a shape from the middle Stages and bends light at the top. The Path sets where it sits: a Body fighter’s hugs the skin like heat-haze and clings to what they touch, a Spirit worker’s hangs a half-step off the body with the shadows falling wrong around it, and a Fate walker’s barely shows, though dust lies still and coins land face up near them. See The Color of Essence.

Crystal States · The three conditions a Soul Crystal can occupy. Fractured (split by trauma or overload, producing erratic casting and identity leak), Refined (balanced and coherent, the baseline healthy state), and Overgrown (power exceeding architecture, producing involuntary discharge). A release’s Overgrown clears with a full night’s sleep; only the permanent set an overfill leaves (below) stays. Crystallized is not a State; self-as-law belongs to the Starred tier alone.

Soul wound · What a Spirit strike leaves once it is past the Shell: a soul knocked out of true, dread laid on a mind, or the Core itself cut. Armor and Durability do not cover it. It is a status line on the window like any wound and stays until treated, and its tells are lost color, a dulled sense and a memory gone thin. A shallow one closes in days and a deep one in seasons. One left untreated past a season, or struck twice in the same place, sets for life. Soul-menders close them as a priced trade, and a healed one leaves a pale place in the Core that reads on Analysis. See Soul Wounds and the Menders.

Banked Essence · A working that takes an incoming load and holds it puts the load in the absorber’s own Crystal and leaves the room out of it. The bank is bounded by the rated Flux Density rather than by the reserve, because a store held at a pressure fails at a pressure. The surplus above rating leaks at the Crystal’s own bleed fraction, one minus its yield (η, the share of a spend that reaches the world) of itself per second. A bank therefore lives about three seconds at a yield of 0.70 and about twenty at 0.95. The clock is the real limit. A bank buys tempo and stores nothing. The leak leaves as heat, sound and structural bleed, so a man holding one is lit up for exactly as long as he holds it.

Overfill · Carrying a bank above the rated Flux Density. To one and a half times rating the Crystal holds and leaks and takes no lasting harm, and that margin is the whole of an absorber’s working room. From one and a half to two and a quarter times the lattice takes a permanent set and the rating itself falls: the Crystal has gone Overgrown. At or past two and a quarter times it bursts. That is a Crystal Fracture Event, in the absorber, from a working that was aimed at him and that he chose to take. Feeding an absorber past his margin is the counter to absorption, and it is cheaper than out-hitting him.

Wellspring · An alchemical and a spiritual transformation and concept. It can be seen as a source of power to pull from, and it is more the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether: a lens on magic already potent. It shapes whatever a Path works (raw Aether in the Shell, Essence in the Core, the claim on the Layer), and off its home plane it changes target and never idea: on the Body it acts on matter, on the Spirit on what a thing means and holds, on Fate on what a thing could have been. Its effect starts small and reaching double only with years of mastery on its home Path, and half again at most away from it. Its kind (Wild, Spirit, Fate or Medium) names its home plane and is never a lock, and a Spirit Wellspring gives no fire on any Path: it shows in what a person can bear, know, keep or let go. A soul gets one at a Core in a dungeon, from a school, from a Draft or blessing, or by birth. There are sixty, fixed, and a Soul Crystal harmonizes with them and invents none. The Titans are the Wellsprings themselves, and each of the Sixty is one way a Titan’s law shows up, carried by Aether. Each one is a transformation a person works on themselves, and the change shows in the world: Aether comes in sixty types that affect the Soul Crystal differently, and each carries its own idea, Core Law, Titan, operation at the bench, hue, ground it favors and hates, contrary Wellsprings and Temperance-range compatibility. The Measurewrights explain the lens as physics, and every figure in the tables is their account, often right about how much and never about why. See The Sixty Wellsprings. Harmonization with the correct Wellspring is one of the four conditions, alongside how its kind sits with the Path, Temperance Stage, and training, that an Ability needs to develop.

Core, in common speech · A site in the world where a Wellspring’s Aether pools. Most lie in dungeons, and some are said to lie in vast territories. A worked Core is a dungeon someone cleared and now holds: it recharges and has keepers, who can close it. Scholars keep the word for the Essence Core and say Wellspring site; the street says Core, and context has to do the rest. A monster’s core is a third thing (Section VII), and people have died of that confusion too.

IV. Ability & Technique

Kind The sort a Wellspring falls under, naming its home plane: Wild (thirty-three, the Physical World), Spirit (fifteen, the Soul Plane), Fate (six, the Plane of Fate) or Medium (six, at home on all three). A qualifier and never a lock. Never invented, only worked within.

Affinity The particular face of a Wellspring’s idea that a practitioner works. Fire within Pyreveil, weight within Petralon.

Form What the hands do, one of the lines every art states beside its Path, Lens and Gates: Strike, Shape, Read, Write, Rite, Make, Sing, Brew, Call, Bind, Claim, Pull. Each has a home Path, which is a qualifier too.

Seam Art A combat art whose Form crosses Paths, Brew for Body and Spirit or Sing for Spirit and Fate, worked at the seams of the Veil. Rare and unstable, hard to counter because it sits on no one plane, and feared for it. Ordinary Draft-making and chanting stay skills and trades like any other; only an art worked at the seam takes the name.

Method The scholars’ older word for a road and a trade taken together, encoded directly into the suffix of every named discipline: fire plus -mancy. The Guild’s clerks still name by it. See the Grammar of a Working.

Ability The internalized governing system a Soul Crystal has developed. Pyrokinesis, for example. The infrastructure. It does nothing visible by itself; it is the platform Techniques are built on.

Technique A specific, repeatable application within an Ability’s framework. Quench, for example. What a practitioner actually does, with defined inputs, outputs, and limitations: a named thing done with a skill. It shows no level on the sheet and takes a bigger name when it jumps.

Taught Technique Learned from an instructor or codex in standardized form. Reliable, but carries the teacher’s design assumptions rather than the student’s own.

Derived Technique Found through the practitioner’s own trial within an Ability. Personal but not identity-defining; most combat techniques develop this way over time.

Signature Technique · A Technique that could only exist because of one specific practitioner’s history, Traits, and Crystal adaptation. By default it hits no harder than a standard Technique; it is only more precise, with zero translation loss between soul and execution.

Kit · What a fighter is known for: one wild concept you can explain in a breath, and the growing list of named moves under it. Ordinary fighters name a move with one big phrase; a signature art takes a title with its moves under it, Art: Move.

The Call · Shouting a Technique’s name as it fires. It costs a beat and buys a surge, and it tells the whole room what is coming.

V. Progression & Output

You call Temperance a buff. It is a confession of how much you’ve survived.

Level · The life, counted: 1 to 500, and past 500 unnumbered at the very top. Moves by anything hard and real a person does, danger overcome above all and beating something stronger most, but also a flood night held or fifty years at a trade. Sets the size of the reserve and the Stat Points to spend, holds a Stage open (see Catalyst), and places a soul’s Mastery under its Stage’s ceiling.

Stage · One of the sixteen structural remakings of the Crystal, Murmuring through Apex, and the thing people mean when they size someone up. Said as a rank: I Initiate, II Apprentice, III Journeyman, IV Adept, V–VII Expert, VIII–X Master, XI–XII Grandmaster, XIII–XIV Archmaster, XV–XVI Paragon. Opens when its Catalyst is met and Level can hold it. Sets the ceilings, the Pressure and what can open. It can be bought, and a bought one stands Overgrown until Level catches up. See How Power Grows.

Threshold · The moment a Stage transition actually happens: the melt, the rebuild and the seat it leaves. It takes a Catalyst and the Level to hold the Stage. A lived one passes the Great Work in its Path’s own terms, nigredo, albedo, citrinitas and rubedo: the soul broken down, purified, woken and fixed.

Release · A practitioner’s Trait let off its safety limit on purpose, in a shape their Crystal has learned to hold, on a clock. The lead Path sets how many: Body three (First from Stage IV, Second from VII, Third from IX), Spirit two (from IV and VII), Fate one, the One, from VII, which counts as its Second. Each has its own Catalyst and is found and mastered by living it; none comes to an Overgrown soul. See also Releases and Domains by Path. See Release Forms.

Reserve · The one store a soul carries, counted in EU and shown on the window as a percentage and a bar. Set by Level and capped by Stage, and it refills as Aether is drawn in. What fills it follows the lead Path: a Body-led soul holds most of it as Charge and a Spirit-led soul as Essence, and a card for someone on both roads gives the split as a share (Charge 70% · Essence 30%). Thread is never part of it, though a pull is paid for out of it.

Temperance · The refinement of the Soul Crystal under pressure, expressed across sixteen Stages, Murmuring through Apex. Temperance is subtraction and never accumulation: each Stage is a structural reorganization triggered by a Threshold Event, which needs a Catalyst, and banked experience alone has never opened one. A crisis, a seclusion, a tribulation, a Draft or a blessing can supply it, so a Stage can simply be bought.

Rank · How everyone says a Stage, in the same nine words the Guild Accord uses for its titles: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. A fighter’s rank is their Stage; a tradesman’s is their Accord title, and the two get mixed up. Older books grouped the Stages into five Bands (Mortal Foundation, Awakened, Sovereign, Mythic, Absolute) and counted a Tier of Standing beside them; both are a scholar’s terms now, the Bands answering to the Initiates through Adepts, the Experts, the Masters, the Grandmasters and the Archmasters.

Grade · The letter scale of output, F through EX+ (with Hollow below the letters). It is worn by things: an item’s power, a missile’s threat, a beast’s or a bounty’s danger, a Draft. No person carries a letter. Under a person’s numbers the same thresholds still run as the Measurewrights’ hidden scale, which is how a stat a full step ahead wins its exchange, and a Measurewright may still say a man hits like a Grade A beast. Older books gave people and skills letters too.

EU · Essence Units · The raw reserve. Total volume of usable power the soul holds at any given moment, Charge in the Shell and Essence in the Core counted together as one figure, and still called Essence Volume in the older books. Set by Level and capped by Stage, and the band and benchmark reserve for each of the sixteen Stages are set out in Fracture of Worlds Part Nineteen. Below ten percent EU a practitioner suffers Starvation (Essence Starvation, in the older books): reduced stat expression and Trait flickering on every road, and then the road’s own bill. A Body fighter’s Shell starts drawing on the flesh itself, and a Spirit worker’s Core frays, names first and then memory.

Flagging · How a falling reserve shows. The shared tells stand for everyone: at half the breath shortens and the aura dims, at a quarter a tremor and a thinning color, at a tenth the aura flickers and the Trait stutters. Each Path adds its own on top. The Body runs hot: heat-haze on the skin, then steam and cramps, then a cracking Shell. The Spirit runs hollow: the colors nearby go flat, then a cold and a slipped word, then names and memories. Fate runs unlucky: small luck curdles nearby, then things go wrong around the puller, then their own name sits wrong in other mouths, and at the end the knots all come due at once. A Path can be read by watching its walker tire. See The Three Paths.

Flux Density · EU/g · The compression ratio. How much power is packed per unit of Crystal mass. Higher Flux Density means greater output from the same reserve. It is the same number on every Path.

AU/s · Aether Units per second · The output rate: how much of the reserve a practitioner spends per second at full output. AU/s = Flux Density × η. Power delivered is AU/s × η, so yield counts twice on purpose, once for how much the layer in use can move and once for what survives the trip.

Yield · Eta · η · How much of what is put in reaches the world, as a distillation has its yield: what arrives as intended effect, divided by what was spent. The older books call it efficiency, and the formulas keep the symbol. What becomes of the rest follows the layer it left by. A yield of 0.50 through the Shell burns half of every expenditure in the Shell’s furnace, coming off as heat, noise and structural bleed; the same figure through the Core is paid as a hollowing, and through the Attraction Layer as a knot. From Glory the Mastery of the road in use sets it (Body through the Shell, Spirit through the Core, Fate for a pull), on one table for all three; below Glory the Stage’s own figure stands for the unread, and above it the same table gives a field average for a stranger not yet read; a read rung overrides that average, and a card’s own figure governs its holder. At full output a higher yield empties the reserve faster; for the same delivered effect it lasts longer. A yield above 1.0 is real. More arrives than the Crystal spent because the surplus is Aether drawn in through the soul while the working fires, pulled in like any other: the practitioner holds the idea open and the stratum pours the surplus Aether through the open door, and above 1.0 the Continuum recognizes the expression as law and supplements it with ambient flow. No reserve is returning more than it held, and the figure on the sheet is correct.

Joule · J · What the Measurewrights reckon one EU is worth outside the Crystal. 1 EU = 1 MJ is their conversion, and never the reading: a Grade reads off EU delivered (EU × yield), at the guild’s old thresholds divided by a million, so no Grade on any item or beast has moved. The conversion, the guild’s chain from EU to joules to tons of TNT, and what a reserve buys at each rung are The Essence Ledger, Fracture of Worlds Part Twenty-Three.

Watt · W · What the Measurewrights reckon an AU/s is worth as power. One AU is one EU, so by their conversion 1 AU/s = 1 MW: power spent is AU/s × 1 MW, power delivered is AU/s × η × 1 MW, and through the Shell the difference burns in the Shell’s furnace and leaves as heat. Time to empty at full output is reserve ÷ AU/s, and because AU/s carries η, a cleaner Shell at full output empties sooner while the same delivered effect costs it less.

Turn · Six seconds. The unit every stated duration in turns is read through, in a duel and on a campaign clock alike: a working held three turns is held eighteen seconds. A reserve spent at R AU/s for one turn is 6R EU gone, delivering 6R × η EU (the guild’s megajoules).

The Essence Ledger · Fracture of Worlds Part Twenty-Three. The join between the four quantities above and the Grade scale: what an EU buys (in joules, by the guild’s conversion), what a reserve costs to spend, how long it lasts at full output, how fast it comes back, and what can be done about all of it from the other side of a fight.

VI. Traits, Refinement and the Seat

A soul is assembled out of what happened to it and what it could afford, in that order.

Trait A standing law held in the Essence Core, formed when lived experience hardens into a statement the Continuum accepts as reliable. Survived into existence, never taught. A Trait belongs to the soul, sealed or open; an open Crystal only arms it. It carries no level of its own: it works at its owner’s Mastery of the lead road, and at its base below Glory, where Mastery is unread. Release and Domain are the same Trait escalating. Older books gave Traits levels.

The Four Reflections Primary, the innate law, present from birth, shaped by first imprint and the lived Path, and never removable. Secondary, forged by trauma or decision, conditional, expensive to fire. Inherited, written into the Core before the Core had a self to consent with, and governed by Tempering Maturity. Passive, stabilized by long habit, always running, cheapest and most identifying. Compound Traits fuse two or more into one law. Higher-Imprint Traits are marked by an Archon, a Titan or a World Spirit Realm and behave as miniaturized Principles.

Trait tissue The bodily expression of a Trait. A Trait biases the Aether Shell’s conductivity permanently, and because the Shell is the Material-Plane layer, the bias is laid down in flesh: mineral into bone, altered myelination, rebuilt vasculature, a resting core temperature off the population mean. Trait tissue heals on the wrong schedule in both directions, which is what makes it diagnostic. A body is a Trait record and a corpse is a better one.

The unwoken · the Dormant A soul whose Crystal has not opened and, in the overwhelming majority of cases, never will, because nobody trains it. The unwoken carry Traits like anyone else. They lack only a woken Crystal to arm them.

The Weathering Refinement under strain without a melt. The soul hardens in place along the direction it was already being used, rather than liquefying and recasting. Produces real strengthening of what a person already was and no capacity to become anything else. The universal process for the unwoken and the standing background process for everyone.

Grain The common word for what the Weathering produces. A man is said to have grain, or to be set. Borrowed from the joiners, the same word the halls use for a Crystal’s seat.

Nucleation What decides the outcome of a Threshold. A melt cools into order only around structures that survived the heat, and in a Soul Crystal the only such structures are Traits, because a law the Continuum has accepted as reliable does not stop being reliable at temperature. Strain gets a soul to the melt. Nucleation decides what the melt produces.

The Seat The residue of a reorganization, from the joiner’s word. A clean seat integrated evenly and reads as one thing. A rough seat cooled around seeds that contradicted each other and keeps the argument permanently, producing practitioners who are unpredictable rather than weak.

Dead Set · Slag A melt that cooled with no adequate seed. The Stage does not advance, Clarity and Compression fall, and a Trait is commonly lost outright. A seat that never took. Recovery is slow and never complete.

Residual Strain Pressure that builds when Level runs past the top of a soul’s rank group with no Catalyst met for the next Stage: over Level 100 below Expert, over 200 below Master, over 300 below Grandmaster, over 400 below Archmaster. Grinding to the top of a group is ordinary; the veteran stuck at the gate is the strange one. Strain shows in the room, degrades gain and raises Overchannel and Backlash risk, and does not stop building: the soul meets its Catalyst or fractures under the weight. In a high-Level unwoken it can break the seal, which is why late wakings happen.

Overgrown The Crystal holding a Stage its Level cannot yet hold, met early or bought. Power spills, lamps lean, Mastery reads low and no Release will come until Level catches up. The heir with a bought Stage and the prodigy who met a Catalyst too young are the same case. Not to be confused with Overgrowth, a Trait outgrowing its bearer’s guidance.

The Flicker Traits dropping in and out under Starvation. A Shell below its working reserve cannot maintain a standing bias, so the law falls out and returns as the reserve moves.

The Rigidity Effect The more absolute a Trait’s law, the less the soul can safely contradict it. Power and inflexibility are the same purchase.

Shear Break A Trait collapsing, inverting, or turning on its bearer after the bearer has repeatedly denied the truth that formed it.

Overgrowth A Trait expanding past the soul’s capacity to guide it. Bodily mutation, Essence warping, unintended Domain formation. The warning sign is a Trait acting when the bearer did not will it.

VII. The Sheet and the Climb

Nobody has ever seen your window but you, which has not stopped anyone asking.

Window · The soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in the owner’s own tongue, unseen by anyone else. It shows numbers, ranks, percentages, bars and Level, in short blocks, fights included, for damage, skill gains and reserve. EU, AU/s and yield never appear in one. Scholars argue about who built it.

[Reserve: 41% ▓▓▓▓░░░░░░]
[Hit: left ribs, two cracked. Vitality 72%]
[Skill: Staff, Apprentice to Journeyman]

The Sheet · What a normal look at the window shows: race and lineage, Level, Stage, Path, class, Mastery from Glory, the Eight Primaries as numbers, reserve, Traits, skills with their ranks, and any blessings. A Sub-Stat appears when it is used or when it moves, and the full set remains to study. Race and lineage shape base stats and what comes easily. A person’s life spends most of their stat points by what they do, and a few are theirs to place at each Stage.

Registered card · The record a guild or assay house keeps on a practitioner: Stage and rank, Level, Mastery, stat numbers, Traits, each named technique with its cost written as time from full, its tell and its counter, and the stack line. In the Archive’s talk “his card” means this paper, and it is only as good as the last reading. The Character Cards in these pages are the Archive’s own copies of the same kind of record.

Practitioner · A person whose Soul Crystal is awake and working, about one in ten. Everyone else is unwoken (the sealed Shell) and touches magic through what they buy.

Path · The layer of the Crystal a soul has learned to lean on, and so the direction advancement actually took: Body (the Aether Shell, in the Physical World), Spirit (the Essence Core, on the Soul Plane) or Fate (the Attraction Layer, on the Plane of Fate). Body is the literal understanding and manipulation of physical reality, climbing from matter as an object to matter as a complete system; it works raw Aether, held in the Shell as the Charge, and pays in the body. Spirit is everything to do with spiritual reality; it spends Essence through the Core and pays in the self, and its force at range lands on souls only, so a Spirit worker who wants matter moved goes through their own Shell at their own Body Mastery. Fate, the hardest of them, fights with Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds, and on rare days pulls Thread to move what a result depends on, and pays in luck, later, by the knot. It is assessed afterward and recorded whether the person agrees or not, nobody chooses one, and it gates how far a stat may rise. A Path stands as Mastered (its gates are no obstacle at the person’s Stage), Walked (three parts in ten of their practice or more) or Studied (knowledge and small uses, open to anyone, opening no gates). No Path is better: each has plain limits, and each can beat each. See The Three Paths.

Path Stage · The highest Stage whose gates a road has opened, carried on the card for each road. Mastered means it equals the soul’s Stage, read again at each Threshold. The clerk writes Body VII Mastered · Fate VI Walked 35%.

Continuum · The lawful fabric of reality, which measures a soul and permits as much as its architecture will bear. It grants no power. A window is that measurement reported to the owner.

Coil · The assay house’s measuring instrument, a great coil of brass a practitioner stands inside. It reads EU, yield, Stage, Level, the stat numbers, Mastery on each road and the seat, none of which it will argue about. A coil reads a bought Stage the same as a fought one; what reads differently is the seat.

Draft · A working poured into a substance and bottled. Tempering Drafts (Essence Drafts, most of them from monster cores) give Level at a risk of Identity Contamination, breakthrough Drafts supply a Catalyst, fight Drafts refill the reserve for a beat and a crash (Core stock for a Spirit worker’s Core, a Salt for a Body fighter’s Shell), and each is graded by the Stage it serves. See The Ladder and the Draft.

Skill · A thing a person does and gets better at by doing it. Rises with use and shows on the sheet as one of the nine ranks, the same words as a Stage’s rank: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. A trade can reach Paragon with no Stage behind it; a combat skill never ranks above its owner’s own Stage rank. Older books wrote skills in the stat letters, Longsword A (now Expert), Midwifery EX (now Archmaster), and later on six craft ranks, Novice to Legend. Drafts, runes, tags and chants are skills and trades like any other. Anyone can level a skill hands alone can do, pouring Drafts included; cutting runes, firing tags, chanting and speaking a working want a woken Crystal.

Class · A name, never a number, that the soul offers for how the person has lived. First offered at Glory, with Mastery I; Stages I to V read no class. It evolves each time the lead Mastery rises, each evolution giving one move that fits it and a lean on where life spends the stat points. The one wild concept stays the person’s own.

Mastery · How well a soul works its own road’s substance: one rung per Path, I to V and Ω at Paragon, written Body IV · Spirit III · Fate II. Never trained as a thing apart. The Stage sets the ceiling (unread below Glory; I at Glory, II at Refraction, III at Transcendence and Invocation, IV at Realization and Dissonance, V from Emanation to Zenith, Ω at the top) and Level places the soul under it: rung I from Level 115, II from 150, III from 215, IV from 260, V from 340. From Glory it sets yield on one table for all three roads. Matter reads Body, a soul or a meaning reads Spirit, a condition reads Fate. A Walked second road reads a rung behind until mastered, a Studied one two behind and stopping at II. Fate climbs like the others, but without a grip (a bloodline’s Work or a blessing) it stops at II; an Epoch bloodline’s instrument reads rung I on its road from Stage III, the one thing read below Glory. A bought Stage raises the ceiling and not the rung. Each road keeps folk names for the rungs, from the old ladders: Body Muridic, Harmonic, Resonant, Luminous, Radiant; Spirit Listener, Answerer, Confessor, Shepherd, Ferryman; Fate Touched, Drawn, Spinner, Knotwright, Weaver. The older books counted them as three ladders, the Aether, Essence and Thread Classes, with a sixth rung on each (Voidic, Sin-Eater or Hollow, Severer); that sixth is now the mark Inverted, a feared and wrong way of holding any rung (Spirit IV Inverted). See Classes and Mastery.

Catalyst · The push that opens a Threshold. The Crystal has a shape it wants for each Stage (see The Sixteen Stages), and crisis, seclusion and tribulation are the old roads to it. Drafts and blessings can supply it too, but a Catalyst opens the Stage only when Level can hold it: Stage II holds at Level 5, III at 15, IV at 30, V at 101, VI at 115, VII at 130, VIII at 201, IX at 215, X at 230, XI at 301, XII at 320, XIII at 401, XIV at 420, XV at 500. Stage II’s Catalyst is the first lens, a Wellspring’s raw Aether touched under pressure. The great houses hoard the top Drafts, and the poor rise by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings.

A bought Stage · One opened with money. It opens the door and no more: a Release must still be found and mastered by fighting, and sects teach what money cannot buy. A purse can buy the Catalyst for any Stage, though at Zenith a Hollow Throne must still recognize the buyer. See The Ladder and the Draft.

Experience · Level moves by danger overcome, so killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills, and crews argue over who got the kill.

Monster core · In a way a monster’s Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster, and read from the beast’s own Stage and Level on the coil. It is the lawful Crystal Draw of The Standing Index. An alchemist renders it into Essence Drafts used for tempering, with Creature-Sourced potency and Identity Contamination. The core’s Path decides the Draft: a Body monster’s renders to Salts, a Spirit monster’s to Core stock, and a Fate monster’s to a reagent for the Layer that is sold only on the black market. Swallowed raw it gives everything at once, and most who try die or come out wrong. See Monster Cores.

Monster · A creature that gains Levels by killing and changes form at its own thresholds, on the same Stage and Level scale as a person, read by Gnosis, with question marks for a weak reader. Monsters walk the Paths as people do: Body monsters are common, Spirit ones rarer, and Fate ones rarest and most feared. Known and bountied by Grade, E up to EX, like Commissions, the Accord setting which title may take which Grade; old hunters still say the nine Threat Tiers (T4 for a Grade B beast). A famous one earns a name, an epithet and a bounty, and its parts make gear.

Cut Crystal · A Crystal taken from a dead practitioner. It renders into the strongest Draft of all, the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable, and the reason Grade IV of the Standing Index (its top Grade of stock, no relation to Body IV) has never balanced.

Dungeon · What grows down in floors around a Core, with something guarding the heart. Kill that and clear the place, or leave it and it grows. Alive and learning: it moves its galleries and breeds what kills crews best, and scholars argue whether it thinks. See Dungeons.

Vow · Small Fate, a condition a person binds on themselves, spoken aloud, anyone’s to make. It shows in the window with its terms. It spends the soul’s own share and pulls nothing, so a vow kept leaves no knot. A broken vow lands at once by its own terms and needs no witness; repeated denial of the truth behind it makes a Shear Break. See Vows and Oaths.

Blessing · A power out of a person’s natural reach, even in Level, granted by a higher spirit, an Archon, an Epoch or a person of higher Temperance. It need not go through Fate, but most do, and a great giver spends almost nothing. It is its own line on the sheet, and it can lie, because the giver wrote the line. See Blessings and Curses.

Curse · Often a blessing with terms you did not read, or from a giver who does not care what you want.

Fate Art · A real working of the Plane of Fate. Rare, mostly by bloodline, reached late by some high-Stage masters along the Fate Path, and considered incredibly overpowered. Most people touch Fate only from the outside, through a blessing or a Master Work Draft. Hataraki can take back or remove a blessing, with two Works or two Moto. A card writes it Fate Art: Work · grip · Thread.

Work · An inherited grip on one function of Fate, the unit of Hataraki. It lowers the chance of failure and never the price, it is no glyph, and one Work does one job. A personal grip, found by nobody’s teaching, can be won once.

Pull · Reaching a thread of Fate down to move what a result depends on, priced by rung against distance from likely, as a share of the reserve, and every pull, landed or failed, leaves a knot besides. Its charge is a stillness, a held breath, the room settling; the warning is a breath for a lean, several heartbeats for a prerequisite removed and longer for a field, and reaching the puller inside it is the counter. A vow spends the soul’s own share, and a pull reaches above it. Not to be confused with drawing in Aether, which the older books also call the pull.

Fate fighter · Someone on the Fate Path. Their everyday weapons are Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds. A real pull of a thread moves what a blow depends on, so the result arrives without having been forced, and it is rare, ruinously expensive, often fails and always leaves a knot. It moves only what already has a way of happening, shows a charge and a tell, and is contested: Dominion Fate against Resilience Persistence and Continuity. Being the hardest road makes it no better, and it loses a straight exchange.

Title · One of the nine ranks of the Guild Accord, the coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. The same nine in every guild, grading trust in that trade, and earned by commissions: a merchant may deal up to it, a craftsman make up to it, an adventurer take Commissions up to the Grade it allows. Fighting guilds also ask the title’s own rank in Stage (a Journeyman’s token wants Stage III); trade titles ask none. The same nine words are a fighter’s rank. A forged token is a great crime.

Item Grade and Rarity · An item’s two ratings: Grade for power, on the Measurewrights’ letters, and Rarity for scarcity (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary, Mythic, Unique). A smith’s title caps the Grade she may make and a merchant’s caps the Rarity he may sell. The old words (Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous, Proscribed) survive as the Accord’s stamps. Bonded gear grows; other gear does not. Plain gear does its own work and no more, while worked gear (runed, Draft-tempered or made of monster parts) also multiplies one of its wearer’s Primaries whole, shown on the window ([Gnosis ×1.15], [Shell draw: light]) and paid for with a draw on the reserve. All gear together multiplies any one Primary by ×1.5 at most, and a piece above its wearer’s Stage works at half its multiplier or fights them. An Appraisal skill and an assay instrument read an item, and a good forger beats a weak appraiser. Beside Grade and Rarity, a material’s virtue decides which workings it holds well and how it fails: stone remembers, salt fixes, gold will not corrupt, quicksilver will not keep a shape.

Master Work · A piece that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named. Each area has its own. A Fate Essence Draft is one: extremely hard and very coveted. See Master Works.

Reading a person · A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward. Anything else is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide or fake it.
