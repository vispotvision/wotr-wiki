Releases and Domains by Path

Releases and Domains by Path

Nov 28, 202626 min read

How many releases each of the three roads holds, what a Release, a Domain and a Realm do on each, what each one spends, and how to tell which you are standing in before it is too late to matter

“The first one cooked the ring. The second one I never saw at all, and I was looking straight at her. The third one I only noticed when I found I had already bet on him.”

A turnstile-keeper at the Sealhall brackets, on the afternoon of the Three Roads

The Sealhall autumn brackets of 688 are remembered for one afternoon on which three fighters called a release inside the same hour, one from each road, two Firsts and then the One, and eleven thousand people in guild colors got the plainest lesson in the Paths the Midmarch has ever been given for the price of a seat.

Bram Oxley, the Dray, went first. He was a carter’s son from Garrow Lock with a neck like a mooring post, and he bawled “FULL LOAD” loud enough to lift pigeons off the far stand. His skin went the dull red of a brick kiln from the scalp down, the sand under his boots sagged into two bowls, and the front rows put their hands up against the heat coming off him. His opponent hit him with a quarterstaff and the staff caught fire.

Mother Edda Quill went second, and half the stands thought she had declined to. She said “Plain Speaking” in the voice of a woman asking for the salt. Nothing in the ring changed. No sand stirred, no banner moved, and her pewter-gray aura hung where it had been, a half step off her body. Then she said one further sentence to the swordsman in front of her, at the same volume, and every person in the top tier heard it as if she were sitting beside them. The swordsman sat down on the sand and wept, with his blade still in his hand, and could not afterward say what he had been fighting about.

Tobias Marl went third. He walked the Fate road, which has nothing to release until the seventh Stage, and he had spent eleven years at the Sealhall rope on presence and Range alone before he stood at Refraction and had something to call. The argument over whether he said anything at all lasted years. The account the Guild accepts is that he said “The House” while adjusting his cuff. The dust in the ring lay down flat. A coin a boy had dropped under the stands landed on its edge and stayed there. The marshal found he had already stepped back to give the man room, the crowd found it had already gone quiet, and his opponent, a perfectly sound Stage V spearman, discovered that everybody in the building including himself had been treating the bout as settled for some time.

A child can say the whole of it: the first one changes his body, the second one changes what reaches you, and the third one changes whose room it is. What follows is how a Measurewright says it.

The pattern every release follows, the Stages, the calling and the learning are set out in Release Forms, and the claim itself in Domains. This page is the three roads laid side by side.

What Holds for Everybody

  • The road sets the count. The lead Path decides how many releases a soul can ever have: three on the Body road, at IV, VII and IX; two on the Spirit road, at IV and VII; and one on Fate, the One, at VII. Each is deeper than the last, and each has its own Catalyst, set out in Release Forms.
  • Called or silent. Called aloud, a release takes its full rise and costs a beat. Silent, it costs no beat and comes in weaker. The owner chooses each time.
  • The Second, or the One, opens and powers the Domain. At Stage VII it holds a seed for minutes. Anyone at Stage VIII can have a true Domain, their own concept made real. A seed can also flicker once, unbidden, as the Catalyst that carries a soul into Stage VII, with no Release behind it, and it cannot be done again by wanting to. The flicker follows the road as the field will: a Body flicker rules matter, a Spirit one meanings, a Fate one conditions.
  • Found on the road’s own ground. A release is found and mastered by fighting, and the seclusion that so often comes before a Second or the One is kept where the lead layer feeds: on Aetheric Density for the Body, in Echo Depth for the Spirit, under Thread Weight for Fate. A Stage’s bought Catalyst is poured by road too, on a Salt base, on Core stock, or on the Layer reagent, the last being the dearest and the most criminal. No bench has ever poured a release.
  • The cap. Self-boosts stack to two steps on the hidden scale over the sustainable figure on the card and no further. A Third Release fills that cap alone. Only outside help goes past it. See Stacking and Spending.
  • One idea. Trait, Release and Domain are a single concept growing, whichever road carries it.
  • A fighter on two roads releases on the lead road, with the lead road’s count. The road behind colors those releases, shows in their texture, and brings no release and no field of its own.

The Body Road

The First Release is a flood. A Body fighter works raw Aether, drawn in and packed in the Shell unrefined, and that packed store is the Charge. Called, the First Release opens the Shell to all of it at once, shaped on the spot by the Wellspring’s lens. The flesh changes and the blow changes with it: the body runs hotter and heavier, the skin takes the release’s look, and every hit carries the Charge into what it lands on. The lost share burns in the Shell, which is the furnace that never goes cold, and comes off him as heat, which is why the front row at Sealhall shielded their faces. None of it is Essence. It is the world’s own power worn like a second hide, and the body pays the bill for carrying it.

The Second Release rules matter. The Charge leaves the skin and fills the ground, and inside the line weight, heat and the state of stone obey the holder. Everyone can see a Body field. The air shimmers, the light changes color and the cobbles do something cobbles should not.

The famous one. Big Annet Rudge of the Hask Moor threshers held the smallest true Domain on the Guild rolls, THE THRESHING FLOOR, eleven paces across and not a hand more. Inside it everything weighed three times what it should, and Annet did not. Men laughed at the size of it for twenty years, generally from twelve paces off. A Stage VIII knight of the Hundred Crowns rode into it at a canter in 671, and what the horse did to itself coming down on its own forelegs is the reason the Moor holds no jousts. He was cut out of his breastplate with shears. The edge of the Floor is marked in whitewash to this day, and the drovers repaint it.

The Third Release is the body remade. Only the Body road has one. It comes out of the tribulation at Invocation, a new wound the Wellspring gives and helps rebuild the body around, and from then on the Third comes up through that wound and the field takes the Wellspring’s second color at its rim. Inside it the Trait’s stated effect becomes local law, for that effect alone, and stands at the cap by itself. The songs call it a Realm. It is the Body road’s door to one, which is a different thing, and the door is a long way further on.

What holds it small. Radius, Sovereignty and Gravity are Fate gates, and without the Fate Path they stop at 400. A Body Domain is therefore real, tight and hard, and a holder with the gates open will out-hold it yard for yard. Annet’s answer was that eleven paces is a long way when you are inside them.

Run long. At half the heat-haze thickens on the skin. At a quarter steam comes off the shoulders, the big muscles cramp and fibers tear. At a tenth the release ends on its own, and a fighter who forces it past that finds the Shell drawing its Charge out of the flesh: the skin splits in fine bright lines like glaze on an overfired pot. Past that lies heat stroke, burst vessels and a Shell that may not close. Bram Oxley held Full Load nine breaths too long in the final and spent the winter wrapped in wet sheets.

The Spirit Road

The First Release lengthens the line. A Spirit fighter spends Essence, refined in the Crystal, stamped with identity and sent out through the Core. The First Release transforms the line that Essence travels and how far it holds: what a thrown working can carry, and to how many souls at once. Its force is force on souls. It knocks a soul out of true, lays dread on a mind, wounds a Core, and it goes past plate and hide as if they were not there, since armor does not cover what it strikes. It moves no stone. The Shell is the only door into matter, and a Spirit fighter who wants the stone moved does it through their own Shell, at their Body Mastery, like anybody else.

So a Spirit release shows on the holder and on the struck, and hardly at all on the room. The aura flares to full color and the sound comes, as with every release. The sand stays where it is.

The Second Release rules meanings. The field governs what a thing is taken to be. The room looks exactly as it did, nobody’s hair moves, and something in it has stopped being what it was. There is no Third on this road. The Second is the deepest a Spirit soul releases, and its door to a Realm is that same Second, held long enough to grow whole.

The famous one. Jessamy Crake kept a pawnshop on Tally Row in Waxgate and reached Stage VIII behind its counter. Her Domain was called UNREDEEMED, and inside it nothing was yours until she said it was paid for. A duelist’s bonded saber, carried nineteen years, lay in his hand like a stranger’s property: the steel was the same steel, and his soul no longer knew it, so the share of his release it carried was simply not there. Men forgot which of their own arts belonged to them. Three collectors from a Kharven house walked in to settle her account in 659, and they walked out again unhurt, and it was a week before any of them could be persuaded that their own boots were theirs to put on. Nothing on her shelves had moved an inch.

What holds it small. The same Fate gates as the Body road, and a body that is no stronger for any of this. A Spirit holder dies out of position while the field stands, to a spear that does not care what it means.

Run long. The lost share is paid in the Core, never as heat, so a flagging Spirit fighter does not sweat. At half the colors near them go flat. At a quarter comes a hollow cold and a slipped word. At a tenth the release ends, and held past it names and memories start to slip. Maxed, a memory goes for good and the holder faints. Mother Quill, in her last season, finished a bout and asked the marshal quite politely which city this was.

The Fate Road

Nothing to release until Refraction. From Adept through the Expert years a Fate fighter fights with Range, Aura Pressure, presence, bonds and vows, and all of those grow with the Stage. There is no First on this road and no Third. What comes at Refraction is one release, the One, deeper than any First and counted as a Second, because it does both jobs the other roads split in two.

The One enlarges the claim. It transforms the claim and the Pressure: how much of the room already answers to the holder before anything has been done in it. It is paid from the soul’s own share of fate, the same purse a vow is paid from, and that purse is the reserve: a Fate soul refines its Aether into Essence as a Spirit soul does, holds it in the Core, and spends it out through the Layer. Tobias Marl kept a flask of Core stock in his coat like any thrower. The release pulls no thread, so the release itself leaves no knot behind. What shows is very little on the body and a good deal around it. Dust lies still, flames stand straight, coins land face up, and people give ground they did not decide to give. A vow spoken inside it sits heavier, and so does the holder’s name.

A true pull made from inside a Fate release is another matter. It is still a pull, it pays a pull’s price on top of the release’s clock, and it leaves its knot like any other.

The One also lays the field, and the field rules conditions. It governs what has to be so before anything can happen in it, and it opens and powers the Domain as a Second does on the other roads. Nothing is prevented by force. The thing simply lacks leave to occur.

The famous one is the Long Sitting of Justiciar Ambrose Tick at Hawes Bridge, told in full on the Domains page: three hundred yards of river valley in which no blow could land until its case was called. It is the model of a Fate field, wide, quiet and held for nine hours, and it shows the other half of the road as well, because the bridge went into the Kessen when it ended.

What holds it. The gates stand open on this road, so reserve is the leash, and the ground is the other. Rich Aetheric ground fills a Fate holder as it fills anyone and crowds the radius of the field and the holder’s Range while it does. Heavy Thread Weight, a battlefield, a court, a crossroads, extends both, and a Fate holder chooses where to stand accordingly. Inside a formed Domain the Fate amplification can pass a Body fighter’s bonus outright, which is the whole of what the road buys here, and it is a great deal.

Run long. At half small luck curdles nearby: a cup slips, a buckle gives, a friend’s dice go cold. At a quarter things go wrong around the holder in earnest and at once. At a tenth the release ends, and forced past it the holder’s own name begins to sit wrong in other mouths. Maxed, every knot the holder has ever left outstanding comes due together. Under all of it runs the Spirit road’s bill, because the reserve being emptied is Essence: a Fate holder below a tenth is losing names as well as luck. Marl overheld the House exactly once, in a card room in Verath, and the roof came in on that table and no other.

Every Domain Is a Layer Claim

Whatever the field rules, the claim that stakes it is made by the Attraction Layer, which every soul has.

  • It needs no Fate Mastery. The claim spends the soul’s own share and reaches for nothing above it, so a hitter who has never felt a thread can stake a Domain as surely as a Moto can.
  • It pulls no thread and leaves no knot. Holding a Domain, of any kind, for any length of time, adds nothing to what the world will collect from the holder later. The Domain’s bill is the reserve, the collapse, and the price in the coin of its own idea.
  • Upkeep reads the lead road’s Mastery. A Body holder’s field is kept at their Body Mastery and paid in Charge, and it throws heat. A Spirit holder’s is kept at their Spirit Mastery and paid in Essence, and it hollows. A Fate holder’s is kept at their Fate Mastery and paid in Essence through the Layer, with nothing to see but the luck going off. See How Power Grows.
  • The Fate gates measure it. Radius, Sovereignty and Gravity decide how wide a field runs, how well it holds contested ground and how steady it stands, and they stop at 400 for anyone who has not walked the Fate Path. That one fact is why Body Domains are yards, Spirit Domains are rooms and Fate Domains are valleys. The gates open by the Fate Path’s own Stage, which climbs with the soul like any road’s, so a Fate holder needs no high rung of Mastery to hold a valley.
  • The ground has a say. Each field is cheaper on its own road’s ground, and rich Aetheric ground crowds any Domain’s radius while heavy Thread Weight extends it.

The Realm, by Road

A Realm is a Domain grown into a field that can hold other fields inside it, still running, with their owners in them. Its edge takes the Wellspring’s second color, pacts, artifacts and law begin to set into the terrain, and the Continuum has to make room. It is a claim, owes no knot and can be held all day. The Archives read it by the road beneath it: a Body Realm rules the matter another Domain stands on, a Spirit Realm what the fields inside it are taken to mean, and a Fate Realm whether a field inside it has leave to stand at all.

The flicker comes first, and early. The crossing into Invocation carries a first flicker of a Realm, once, needing no release, on every road and in that road’s terms. The Ember Deacon’s field closing round the marshal’s at Dornhold was such a flicker, and it was enough to burn a town for nineteen years.

The door opens at Realization, on every road, and never before. Realization’s own Catalyst is the Domain made a thing the Continuum must make room for, and no door can open before the Continuum has been made to do it. So a Realm is a Stage X thing at the earliest, and most who ever hold one get there at Dissonance or Emanation. That is late enough to be rare and early enough that a Stage X Master with a Realm is a soul the Concord’s Strategic Division keeps a file on.

Each road needs what came before. The Body’s door needs the Third Release held. The Spirit’s needs the Second held as a true Domain for years, grown whole. Fate’s needs the One. Without them there is no door to open. Each door has its own Catalyst on top, a single deed done at Stage X or above with another soul’s field in the holder’s hands, met under contest, one deed for one door, and never bought. A bought Stage X has no Realm and no door, and nothing sells one.

The holder’s own idea rides on top. A Realm is the same concept as the Trait, the Release and the Domain, at its last size. What follows is how each road holds other fields, and the holder’s own concept brings its own flaw and its own price on top of these.

The Body: through the Third

The door’s Catalyst: another’s world carried, and not let fall. With the Third released, the holder lets another soul’s true Domain stand on their own ground and bears its whole weight in the remade body for its full span, an enemy’s at full strength or an ally’s in collapse, without breaking it and without letting it fall on what stands beneath. A Body Master who catches a dying ally’s Domain as its owner’s reserve fails, and carries it in her own body until it spends itself so that the collapse never reaches the town beneath, meets it. So does the Master who stands under an enemy’s field for the whole of its hour, every weight it lays on the ground taken into his frame, so that the people sheltering behind him are never inside its law. A Master two Stages up who simply overwrites the enemy’s field from above has carried nothing, since any field two Stages up does that. The holder who carries it until the body gives, and lets it come down on the street, has failed the one thing asked: the deed is the not letting fall.

What the Realm is. Matter as foundation. The holder’s field becomes the ground other fields stand on, so a Domain inside a Body Realm keeps running and keeps its own law, and every grain of matter it rests on belongs to the holder.

Its idea is the foundation that bears worlds: to be what other law stands on. The masons’ benches call it Petralon’s idea at the size of a soul, and the operation is coagulation, the holder fixed as the ground and set to bear.

Its flaw. A foundation bears whatever presses down on it, and nothing ever asked it to hold on. A Body Realm cannot grip. Whatever lifts away, uproots or draws out from it parts it, and a field inside that chooses to rise rather than press goes out through the top.

Its contrary is the pull and the rising: a Fate pull on what the ground is there for, a field built on ascent or on slipping the load (Ascensio and Cymorath stand against it among the Sixty), and ground already holding another idea.

Its price. What is ground is walked on, and what bears a world keeps some of it. Each holding leaves the weight of the fields carried settled in the holder’s body for good. They grow heavier by the size of what they bore, floors give under them, horses will not carry them, and in the end they cannot be lifted, carried off a field wounded or pulled out of the water. The Measurewrights call it settling.

The Spirit: through its Second, grown whole

The door’s Catalyst: another’s whole meaning held inside your own, and both left true. Inside their own Domain, the holder takes in a rival’s true Domain and understands its law so wholly that it runs inside their field meaning exactly what its owner means by it, while the holder’s own meaning stands around it unchanged, for the whole of the contest. A court mancer who holds a heretic’s Domain inside her own for the length of his trial, so that the bench hears what his law truly is and judges that, meets it, and she may judge against him anyway. A Spirit Master facing a rival’s field of grief who takes it in whole, every mourning in it meant as its owner means it, and goes on fighting from inside her own, meets it too. The Master who holds the rival’s field by taking it to be something else, a madness, a lie, a trick, has laid her own reading over another’s law, and the Crystal counts nothing. So does the holder whose own meaning yields to what she took in. She held one meaning, and the door asks two.

What the Realm is. Meaning as the reading of readings. The holder’s field becomes the sense in which other fields are read, so a Domain inside a Spirit Realm keeps its law and has that law taken in the holder’s understanding.

Its idea is the interpreter’s house, every meaning inside it held whole and given its true reading: the sin-eater’s office grown into a law, or the interpreter between two courts whom both trust because she lies to neither. The operation is conjunction, two meanings joined in one vessel and each kept.

Its flaw. A reading needs something to read. What means nothing, and what refuses to be read, silence, the unrecorded, a thing with no name to read it by, gives it nothing to hold and passes through. And whatever part of a field the holder has not truly understood is not held at all.

Its contrary is unknowing: a working that withdraws recognition (Null-Light’s Mugan is its direct contrary), forgetting on purpose, the hollow law, and the refusal of a soul that will not be read.

Its price. What is wholly understood is half believed. Each holding leaves the held field’s doctrine in the holder’s Core as a second voice that argues, and the holder comes out of every Realm a little persuaded by the person they fought. The old ones carry a crowd of enemies inside them and agree with most of them about something.

Fate: through the One

The door’s Catalyst: a claim others stand on, kept for them. The holder grants standing to another’s claim, a rival holder’s Domain, a truce between two holders, a city’s charter, by putting their own One beneath it as surety, life and standing pledged; and when the other claim is broken or attacked, the holder keeps it for them at their own cost, against whoever breaks it, kin and allies included. A Fate Master who stands surety on his One for a truce between two Domain holders, and enforces it against the elder of them when she breaks it, though she is his grandmother, meets it. So does the Master who pledges her One beneath a free town’s charter when a duke comes to revoke it, and holds it against the town’s own council when they try to sell it. The surety who keeps the truce by pulling threads, so that each side’s attack never quite happens, has made pulls and not a claim, and opened nothing. The surety who stands by while the claim breaks has failed, however good the reason.

What the Realm is. Condition as warrant. The holder’s field becomes the leave under which any field inside it may stand at all, so a Domain inside a Fate Realm runs only on the holder’s leave, and the leave can be given or withdrawn.

Its idea is the warrant and the surety: to be the condition under which other claims stand, as the surety answers for another’s default and the tithing answers for each of its own. The operation is sublimation, the holder’s claim raised above the others into the vessel their leave comes down from.

Its flaw. A warrant is only as good as the one who warrants. The Realm stands on the holder’s own standing, so if the holder breaks a word of their own while it stands, inside it or outside, every field in it loses leave at once, theirs first. And it governs only what needs leave: a thing that is simply so, a sound body with no strap to give or an avalanche with nothing to exploit, passes through it untouched.

Its contrary is necessity and soundness, whatever needs no leave; the refusal of a soul that does not recognize the holder’s standing; and ground that already holds another idea.

Its price. The surety answers for the default. Every claim given leave inside the Realm that is later broken, whenever and by whomever, is billed to the holder’s standing, and never as a knot: their word weighs less in every mouth for a season, and a holder who has warranted too many faithless claims comes at last to a day when their own vow will not take.

All three

  • On the stack a Realm counts as the field beneath it does, toward the two-step cap over the sustainable figure, and only outside help goes past it.
  • Cost and collapse. Reserve for its span, written on the card as time from full, and Overgrown for a day and a night afterward. Held past its limit, the Trait may fail as the card names. A collapse is a Resonance Shockwave sized to everything inside it, the held fields included, and at Dornhold that was a great deal.
  • Against Stage. A Realm still yields to a field two Stages up. Holding another field is not out-ranking it.
