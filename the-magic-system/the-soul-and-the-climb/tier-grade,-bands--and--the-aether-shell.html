Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell

Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell

Nov 17, 202650 min read

“Power is how cleanly you pass the infinite through the finite, whatever you happen to be holding.”

Three separate measurements, constantly confused for one another. Level counts how many times a Crystal (the Soul Crystal, which is the soul itself, holding power and sending it outward) has densified. Tier Grade reports raw magnitude. Aether Class reports how much of that magnitude survives the trip out through the Shell (the Crystal’s outer layer, which turns inner power into outward force), and its two sister ladders, Essence Class and Thread Class, report the same for the soul’s other two layers. A practitioner is described by all three measurements and by the gaps between them.

A slate hangs under glass in the market hall at Harl Cross, chalked on the night of the Barn and the Clerk. Gerda Holt, whom the Salt Dogs’ songs called the Barn, stood a head over the rail in a rust-colored leather apron with Level 198 on her sheet, an A-Grade Ardency (the stat for how hard a soul hits through the layer it leans on), and, on the Body Path, a Shell that read Aether I under the Class name Wallbreaker. Mun Seo-yeon kept the accounts at a rope-walk, had come straight from work in her sleeve-guards, and carried Level 170, a B-Grade Ardency and, on the Spirit Path, a Core that read Essence III under the Class name Auditor. The slate went five to one on the Barn, because everybody in the Cross bet the first number they could read. Holt’s opening punch missed and took the front off the grain hall and the front off the hall behind it, with orange steam pouring off her shoulders, because what she threw was the Charge, raw Aether packed unrefined in her Shell, and better than a third of everything she spent was leaving her as heat. By the differential rule (below) she wins any direct exchange, and Mun declined to offer her one. She went round and round the market in her sleeve-guards, spending a fraction of the price for every blow she threw from the Core, blows that left the Barn’s hide alone and landed on the soul inside it, and taking one in return that cost her two ribs and most of an ear, while every roof in the Cross howled at her to stand still. What Mun lost on each spend she lost in the Core, where no steam shows: the colors of the awnings went a little flat around her, and that was all the crowd got. Seven minutes in, the Barn’s color began to stutter and her arms came down, at a tenth of her reserve, with fine red lines opening in the skin of her forearms where the Shell had started drawing on the flesh, and most of the east side of the market in ruins. Mun walked up and broke her jaw, by hand, since a Core has no door into a jawbone. Two bookies went in the river that night, and the Salt Dogs now sing a different verse.

Three numbers were on that slate, and the crowd had read one.

Level and the Five Bands

Level is the count of how many times a Soul Crystal has meaningfully densified. Age has nothing to do with it and neither do training hours. It is the sediment of genuine contact with consequence, counted, compressed, and expressed as a number, and it moves by danger overcome. Killing one thing stronger than you moves it more than a hundred weak kills, which has made a science of the last blow, and crews argue over who got the kill long after they have stopped arguing over the money.

The ceiling is 500 for every practitioner below Stage (one of the sixteen rungs of a soul’s refinement) XV. This is as much as a mortal Soul Crystal can contain before the distinction between practitioner and Principle collapses entirely. A soul below Stage XV that reaches Level 500 and goes on taking pressure gets no more Levels for it. It begins the Apotheosis approach (the slow crossing from practitioner into living law), and the Archives stopped trying to put a number on that some time ago. Paragons (the highest standing, Stages XV and XVI) exceed Level 500: Level keeps rising past it with no ceiling and no sixth Band, and the Codex declines to number it.

Nobody breaches a Band ceiling without first passing the Temperance Threshold (the crisis that refines a soul up to its next Stage) that cluster demands. The Continuum simply halts the counter and goes on building pressure, called Residual Strain, until the Threshold is passed or the soul fractures under the weight.

A soul accumulates Level by danger overcome and Stage by Catalyst (an event hard enough to force the next Stage, or the same key handed over in a Draft or a blessing), and the two arrive on unrelated schedules. The Catalyst is one demand to a Stage, and it arrives in the terms of the layer a soul leans on: in flesh and matter for a Body soul, in a soul or a meaning for a Spirit one, in a bond, an oath or the odds for a Fate one. The Sixteen Stages gives all three forms for every floor. Nothing stops a practitioner from grinding to the top of Band II at Stage IV if no Catalyst has ever reached them and they cannot pay for one. They will be strong, wrong-shaped, and stopped dead at the gate, and their sheet will read as an error to anyone who cannot tell the two columns apart.

The reverse case is the house heir. Stages can be bought, and a soul that takes Catalysts faster than it takes pressure arrives at a high Stage on a thin Level, holding architecture it has not earned the density to fill and with no Release to show for any of it. It clears every gate on this table and is the most common way for a great family’s second son to die in his first real fight.

A blessing can hand someone Levels out of their natural reach, and the counter stops at the Band gate like anyone’s. The gate asks for the Threshold, so a blessed Level clears it only when the same blessing also supplies the Catalyst, and the rich have learned to read that clause.

Band I is the age of first contact, when the Crystal is porous and half made, and no later years will ever go as fast, because it is still learning the shape of what it holds: the Charge (Aether pulled in from the world and packed raw in the Shell) on a Body soul, and Essence (Aether refined in the Crystal and stamped with its owner) on a Spirit or a Fate one. Everybody on a market street who can warm a kettle is in this Band. They are a real danger to a tavern and a matter of no interest to an Archon (one of the fourteen great powers that embody cosmic law).

Band II comes after the first meeting with something a great deal bigger than you. The Crystal starts to show its character, a signature its owner will carry for life, and tricks start to cohere into a style. On the far edge of it, at Stage VII, comes the first flicker of a Domain (a space where one’s own law outranks the world’s).

Band III is where a room starts doing what it is told. An aura leans on weaker ones with no effort at all, a technique carries the weight of doctrine, and the dogs get up and leave before the visitor has reached the gate.

Band IV has stopped being counted in techniques. It is counted in what a person’s being there does to the land around them, the crops and weather and sleep of a valley, and the Domain that had to be held is now simply how things are in that direction.

Band V is measured against Archons and Titans. Individual combat has become a side issue, and the serious arena is the contest of Domains, Principles and whole lifetimes of doctrine pressing on the Continuum (the whole lawful order of reality) itself.

The Tier Grade System

A Tier Grade is the letter that says what size of thing a stat can meaningfully hit, hold off or lift. It is read off the stat’s number and confirmed by the Stage, and no stat holds a Grade its Crystal has not crossed the Threshold to carry. Every child in the four quarters knows the letters, and the bookies quote them like the price of bread.

The table measures a Sub-Stat (one of eight finer measures inside each main stat). The Stage’s ceiling and the Path gates (limits set by the Plane a soul’s growth leans toward) both bind the Sub-Stat, and every Grade letter in the gate tables and the Resonant Pairs is a threshold on that Sub-Stat’s own number.

A Primary is the combined total of its eight Sub-Stats, and its Grade is read off the mean. Points go on Sub-Stats only, and the Primary is the arithmetic afterward. Eight Ardency (the stat for how hard a soul hits through the layer it leans on) Sub-Stats totaling 2,400 give a mean of 300 and an A-Grade Ardency. The total is what the sheet records, and the mean is what an assessor reads. Dominion alone divides by seven, because Throne stands outside its total. The full treatment is in Fracture of Worlds, Parts One to Three and Parts Four to Ten.

The differential rule. One full Grade over an opponent in the stat in use wins a direct exchange of that stat without much of a contest. Two Grades is one-sided. At three, the lower fighter has stopped being in a fight and is surviving an event.

What a practitioner can pay for a row of this table. The attack-output column reads EU delivered; a reserve is EU (Essence Units, a soul’s stored power, whether it is held as Charge or as Essence). EU delivered = EU spent × η, the yield (η) being the share of a spend that reaches the world, as a distillation has its yield, and the Grade is the row whose band holds the result. The joules beside it are the Measurewrights’ conversion, 1 EU = 1 MJ, and the tons of TNT follow from those. It is worked end to end, EU to Grade to the guild’s joules and tons, in The Essence Ledger, Fracture of Worlds Part Twenty-Three.

Inside one Grade. Two fighters on the same letter settle it by technique, wits, position and how their Sub-Stats are built. A fighter at the top of B-Grade, stat value 275, hits roughly five times as hard as one at the bottom, stat value 176, and both are B-Grade. That is a real gap and not always a decisive one, because technique, a Wellspring’s lens (one of the Sixty, the governing idea that changes how a soul uses its power) and Path bonuses can close it or turn it round. At its home Path a Wellspring can even double its effect, starting small and reaching double only with years of mastery, and half again is the most it reaches away from home, and that shows up here as a fighter whose row on the table undersells them. A Path’s force bonus and a lens both apply after the Tier is read, so neither has ever moved the letter, and neither counts on the two-Grade cap.

The Sharp-Blunt Asymmetry

Edged weapons and piercing techniques concentrate energy into smaller contact areas, producing higher local pressures than blunt-force equivalents at the same total energy yield.

A swordsman at F-Grade delivering 0.2 MN of force through a blade edge contacting roughly one square millimeter produces a local pressure of 200 GPa, exceeding the compressive strength of virtually all natural and most Essence-reinforced materials at that Tier. The same 0.2 MN delivered through a fist contacting one hundred square centimeters produces 20 MPa, sufficient to crack stone but not to bisect steel.

The Continuum tracks total energy for Tier Grade comparison but tracks local pressure for material interaction. The ratio is ten thousand to one at any force, which is why a B-Grade swordsman can cut through materials that a B-Grade brawler can only dent, and why a B-Grade brawler can shatter structures that a B-Grade swordsman can only slice.

Overchannel Spikes

The rows above are sustainable output. An overchannel, forcing the Crystal past its safe ceiling, can spike Strike Force and Lifting Strength to two to five times that for a moment, depending on the Ardency Overchannel Sub-Stat’s Grade and how much Crystal Fracture the fighter is willing to risk.

It always bills. Every overchannel leaves structural damage that must be healed by rest, alchemy or the next Threshold, and a fighter who makes a habit of it builds up fractures that eventually stay. Some fractures become architectural features. Most become liabilities.

The three-copper bolt. A crossbow bolt is the cheapest thing in the world that can kill a high-Stage fighter in a room, because the Speed Grade meets it against the fixed threat Grade every missile carries by its kind (sling stone, hunting arrow, war-bow shaft, crossbow bolt, ballista bolt, a worked missile taking its maker’s Grade) and because reinforcement, the aura coat a fighter trusts to stop it, goes out on anyone asleep, drugged or surprised. Margrave Ulf Stavang, Stage VIII and the terror of three rivers, died at his own daughter’s wedding from one bolt out of the musicians’ gallery, loosed by a boy paid six coppers, because he was looking at the soup. The Reaction table above promises a reading of the loose. It has never promised that you will be looking.

The Sheet and the Window

Everyone in the trade has a sheet, whether or not they have ever seen it written out.

A window is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner in their own tongue. The Soul Crystal is the soul and the Continuum measures it, so the report is simply there when the owner looks, and no one else can see it. Scholars argue about who built it, and a Measurewright will tell you nobody did. A window shows Grades, percentages, bars and levels. It shows no EU, AU/s or yield, the assayers’ figures set out further down this page, and it has never once told anyone the state of their wounds in a way that made them feel better.

A normal look shows the Eight Primaries with their Grades, and a Sub-Stat appears when it is used or when it moves. The full sixty-four remain there to study for anyone with the patience. This one is the Clerk’s, on the evening of the Harl Cross market:

The last line is the reserve, and it is named for what fills it: Essence on a Spirit-led soul like the Clerk’s, Charge on a Body-led one like the Barn’s. A Fate-led soul’s bar reads Essence too, because Thread is never stored and what a Fate worker holds is refined like a Spirit worker’s.

Life spends most of the points. What you do raises those stats, and what you do is mostly what your lead layer does, so a hitter’s points pile up in Ardency, Vitality, Dexterity and Tempering, a thrower’s in Gnosis and Harmonics, and a Fate walker’s in Dominion and Resilience. A few points are yours to place at each Stage, and the grant a Threshold hands over leans the same way as the road. That is why a cobbler’s son and a duke’s son with the same Level read so differently and why a sheet read properly is a biography. Race and lineage show on the sheet and shape base stats and what comes easily, and that goes for classes and suitable Wellsprings too. Skills rise with use and show as Grades: every practitioner has them, from Footwork to Appraisal to Rendering, and techniques are named things done with them. Drafts, runes, tags and chants are all skills and trades of this kind, open to anyone who will put in the years, though all but the pouring want a woken Crystal. Traits have their own levels. Techniques show no level and take a bigger name when they jump. A blessing gets its own line, and since the giver wrote that line, it can lie.

A window speaks only to its owner. Reading anybody else is the work of a stronger Gnosis Perception against a weaker Resilience Ward; everything else is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide or fake what they show. Instruments read everyone. Monsters are on the same scale: a monster has a Level, Grades and a Threat Tier, T1 to T9 like a commission, gains its Levels by killing and changes form at its own thresholds. Each Threat Tier (the T1 to T9 danger grade, no relation to the Tier Grade letters above) answers to a band of Stages, which the Bestiary sets out, so a monster has Pressure like anything else at that height, a Path, and rungs on the three ladders. How it lives sets the Path, and the kind of its Wellspring only leans it: a thing that hunts by weight and hide is a Body monster whatever it was born beside. A famous one can show a Release. None takes a class, since a class is what a soul says about a life it has thought over. A weak reader gets question marks where the numbers should be, usually at the moment it matters most.

The Aether Shell and Aether Class

Where Temperance measures the refinement of the whole Crystal, each layer tempered in its own coin (the Shell packing and holding more raw Charge, the Essence Core, the inner layer of identity, memory and Traits, refining its Essence, the Attraction Layer firming its claim), and Tier Grade measures raw output magnitude, the Aether Class System measures how cleanly the Aether Shell conducts power outward into matter. What it conducts is raw Aether: on the Body Path the Shell holds it unrefined as the Charge, the Wellspring’s lens shapes it there, and it leaves by the same door. It is the Shell’s transparency, and that transparency is how strong a person’s influence on the physical world is: of what the Essence Core intends, the share that actually arrives. The Shell is also the only door into matter that a soul has, so a Spirit worker who wants a stone moved moves it through their own Shell, at their own Aether Class, or leaves it where it is. The Core and the Attraction Layer have ladders of their own, set out below. Beside all three, and no part of any of them, sits the name.

The Class the Soul Offers

At thresholds, starting at Glory, the soul offers its owner a named class that fits how they have lived. The name arrives on the window, and it is usually embarrassing: Wallbreaker, Auditor, Ferryman, Gravedigger’s Friend, Last Man Standing. It gives abilities, usually skills and an easier road to certain kinds of move, and it shapes what grows fastest from there. Some are shared by thousands, every hedge-rank Wallbreaker in the four quarters having roughly the same shoulders, and some have been held by exactly one person in the history of the Archives. The offer comes through the lead layer, so it carries the flavor of the road: the Shell offers names about what a person did to matter, the Core names about what they came to know or carry, the Layer names about what they kept and what they are owed. The Barn was offered Wallbreaker and the Clerk Auditor, and nobody at Harl Cross needed that explained. Surprises are allowed, and the Archives hold a Body-led drayman of forty years’ standing whose soul offered him Witness, to his lasting annoyance.

It evolves by how it is lived. Each time the person’s leading ladder climbs, the class is offered again in a form chosen from what they actually did with the last one (for a Fate-led soul, whose Thread rung may never move, it is whichever of the three ladders stands highest that counts), so two Auditors on the second rung can come out on the third as a Reckoner and a Debt Collector, and a soul with a false account of itself will find the window declining to share that account. The class has no ladder of its own. The ladder below, Dormant through Absolute, belongs to the Shell, and from Glory it sets the yield (η, the share of each spend that arrives as effect) for whatever goes out through the Shell: the name changes, and the share that arrives follows the layer.

The one wild concept stays the person’s own. A class is what the soul says about a life. A concept is what the person decided to do about it, and the class gets no vote on that: a Stage VII Auditor may well fight with soup. Cooks and Measurewrights will both tell you the two sometimes lean together, and the songs mix them up constantly.

Scholars of a certain age still score a Shell by a sum they call the Aether Index, conductivity against purity with something added for how long it holds, and it is a fine way to pass a winter, since nothing on any sheet has ever read it.

The jump from Class V to Class VI produces the most dramatic qualitative shift, from conventional force expression to inverted, negation-based expression. The jump to Class Ω produces output the stat system can no longer meaningfully track.

The Other Two Ladders

The Shell is one layer of three, and the other two are graded the same way, Ø to Ω, read from Glory. Essence Class is the Essence Core and a person’s influence on spiritual reality. Thread Class is the Attraction Layer and how much of Fate they can pull down and hold. VI on each ladder is lateral, an inverted way of holding that power, as it is here.

Each ladder sets the yield of workings through its own layer, and the Thread column is the steep one: its numeral gives a yield two rungs below the same numeral elsewhere, most souls stay Ø for life, and it opens no earlier than Glory unless a bloodline or a blessing gives a grip. On the first two rungs a pull does work, and so little of it arrives that it takes a huge share of the reserve and often fails anyway. A person’s Stage sets the ceiling of the leading ladder and practice moves them up to it. A Walked second Path’s ladder runs a rung behind until mastered, and a Studied ladder runs two behind and stops at II, or at I for Thread. The Thread rung has no Stage climb at all, only a grip plus decades. The Fate Path itself is another matter: its Path Stage, the gates it has opened, climbs with the soul’s Stage like any road’s, and only the rung waits. Classes and the Three Ladders has the one table, and what each rung can actually do.

How a Shell Ascends

A Shell climbs in one of two ways, and which one depends on whether its owner leans on it.

For a Body-led soul the rung climbs by working Charge. The Shell is the layer they use every day, and use is what clears it: packing the Charge, shaping it, driving it out and taking the recoil, year on year, until less of each spend is lost on the way. It is practice, the same practice that moves a thrower up the Core’s ladder, and the Stage only says how high it may go. Conquest has never moved a rung, though conquest has moved a great many Class names.

For anyone else the Shell climbs by refinement, since a Spirit or a Fate soul does not work its Shell hard enough to clear it by use. There are four roads, and a Body fighter may take them too. Each step removes interference: ego noise, denial, unprocessed fracture, clinging to false narratives.

Temperance Refinement gradual clarity through discipline and self-confrontation. The most common and safest path. A Threshold tempers each layer in its own coin, the lead layer first and hardest, and the Shell comes out of the melt able to pack and hold more than it went in with, whichever layer led.

Wellspring Baptism deep immersion in a Wellspring’s Aether, usually at a Core (a site where that Aether pools), forcibly realigning the Shell’s resonance. Potent, perilous, often irreversible.

Aether Forge Rites Mechanica (forbidden working that takes power without lawful consent) and ritual that widen conductivity or restructure the Shell directly, sometimes at the cost of memory, autonomy, or Essence stability.

Higher Resonance Infusion alignment with Archonic principle, Titanic depth, or world-bearing law that rewrites the Shell from above, a blessing being the commonest way to receive one.

Variants and Anomalies

Rot-Born Conductors channel Aether through damaged or corrupted Shells. Output can be immense, but each working corrodes the lattice further. Every stat expression carries a hidden cost in Crystal integrity.

Synthetic Aetherics bear worked or grafted Shells. They may rise quickly through lower Classes yet suffer accelerated decay, instability, or identity drift. Their Class can outpace their Core’s actual Temperance by a dangerous margin.

Split-Shell Entities possess dual polarity, luminous and voidic channels braided through one soul. Capable of rare paradoxical feats but prone to fracture, inversion, or self-annihilation. May register as Luminous and Voidic simultaneously.

The Soul Crystal’s Developmental Tiers

“To refine the soul is to remove everything that was never truly part of the design, and polish has nothing to do with it.”

The Crystal is the soul. It evolves through Developmental Tiers (what the whole stone has grown into, a different thing from a Tier Grade, a Threat Tier or a Tier of Standing) representing the synthesis of all three layers at once. Each Threshold melts the layers down and rebuilds them, the layer the soul leans on first and hardest, and each is tempered in its own coin: the Shell comes out packing and holding more raw Charge, the Core’s Essence comes out more finely refined, and the Layer’s claim comes out firmer, with no Thread rung moved by any of it. These Tiers are correlated with but not identical to Temperance Stages or any of the three class ladders. A Crystal can run ahead of or behind its Shell or Core, and the gap is itself diagnostic. The Tier is what the Crystal as a whole has grown into, read against the Stage, and it lags a bought Stage until Level fills it. A house heir who drank his way to Transcendence has a Stage VIII door on a Clear Crystal, and it is a good part of what the coil means when it reads his seat.

LayerPlaneWhat it doesThe Path that leans on it
Aether ShellThe Physical WorldMeets matter: the blow, the block, the weight, the heatBody

Attraction LayerThe Plane of FateA soul’s own share of fate: its bonds, its oaths, its claims and its odds, and its reach toward what has to be so before anything can happenFate

Every soul has all three layers, the unwoken included, and a Path is the one its Crystal has learned to lean on. Essence is Aether refined in the Crystal and stamped with identity. The Core is the center of identity and the Crystal is the soul, so Essence is the soul’s own substance, and spending it is spending yourself. That is the Spirit Path’s bill, and a vow’s. The Charge a Body fighter holds is Aether that never went through the refining, packed raw in the Shell, so spending it is spending the world, and the body pays for the handling. Thread is held by nobody: a Fate worker pulls it down through the Attraction Layer one working at a time and pays from the soul’s own share, which is part of the reserve. A Fate-led soul refines its Aether into Essence as a Spirit one does, and that Essence is what its pulls and its vows are paid in. The Core still authors all three, since the will, the intent and the lens come from the soul whichever layer does the work.

The names are a jeweler’s, each for what a reader sees in the stone: a seed, a stone full of cloud, one that has taken faces, one seen clean through, one that splits light into color, one with nothing in it under any glass, and one with a star caught inside. Old books, songs and assay slips call the first seven Dormant, Awakened, Harmonic, Resonant, Radiant, Sovereign and the Crystallized Soul. The Measurewrights retired those because Harmonic, Resonant and Radiant are also rungs of the Aether Class above, and Awakened and Sovereign are also Bands II and III, and none of those five has changed its name.

What a tier looks like from outside. The aura is the tier at arm’s length. Its hue is the Wellspring’s and never changes with the climb; its clarity is the tier’s, smoke at Clouded, clearing through Faceted and Clear, throwing its own light at Fire, and bending the daylight around it by Starred. Where it sits is the Path’s: on the skin like heat-haze for the Body, a half-step off the body for the Spirit, and hardly anywhere for Fate. The Color of Essence sets Stage, tier, clarity, aura and body side by side.

Crystal States

Separate from Tier. A Crystal at any Tier can occupy any of these.

Fractured · split by contradiction or strain. The Core leaks, the Shell destabilizes, the Layer weakens. Thought becomes difficult to trust. Can heal through Temperance advancement, alchemical treatment, or the integration of fracture into architectural feature, which is the Dissonance path.

Refined · balanced and coherent. Essence moves in rhythm, Aether conducts cleanly, Attraction Force binds without overreach. The standard healthy state, and rarer among veterans than the recruiting bills on the tavern doors suggest.

Overgrown · swollen with more pressure than integrated. Power spills outward, emotions distort nearby space, the soul wounds its own surroundings. Common at Stage transitions when power accumulates faster than Temperance can shape it. This is what forced advancement produces.

Crystallized, which old slips sometimes enter as a fourth State, is not one. It is the old name of the Starred tier, and self-as-law belongs to that tier alone.

Reserve, Rate, and Yield

Nobody’s reserve refills out of thin air, thin air being precisely the trouble. Every practitioner draws Aether (mana, in the plainer books) in from the ground they stand on, and nobody on any road has ever worked a grain of it they did not first pull inside, whatever the songs about wind-callers say. So where you stand matters: rich ground fills you faster and thin ground starves you. The scholar’s term for the richness is Aetheric Density, and a sect that holds a rich valley will fight for it like a fair crowd holding the good bench. A Wellspring site, a Core in common speech (no relation to the Essence Core inside the Crystal), mostly lies at the heart of a dungeon. A worked Core is a dungeon someone cleared and now holds: it recharges and has keepers, and its keepers can close it. The old counter to anybody with a large reserve and a long fight ahead of them is therefore to take the fight off rich ground and onto salted, bled or thin ground, and let the figures below do the work.

Density fills every reserve, since every road draws Aether. Two other readings of the ground matter to two of the roads, and The Three Grounds has them in full. Echo Depth (graveyards, temples, old homes thick with memory) raises the yield of Spirit workings, and Thread Weight (battlefields, coronation halls, crossroads) raises it for Fate pulls, each by a tenth to the band, exactly as Density does for what leaves by the Shell. The three do not all pull one way. Rich Aetheric ground still crowds a Fate fighter’s Range and the radius of a Domain, the air being too full of everything else, and heavy Thread Weight extends them. Seclusion before a Threshold is kept on the road’s own ground for the same reason: a Body soul on Density, a Spirit soul in Depth, a Fate soul under Weight.

The Three Routes

What happens to the Aether once it is inside depends on the road, and the three roads spend three different things. Fighters call them Aether Manipulation, Essence Manipulation and Thread Manipulation, and for once the common names are exactly right.

RoutePath and layerWhat is workedWhere it is heldThe lost share becomesWhat it costs
ChargeBody · Aether ShellRaw Aether, unrefinedPacked in the ShellHeat, the Shell’s furnace burningThe body: Shell strain, heat, torn fiber, recoil
EssenceSpirit · Essence CoreAether refined in the Crystal and stamped with identity (spiritual energy)The CoreHollowing in the Core, and never heatThe self: hollowness, color, memory
ThreadFate · Attraction LayerA thread pulled down from FateNot held. Pulled per working and paid from the soul’s own share, which is part of the reserve, held as EssenceA knot, which the world collects laterLuck, afterward

The Charge route. Aether is drawn in, packed in the Shell, shaped there by the Wellspring’s lens and driven out through the Shell. It never passes through the refining, so a Body fighter’s fire is Aether burning in the Wellspring’s shape and has none of the fighter in it. The Core decides and the Shell does the work. Every part of the spend that fails to arrive burns off in the Shell’s furnace as heat, which is why the Barn steamed.

The Essence route. Aether is drawn in, refined in the Crystal into Essence, stamped, and spent through the Core. What fails to arrive is paid where it was spent, as a hollowing in the Core, and blood and pallor are only the report of it. A Spirit worker at full stretch does not get hot. The Clerk went round the market for seven minutes with a dry collar. Force thrown this way lands on souls: a soul knocked out of true, dread laid on a mind, a Core wounded. It has no purchase on a jawbone.

The Thread route. No Thread is stored. A Fate worker’s reserve is Essence, refined in the Crystal like a Spirit worker’s, and a thread is pulled down through the Attraction Layer for the one working and paid for out of that reserve, the soul’s own share spent through the Layer. The share that fails to arrive becomes the knot. A vow draws on the same purse: it drains the reserve like any working, goes out through the Layer, pulls nothing and leaves no knot, and it refills the way everything else does. A Measurewright still grumbles about all three names, chiefly at students who hear Aether Manipulation and conclude they can skip the body’s bill.

One reserve, one number. A soul has a single capacity, written in EU on its card and its assay slip, and the guild reckons 1 EU at 1 MJ whichever road it leaves by. What fills that capacity follows the lead Path: a Body-led soul holds most of it as Charge, and a Spirit-led or a Fate-led soul holds it as Essence. Scholars write EU for both, and a Measurewright will tell you at length that the U ought to stand for something else now. Someone who walks Body and Spirit together refines part of what they draw and leaves the rest raw, and the card gives the split as a share (Charge 70% · Essence 30%), set by how they practice. The two do not trade back: Charge once held is never refined into Essence, and Essence never goes back to raw. A sealed Shell holds no Charge at all, which is the plain meaning of unwoken, and waking breaks the seal.

Topping up in a fight. A fight Draft follows the road of whoever drinks it. Core stock feeds the Core with Essence that arrives unstamped, and the crash afterward is the stamping. A Body fighter’s fight Draft is a Salt: raw Aether in a fraction that packs straight into the Shell. It goes down like swallowing a burn, and it leaves a crash of cramps and fever where the Spirit worker gets the stamping. There is no fight Draft for Fate. A Fate fighter’s reserve is Essence, so she drinks Core stock like a thrower and takes the same crash.

EU · Essence UnitsThe raw reserve. Total volume of usable power the soul holds at any given moment, Charge in the Shell and Essence in the Core counted together as one figure, still called Essence Volume in the older books. Set by Level and capped by Stage. A practitioner operating below ten percent EU suffers Starvation: reduced stat expression and Trait flickering on every road, and then the road’s own bill. In Charge Starvation a Body fighter’s Shell starts drawing on the flesh itself, and the skin splits in fine lines as the Charge is taken out of the meat. In Essence Starvation a Spirit worker’s Core frays, names first and then memory, since what is running out is the self. A Fate worker’s reserve is Essence, so hers is Essence Starvation as well, the Core fraying underneath, with the Fate tells laid over it: luck curdling, then her own name sitting wrong in other mouths, and at the very end every knot she owes falling due together.
Flux Density · EU/gThe compression ratio. How much power is packed per unit of Crystal mass. Higher Flux Density means greater output taken from the same reserve. It is the same number on every Path.
AU/s · Aether Units per secondThe rate a practitioner spends at full output. Governed by Ardency Flux, Ardency Alacrity, and Tempering Yield. AU/s = Flux Density × η, and the power that actually lands is AU/s × η. So the yield counts twice, on purpose: once for how much the layer in use can move, and once for what survives the trip.
Yield · ηThe share of a spend that arrives as intended effect; the Measurewrights’ older word is efficiency. The class ladder of the layer in use sets it: Aether Class for a working that leaves by the Shell, Essence Class for one spent through the Core, Thread Class for a pull. What becomes of the rest follows the layer too. An η of 0.50 through the Shell burns half of every expenditure off in the Shell’s furnace as heat, with noise and structural bleed; the same figure through the Core is paid as hollowing, and through the Layer as a knot. This is why a Spirit master’s great Flux, turned on matter through a Shell he never trained, mostly burns off as heat on his own skin. The single most important variable in sustained combat. At full output a higher yield empties the reserve faster, and for the same delivered effect it makes the reserve last longer, which is the whole of what happened to the Barn. Governed by the class ladder of the layer in use (Aether, Essence or Thread Class), Tempering Clarity, and Ardency Compression: the ladder sets the band, and Clarity and Compression set the place inside it. Two more things multiply with it. The ground does, by its band, Density for the Shell, Echo Depth for the Core and Thread Weight for a pull. A Wellspring worked away from its home plane does, lowering the yield again, and the share lost that way is paid in the road’s own coin like any other: heat for the Body, hollowing for the Spirit, a heavier knot for Fate.

None of these four figures appears on a window. They are how assayers and scholars do the sums, and the owner sees only the bar, the percentage and the shake in their own hands.

Yield by Tier of Standing

Which ladder to read. A Tier of Standing is one of the nine guild titles, Initiate to Paragon, as a fighting hall reads them against the Stages. From Glory each class ladder sets the yield of workings through its own layer: Aether Class for what leaves by the Shell, Essence Class for what is authored in the Core, Thread Class for a pull. The table below is two other things: the figure for the unclassed at Stages I to V, and a field average above them, what the fighting halls expect of a stranger at that Stage before anyone has read him. A read rung overrides it, and a registered card’s own figure governs its holder. So a Stage VII Clerk on Essence III runs 0.90 to 0.95 through the Core, whatever the Expert row says, and a Radiant Shell at Stage VIII runs above 1.0 while the Master row reads 0.70 to 0.80, because most Masters are still some way under the ceiling their Stage opened.

Domains

A Domain is a space where a soul’s own law outranks the world’s, and it has a page of its own. The short of it, since people will ask: a soul grows one by living, and the Second Release opens and powers it, by name, called aloud. The Stage grants it and a Grade never does. Dominion (the stat of presence and authority over space) and the clarity of the leading ladder decide how wide it runs and how steady it stands, with the Fate gates on Radius, Sovereignty and Gravity capping it at A-Grade for anyone who has not walked that road. What it rules follows the road, as its page sets out: matter for the Body, meanings for the Spirit, conditions for Fate, and the first flicker of one follows the road in the same way. Rich Aetheric ground crowds a Domain’s radius, and a Fate fighter’s Range with it, and heavy Thread Weight extends both. Anyone at Stage VIII can have one, a bought Stage VIII has the right and none of the finding, and the two-Grade boost cap stands inside it.

StageWhat the Domain is
The Threshold into VIIThe flicker. A one-time burst of the person’s own Trait, usually seconds long, with no Release under it. It follows the road: a Body flicker rules matter, a Spirit one meanings, a Fate one conditions.
VII · RefractionThe seed. With a Second Release the flicker can be called back and held for minutes. It collapses if attention breaks.
VIII · TranscendenceThe first true Domain, held for hours and steady under small disruption.
IX – X · Invocation to RealizationThe Catalyst into IX is a first flicker of a Realm, on the same road as the Domain under it, and the Third Release makes one: a field that can hold other fields. Pacts, artifacts and law set into the terrain.
XI – XII · Dissonance to EmanationIts effects leak with nobody holding them. Traits become local law, and the Domain has turned from something done into something that simply is the case.
