The Tell and the Knot: Pulling a Thread

The Tell and the Knot: Pulling a Thread

Nov 28, 202617 min read

How to see a pull coming, how long you have, what the world takes back afterward, and what the Fate road does to the people who walk it too hard

“Luck is a loan. Ask who holds the note.”

Painted over the door of the Knot-Book Office at Sealhall, where Fate users of the Accord are invited to register what they owe, and mostly decline.

Corvin Ash, called Lucky Corvin by people who had not thought it through, fought the Thornmarket lists for nine years in a coat of green and silver checks, at Fate II, with a grip his grandmother’s blessing had given him and a reputation for winning bouts he ought to have lost. In the semi-final of his last season he was behind on every slate in the yard against a Stage VIII hitter named Brannoch Hale, and the crowd saw him do the thing he did. He went still. His breath stopped on a count of its own. The flags on the list poles, which had been snapping in a river wind, hung straight for the space of a breath, and the dust in the yard settled as if somebody had laid a hand on it. Hale’s pommel nut, which his armorer had been meaning to see to, turned a quarter and let the blade roll in his grip on the downstroke, and Corvin’s point was already inside it.

He bled from one eye, took his purse, and bought the yard a drink. An old Moto woman at the rail, who had watched the whole thing with her tea going cold, was heard to say that she would not ride with that man this week for all the silver in the Concord.

Six days later Corvin Ash rode over the Thornmarket bridge on a horse he had owned for four years, and the girth, which he had checked himself that morning, parted at the buckle. He went over the rail in his green and silver checks and broke his back on the pilings. He lived. He does not fight. The strap had been sound, as far as anyone could tell, and the saddler who made it left town.

That was a knot, and the old woman had seen it tied. This page is about both halves of what she was watching: the tell that comes before a pull, and the debt that comes after.

I. What Is Being Pulled

The Fate Path spends neither of the things the other two roads spend. A Body fighter works raw Aether through the Shell and a Spirit fighter spends Essence through the Core. A Fate fighter who reaches up takes hold of Thread: a strand of what has to be so before a thing can happen, pulled down through the Attraction Layer, which is the soul’s own share of fate. Thread is not held in reserve and cannot be stored. It is pulled for one working, paid for out of the soul’s share, and let go.

Most of what a Fate fighter does pulls nothing. Range, Aura Pressure, presence, a bond, a sworn company fighting better under her eye, a Domain: all of these spend the soul’s own share and reach for nothing above it, and none of them is the subject here. A pull is the rare thing, the reach above, where a strap that was wearing gives on the right step or a cut is made to have always been going to land. The puller’s Fate Mastery measures it, read from Level like any road’s and stopping at II for anyone without a grip, a Work or a blessing. The price table on Classes and the Three Ladders says what share of the reserve it takes. When the halls stopped reading Fate two rungs low, that table was set again against the new rungs, so that no puller’s lean grew a copper cheaper by it, and the two matters below are what the price table leaves out.

II. The Tell

Every pull has a charge, and the charge is a stillness. A Body working announces itself with heat and a Spirit working with color and a cold. A pull announces itself by things lining up.

What anyone can see, trained or not:

  • The puller goes still, in a way that is different from waiting. The eyes stop tracking the fight. The breath is held on a count that is not the count of the exchange.
  • The room settles. Dust lies down. Flags and lamp flames stop moving for a moment. A dog that was barking stops. The air goes slightly too tidy, the way a table looks when someone has squared everything on it, and people at the rope stop chewing without knowing why.
  • Small things agree. Dice in a cup at the back of the hall come up the same. Two strangers say the same word at once. This is the surest sign for the unwoken, and the one they talk about afterward.

What a trained sense feels, on top of that:

  • The odds lean. Anyone with Gnosis Perception worth a slip feels the floor of the likely tilt a degree toward the puller, the way a deck tilts before the boat has visibly moved. It has a direction, and a good reader can say whom it is leaning against.
  • A thread drawn taut. Closer in, and to a finer sense, there is a line under tension between the puller and one particular thing: a strap, a stone, a man’s left foot. Fate users feel it as plainly as a rope across a doorway. A Spirit fighter sees it another way, as a strain on the puller’s Core, since every pull is made by a soul and shows there before it lands.

A pull cannot be hidden entirely, by anyone, at any rung. A master makes the stillness short and the settling small, and a Work of Hataraki makes the grip sure, and the room still squares itself. The Moto teach their children to stand still all the time, which hides the first tell very well and does nothing about the dust.

How long you have

The warning runs as long as the pull takes to set, and the pull takes longer the more it asks.

The pullThe warningWhat it looks like
A lean (a thing already on its way, helped along: the strap that was going, the wet stone)A breathThe stillness, the settling, and then the foot comes down wrong. A fighter who is watching for it has time for one action
A prerequisite removed or assigned (this cut was always going to land, this guard was never going to be there)Several heartbeatsThe puller is visibly elsewhere for the length of a slow exchange. The taut thread can be felt across a yard. A whole ring has time to notice, and a veteran has time to cross it
A field (weather, supply, panic, which company breaks first)Hours, and more often daysNo single stillness. A long run of small things going one side’s way, which a Fate reader on the other side can feel building like weather. The old woman at Akagawa was two days arranging it

The counter

Reach the puller inside the warning. A pull that has not set has moved nothing, and a puller who is hit, shoved, soul-struck or simply made to breathe has to begin again, having already paid. That is the whole counter, and every veteran knows it: watch for the stillness, and when the dust lies down, go. A Body Path fighter closes, since the person at the far end of a breath’s warning is an ordinary body at its Stage. A Spirit fighter strikes the Core the thread is held in, and a wounded soul loses its grip. Another Fate user reads the lean and leans the other way.

The other counters are older than the warning. Be sound: a pull moves only what already has a way of happening, and well-kept gear on a fighter with no bad habits offers nothing to take hold of. Be bound: Persistence and Continuity push back against Dominion’s Fate, and a soul well enough anchored in its own going-on refuses the result. And in war, find the other side’s Fate users before the first spear is leveled, which is why both sides try.

III. The Knot

Every pull leaves a knot, and the world collects.

A thread pulled out of true does not go back by itself. Something that was not going to happen has happened, or has happened early, and the slack has to come from somewhere, so it comes later, from the puller, in kind. A strap snaps on her next week. A friend’s sound plan fails for no reason anyone can find. The letter that mattered goes astray. The scholars’ account is that the share of a pull that never arrives as effect does not burn off as heat, as the Shell’s share does in its furnace, or turn to hollowing, as the Core’s does: it stays on the Layer as a debt, and a debt on the Layer is collected by events. The Measurewrights argue about the wording. Nobody who has pulled a thread argues about the bill.

It is kept like a bill. Serious Fate users keep a knot-book, a ledger with what was pulled, when, how far from likely, and a blank beside it for what came back. The Moto houses keep one for each child of the blood from the first pull, in a steward’s hand, and settle nothing important in a week when a knot is known to be open. A window notes it plainly:

[Pull: landed · Fate II]
[Knot: owed · a lean · due within days]

and strikes the line when it has been paid, usually some hours after the owner has worked that out for herself.

The law of the knot

  • Every pull leaves one. Landed or failed. A pull that fails has still drawn the thread, and the failed ones are the bitterest to pay for.
  • Its size follows the pull’s distance from likely. Helping along a strap that was going leaves a small knot: a stumble, a lost purse, a bad night. Making a sound guard never have been there leaves a large one, the kind that takes a limb, a ship or a friendship. A knot is the same size as the liberty that was taken.
  • It falls due within days for a lean, and commonly inside the week. It falls due within the arc of events for a removed prerequisite: before the war ends, before the season of the feud is out, before the business the pull was part of is finished. A field’s knots come due in a long ugly run, and the people who move fields are usually not on their feet to see it.
  • It lands on the puller or on what is hers. Her own strap, her own footing, her horse, her friend’s plan, her house’s harvest. The world is not particular about which, and the collection has a way of falling on whatever she was relying on that day. This is why a Fate fighter’s friends count the days after a bout.
  • It comes as an ordinary accident. A knot paying out has no charge and no tell. It is a strap, a stair, a wind. The only warning is the ledger.
  • It cannot be pulled away. Leaning on the odds to dodge a knot is a second pull, and leaves a second knot, larger. People have tried to run ahead of their debts this way for several years together. The Archives hold what was left of them under compounding.

What does not leave one

A kept vow leaves no knot. A vow spends the soul’s own share and reaches for nothing above it. Hyeon Do-sun at Kell Harbor pulled no thread and owed the world nothing afterward: he had paid in advance, for most of his life, in the coin the vow named. This is the whole difference between the shallow water of Fate and the deep, and it is why a sworn hitter can lean on her oath every day of her life and never keep a knot-book.

Range, Aura Pressure, presence, bonds and a Domain leave none, for the same reason. A glyph’s term and a seal leave none.

What a Work does and does not do

A Work of Hataraki is an inherited grip on one function of the world. It makes the hand sure. A Work lowers the chance of failure, and it has never lowered the price or the knot. A Moto who pulls pays the same share of the reserve as anyone at her rung, bleeds the same, and owes the same, and the one advantage of the blood is that she more often gets what she paid for. The house’s real art is the bookkeeping: knowing to the day what is owed, arranging to be somewhere dull when it falls due, and never, on any account, pulling twice in a week.

Knot-cutters

There is a way to hand a knot to something else, once.

A knot-cutter is a worked piece, small, usually worn: a ring of twisted iron, a cord with a lead seal, a little hooked blade on a chain that has never been sharpened. Like the other gear of the Fate road (oath-rings, luck-tokens) a worked one lends a real margin to Dominion and to Resilience’s Continuity while it is whole. Its particular use is that it can take one knot’s debt onto itself. When the collection comes, it comes for the piece: the ring cracks across, the cord rots through in a night, the little blade is found in two halves in a sound sheath. It breaks when used, and it is used once.

They are made by Knotwrights, the fourth rung of Fate, and by nobody below, each takes its maker weeks in bed, and they are priced accordingly: by private treaty, in the range of a good warhorse for one that will carry a lean and a small estate for one rated to a removed prerequisite. None has ever been made that would carry a field. A cutter takes the debt of a knot no larger than it was made for, and a knot too big for it breaks the cutter and comes on regardless, which the buyer discovers on the day. Half of those sold are fakes, and the fakes are very good until the week they are needed.

The Moto do not wear them. Asked why, a steward of the Kōkan Line is supposed to have said that a house which pays its debts through a servant forgets what it owes.

IV. Flagging on the Fate Road

Every practitioner shows the shared tells as the reserve falls: at half the breath shortens and the aura dims, at a quarter comes tremor and a thinning color, at a tenth the aura flickers and the Trait stutters. A Fate fighter shows those faintly, because her aura barely shows to begin with, and it is easy to think she is fresh. Look at what is around her.

ReserveWhat a Fate fighter shows, on top of the shared tells
HalfSmall luck curdles nearby. Milk turns in the next room, a die cracks, a bootlace goes. Nothing anyone would remark on alone
A quarterThings start going wrong around her now, and visibly: a bench leg gives under a bystander, a lamp chain parts, her own second drops the water. Sensible people step back from the rope
A tenthHer own name sits wrong in other mouths. The herald stumbles on it, her friends reach for it and get a cousin’s, and a clerk writing it down has to be told twice. The Layer is where a name is held, and it is running thin
MaxedEvery knot she owes comes due at once. Whatever is open on the ledger, small or large, pays out in the same hour, on her and on whatever she was leaning on

The last row is what happened under the plain banner at Akagawa. The old woman had eleven years of careful setting behind that day, and she had been paying the small knots as she went, the way the house teaches. She emptied herself moving the field. Everything still owed fell due between noon and dusk, and four of her people carried home a woman who did not walk again or know her own daughters.

So a watcher can tell the three roads apart by how a fighter tires. A flagging Body fighter runs hot: heat-haze on the skin, steam off the shoulders and cramps, the skin splitting in fine lines, and at the end heat stroke. A flagging Spirit fighter goes cold and pale: the colors near her flatten, a word slips, then a name, and at the end a memory is gone for good (Soul Wounds and the Menders has that table). A flagging Fate fighter looks perfectly well, and you should move your chair.

V. The Second Case

Ser Ysolde Marrin of the Hundred Crowns, Fate III, a Spinner, with a grip a shrine’s blessing gave her in her girlhood and no house behind it, removed one prerequisite in her life. At the siege of Low Pennycrown she stood on a cart in a scarlet surcoat for the length of eleven heartbeats, in full view of both armies, with every banner on the field hanging straight, and made it so that the postern had never been barred that night. Three crossbowmen on the wall felt the air go tidy and loosed at her, and all three were a heartbeat late. The town fell by morning. She lost the sight of her left eye and most of a year.

Her knot did not come that week or that month. Her captain kept the ledger open and the company walked carefully around her until the snow. It came at the peace, in the spring, at the very end of the business the pull had been part of: the treaty that would have given her the town she had opened was sealed by a clerk who, for no reason he could afterward give, copied the wrong name into the grant. The error was sound in law. The town went to a man she despised, and she spent what was left of her fortune failing to overturn it.

She is said to count it cheap. A prerequisite removed has taken lives, and hers took a town.

Cost. The share of the reserve on the price table, the blood, and the knot: small and soon for a lean, large and later for a prerequisite, and all of them at once for anyone who empties herself.

Counter. Watch for the stillness and reach the puller before it sets. Be sound, be bound, or bring a Fate reader of your own. And if none of that is to hand, wait a week and stand well back.
