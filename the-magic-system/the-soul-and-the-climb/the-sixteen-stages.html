The Sixteen Stages

The Sixteen Stages

Nov 17, 2026132 min read

What Temperance (the climb of a soul from Stage to Stage) Does to a Soul, Stage by Stage, Told Plainly

Compiled for the fighting halls of the Guild Accord by the Archives, following the Prime Instructors’ complaint that the account of the Stages in the Codex (the Accord’s master register of laws and figures) runs to eleven lines and has never once been sufficient to teach from.

It began with a pie. On the second day of the harvest fair at Pollard’s Cross, a twelve-year-old apprentice of the Copper Heron school, Stage II and in love with the pie-seller’s daughter, wanted a mutton pie hot and got it hot, and the pie went off like a dropped coal across the blue sash of a Stage III journeyman of the Tannery Hounds. The journeyman answered with the first technique he had ever built by himself. His sister, Stage IV, came through the awning. By noon a Stage VI instructor of the Herons had arrived, with the small flames on every stall leaning toward him as he crossed the square, and the crowd, which had been eating, went on eating and began to take bets. By mid-afternoon a Hounds elder at Stage VIII had opened a Domain the size of the horse market, the horses inside it were picking their way very carefully across a floor that had agreed to be somewhere else, and a man was charging a copper a head to stand on his wagon. The walls went, then Cooper Street, then the bend of the river.

At sundown a woman named Aldith Fenn, Stage X, who had been fishing four miles upstream, walked down in her hip boots, wet to the waist, and asked them to stop. They stopped. Three hundred and six people were dead, the pie-seller among them, and the ground at Pollard’s Cross has not frozen in the two centuries since.

Every child in the four quarters can skip rope to the names:

Murmur, Well, and up you Rise,
Bloom and Splinter, Glory, Eyes,
Cross the water, Call, and Stay,
Crack, and Shine, and Rule the Day,
Top of the world and one more after,
And one more yet, and skip it faster.

Those are the Sixteen, in order, and the Archives can find no fault with the rhyme. Every rung of it is famous, and every rung of it has a body count the children know by heart.

How This Is Written

“Temperance is a confession of how much you have survived, and nobody has ever confessed for free.”

The Codex records each Stage with exacting precision: its Tier of Standing (the title a fighting guild’s token carries for it; in a merchant’s or a smith’s guild the same nine words grade trust in that trade, and a Grandmaster merchant may not be able to throw a punch), its Grade ceiling (the highest letter grade, F to EX+, its stats may reach), its stat ceiling, and the Catalyst Condition (the life event a soul passes through to cross, or the Draft or blessing that stands in for it) the Crystal (the Soul Crystal, which is the soul itself, melted down and rebuilt layer by layer at each crossing) requires before it will authorize the transition. Those figures are correct, they are reproduced here without alteration, and they have never taught anybody anything.

What follows sits underneath them. Each Stage is given in the same order. The ledger line is the Codex’s own figure, with the Crystal’s tier set beside it. Under it stands the Catalyst, first as the Codex has it, which is the one demand the Crystal makes of every soul at that floor, and then in its three forms, because a hitter, a thrower and a puller are asked the same question and no two of them are asked it in the same place. The one they tell comes next, because no minstrel was ever paid for a ledger line and nobody ever feared a Stage they had only read about. Then what the Stage is from inside the person it is happening to, with a line for each road; what it is on each Path, since a hitter, a thrower and a puller standing on one rung are holding three different things; what it looks like, to the household or the hall or the field; what it costs to spend at that height; what the common people call it, which the Archives has three times recommended cutting and which predicts better than half the Index; then the crossing, lived, which is how the moment of passing shows on each road to the people standing nearby; and last bought versus lived, a line or two on what the purse leaves out. The whole climb is written by Path, since the Archives were told, correctly, that an account of one generic soul going up a stair describes nobody who ever went up it. The persons in the stories are mostly long dead and the stories have been improved; the Archives vouches for the corpses.

Two facts govern everything below and neither is negotiable.

The first is that a Threshold (the crossing from one Stage to the next) requires both a quantitative floor and a Catalyst Condition, and both can be bought. Every Catalyst named below is the lived road. A Draft or a blessing will stand in for any of them.

The second is that a Threshold is a structural reorganization, and whatever is in the Crystal at the moment of reorganization is built into what comes out.

A soul is therefore assembled out of what happened to it and what it could afford, the rich get to choose the order, and every hall’s certification is an instrument for reading the result.

The Levels on each ledger line below are where that Stage’s practitioners usually sit, not where they must; only Part One’s Band (a block of a hundred Levels) gates are hard: Stage IV before Level 100, VII before 200, X before 300, XII before 400. Level itself moves by danger overcome, whichever road the Stage came by.

The Climb by Path

“Same stair, same step, and three different things waiting on it.”

A Stage belongs to the soul, and every soul climbs the same sixteen over the same floors. What the Crystal asks at each floor is one thing, and the Codex’s Catalyst line states it. Where it asks is another matter. A crossing melts the Crystal down and rebuilds it layer by layer, the layer a soul leans on is rebuilt first and hardest, and so the demand arrives in that layer’s terms.

  • A Body soul meets it in the flesh and in matter: a load survived that should have killed, a material understood to the grain, a body broken and rebuilt.
  • A Spirit soul meets it in a soul or a meaning: a grief borne, a truth faced, a self that can no longer be held as it was.
  • A Fate soul meets it in a bond, an oath or the odds: a vow kept at ruinous cost, a chance refused, a debt paid in full.

Each Stage below gives the shared demand and then the three trials. Five things hold for all sixteen.

The floor is shared. The quantitative floor, the Grade cap, the stat ceiling and the Band gates are the same on every road. Only the trial differs, and the Crystal counts the trial that lands in the layer it leans on. The halls keep long lists of hitters who waited years for a grief to do what only a load could.

Two roads, one trial. A soul walking two Paths meets the Catalyst of its lead Path, the one that holds the larger share of its practice. The second road is asked for no trial of its own. It follows, and its gates open a Stage late until it is mastered (a third road’s open two Stages late), as The Three Paths sets out.

Fate climbs like anyone. A Fate soul crosses the same floors by Catalysts made of bonds, oaths and odds, and not one of them is a pull. A vow kept spends the soul’s own share, draws no thread and leaves no knot. No Catalyst on this page moves Thread Class by a rung. Only a grip and decades do that, and a Fate Master at Stage VIII reads Ø as comfortably as a Fate child at Stage I.

The crossing shows by road. A lived breakthrough lights the valley, and the road decides what kind of light. Every lived crossing passes the four stages of the Great Work in its own road’s terms, nigredo, albedo, citrinitas and rubedo: the lead layer broken down, purified, woken and fixed, in the flesh for a Body soul, in a soul or a meaning for a Spirit one, and in a bond, an oath or the odds for Fate. Body’s comes out through matter: heat, the ground answering, the body remade in front of people. Spirit’s comes out through souls and meaning: a room gone quiet, colors and memories shifting in everyone near. Fate’s comes out through the odds and the world settling: luck turning across the valley, oaths felt to tighten, things that were going to happen not happening. It scales with the Stage, from a kitchen at Murmuring to a kingdom at Principality. A bought breakthrough is a Draft in a quiet room on every road, and the three quiet rooms look exactly alike.

Seclusion is taken on the road’s own ground. A soul that goes into a guarded seclusion to meet its Catalyst goes where its road draws cheapest: a Body soul onto rich Aetheric ground, a Well or a deep mine; a Spirit soul down into Echo Depth, an old temple or a graveyard thick with the remembered; a Fate soul onto Thread Weight, a battlefield or a crossroads where great things were decided. The wrong ground does not stop a crossing. It makes the months longer and the slag worse, and the halls that rent out seclusion cells price them by road. See The Three Grounds.

The Bought Stage

A breakthrough Draft or a blessing delivers the Catalyst, the Crystal goes to the melt, and the soul comes out on the next rung with the stat ceiling, the Band gate and the color to prove it. A house child may be four Stages up before the first real fight. The trade and its law are on The Ladder and the Draft, and every lord in the four quarters lies awake over the dungeon luck on the other side of it.

What a bought Stage lacks is exact, and the halls can list it from across a yard:

  • Every Release. A First is possible from IV, a Second from VII, a Third from IX, and each must be found and mastered by fighting. A rich Stage IX may have none, while a poor fighter at the same Stage has all three.
  • The Domain. Anyone at Stage VIII can have one. Somebody still has to find theirs.
  • The Level. The new ceiling sits empty until danger overcome fills it, and a thin Level at a high Stage is the commonest cause of death among heirs.
  • The rung. A Stage sets how high a ladder may go, and practice does the climbing. A bought Stage opens the ceiling and leaves the rung where it was, which is how a rich Stage VIII can stand under a roof marked Radiant and read Aether I.
  • The Crystal’s tier. The tier is what the Crystal has grown into, and it lags a bought Stage until Level fills it.
  • The Path gates. The Crystal goes on reading what the soul actually does.
  • Techniques and skills. Those rise by doing them.
  • Whatever the Catalyst would have done. A bought Flourishing has no Spirit Axis under it. A bought Glory never made the decision. A bought Transcendence carries the wound exactly as it was, now at S-Grade.
  • The valley. A lived breakthrough lights the valley, and people for miles can name the night. A bought one is a Draft in a quiet room.

Sects teach what money cannot buy: Releases, Domains, techniques, skills and the rites for their Wellspring, with real fights, a crew and a name thrown in, and they know precisely what they are selling when a duke’s daughter turns up at the gate at Stage VII asking to be hit.

The Crystal tier is what the Crystal as a whole has grown into, named in the jewelers’ words for a stone: a Seed before waking, then Clouded, Faceted, Clear, Fire, Flawless, Starred and Absolute. It is read by Stage, and it lags a bought Stage until Level fills it. Books older than the present Bench call the same tiers Dormant, Awakened, Harmonic, Resonant, Radiant, Sovereign and Crystallized Soul. The Measurewrights renamed them because three of those words were also rungs on the Shell’s ladder, and an assay slip that says Resonant twice and means two different things is a lawsuit.

The two ceiling columns are the highest rung a soul’s leading ladder may reach at that Stage, Aether Class for the Body Path and Essence Class for the Spirit, copied from the one table on Classes and the Three Ladders, which governs. A ceiling is a roof. Practice moves a soul up to it, and most people at any Stage are standing a rung or two under theirs. The sixth rung of each ladder is lateral and stands beside the others from Refraction up.

Thread has no Stage climb. The Fate Path’s ladder moves by a grip, from blood or a blessing, plus decades of use. Study takes anyone to the first rung and no further, kept vows take a sworn soul to the first or second, and most souls read Ø at Stage I and at Stage XII alike. A Fate fighter’s everyday weapons, Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds, need no rung at all, and those do grow with the Stage.

Inner Refinement: Band I · Mortal Foundation

Stages I through IV. Better than nine souls in ten will live and die inside this Band. Everything the world thinks it knows about magic, it knows from watching people who never left it, and there are a great many of them to watch: every market has its hedge-worker and every town its sect, with its outer and inner disciples, the core disciple nobody can stand, and an elder who will tell you about it.

What changes in this Band. The Crystal goes from Seed to Clouded at the waking and stays Clouded for all four Stages. No ladder is read here: there is no Aether Class, no Essence Class and no Class name, and the share of a spend that arrives is the Stage’s own figure, climbing from about three parts in ten at Murmuring to not quite half at Flourishing. On the Body road matter answers to blows and the coat and to nothing else. On the Spirit road a line can be thrown at a soul and little more. On the Fate road there is Pressure, presence and the first bond. The Path becomes legible at Ascension, and the First Release can be found at Flourishing. Auras in this Band are clouded and smoky and cling to the hands.

I · Murmuring

Tier 1 · Initiate · Grade E · Ceiling 50 · Levels 1 to 25 · Clouded Crystal

Catalyst · none is asked. The Crystal has opened, in its own season or under a Waking Draft

No trial is set and no Path is read, so what follows are occasions and not Catalysts: the kind of day on which each layer tends to open first.

  • Body. A Shell that is going to lead tends to open on something that happened to the body: a fever broken, a fall onto river ice, the first day of real work with a flail.
  • Spirit. A Core that is going to lead tends to open on a feeling too large for the child holding it: a temper at a naming feast, a first funeral, a fright in the dark.
  • Fate. A Layer that is going to lead tends to open on a near thing: the cart that missed, the promise a small child made in earnest and kept. It opens the Crystal and nothing more. No thread is touched, and the Thread rung stays wherever blood or a blessing put it.

The one they tell · The dogs of Thorn Mill went quiet on a market day, all twelve of them, in the middle of a fight over a pig’s ear. By the week’s end the whole parish was laying coin on which child it was, and the smart money was on the miller’s boy, Little Jem Sorrel, seven years old, who lost his temper at his sister’s naming feast and sent every cup on a forty-foot table into a shiver, until the ale crept over the rims and the priest stopped with his mouth open. That was all it did. His mother sat up with him for a year afterward, and three generations of instructors have quoted what she told the parish: a house with a Murmuring child in it is a house that has begun keeping a wolf, and the wolf is very small.

From inside · A hum under the sternum that answers mood and answers nothing else. Wake angry and the water in the cup by the bed is trembling before you have understood why you are angry. Want something badly and feel a pressure behind the eyes that goes nowhere and does nothing and does not stop. There is no direction in it. There is no volume to adjust. It is weather happening inside a body that has not been told there is now weather.

Children in Murmuring feel no fear of it, which adults consistently fail to predict, and a good deal of irritation. It is a noise, and they were already living in a body full of noises.

What opening does not do is hand the child anything new. Traits (standing laws a soul has survived into being) sit in the material of a soul from the beginning and run in people whose Crystal never opens at all. Murmuring arms what was already there. The quiet child who never took a fever had the Trait at four; at eleven the Crystal simply gives it a conductor and a bill.

From inside each road, so far as a lean can be called a road:

  • Body. The hum sits in the hands and the teeth, and the child is always a little too warm.
  • Spirit. The hum is other people. The mood of a room reaches the child before the room does.
  • Fate. There is hardly a hum at all, only a sense, now and then, that something has just been decided.

On each Path · No Path is read at Murmuring. The Crystal recognizes a road by years of use, and a Murmuring child has used nothing. What shows is a lean, the same grain the unwoken carry. All three ladders are unread, and what gets out of the child does so at the Stage’s own figure, about three parts in ten.

  • Body. The seal on the Shell has broken and the first Charge sits in it, a cupful of raw Aether (mana, in plain words) that nobody packed on purpose. Matter does nothing at the child’s word. It shivers: cups, water, a loose shutter. A lean toward this road shows as a child who is warm to hold and hard to knock over.
  • Spirit. The Core was the first layer to wake, and it refines a trickle of Essence (spiritual energy) without being asked. Souls do nothing for the child either, though they notice: the dogs, a grandmother, a baby that stops crying. A lean here shows as a child who knows who is in the next room.
  • Fate. Nothing, for nearly everyone, at this Stage and at every Stage after it. A child born to a gripping bloodline, or blessed, may already read Thread I, Touched, and know the strap is about to go. No other child will, and no amount of Murmuring changes it.

What it looks like · Almost nothing, and the almost is what matters. A quality of stillness in a room that people notice and do not name. Animals get it first. Dogs go quiet around a Murmuring child a full season before the parents work out what is happening, and every farmer in the four quarters knows to watch the dogs and not one of them would call it a diagnostic.

To a trained eye the aura is near-black with the hue already in it, a dark smear that is gone before it travels a handspan, and far too thin to sit differently on one road or another. The Crystal has gone from Seed to Clouded, the lapidary’s word for a stone you cannot yet see into: the smallest change of color on the whole ladder and the largest change of kind. The body is a child’s body. The Body-leaning ones run a little warm, the Spirit-leaning ones go very still when watched, and around the rare gripped child dropped things tend to land the right way up.

What it costs · Very little, because there is very little to spend. A Murmuring reserve is emptied by one bad temper, and the bill is a child’s bill: a flush, a sweat and a night’s fever where the lean is to the Body, and a flat gray afternoon with a word that will not come where it is to the Spirit. Nobody at this Stage pulls a thread, so nobody owes a knot. The three exhaustions set out on The Three Paths (Body runs hot and cracks, Spirit runs hollow, Fate runs unlucky) are here in miniature, and every mother of a Murmuring child has nursed one of the first two without knowing its name.

What the folk call it · Carrying a bee. A boy with a bee in him. The remedy is iron in the bed, on the reasoning that the iron takes the hum away from the child, and the reasoning is wrong in every particular and correct in its outcome, because iron does conduct and an ungoverned Crystal does shed into the nearest better conductor. A Murmuring child sleeps better with a horseshoe under the mattress. The Accord sells the guild-milled version of this as Sour Iron Filings, at a markup, to the people who invented it.

The crossing, lived · Nothing lights at Murmuring. The scale is a kitchen, and the signs are the ones the dogs already gave.

  • Body. The room warms by a degree, the cups shiver on the shelf, and the cat leaves the hearth.
  • Spirit. The house goes quiet all at once. The baby stops crying, and two people at the table find they were thinking of the same dead aunt.
  • Fate. For a day everything dropped in that house lands the right way up, and nobody remarks on it until later.

Bought versus lived · A Crystal that opens by itself does so in its own season, and the dogs give a season’s warning. A Waking Draft opens it on the day the family chooses, costs more than the house, and often kills or warps the child who drinks it. The ones it spares have the hum and none of the weather that would have taught them what it answers to.

II · Welling

Tier 2 · Apprentice · Grade D · Ceiling 100 · Levels 26 to 50 · Clouded Crystal

Catalyst · first genuine contact with a Wellspring’s raw Aether under personal pressure (a Wellspring being one of the Sixty, the governing idea that changes how a soul uses its power, pooling at sites common speech calls Cores, most of them at the heart of a dungeon): touched and survived, where watching and instruction count for nothing

The touch is the same for everyone, since every road draws Aether. The pressure is what differs.

  • Body. The pressure is on the flesh. The child is in the Core’s heat, cold or water past what a body that size should stand, and the raw Aether comes in through the skin and stays in the Shell because there was nowhere else for it to go. Most carry the scar.
  • Spirit. The pressure is on the self. The child touches the Wellspring while holding something too heavy to hold, a death in the house, a shame, a terror, and the Core takes the Aether in and stamps it with that. They carry no scar, and they can tell you the hour.
  • Fate. The pressure is a stake. Something rides on the touch: a dare taken at bad odds, a promise to go down to the Core and come back, a brother’s life. The Aether is touched as anyone’s is, and what the Layer keeps is that the odds were met. Nothing is pulled, and Thread does not move.

The one they tell · Annis Cobb of Thatcher’s Row wanted the bread oven hotter. She was eleven, it was the coldest morning of that winter, and the week before, down at the old Core in Cobble Fen, she had touched something she had told nobody about. The oven went, then the bakehouse, then nineteen roofs in a long sheet of orange that brought the whole valley out to look. Four people died in it, among them the baker, the kindest man on the Row. By the next market a sect recruiter in a green coat had paid her parents forty silver marks and driven her off in a cart, and the town was selling views of the scorch at a copper a head before she was out of sight.

From inside · It spills. Direction arrives before volume does, so the child now knows what they are reaching for and has no capacity whatever to reach for a measured amount of it. They want the cup and the cup goes over. They want the fire hotter and the linen scorches. The Shell (the Aether Shell, the soul’s outer layer that turns power into force) is forming and it is porous, and living inside a porous Shell at eleven years old is the experience of being blamed daily for things that are entirely your doing and entirely not your fault.

The Catalyst is worth sitting with. Instruction will not do it and neither will watching: a Wellspring’s Aether has to be touched, under pressure, and survived. Which means the lived gate into Welling is an accident that went badly and did not go worst. Most practitioners in the four quarters can name the day. A good number of them have a scar from it. A house child takes the same touch off a silver spoon, in a warm room with a physician standing by, and arrives at Stage II with no scar and no story, which the other children in the yard will shortly correct.

From inside each road:

  • Body. It spills as heat and force out of the hands, and the palms are never quite healed.
  • Spirit. It spills as feeling, and the child learns that the quarrel downstairs is somehow their doing.
  • Fate. It spills as wanting. The child wishes for a thing a little too hard and people give way without being asked, by Pressure and never by thread, and resent it afterward.

On each Path · Still unread on every ladder, with about a third of each spend arriving. The lean hardens here, because spilling has a direction.

  • Body. The Shell is forming and porous, and the Charge in it slops. Matter now does one thing for them, badly: it takes heat, force or motion from their hands in an amount they did not choose. The oven goes. A Body-leaning child has blows and the coat (the thin skin of their own aura over the body) and no more, and neither holds for long.
  • Spirit. The Core refines more than it can keep, and what spills is Essence, stamped and unaimed. It lands on souls. Around a Spirit-leaning child it is the household that sours, with every temper in it a little shorter and the baby awake all night. Nothing can yet be thrown on purpose.
  • Fate. Nothing yet, for most. The first Fate gate stands here all the same: Attunement will not pass D unless the Fate Path has been shown by this Stage, so a child who is going to walk that road has usually begun keeping small promises with unusual seriousness. A blooded or blessed child reads what they read before. The Stage has moved nothing.

What it looks like · This is the year the household finds out. Broken crockery, soured milk, a scorch on a table nobody set a pot down on. Welling is when families discover what they have and decide what to do about it, and it is the year most children are sent away, and it is the year most accidents happen, in that order and for that reason.

The aura is dark char with the hue stirring under it like dye in dirty water, and for the first time it shows outside the body, clinging to the hands during a long working. On a Body-leaning child it sits on the skin like heat over a road and stays a moment on whatever they have put down. On a Spirit-leaning child it stands a finger’s width off the hands, and a careful eye will see the shadow of those hands fall slightly wrong. The Crystal is Clouded still.

What it costs · The spill is the cost. A Welling child spends without meaning to and so runs dry daily: scalded palms, a hot dry skin and cramps in the calves by evening on the Body side, and on the Spirit side a cold in the chest, a dull week and a playmate’s name gone until morning. A reserve this small refills overnight on decent ground, and most of the damage is to the crockery.

What the folk call it · The spilling year. The remedy is water. The child carries full buckets, all day, up the hill and back down, and is beaten for what they slop over the rim. It looks like cruelty and half the time it is cruelty and it is also, on the instrument, the correct training, because measuring out with the body teaches the Shell to measure out and no better method has ever been found. Every Prime Instructor in the Accord has quietly kept the practice. The published curriculum calls it graduated load discipline and does not mention the hill.

Trade note · The guild ration begins here, with Metering Tonic, which halves the spillage and produces a practitioner who measures out worse afterward than one who was never given it. The finding is fourteen years old. The ration has not changed.

The crossing, lived · The scale is a lane, and it is over in a breath.

  • Body. A flash of heat off the child and a ring of ground the size of a cart left scorched, frosted or soaked, in the Wellspring’s own manner, with steam standing where there was no water.
  • Spirit. Everyone in the lane feels one child’s fright in their own chest for the space of a breath, and the colors of the washing on the lines look wrong until evening.
  • Fate. A day of small strange luck along the lane: a lost ring found, every hen laying, the dice at the alehouse coming up alike. Nothing was pulled. The soul’s own share has grown, and the lane is feeling it settle.

Bought versus lived · The silver spoon delivers the touch and none of the pressure. A bought Welling has the direction and has never once been afraid of it, and the hill and the buckets are still owed.

III · Ascension

Tier 3 · Journeyman · Grade C · Ceiling 175 · Levels 51 to 75 · Clouded Crystal

Catalyst · first technique developed independently from instruction, built from the practitioner’s own understanding of their Essence (the soul’s own stuff of power) rather than received doctrine

The Codex says Essence, and means whatever the lead layer works. The thing built is the practitioner’s own on every road, and what it is built out of differs.

  • Body. Built from the Charge and one material known to the grain: a rope, a pestle, the hinge of the builder’s own shoulder. The understanding is of what that one thing does under load, learned by the hands, and the technique is that understanding driven out through the Shell.
  • Spirit. Built from one truth about a soul, worked out alone and usually unwelcome: why the big boy flinches, what the builder is in fact afraid of. The technique is that truth sent down a line.
  • Fate. Built from a bond, a promise or a reading of the odds: the stare that makes a bigger boy step back, the word that holds because it has never yet been broken. It is made of Range, Pressure and presence. It pulls nothing, and Thread stays at whatever blood has given.

The one they tell · The first thing anybody builds is usually stupid, and Kim Dal-rae’s was the stupidest in the Mahuo country. She was fifteen, she pounded rice for a living, and what she worked out by herself over one bad winter was that a thing she threw would go straight back to wherever it had last been at rest. She called it Jeolgu Gwihwan, Pestle Goes Home, and the meaning got around fast on the day the tax-farmer’s man walked out of her yard with a sack he had taken off her aunt, because the pestle she threw at him went home through the sack and through the man and through the gatepost, in that order. She did it again to check. Then she sat down, and then she ran, and the ninety-mark bounty three counties posted on her ran for forty years. She outlived it and had it framed.

From inside · The first thing you made yourself, working.

Everything until this moment was received. Somebody showed you and you copied it and it held, and you were proud of it, and it was never yours. Then one day you build a thing out of your own understanding of what your Essence actually does, and it holds, and the whole of Stage III is the difference between those two experiences. There is nothing else in it.

Some people weep. Most do not. The recorded pattern across four centuries of instructor notes is that the practitioner does the thing, and then does it again to check, and then sits down. Drink past this rung and you still owe it, and you will build your first real technique later on, at a higher Stage, in front of people who have stopped being polite.

From inside each road:

  • Body. The thing built lives in the hands, and the body knows it worked before the mind does.
  • Spirit. The thing built is something understood, and it cannot afterward be un-understood.
  • Fate. The thing built is something other people now believe about you, and you feel it hold when you lean on it.

On each Path · The Path becomes legible here, and the first gates close behind it: Fortitude will not pass C unless the Body Path has been shown by this Stage, nor Alacrity without the Spirit. The ladders stay unread, and four parts in ten arrive. The Codex says Essence in its Catalyst line, and for a hitter the understanding in question is of the Charge.

  • Body. The first technique is nearly always a blow: Charge shaped in the Shell by whatever lens the soul has touched, and driven out through a hand, a foot or a pestle. The Body Path’s bonus to Strike Force, Durability and Lifting Strength begins, at about five parts in a hundred. Matter still obeys nothing but impact. Blows and the coat.
  • Spirit. The first technique is a line: Essence sent out through the Core that holds together across a few paces and lands on a soul. It knocks a man’s nerve out of true or lays a moment’s dread on him, and leaves his coat unmarked. It moves no pestle. The Shell is the only door into matter, and theirs is a Stage III Shell like anybody’s.
  • Fate. A Fate Path Journeyman has no pull and needs none. Their first technique is made of Range, Aura Pressure, presence or a bond: the stare that makes a bigger boy step back, the promise that holds. They read Thread Ø, as they most likely will for life.

What it looks like · The person becomes legible. Before Ascension you could say a child had something. After it, you can say what they are, and the recruiters can price them, and the family can plan, and the Path (the route, body, spirit or fate, the Crystal habitually works through) is visible to anyone who knows how to look. This is the point at which a life stops being a question and becomes a known quantity, and there are people who never forgive it.

The aura is smoke now, tinted plainly with the lens’s color, a brown smoke over a fire school and a blue one over a storm hall, and it is the first thing about them a stranger can use. A Body Journeyman’s smoke hugs the forearms and trails off the weapon. A Spirit Journeyman’s hangs half a step off the body. A Fate Journeyman’s can hardly be seen, and the dust on the practice floor lies oddly flat around their feet. The Crystal is Clouded, with the stamp of its owner showing in it for the first time.

What it costs · This is the first Stage with a reserve worth emptying, and so the first at which the roads tire differently enough to read. At half a hitter is flushed and the haze thickens on the skin, at a quarter the calves cramp, and at a tenth a Journeyman’s Shell splits the knuckles and little else. A thrower at half has gone a shade gray, at a quarter says the wrong word, and at a tenth forgets the name of the drill. A Fate Journeyman run dry has spent nothing but their own share, owes no knot, and has a bad week of small luck. Good instructors take all three to a tenth on purpose, once, so that they will know it when it comes.

What the folk call it · Coming into the road. The first true year. A family that can afford it holds a feast, because the child’s future has just arrived and can be celebrated. A family that cannot afford it does not hold one, and the child notices, and a surprising number of Accord operatives have that particular non-event somewhere in their fracture record. The Archives has never studied this. Three Prime Instructors have asked it to.

The crossing, lived · The scale is a practice yard, and the instructors can tell the road from the far wall.

  • Body. The Charge sets in the Shell with a wash of heat across the yard, the packed earth takes the print of their feet and keeps it, and they stand up visibly thicker through the shoulders than they sat down.
  • Spirit. The yard falls silent in the middle of a sentence, and everyone watching remembers, sharply and for no reason, the first thing they themselves were ever proud of.
  • Fate. The dust on the yard lies flat, a quarrel that had been brewing all week between two other students goes out of both of them, and a rope that was about to part holds another year.

Bought versus lived · A purse can supply the Catalyst and cannot supply the technique. A bought Ascension has the Grade and the smoke and has never built anything, and that debt is collected later, at a higher Stage, in front of people.

IV · Flourishing

Tier 4 · Adept · Grade B · Ceiling 275 · Levels 76 to 100 · Clouded Crystal

Catalyst · formation of the first genuine Spirit Axis, a bond with another soul that carries mechanical weight in the Harmonics (the stats for pulling in power and holding it in tune)

An Axis is an Axis on every road, and the Harmonics weigh it the same. How two souls come to hold each other’s load is the road’s own business.

  • Body. The bond is made by bearing load together: a shield wall of two, a man carried out on the back, a wound taken that was meant for the other. The Shell learns the other’s weight and the Axis forms around it. Hob Tarrant and Mags Plowright, below, are the standing case.
  • Spirit. The bond is made by being known. One soul lets another see what it is, the grief and the shame included, and is not put down for it, or sits with another’s grief until part of it is their own. The Core takes the second weight directly.
  • Fate. The bond is made by a word given: an oath sworn to one person, in Witness Wax or without it, and kept the first time keeping it costs something real. This is the road’s home ground, and the Layer needs no telling what a bond is. It is a vow, so it spends the soul’s own share, pulls no thread, leaves no knot and moves no rung.

The one they tell · Hob Tarrant and Mags Plowright were witnessed to each other in Witness Wax at Stoneybridge, a drover and a camp cook, and the pair of them held the Ford of Ell against a sixty-man raid with a goad, a cleaver and a great deal of swearing, their two charcoal auras, his with a rumor of rust in it and hers of amber, running together into a single roaring hoop that the minstrels got almost right. Then a fever took her, the plain kind. Hob lived eighteen more years at full power, and for all of them a bright thread of rust leaned off his left shoulder, always north, always toward one hill in the Stoneybridge burying ground, and everybody on the drove roads knew it. Reavers learned to come at him from the south. One day in his sixties they did.

From inside · The noise stops.

Identity settles. The resonance signature that will be recognizably yours for the rest of your life finishes arriving. Techniques that were separate tricks cohere into something with a style to it, and for most practitioners this is the most physically pleasant experience of their lives, a warmth low in the chest that does not go away for weeks and that nobody who has had it describes well.

And on the lived road it cannot be reached alone.

The Catalyst is a Spirit Axis. Which means every Flourished practitioner in the four quarters who walked here, every hall-raised Adept, every guild officer, every Baron’s champion and every hedge-mage who ever stabilized without a vial, arrived because somebody mattered to them enough to hold load. The Stage that stabilizes the self is gated on attaching the self to another person, permanently, in a way that registers on an instrument. A Stage IV who drank the Catalyst has the warmth in the chest and nobody on the other end of it. The coil reads the Stage the same however it was crossed, and it reads the seat differently (Appendix A), and the empty bond shows there.

From inside each road:

  • Body. The warmth sits in the muscle, and they stand like someone whose back is covered, because it is.
  • Spirit. The warmth is a second presence in the chest, and solitude is never again quite complete.
  • Fate. The warmth is the weight of a promise that holds, and the world feels fastened where it had been loose.

On each Path · This is the last Stage at which more than half of every spend is lost, and the first at which a First Release can be found. The ladders stay unread. The three Firsts are laid side by side on Releases and Domains by Path.

  • Body. Techniques cohere into a style, and the Charge sits evenly through the Shell for the first time. Matter still answers only to the blow and the coat. A First Release on this road is a flood: the Shell thrown open to the whole Charge at once, the flesh running hotter and heavier, every hit carrying the Charge into what it lands on, and heat coming off the skin that the front row can feel.
  • Spirit. The signature has settled, so a thrown line now arrives recognizably theirs, and the Axis is felt in the Core as a second weight. A First Release here lengthens the line: further, to more souls at once, through plate as if the plate were not there. It stirs not one grain of sand.
  • Fate. For most, the bond itself is the art. A First Release on this road enlarges the claim and the Pressure: dust lies still, flames stand straight, and people give ground they did not decide to give. It is paid from the soul’s own share, pulls no thread and leaves no knot. A gripped soul reads what blood and years have given it, and the Stage has added nothing.

What it looks like · Consistency. Their power looks like them. You can hand them a problem they have never seen and predict roughly what they will do to it, and being predictable in that specific way is the difference between a talented person and a useful one.

What is not visible from outside is that an Axis is a load-bearing member. The architecture built on it has to stand up whether or not the person is still alive to stand under it. Every Prime Instructor has seen what a Flourished practitioner does the year their Axis dies. The power holds. The power is entirely fine, and it is still shaped exactly like a bond to somebody who is not there.

The aura is warm charcoal with a rumor of the hue in it, readable at a distance by any competent Mancer, and it is still a suggestion. On the Body road it lies on the skin like heat-haze and clings for a breath to a cup or a hilt after the hand has left it. On the Spirit road it hangs its half step off, and where an Axis runs a thread of it leans toward the other person, as Hob Tarrant’s leaned north. On the Fate road there is next to nothing to see, and a dropped coin near them comes up heads more often than it should. The Crystal is in the last of its Clouded years, and the body has begun to follow the road: a Body Adept is heavier than they look and warm to the touch, a Spirit Adept is lighter on a plank than their weight allows, and animals settle near them.

What it costs · A Release is the first thing on the ladder that can empty a reserve in minutes, and the Adepts are the people who find out what lies under a tenth. Held too long, a Body First ends in steam, cramp and skin split in fine lines along the forearms. A Spirit First ends in a hollow cold and a name gone. A Fate First ends in a week in which every buckle gives. Each release ends by itself at a tenth, and the injuries belong to the ones who force it past.

What the folk call it · Taking root. The anchoring. A bond-witness is held and somebody attests, and the parish keeps the roll in Witness Wax, and parish witness rolls are the oldest continuous documents in most of the western quarter. A village can tell you who was witnessed to whom nine generations back and cannot tell you who held the land.

  • The line the Accord has never published Guild halls know the Catalyst. Which means the fastest way to Flourish a cohort is to put a cohort through something together, at risk, in circumstances that make them matter to each other, and every Prime Instructor has known this since before there was an Accord.

    What is published is the training casualty figure. The Strategic Division reviews it annually and has never once moved to reduce it.

The crossing, lived · The scale is a parish, which is why the parish keeps the roll.

  • Body. Two auras run together where the pair stand, the heat reaches the back of the crowd, and the ground under them firms, mud going hard as a threshing floor.
  • Spirit. The witness-hall goes still. Every married couple in it thinks of the day they were witnessed, colors deepen for an hour, and old quarrels in the parish are found by morning to have lost their heat.
  • Fate. Across the parish every oath is felt to tighten. Debtors remember what they owe, a betrothal that had been wavering stops wavering, and the Wax on the roll sets harder than the weather allows.

Bought versus lived · A bought Flourishing has the warmth in the chest and nobody on the other end of it, and it has no First Release, since a Release must be found by fighting. The Crystal reads Clouded either way. The seat does not read the same.

The Breaking Band: Band II · Awakened

Stages V through VII. The Codex calls this the completion of the inner phase. The instructors call it the years you find out.

What changes in this Band. The Crystal breaks into Faceted at Splintering and comes Clear at Refraction. At Glory the ladders are read for the first time, the soul makes its first offer of a Class, and the share of a spend that arrives passes from the Stage’s figure to the rung of the layer in use. The ceiling is the first rung at Glory and the third at Refraction, so this Band holds the whole distance from matter as an object to matter by recognition, and from Listener to Confessor. The Second Release and the Domain seed arrive at its end. Auras gain light and take a shape, and civilians begin to see them.

V · Splintering

Tier 5 · Expert · Grade B, unstable from 276 to 350 · Ceiling 350 · Levels 101 to 150 · Faceted Crystal

Catalyst · surviving contact with an entity or force more than two full Grades above the practitioner’s ceiling. Survival is the whole requirement, and defeating the thing adds nothing.

Survival is the whole requirement on every road. What has to be survived is not the same thing.

  • Body. The contact is a blow or a load: a drake’s tail, an avalanche, the hand of a Master who was not trying. The body takes what should have ended it, the Shell breaks, and the flesh is rebuilt around the break.
  • Spirit. The contact is with a soul or a meaning far too large: the full regard of a Master, the dread of a Well-born thing, the grief soaked into a battlefield. Nothing touches the body. The Core takes it and breaks, and what is asked is that the same person be there afterward.
  • Fate. The contact is with a weight of fate far above their own: standing inside a greater soul’s claim and keeping a bond while it is leaned on, being the one obstacle to a great house’s oath, living through a day on which the odds were plainly arranged against them by something that can arrange odds. The Layer breaks among the bonds. The Fate soul pulls nothing in surviving it, and Thread does not move.

The one they tell · A Well-spawn drake at Cold Ferry took Yrsa Kolbeinsdottir across the chest with its tail when she was a Stage IV caravan guard with nine good years behind her, and she went through the side of a barge and lived. The Crystal broke where she was already broken, which happened to be a sister. For the twenty years of her loud and highly profitable career after that, every blow she threw went true until somebody said the name, whereupon hairline veins of blue ran through her aura and it stuttered like a struck lamp. Every rival in the north learned it. Whole companies would stand in line abreast bawling “Helga! How’s Helga?” across a field, at odds posted in advance, and Yrsa, who was nobody’s fool, took to fighting with tallow in her ears and killing the loudest one first.

From inside · The architecture that has held you since Murmuring reaches the limit of what it can be and has to break to become larger. There is no version where it does not break. The only question anybody has ever been able to influence is where.

It fractures along emotional lines. The break runs past the injury and past the encounter that triggered it, and follows whatever the soul was already carrying badly. A practitioner who goes into Splintering with an unresolved thing comes out of it with that thing structurally present in their Crystal, permanently, in a way the coil (the assay instrument that reads a Crystal) reads.

Splintering takes a flaw the soul already had and promotes it to a load path.

Practitioners who fail do not die, mostly. They lose Sub-Stat (the finer stats beneath each main stat) progress and they sit at the ceiling of Stage IV for the rest of their working lives, and the four quarters are full of them, and they are the reason Tier Four is as crowded as it is.

From inside each road:

  • Body. The body stops being trustworthy. The hand relied on for nine years closes a beat late.
  • Spirit. The self stops being trustworthy. You are not certain the grief you woke with is yours.
  • Fate. Your word stops being trustworthy, to you. Every promise you ever made feels heavier, and you count them at night.

On each Path · The ladders go unread for one last Stage, and the Stage’s own figure jumps to something over six parts in ten: the Crystal has learned to stop leaking, mostly by breaking. The fracture runs through whichever layer the soul leans on.

  • Body. The break shows in the Shell. The Charge packs unevenly around the flaw, so a blow that has gone true for six years lands heavy or light without warning, and the coat thins over the same place every time. Matter still obeys only impact, and nothing new opens.
  • Spirit. The break shows in the Core, and since the Core is where the self is kept, this is the worst of the three to live in. A thrown line carries the flaw with it, and the struck sometimes feel the thrower’s grief where they should have felt their own dread.
  • Fate. The break shows in the Layer, among the bonds: an Axis that held load for years strains, and a vow sits heavier than it did. For the gripped, nothing moves on the Thread ladder, and nobody sensible pulls in the bad year.

What it looks like · A person who was reliable stops being reliable. Techniques that behaved for six years behave differently, or behave the same and cost more, or work perfectly and leave the practitioner shaking. The hall watches for it and the hall cannot do anything about it, and the honest instructors say so.

The aura is fracture-streaked: hairline veins of the lens’s clear color run through the smoky field and vanish again, a flicker anyone with the Gnosis can see. On a Body road the veins run on the skin, and on Yrsa Kolbeinsdottir they ran blue. On a Spirit road they hang in the air beside the person, where the shadows were already wrong. The Crystal has become Faceted, and the jewelers chose the word with some care. A facet is a face cut on a stone so that it will return light, and Splintering does the cutting. The three layers come out of it turning over in a rhythm the Continuum can read, and a Wellspring can now be harmonized without tearing the lattice.

What it costs · Everything costs more than it did and nobody can say by how much, which is the cost. Techniques that behaved for years leave the practitioner shaking. The road’s own bill arrives early and out of order: a hitter cramps at half, a thrower loses a word at half, and the small luck around a Fate fighter curdles on a full reserve. A failed Splintering is paid for in Sub-Stat progress and a working life at the ceiling of Stage IV.

What the folk call it · The cracking. The bad year. In the eastern quarter, going to the wall. There is no folk remedy, which is itself notable in a body of practice that has a remedy for everything, and the absence is the folk tradition being accurate about something the Accord’s literature is not.

Trade note · Splinter Ration leaves the fracture coming and introduces a controlled stress-riser so the Crystal breaks at the riser rather than wherever the trauma happened to sit. That is the entire product, it is reserved to Tier Five, and bonded practitioners receive it while village talent does not. More of who a person becomes is decided by that supply line than by anything they will ever choose.

The crossing, lived · The scale is a mile, and it is the first crossing strangers notice.

  • Body. Heat, and a shock through the ground that people a mile off take for a rockfall. Where the survivor lies the earth is glazed or split, and the body that gets up is plainly not the one that went down, scarred along the line of the break.
  • Spirit. For a mile around, people stop what they are doing and cannot say why. Colors go thin for a day, and everyone near is visited by the memory they like least.
  • Fate. For a mile around, luck goes strange for a day and then settles a little higher or lower than it was. Every net comes up empty or full, and things that were certain to happen that afternoon, a foaling, a hanging, do not happen.

Bought versus lived · A breakthrough Draft spares the buyer the meeting with something two Grades up, and it does not spare them the break. The lived fracture runs wherever the soul was already carrying something badly. The bought one runs where the Bench put the riser, which is why the houses pay for it.

VI · Glory

Tier 5 · Expert · Grade A · Ceiling 400 · Levels 151 to 175 · Faceted Crystal

Catalyst · willingly accepting genuine soul-cost to preserve something valued above the practitioner’s own structural integrity

The decision is the Catalyst on every road, made in advance and in cold blood. The layer it is paid from is the road’s.

  • Body. The cost is taken in the Shell and the flesh under it. The Charge is driven past the floor on purpose, for something, in the knowledge that part of the Shell will not close again: a hand that never afterward holds a coat, an arm that stays cold, years burned as fuel. The ruin is the receipt.
  • Spirit. The cost is taken in the Core, which is the self: a memory given up whole, a capacity spent, a color gone out of the world for good. Tsao Qinglan’s entry in the Red Abacus ledger reads like this one, and three Halls have argued for two centuries over which road she was standing on when she paid it.
  • Fate. The cost is taken from the soul’s own share, as a future given up by oath. The soul refuses, aloud and for good, a chance that was truly theirs, an inheritance, a marriage, a long life, so that something else may stand, and the Layer closes over the place. It is a vow and no pull: no thread, no knot, no rung.

The one they tell · When the Yellow Dike went at Lanjiang, Tsao Qinglan of the Red Abacus Hall stood in the breach with forty outer disciples behind her and the river coming, and she named her price aloud, as her Hall’s rules require of anything expensive. She spent the part of her Crystal that knew how to be happy, or so the Hall’s ledger enters it, and nobody has found another way to read the entry. The water stopped at her shins in a brown wall three stories high, held up on nothing but deep teal light, for as long as it took the lower city to climb the hill. For the rest of her life every flame in any room she entered leaned toward her, every candle and cook-fire, and each year Lanjiang hung its streets in silk and carried her through them under ten thousand paper lanterns. She attended, every year, and never once smiled at it.

From inside · The fractures have become features. What was damage the year before is now architecture, and the practitioner can feel the difference, and the feeling is unaccountably good in a way that troubles the more thoughtful of them for years afterward.

The Catalyst is a decision, and it is the first Catalyst in the ladder that is. Murmuring happens to you. Welling happens to you. Ascension is earned but arrives sideways. Flourishing requires another person. Glory requires that you look at something that will cost you structurally and choose it anyway, knowing the price, in advance, in cold blood.

Which means every A-Grade practitioner in the four quarters who came by this road has, at some point, deliberately damaged themselves for something. The Accord’s own literature describes this as the culture of duty. The instructors describe it as the year they learn what they will pay for.

Trade note · A breakthrough Draft at this grade takes the soul-cost without asking what it was for. The buyer pays the price and never learns what they were buying, and it is very hard to find out afterward which part of them went.

From inside each road:

  • Body. There is a place in the body that is gone, and the rest has grown stronger around it, as a tree grows around a nail.
  • Spirit. There is a place in the self you can walk around and not enter, and you know its exact shape.
  • Fate. There is a life you were going to have, and you can feel it not arriving.

On each Path · The ladders are read here for the first time, the ceiling on a leading ladder is the first rung, and the soul makes its first offer of a Class. From this Stage the share of a spend that arrives is set by the rung of the layer in use and no longer by the Stage: on a first rung, six or seven parts in ten.

  • Body. Aether I, Muridic, in any woken soul, since everything a person does in the world has to leave by the Shell. The Charge leaks unevenly and loudly. The rung opens the first step of the Material Force, set out under Forms: matter as an object. What the hand is on does as it is told, the post, the door, the other man’s sword arm, and nothing the hand is not on. A smith or a mason whose trade skill reads B or better works one rung higher on their own material wherever the ceiling allows it, and at Glory the ceiling does not.
  • Spirit. Essence I, Listener, for a soul that has used its Core on others. Souls and meanings begin to reach them unasked: the dead in a room, the grief soaked into a coat, a lie going sour in a man’s voice. None of it can be answered yet. A thrown line still lands on a soul, at a first rung’s losses, and the lost share is paid in the Core.
  • Fate. The Thread ladder opens no earlier than this for anyone without blood or a blessing, and for nearly everyone it opens on Ø, Unthreaded, and stays there. Study can take a soul to I, Touched, and keeping every vow one ever made reaches I or II at the most, over years. What a Fate Expert has at Glory is the Echo field and a Pressure civilians can feel, and that is a great deal.

What it looks like · The Aura, a light in the practitioner’s own color that flares brighter as they push and is now plain to any civilian, starts doing things to rooms, gently at first. The air moves differently near them, small flames lean, animals behave, and people who have never heard of Aether Class (how much of a soul’s power survives the trip out through the Shell) find themselves standing slightly further away without deciding to. The Echo field forms here. This is the Stage at which a practitioner stops being a person with abilities and starts being a presence, and most of them handle it badly for a year or two.

The color is medium gray with the hue standing in it, and luminance enters for the first time, so that the color no longer has to fight its way through. The aura also takes a shape here, laid on it by the Class, and holds it. A Body aura hugs the skin like heat-haze and clings to what they touch. A Spirit aura hangs a half step off the body, and the shadows around it fall wrong. A Fate aura barely shows, and near its owner dust lies still and coins land face up. The Crystal is Faceted and turning cleanly. A Body Expert has grown dense, heavier on a scale than the eye allows and warm as a new loaf. A Spirit Expert has grown light, with a stillness dogs lie down for.

What it costs · A first rung loses a third of every spend, and Glory is where the lost share becomes large enough to trouble bystanders. A Muridic hitter at full output warms a room and at a quarter steams like a horse, and held to a tenth the skin splits along the hands. A Listener who overspends comes home hollow and cold, with a color gone flat for days and somebody’s name mislaid, which the menders will tell you is cheap. A gripped Fate fighter who pulls at this height is pulling under the bottom of the table, with nearly all of it gone before anything moves, and the knot is owed whether the pull lands or fails.

What the folk call it · Being lit. He is lit up. In the Old World temple registers the term is kindled, and the ecclesiastical usage and the tavern usage have not diverged in six hundred years, which the Archives finds remarkable and has not explained.

The crossing, lived · The scale is a town, and this is the crossing the folk name is taken from.

  • Body. The light is plain to every civilian for the first time. Heat rolls through the streets, the ground keeps the shape of what was done on it, and the body that walks out is denser than it went in and marked where it paid.
  • Spirit. The town goes silent street by street. Every lamp flame leans, colors shift a shade in every eye and do not all shift back, and each person present finds one memory brighter and one fainter than it was.
  • Fate. Luck turns across the town in one evening. Debts are forgiven that nobody meant to forgive, a fire that was about to start does not, and every sworn person inside the walls feels their oaths draw tight as the refused chance closes.

Bought versus lived · A bought Glory opens the ceiling and leaves the rung to be earned, pays a soul-cost without learning what it was for, and is offered a Class that reads the life as it was actually lived, quiet room included. The lived one lit a breach at Lanjiang. The bought one is a Draft, a physician and a window that says Well-Attended.

VII · Refraction

Tier 5 · Expert · Grade A with late push toward S · Ceiling 475 · Levels 176 to 200 · Clear Crystal

Catalyst · first Domain seed activation, however fragile or small. Projected fields become coherent. This seed is a one-time flicker of the person’s own Trait at the Threshold, and it needs no Release. The Second Release is what later lets a seed be called and held.

The seed is the soul’s own Trait let out for a few seconds, on every road. What lets it out, and what it lands on, follow the lead layer.

  • Body. The seed fires in matter. The body is pressed to the point where its Trait can no longer be kept inside the skin, by a load, a blow or one insult too many, and for seconds the ground and the things on it take the body’s law. Gaffer Tom Underhay’s pints fell upward.
  • Spirit. The seed fires in meaning. The soul faces something true about itself squarely, usually aloud, and for seconds the room agrees: everyone in it takes a thing for what the holder says it is. Nothing on the table moves.
  • Fate. The seed fires as a condition. The soul stakes a claim against the run of things, a vow held at the moment it ought to have broken, and for seconds what must be so before anything can happen is what they say it is. Every seed is a claim of the Layer, so a Fate soul comes to this one on home ground, and it still runs at Thread Ø: no pull, no knot, no rung moved.

The one they tell · Gaffer Tom Underhay of the Salt Road Foot had sat at Level 199 for twenty-two years and told everyone about it. He was fifty-seven, with a wall eye and a pension dispute, and on the night of the Mule and Lantern brawl somebody told him the Refraction wall was for men with better fathers. He stood up, and for four seconds every pint in that taproom fell upward. There were sixty witnesses. They describe it wrongly, each in exactly the same way, and all of them will swear the beer turned green. The Foot carried him the whole length of the garrison road on a door, with a band in front. Next morning he looked inside his own Crystal, found out what his fracture record had to say about his father, and did not speak to anyone for a year.

From inside · The soul sees itself from outside.

The Crystal that has been the unconscious medium of everything you have ever done becomes an object you can look at, and looking at it changes it, and knowing that it changes it changes it again. Gnosis (the stat of perception and understanding) undergoes a qualitative jump here that no other Stage produces, and practitioners in Refraction are notoriously difficult to be around because they have just been handed the ability to examine their own architecture and have not yet learned to stop.

The trouble is that self-examination at this depth reveals the fracture record. Everything Splintering promoted to a load path is now visible, labeled, and impossible to un-see.

From inside each road:

  • Body. You see the Shell from outside: every old injury and every place the Charge sits badly, laid out like a joiner’s drawing.
  • Spirit. You see the Core from outside, and there is the grief, labeled.
  • Fate. You see your bonds from outside, each promise a line leaving you, and you can count which ones you kept.

On each Path · The ceiling on the leading ladder rises to the third rung, with the second the usual step early in the Stage, and the Second Release can be found. It opens and powers the Domain, and at this Stage it holds a seed for minutes. Releases and Domains by Path has the three kinds of field side by side.

  • Body. Aether II, Harmonic, and then III, Resonant. At II matter answers through contact: what the hand touches carries the command onward, the post through the wall behind it. At III it answers by recognition: she knows what the stone will do under load, and it does that at her word. Losses fall from about a fifth to under a tenth, the largest single gain in fidelity on the ladder. A trade skill at B or better lifts the rung by one on its own material, and never past III. A Body Second Release rules matter: the Charge leaves the skin and fills the ground, and inside the line weight, heat and the state of stone obey the holder. Everyone can see it.
  • Spirit. Essence II, Answerer, and then III, Confessor. At II a shade or a bound spirit will talk back, and a thrown working means something when it arrives. At III they reach into a soul and find what is lodged there, and a line written on a thing carries their say on what the thing is. A Spirit Second Release rules meanings: the room looks exactly as it did, and something in it has stopped being what it was.
  • Fate. Persistence will not pass S unless the Fate Path has been shown by this Stage. Most still read Thread Ø, and the seed asks for nothing more: every Domain is a claim staked by the Attraction Layer, it runs at Thread Ø, and it pulls no thread and leaves no knot. A Fate Second Release rules conditions, which is to say what has to be so before anything can happen. A gripped soul reads whatever its blood and its decades have earned, and the Stage has added nothing to that.

What it looks like · A Domain seed. Small, fragile, lasting seconds at its first firing, and minutes for those who later earn a Second Release and can call it back, and the first genuinely uncanny thing most people will ever witness, because a Domain seed is a piece of the world briefly agreeing to different terms. Bystanders describe it wrongly and consistently, and the consistency of the wrongness is the best evidence Archives has that they are describing something real.

The wall at two hundred. Band II ends at Level 200 and the Threshold that opens Band III is Refraction. A practitioner who cannot produce a Domain seed or pay for one does not slow down. The counter halts and the pressure keeps arriving, accruing as Residual Strain, degrading gain and raising Overchannel (forcing more through the Crystal than it can carry) and Backlash (a working’s force turning on its maker) risk continuously, and it does not stop building.

This is where most careers that reach Band II end, in a long loud stall in the high hundred-and-nineties: strong, competent, slowly becoming more dangerous to themselves than to anything they are pointed at, telling anyone who will buy the next round exactly who cheated them and which house brat drank past them, and dying of something else first. Every veteran corps in the four quarters is largely composed of these people, and their bitterness is a structural feature of the Accord’s officer class rather than a matter of temperament.

The color is dual-toned, base gray and the lens’s color in one field without mixing, and it shifts with the angle it is seen from. Inside a seed the light itself changes temperature, which is what the sixty witnesses at the Mule and Lantern were calling green. A Body seed is plain to everyone: the air shimmers and the cobbles do something cobbles should not. A Spirit seed changes nothing the eye can find. A Fate seed lays the dust flat. The Crystal is Clear: it conducts by itself, power moves in it even in silence, and its owner can see into it, which is the whole trouble with the Stage.

What it costs · The seed is the dearest thing a soul has yet tried to hold, and it collapses when attention breaks. A Body seed throws its lost share as heat into the ground it rules and leaves the holder steaming. A Spirit seed hollows, and its holders come out of it pale, vague and unsure whose turn it is. A Fate seed costs only reserve while it stands and curdles the luck of the room when it falls. Those who cannot cross pay the other bill, Residual Strain, and go on paying it.

What the folk call it · The double sight. Twice-eyed. In the eastern quarter, a Refracted practitioner is said to have swallowed their own reflection, which is a considerably better description than the Codex manages in the same number of words.

The crossing, lived · The scale is a hall and the street outside it, for a few seconds, and then for as long as the witnesses live.

  • Body. The air shimmers over a taproom’s worth of ground, loose things lift or sink or sweat, the cobbles keep the mark, and the holder stands up out of it steaming and half a stone heavier.
  • Spirit. A hall goes quiet in the middle of a brawl. Nothing has moved, and each person in it finds that one thing they were sure of has changed places with one they were not, and an old sorrow has shifted its weight.
  • Fate. The dust lies flat from wall to wall, every wager in the house settles itself, and something that was about to happen in that street, a knife coming out, a roof beam finally going, does not.

Bought versus lived · The Vitrifier delivers the flicker and the Stage. It does not deliver a Second Release, so a bought Refraction has a seed that fired once in a quiet room and cannot be called back, a ceiling at the third rung over a Shell that still reads whatever it read before, and a Crystal that will not come fully Clear until Level has filled it. Gaffer Tom was carried down the garrison road on a door with a band in front. Nobody carries a house heir anywhere.

Sovereign: Band III

Stages VIII through X. The Codex says a practitioner at this Band exercises genuine metaphysical authority. What it does not say is that authority is a relationship, and a relationship has two parties.

What changes in this Band. The ceiling on a leading ladder rises to the fifth rung at Transcendence and stays there for seven Stages, so everything from here to Zenith is done under one roof: matter by law, the Shepherd and the Ferryman. The Crystal passes through Fire at Transcendence and is Flawless from Invocation. The true Domain arrives, then the Third Release and the Realm, then workings that outlast their maker. Auras light rooms, and the land starts keeping records.

VIII · Transcendence

Tier 6 · Master · Grade S · Ceiling 550 · Levels 201 to 250 · Fire Crystal

Catalyst · resolving a core internal contradiction documented in the Crystal’s fracture record. A wound carried as structural damage and consciously processed into architecture. The first true Domain becomes possible.

The wound is the load-bearing one, the one Splintering promoted, and it lies in the layer the soul leans on. Each road has its own way of making a wound carry weight.

  • Body. The wound is in the body and the Shell over it: the break that set crooked, the hand that fails under load, the place the Charge has packed badly since Splintering. It is made structure by being broken again on purpose and rebuilt to carry weight through the scar. The halls call it going back under the hammer.
  • Spirit. The wound is a grief or a truth: the death that was their fault, the thing they are. It is faced whole, named, and made to hold weight, as Asahina Shigeko put hers on an unbarred gate.
  • Fate. The wound is a broken oath or a debt left standing. It is made structure by being paid in full, to the last coin and to the heirs if the creditor is dead, or by the oath being sworn again and kept this time at whatever it costs. No thread is pulled in the paying.

The one they tell · The Gate of Asahina Shigeko is the reason the Free Companies will still not march past a shut door at dusk. She had left a gate unbarred behind her when she was twenty-six, and a household died of it, and she carried that wound for forty years until she stopped on the Kaname road one autumn evening, with the maples going scarlet over her head and six hundred armored men coming up the hill, and put her whole weight on it. Kaerazu no Mon, she said, almost too low to hear: the Gate of No Returning, and the word went down the column from mouth to mouth. The road became a place where every door behind you was the one you should have shut. Six hundred men walked into it. They walked out three weeks later in their own home villages, with their armor rusted through and their swords still in their hands, and in sixty years not one of them could tell you what they had been so frightened of, or get through a doorway without looking back.

From inside · The world is read as symbol, and this is less mystical than it sounds and more disorienting. Objects retain their weight and gain a second weight. A door is a door and is also a threshold, and both facts are operative, and the practitioner has to keep functioning while holding them.

The Catalyst is the hardest on the lived road and the only one no hall can arrange, and a purse goes around it without touching it. Refraction showed the practitioner their fracture record. Transcendence requires them to take one of the wounds in it, the load-bearing one, the one Splintering promoted, and consciously make it structure. The wound stays a wound, unhealed and unreleased, and it is made to hold weight on purpose.

Practitioners fail at this for decades. There is no shortage of S-Grade-capable souls sitting at the Stage VII ceiling in comfortable positions who simply never did the work, and the halls are polite about it, and everybody knows.

From inside each road:

  • Body. The scar is the strongest part of you now, and you put your weight on it first.
  • Spirit. The grief is a room in the house, where it used to be a hole in the floor.
  • Fate. The debt is paid, and for the first time in decades nothing is following you.

On each Path · The ceiling on the leading ladder rises to the fifth rung, with the fourth as the step before it, and it will not rise again short of the Apotheosis approach. The first true Domain becomes possible, held for hours.

  • Body. Aether IV, Luminous, and V, Radiant: matter by law. The Shell is thin and lucid, and a river can be told how deep it is. At V more begins to arrive than the raw numbers suggest, and the reach runs as far as there is matter to read: armor made too heavy to lift, a blade rusting in the hand. It still needs existing substance, and every command comes back as recoil through bone and Crystal. A Body Domain rules matter, in yards, tight and hard, because Radius, Sovereignty and Gravity stop at A-Grade off the Fate Path.
  • Spirit. Essence IV, Shepherd, and V, Ferryman. A Shepherd holds many souls at once: a frightened company steadies around them, and a dying man’s soul stays where it is put until morning. A Ferryman carries souls across the Veil. She guides, questions, lays to rest, carries a message and binds a lingering dead, and never returns a life. A Spirit Domain rules meanings, by the room, and nothing on the shelves moves.
  • Fate. A Fate Domain rules conditions, and with the gates open it runs to valleys, with reserve the only leash. It is a claim and not a pull: it runs at Thread Ø and owes no knot. Thread itself has not moved. The few who hold III, Spinner, or better are mostly met from this Band up, for the plain reason that a grip takes decades and so does reaching Stage VIII.

What it looks like · A Domain, a true one, well past the seed, grown by living and called aloud: a standing region of the world operating on the practitioner’s terms while they are present in it, and the first time most people encounter one they are frightened in a way they cannot afterwards justify. Anyone at this Stage can have one, their own concept made real, with the fame, the rivals and the bounty that follow. A bought Stage VIII has the right to one and no Domain until they have fought their way to it, and a person standing in a duke’s colors at Stage VIII with nothing to open is a sight the songs enjoy.

The color is silver-gray suffused with the lens’s hue, bright enough to light a dark room and to throw its color on the walls: dark with light in it has become light with the memory of darkness in it. The Crystal is Fire, the jeweler’s word for light split into color inside a stone, and it is the first tier at which the Crystal itself gives enough light to be seen by an eye with no training. A Body Master is as dense as green oak and warm from across a room, and the eyes have begun to catch lamplight like a cat’s. A Spirit Master shows a faint translucence at the edges against a strong light, at the rim of an ear or the fingers of a raised hand. A Fate Master looks like anyone, and crowds part for them a moment before they arrive.

What it costs · A Domain is paid for from the reserve, in the coin of its own idea, and by the collapse when it falls. A Body Domain burns its lost share in the Shell’s furnace and throws the heat into the ground it rules, and one held to a tenth leaves the holder split and steaming in a ring of baked earth. A Spirit Domain hollows, and Masters come out of a long one cold and gray and short a memory they will not get back. A Fate Domain run long sours the luck of everyone inside the line, the holder first, and maxed it calls in every knot the holder ever left outstanding. A Radiant Shell or a Ferryman’s Core loses almost nothing, which only means the reserve empties on purpose.

What the folk call it · Folklore thins here, because folklore is what a community builds around a thing it lives beside, and communities do not live beside Stage VIII. What exists instead is story. Named individuals, remembered feats, and the beginnings of the process by which a person becomes a figure. The Accord’s Lattice Ledger and the ballad tradition begin describing the same people at roughly this Stage and stop agreeing almost immediately.

The crossing, lived · The scale is the valley, and this is the crossing the saying is about.

  • Body. The whole valley sees it: a column of the lens’s color, heat that brings blossom out of season or leaves bare scorched rock, the ground answering in a ring of raised or sunken earth, and a body walking out remade, with the old scar shining through it like a seam of ore.
  • Spirit. The valley goes quiet, birds included. Colors deepen for everyone in it, each person recalls the door they should have shut, and for one night nobody within sight of the place lies to anyone.
  • Fate. Luck turns across the valley in a night. Lost things come home, old feuds find themselves settled, every oath on every parish roll is felt to tighten, and a flood or a death the whole valley had been expecting passes it by.

Bought versus lived · A bought Transcendence has the right to a Domain and no Domain, a roof marked Radiant with a Shell under it that may read Aether I, and the wound exactly as it was, now at S-Grade. The Crystal shows little of its Fire until Level fills the tier. A lived one lights the valley, and the valley remembers the date.

IX · Invocation

Tier 6 · Master · Grade S with late push toward SS · Ceiling 625 · Levels 251 to 275 · Flawless Crystal

Catalyst · calling on Wellsprings, Titans, or Archonic law as co-authors of expression, and with it a first flicker of a Realm, a field capable of holding other Domains. Like the seed before it, that flicker comes once and needs no Release. The Third Release is what makes a Realm.

Something larger is asked in as co-author on every road, and what is put at its disposal is the layer the soul leans on.

  • Body. The asking is done with the body. The soul stands on the Wellspring’s own ground and lets the lens take the Charge in the Shell without steering it, which is to hand the body over. What must be survived is the answer arriving in the flesh.
  • Spirit. The asking is done with the self. The Core is opened to a second voice, and the soul must remain the first one: it lets a larger meaning in and is still there afterward to disagree with it.
  • Fate. The asking is done by terms. The soul offers a pact, stated aloud, to the law behind a Wellspring or to an Archon’s, and is answered with terms of its own, which it accepts knowing that such things notice who breaks them. A pact is an oath. It spends the share, pulls no thread and moves no rung.

The one they tell · At the siege of Dornhold the Ember Deacon Clement Tull asked. That is how the song puts it, and for once the song is precise. The Duke’s marshal had opened a Domain over the walls, an honest thing of iron-gray weight that had been breaking ladders for a month, and Tull knelt in the ditch in a scorched orange cope and called on the Wellspring by its name, and something that was not Tull answered.

His field went round the marshal’s field and closed, and the marshal’s Domain hung inside his like a fly in amber, still running, with the marshal in it, and a second color came up at the edge of the orange that nobody in the camp could look at for long. The town surrendered by noon. Tull asked the fire to stop, and the Wellspring, which had preferences of its own, declined. Dornhold burned for nineteen years. Pilgrims came to watch, and the Strategic Division opened a file.

From inside · You stop working alone.

Every technique before this was the practitioner’s Crystal doing something. From Invocation, the practitioner asks, and something larger answers, and the answering is literal, unreliable and expensive. The Wellspring is a co-author. Co-authors have their own preferences and they exercise them.

The recorded psychological pattern is the most consistent in the ladder. Practitioners entering Invocation describe an enormous relief followed within a year or two by something considerably worse, as they work out that the relief was the sensation of no longer being solely responsible for their own output, and that the thing they handed responsibility to did not sign anything.

From inside each road:

  • Body. Your hands do a thing you did not quite tell them to do, and it is better than what you would have told them.
  • Spirit. There is a second voice in the Core, and it does not always wait to be asked.
  • Fate. You are a party to a contract, and the other party does not sleep.

On each Path · No ceiling moves. The Third Release can be found, which fills the two-Grade cap on boosts by itself and makes a Realm, a field that can hold other fields. And the Wellspring begins to answer. The Archives read a Realm by the same division as the field under it: matter, meanings, conditions.

  • Body. The ceiling is still V, and most Masters stand a rung or two under it. What changes is who is shaping the Charge. The lens in the Shell now has an author besides the fighter, so a Body working at this Stage is the world’s own Aether, in the Wellspring’s shape, at the Wellspring’s suggestion. Tull’s fire was that. A Body Realm rules the matter another Domain stands on.
  • Spirit. The Core holds a second voice. Essence is still the self, refined and stamped, and now the stamp is countersigned: a Mancer reads two authors in it. A Spirit Realm rules what the fields inside it are taken to mean.
  • Fate. A Fate Realm rules whether a field inside it has leave to stand. The difference between a claim and a pull matters more here than anywhere, and it is the first thing the Strategic Division’s file records: a Realm is a claim, owes no knot and can be held all day, and a pull of the same size is a Weaver’s work and the masterwork of a life.

What it looks like · For those with a Third Release, a Realm. The Domain has become capable of holding other Domains inside it, which is the point at which Stage IX practitioners become a strategic rather than a tactical consideration, and the point at which the Strategic Division starts keeping a file.

The aura is bright, and no longer wholly the practitioner’s own: a second hue comes up at the edges during an invocation, the Wellspring’s, and recedes after. The Crystal is Flawless, which in the trade means a stone with nothing in it that is not the stone. The old inclusions are all still there. They have become structure, and Trait law and Domain law have begun to be the same law. A Body Master at this height throws warmth like a stove, a Spirit Master has a stillness that silences a rookery, and around a Fate Master the weather is a little better than it is a mile off.

What it costs · A co-author takes a share. The Third Release fills the whole boost cap by itself and empties a reserve in the time it takes to say so, and on each road the bill is the road’s own: heat enough to cook a ring and split the holder to the elbow, a hollowing that takes a year of memory in one evening, or a run of luck so bad that their own name sits wrong in other mouths. Above that stands the cost no ladder lists, which is that the Wellspring may decline to stop.

What the folk call it · Nothing. Ordinary people do not have a word for Stage IX and have never needed one. The ecclesiastical traditions have several and they disagree with each other and with the Accord, and the disagreement is four hundred years old and is really an argument about whether the Wellspring answering constitutes divine favor or merely a lawful process, which is the same argument the Holy See and the Codex have been having about everything since Year Zero.

The crossing, lived · The scale is a county, and for the first time the light is not all the practitioner’s own.

  • Body. A second color stands in the sky over the place, the Wellspring’s, and the county’s matter answers in its manner: every hearth burning high, or every well rising, or frost in June. The one who asked gets up giving off a heat that is not altogether theirs.
  • Spirit. A county’s worth of people stop talking at once and feel themselves briefly overheard. Dreams are shared for a week, and the colors that come back are a little more the Wellspring’s than they were.
  • Fate. Across the county things settle as though a judgment had been handed down. Suits long at law resolve, the season turns on the exact day the almanac gave, and every sworn person feels their oaths draw tight, the way a line does when something large takes the other end.

Bought versus lived · The Draft stands in for the asking, so a bought Invocation has the Stage and has never been answered. It has no Third Release and no Realm, and a Wellspring that has never been asked anything by them, which those who know count as the one mercy in the arrangement.

X · Realization

Tier 6 · Master · Grade SS · Ceiling 725 · Levels 276 to 300 · Flawless Crystal

Catalyst · truth as lived consequence. The world answers back. Pacts, artifacts and law imprint on terrain. The Domain becomes a reality the Continuum (the lawful fabric of reality that workings write into) must accommodate.

This Catalyst is the slowest on the ladder, and no road has a short way through it. A truth has to be lived, without exception, until the world answers, and the road decides what it is lived in.

  • Body. The truth is lived in work on matter: decades at one wall, one forge, one road, until the thing has been told the same so many times, by hands that never once told it otherwise, that it keeps the word unasked.
  • Spirit. The truth is lived in witness. Odo Pennyfather sat thirty-six years on one stone and never let a false thing pass in his hearing, and the meaning set.
  • Fate. The truth is lived in a kept word: every pact honored and every debt paid on the day, for so long that the ground where they were sworn begins to hold other people to the same. Nothing is pulled. The Layer has simply never been given a reason to doubt.

The one they tell · A Stage X notary named Odo Pennyfather sat on one flat stone at Cray Hollow for thirty-six years witnessing bargains, because he liked the view. He has been dead for two centuries and nobody has told a successful lie on that stone since. Liars get a nosebleed, a slow drip onto the rock, and the rock keeps the stain. Cattle dealers pay two silver marks to close on it, a man at the foot of the path sells handkerchiefs, and three kings have sent their regrets. A Moto envoy once stood there for a whole afternoon saying only true things about the weather and was generally held to have won. The last man who tried to swear away a murder on it bled out through the nose before the clerk had finished the line.

From inside · The practitioner stops being the party that has to be accommodated.

At Realization the Continuum adjusts, a little, locally and grudgingly and within limits that are extremely specific, but the direction of the accommodation has reversed and it never reverses back. What the practitioner is now costs the world something to permit, and the world permits it.

The lived experience of this is reported as a species of loneliness that has no good analogue lower on the ladder, and practitioners who describe it well tend to describe it in terms of weather. You are no longer standing in it. You are part of what other people are standing in.

From inside each road:

  • Body. The things you made no longer need you, and you can feel them standing.
  • Spirit. The things you meant go on meaning it after you have left the room.
  • Fate. The things you swore are kept by the place now, and you are only one of the parties.

On each Path · No ceiling moves and no Release remains to be found. What changes is that workings stay.

  • Body. Matter keeps what it was told. A wall a Stage X hitter has leaned on for a season carries its load a century longer than its mortar should, and things made at this Stage do not need maintaining. Those on the fourth or fifth rung command by law, and at Realization the law outlasts the command.
  • Spirit. Meanings keep. A stone where true bargains were witnessed for thirty-six years goes on knowing a lie, and Odo Pennyfather’s is the standing case. Ground thick with memory is what Spirit workers pay to stand on, and a Stage X makes it by sitting still.
  • Fate. Pacts hold in the ground. A vow sworn in a Realized Domain binds the place along with the parties, and such places grow heavy with what was decided there, as The Three Grounds sets out. Thread itself stands wherever blood and decades have put it.

What it looks like · Terrain changes. Pacts hold in the ground. Artifacts made at Realization do not need maintaining. Regions where a Stage X practitioner spent a long time keep a set that outlasts them by generations, and half the sites the Measurewrights (trained assayers who measure power and sites) cannot attribute in the Bearing and the Holding are almost certainly this and cannot be proven to be.

The color is pale and clean, the gray gone to a whisper, and the residue of a working holds the hue for hours or days: ground a Stage X has worked on keeps their color long enough for a Mancer arriving a week later to say who was there. The Crystal is Flawless. A Body road shows in a weight that makes floorboards complain and in eyes that shine back in the dark, a Spirit road in a body that seems lit from behind at its edges, and a Fate road in nothing on the person at all and in a market that rearranges itself as they walk through it.

What it costs · The reserve at this height is a lake, and emptying it is a public event. A Body Master at a tenth has baked the field and stands in it cracked like a dry riverbed. A Spirit Master at a tenth has forgotten a decade and may not know it. A Fate Master’s knots, where there are pulls behind them, are no longer the size of a snapped strap: a removed prerequisite falls due within the arc, and what falls due is a friend’s plan, a harvest or a bridge. The terrain keeps a copy of whichever it was.

What the folk call it · The place remembers him. Every quarter has a version and the versions are the same version. This is the point at which a practitioner stops generating stories about themselves and starts generating stories about a location, which is how you can tell, from the shape of a local legend, roughly what Stage the person at the bottom of it reached.

The crossing, lived · The scale is a district. It is the slowest crossing on the ladder to show, and the only one that never stops showing.

  • Body. The land takes the print: a hill that holds its slope through a flood, ground that stays warm, stone that will not weather. The body is by now what it will be for the rest of its life.
  • Spirit. A district’s worth of people find that a place has become solemn. Colors sit deeper there, memories told on that ground come out truer than the teller intended, and it stays so.
  • Fate. Across the district bargains start being kept. Odds on that ground run fair for everyone, cheats go hungry, and what was sworn there is found to bind people who never heard it sworn.

Bought versus lived · The world answers a life, and a bought Realization has not lived the one in question. The Stage and the ceiling are real. The place does not remember them, because they were never anywhere long enough, and the Crystal’s tier trails the Stage until Level fills it.

Mythic: Band IV

Stages XI and XII. Two Stages that are one motion: the deliberate approach to annihilation, and what radiates outward from a soul that came back.

What changes in this Band. No ceiling moves and no Release is left to find. The Crystal stays Flawless in tier, whatever its State. What changes is direction: at Dissonance the soul turns on itself, and at Emanation what it is begins to leak into the district with nobody holding it. The costs change hands at the same time, from the practitioner to the people who live nearby.

XI · Dissonance

Tier 7 · Grandmaster · Grade SS, fracture risk above 700 · Ceiling 750 with structural instability · Levels 301 to 350 · Flawless Crystal

Catalyst · deliberately entering contact with a force or truth capable of ending the practitioner’s existence and remaining coherent long enough to choose how they meet it

Every road walks toward the thing that can end it and chooses how to stand. The roads differ in what that thing is.

  • Body. It is a force. They walk into the heart of a Well, a Titan’s weather or the blow of something far above them, with the Shell already at fracture risk, and what they choose is the stance they are broken in.
  • Spirit. It is a truth, one the self cannot contain and remain the self. They look at it without turning away, and what they choose is who is doing the looking.
  • Fate. It is their own end, already arranged: a death at odds nobody could lean on, a doom, an oath whose keeping kills. The chance to step aside is offered, and it is a real one. They refuse it and choose the manner of meeting what comes. It is the largest refusal on the ladder, and it pulls nothing.

The one they tell · The Harrow Road rippled for a week, the summer Grandmaster Reva Stroud stopped being able to decide. From inside · Splintering was survival. Dissonance is chosen.

The practitioner walks toward something that can end them, arrives, remains coherent, and chooses their manner of meeting it. That is the whole Catalyst and every word of it is load-bearing. Survival will not satisfy it and victory will not satisfy it: the Crystal wants a soul that remains coherent long enough to choose.

Power and identity clash here, openly, in the Crystal, and the clash is the Stage. From inside each road:

  • Spirit. There are two of you in the Core, and both are telling the truth.
  • Fate. What you have sworn and what you are have parted company, and everything near you waits to see which will be kept.

On each Path · No ceiling moves. The clash of power and identity runs through the leading layer, and each road has its own way of being trapped.

The color is brilliant, with a visible interference grain: standing-wave textures that shift with the Crystal’s pressure, brighter than Realization and plainly unsteady, pitch flaring on a torch where a lamp would hold. A Body Grandmaster flagging in Dissonance cracks in the Shell and in the Crystal together. A Spirit one loses memory to the hollowing and identity to the clash, and cannot always tell which went. A Fate one finds the knots falling due early. The protocol’s bowl of mutton and barley is aimed at the plainest fact about all three: a soul that has stopped eating has stopped deciding, and this Stage is passed by deciding.

What the folk call it · The singing. Along the Harrow Road, gone to the bands. XII · Emanation

Tier 7 · Grandmaster · Grade SSS · Ceiling 950 · Levels 351 to 400 · Flawless Crystal

Catalyst · emerging from Dissonance with a Crystal denser and cleaner than before entry. The fractures must have become features.

Coming back is work, and it is done in the layer that broke.

  • Spirit. The coming back is done in the self. The two who argued in the Core are made one person who contains the argument, and it is counted done when they can say the truth that nearly ended them in an ordinary voice.
  • Fate. The coming back is done in the accounts. Every debt outstanding is paid in full, and for those who ever pulled, every knot is let fall due and met standing, so that the Layer comes out owing nothing to anyone. It is settlement and no pull, and no rung moves for it.

The one they tell · When the chandler’s wife at Vall told the Accord’s Envoy, Grandmaster Cuthbert Ashe, that she would not be leaving her husband, he took it well. On each Path · No ceiling moves. What used to be done is now simply the case, and the leading layer leaks.

What it looks like · The room is different because they are in it, and stays different for a while after they leave. Crops. Weather. Sleep. Whether arguments in a household escalate. Appendix A: The Seat

“Seated clean, and you can hear it. Seated rough, and so can everybody else.”

Each Threshold is a structural reorganization, and whatever is in the Crystal at the moment of reorganization is built into what comes out. The residue of that is called the seat, from the joiner’s word, because the trade took its vocabulary from people who fit things into other things for a living and had no patience for metaphysics.

A soul’s carry is whatever it has taken on from the stock it was tempered with, the origin that comes along with the Essence, graded by provenance Grade on The Standing Index (so “Index Grade IV” below is a grade of stock, and has nothing to do with a Class on the Shell).

A clean seat is carry that integrated evenly. The signature reads as one thing. Output is consistent, Traits hold, techniques behave the same way in the fourth hour as in the first.

A rough seat is carry from four Index Classes and nine sources, none of it fully integrated, the signature reading as a chord with a wrong note in the middle of it. Rough-seated practitioners are not weaker. Several of the most feared individuals in the four quarters are catastrophically rough-seated and that is precisely why nobody can predict them.

What decides a seat. A Threshold is a melt, and a melt does not cool into order unaided. Something must survive the heat for the new lattice to grow around, and the only structures in a Crystal that do are Traits, because a law the Continuum has already accepted as reliable does not stop being reliable at temperature.

Seeds that agree cool clean. Seeds that contradict each other cool rough, and the lattice keeps the argument. A soul walking two Paths seats rough for that reason, whatever stock it was tempered with, and a soul on three runs a high risk of what comes next. No adequate seed at all and the melt cools amorphous: the Stage does not advance, Clarity and Compression fall, a Trait is commonly lost outright, and the halls record a Dead Set. The trade word for the result is slag.

Carry, Drafts and rendered stock work by supplying seed the soul did not produce, and at the breakthrough grades the Catalyst along with it. That is the whole of what they do, it is real, and it is why the coil, which reads a bought Stage and a fought one the same, reads a purchased seat differently from an earned one.

The coil reads a seat in under a minute, and everything below follows from that.

What the reading is used for

Certification · Examinations read the seat before they read output, and a rough seat bars its owner from any appointment or certificate above Tier Four, whatever title their token reads off the Stage table and whatever they can demonstrate. The stated reason is structural safety and the stated reason is true. The Accord has never addressed the other thing that is also true.

Marriage · Negotiations between the great houses now involve a coil reading as routinely as a survey of the land, and for the same reason, since both are asking what the children will inherit.

Everything else · Assay houses will read a seat for anyone with a silver mark. They are the only institution in the four quarters that will tell a person the truth about themselves for a fixed fee and no further comment.

The tells, for those who cannot afford a coil

Folk diagnosis. Unreliable in the individual case, correct often enough to have survived four centuries, and it has ended marriages.

What the coil is actually reading

A highborn heir tempered on entered, warranted, three-mark stock (all three Index readings on file and good) is seated clean, and the visible consequence is that their power is boring. Legible, consistent, well-behaved and boring, and the halls certify it to Tier Seven without a raised eyebrow because it will never surprise anybody.

A gutter-seated practitioner out of an eastern district is unpredictable, occasionally extraordinary, and barred from every certificate above Tier Four.

Both are measurements. Neither is a lie. The instrument is honest and it is reading, with perfect accuracy, what somebody’s parents could afford.

Appendix B: The Threshold Trade, by Stage

Which market reaches which Stage, and what that decides.

Two kinds of bottle are sold at a Threshold, and only one of them cares which road the buyer is on. A ration builds the quantitative floor, and the floor is shared, so a ration is the same for a hitter, a thrower and a puller. A breakthrough Draft stands in for the Catalyst, the Catalyst is met in the layer a soul leans on, and so a breakthrough Draft is poured by road, on one of three bases:

  • Body: a Salt base. Raw Aether in a fraction that packs straight into the Shell, parted for the most part from the stones of Body monsters. It stands in for a trial of the flesh. It goes down like a burn, and the melt that follows runs hot.
  • Spirit: Core stock. Essence that nobody has stamped, from the stones of Spirit monsters for choice. It stands in for a trial of the self, and the melt is cold and quiet.
  • Fate: the Layer reagent. The skin shaved off a Fate monster’s stone. It stands in for a trial of bond, oath and odds, and it is the dearest of the three by a wide margin and the most criminal, since no bench can tell it from Layer shaved off a dead practitioner and no realm licenses an ounce of it. A Fate soul’s breakthrough is therefore bought in a cellar at every Stage, and the houses that raise Fate heirs keep a man for it.

A soul on two roads buys for its lead road. A Draft poured on the wrong base still melts the Crystal, and it sets around a trial the soul was never going to count.

The law sets no upper limit, and the Drafts that carry a soul this high are rendered from Crystals of that height or made as [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Master WorksMaster Works]], and they sit in great-house vaults, in graves, or in the bellies of ambitious second sons. A blessing from something large enough does the same work. On the lived road Dissonance is walked into, Emanation is emerged from, and Zenith is conferred by a Hollow Throne. A purse can buy the Catalyst for any Stage, and at Zenith a Throne must still recognize the buyer, so money buys the attempt and not the seat. A number of very rich people have sat a long time on a very cold mountain, in excellent furs, finding this out.

The lived road has its ground as well. A soul that goes into seclusion for a Threshold does it where its lead layer feeds: a Body soul on Aetheric Density, at a mine head or a wild valley; a Spirit soul in Echo Depth, among graves, in an old temple or the house it was born in; a Fate soul under Thread Weight, on a battlefield, in a coronation hall or at a crossroads. The sects and houses that hold such ground charge for the bench, and the price of a season on it is the cheapest entry on this page.
