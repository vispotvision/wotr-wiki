Windows and the Sheet

Windows and the Sheet

Nov 17, 202627 min read

“Everyone in this room knows exactly how much they have left, and not one of them can prove it to you. Bet accordingly.”

Bookie’s patter, Sealhall arena, on any day of the year

Forty thousand people were on their feet in the bone arena at Sealhall, under the blue and orange banners and the great curved ribs of whatever died there, on the last day of the Waxgate Sweepstakes. Dagny Thorn, known from the arena steps to the salt road as the Bad Debt, had just taken a shoulder-high hammer-swing from Seo Min-ho that lifted her clean off the sand. It broke her right collarbone so badly that the end stood up under the skin like a tent peg and her arm hung off it dead to the fingertips. She got up with her mouth full of blood. This is what she was looking at, and it was for her eyes alone:

[Hit: right collarbone broken. Arm down. Vitality 44%]
[Charge: 3% ▓░░░░░░░░░]
[Skill: Taking a Punch, B to A]

She spat a tooth, spread her one good arm to the whole arena, and bellowed.

"HALF MY RESERVE LEFT, Min-ho! How's Yours?"

Min-ho’s own window said thirty percent, and his left eye was swollen shut and full of blood. He looked her over with the eye he had. His Gnosis Perception (the stat of sensing what a soul is) sat a full Grade under her Resilience Ward (the stat that keeps other people out of yours), so all his soul would give him on her was question marks. He saw a fighter still standing, grinning, and roaring about it. He had a wife in Verath. He dropped the hammer and yielded, and the Bad Debt won the Sweepstakes on an arm’s length of pure lying, with her color stuttering thin around her ribs in front of everyone who knew to look.

One man did know to look. Up in the fourth tier a Measurewright (one of the guild that keeps the world’s tables and argues about them) with an A-Grade Perception had read the whole window off her before she opened her mouth, the moment the swing landed, and had gone down to the rail and quietly doubled his money. The Accord’s inquiry ran for three months and found nothing, because shouting a number at a man is no crime and no one had asked her to swear to it. That is the window in one afternoon: a private report, a public lie, a stronger eye in the stands, and a very great deal of money riding on the gap.

What a Window Is

A window is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in its owner’s own tongue, and no one else can see it. The Soul Crystal is the soul, and the Continuum (the whole lawful order of reality) measures it. The report follows from those two facts, or so the Measurewrights will tell you for the price of a drink. It comes when called and it comes unasked, in the middle of a fight, the moment there is something to report. A Moto reads hers in Moto, a Kharven drover reads his in the language he swears in, and a Marrowkin window puts the doer at the front of every line and the tally at the back, as that language has always done.

Everyone has one, the unwoken included. A baker has a Baking skill and a little sheet. Waking (the opening of the Crystal, which turns a soul into a practitioner) opens the rest of it.

Nobody else can see it, and the owner cannot show it. No trick of light, no shared Domain and no amount of pointing will put your window in front of another person’s eyes. All anyone can see is your eyes going to the middle distance, and every people has its own slang for that. A Kharven is checking the bill. A Marrowkin takes the tally. Accord hands read their own file. Soldiers from the Ochre Fields say a man is counting his brothers, and they will fight you if you ask why.

The word people use for the whole of what it shows is the sheet.

What It Shows, and What It Hides

A window shows Grades, percentages, bars and levels. A normal look gives the full sheet:

[Mun Seo-yeon · Level 170 · Stage VII, Refraction · Path: Spirit]
[Human, Sum-gol lineage]
[Class: Auditor]
[Aether I · Essence III · Thread Ø]
[Ardency B · Vitality D · Gnosis C · Dexterity C]
[Harmonics D · Resilience E · Tempering E · Dominion F]
[Trait: Every Column Balances · Level 44]
[Skills: Bookkeeping A · Footwork B · Appraisal C · Knife D]
[Blessings: none]
[Essence: 58%]

From the top: name, Level, Stage and Path, which says which of the soul’s three layers the Crystal leans on: the Aether Shell in the Physical World for Body, the Essence Core on the Soul Plane for Spirit, the Attraction Layer on the Plane of Fate for Fate. A soul on two Paths shows both, the one behind second (Path: Body, Spirit), and a Path only studied shows nowhere. A registered card spells it out by Path Stage, the highest Stage whose gates that road has opened: Body VII Mastered · Fate VI Walked 35%. A Work of Hataraki or any other real grip is no Trait, technique, blessing or Class, so a card gives it a line of its own, Fate Art: Work · grip · Thread, as in Fate Art: Ruin · inherited · Thread I on a Moto who may stand on any road at all. Then race and lineage, which shape base stats and what comes easily. Then the Class, the name the soul has offered for how the person has lived, and under it the three ladders on one line, from Glory on: Aether Class for the Shell, Essence Class for the Core, Thread Class for the Layer, nearly always ending in Ø. Then the Eight Primaries with their Grades, each Trait with its level and each skill with its Grade, the blessings, each on its own line, and the reserve, which the window names for what fills it. A Spirit-led soul like the Clerk’s reads Essence, Aether refined and stamped in the Crystal and held in the Core. A Body-led soul reads Charge, raw Aether packed unrefined in the Shell. A soul on both roads gets both bars, one under the other. A Fate-led soul reads Essence as well: a Fate worker refines Aether in the Crystal as a Spirit worker does and pays her pulls and her vows from that, and Thread never appears as a bar on any window, because it is never stored and is only pulled one working at a time. A soul that has not yet settled on a road reads plain Reserve. It is one capacity whatever the word on it, and people go on saying reserve aloud for all of them. A Sub-Stat (one of the eight finer measures inside each Primary) appears when it is used or when it moves, and the full sixty-four remain there to study for anyone with the patience. Vows, crew bonds and contracts get their own lines when the owner has them.

A Trait’s lean shows in the letters. A Trait that is always running shifts the stat itself, and the window shows the shifted Grade with nothing to mark it. A Trait that runs only in its own conditions shows both letters, the plain one first, and names the condition:

[Absorption C · B while It Comes Out in the Wash holds]

The second letter is live only while the condition is, and both kinds of lean count on the two-Grade cap on boosts.

Worked gear shows, and plain gear does not. A plain helm is a helm and the window has nothing to say about it. A worked piece (runed, Draft-tempered, or made of monster parts) multiplies the Sub-Stats it was made for, and the Primary and its Grade are read again from there. It also takes a small steady draw on the reserve to keep it lit, which the window gives as light, steady or heavy, and names by the layer the piece draws on. Armor and weapons draw on the Shell, soul-wards and charms on the Core, oath-rings and luck-tokens on the Layer, and all of it is paid from the one reserve:

[Worn: Ashjaw Helm · Rare · Grade B]
[Perception ×1.15 · Threshold ×1.1]
[Shell draw: light]

Bonded gear’s multipliers grow with the bearer, and the block changes as they do. All of a person’s gear together multiplies any one Sub-Stat by half again at most, a cap of its own beside the two-Grade one. The sustainable Grade is read with the gear off. Gear multiplies on top of it and can move the letter the window shows, and the two-Grade cap on boosts counts from the bare Grade underneath. A piece above its wearer’s Stage works at half its multiplier or fights them, and the trade-offs are real, so a suit of heavy plate reads [Fortitude ×1.3 · Celerity ×0.85] and means both. A true relic gives its bearer the same question marks it gives everyone else. Worn Power has the whole of it.

A commoner’s window is small and useful. It holds a Level, a few trade skills and her health, with no Essence and no class:

[Hanne Oster · Human · Level 6]
[Health: good · Back: complaining]
[Skills: Baking B · Sums E · Getting Up Early C]

Hanne Oster has read that every morning for forty years, and she reads it the way you would read the weather.

It hides a good deal, and the list is worth learning by heart.

  • EU, AU/s and yield (η). Essence Units, the output rate and the yield, the share of a spend that arrives, the assayers’ sums set out in Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell. The owner gets the bar, the percentage and the shake in their own hands.
  • Raw stat values. Letters only. A Measurewright can cost your sheet in numbers with a coil and a fee. Your own soul has never told you that you are a 288.
  • Any level on a technique. Techniques show no level and take a bigger name when they jump.
  • Other people. Reading anyone else takes Gnosis (below), and the result is your own soul’s account of them, wrong in its own ways.
  • Why. It reports the blow after the blow. It has never warned anyone of anything, and it does not explain itself.
  • The truth of a blessing. The giver wrote that line.

The body comes first and the window follows. The tells on the body have always arrived ahead of the report: at half the reserve the breath shortens and the aura dims, at a quarter the hands shake and the color thins, at a tenth the aura flickers and the Trait stutters. Those are shared by everyone. On top of them each road tires in its own way, because each is spending a different thing: a Body fighter runs hot and the skin splits, a Spirit worker runs hollow and loses colors and then names, and around a Fate worker the luck curdles. Below a tenth lies Starvation, Charge Starvation for a Body fighter and Essence Starvation for a Spirit or a Fate one. The Three Paths gives the table under How It Reads. An old teacher will tell you to fight your lungs and read your window afterward, and the practitioners who live longest take the advice.

In a Fight

Windows come often and in detail once blows land, in short blocks, for damage, skill gains and reserve. Each block follows the thing it reports. Here is the Clerk’s evening at the Harl Cross market, from the middle of it:

[Left ribs: two cracked · Vitality holding]
[Skill rising: Footwork B, 91%]
[Essence: 41%]

A sequence off a sharper afternoon, read in order by a mid-rank Body fighter with a bad morning coming:

[Pressure: Stage VIII above you. Focus down a Grade.]

[Evaded. Reflex B, in use.]
[Hit: left ear gone at the rim. Vitality 96%]

[Coat: thin on the left. Charge: 51% ▓▓▓▓▓░░░░░]

[Hit: left forearm broken through. Grip gone. Vitality 71%]
[Charge: 44% ▓▓▓▓░░░░░░]
[Skill: Taking a Punch, C, 88%]

Pressure is felt first. The felt weight of a stronger soul shows in the room, in the lamp flames, the dogs, the sweat on your lip, and only then does the window report it as a line. A Grandmaster merchant with no fighting Stage has no Pressure at all, and his bookkeeper has been looking for the line for years.

A wound is a status line, and it stays. It says what the wound stops you doing, and it sits on the sheet until someone treats it. Healers and healing Drafts fix it for a price, slowly at low Grade and fast at high, so a rich fighter walks off what cripples a poor one for life. A retired spearman at Tollgarth has been reading the same line for thirty-one years:

[Right knee: torn. Will not bear weight. Untreated.]

A soul wound is a status line too. A blow that goes past the Shell and lands on the Core leaves no mark on the skin, and the window reports it like any other injury, by what it has taken: a color gone flat, a sense dulled, a memory worn thin. A shallow one closes in days and a deep one in seasons. One left untreated past a season, or struck a second time in the same place, sets for life. Soul-menders are a trade and they charge like one, and even a wound well mended leaves a pale place in the Core that a good Analysis can read years afterward.

[Soul wound: Core, shallow. Reds gone flat. Untreated.]

[Soul wound: Core, deep. Your mother's voice: thin. Set.]

A crew shows. A real bond shows as a line on the window, shares a little Level gain and puts the crew’s bars in the corner of a fight. It cannot be bought or declared. It has to be earned, and betrayal breaks it visibly, on the window of everyone it touches, in the middle of whatever they were doing:

[Crew: The Wet Sevens · bond holding]
[Hob ▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░░ · Marit ▓▓▓░░░░░░░ · Dov ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░]

[Crew: The Wet Sevens · bond broken: Dov]

A vow shows its terms. A vow is small Fate, a person binding their own probability, and anyone can make one. It spends the soul’s own share, where a pull reaches above it, which is why a Thread Ø fishwife can swear one that holds. The share is part of the reserve: a vow drains it like any working, spent through the Layer, and it refills the same way. Spoken aloud, with or without a witness, it arrives on the window with its bargain and its terms. Breaking it lands at once, by those terms. Each vow counts against the two-Grade cap on boosts. See Vows and Oaths:

[Vow: "This hand stays down until he is dead."]
[Bargain: Ardency one Grade up · Left hand dead until then]
[Breach: lands at once]

A pull leaves a knot, and the knot shows. A vow kept costs nothing further, because it spends only what was the soul’s own. A true pull on a thread reaches above that share and bends what was likely, and every one of them leaves a knot that the world collects later: a strap that gives on the puller next week, a friend’s plan that fails for no reason anyone can find. The window enters it at the moment of the pull and keeps it until it is paid. Its size follows how far the pull was from likely. A lean on the odds falls due within days, and a removed prerequisite within the season’s troubles. An inherited Work makes the pull less likely to fail and takes nothing off the knot.

[Pull: his girth-strap gave]
[Knot: one, small. Owed within days.]

[Knot: paid. Your own bootlace, on the stair.]

A contract has a sheet. A bound spirit or beast has its own window, read by its holder. Its Level grows, its terms show, and it can evolve, or be stolen by contract:

[Bound: Old Sorrel, marsh-spirit · Level 41]
[Terms: one drowned thing at each new moon]
[Owed: 2]

At the Thresholds

A window earns its keep at the big moments.

A Level. Level moves by danger overcome, so killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills, and crews have come to knives over who got the kill. The window settles that for the person holding it, and for nobody else.

[Level: 61 to 68]
[Trait: Stays Tied, Level 12 to 15]
[Skill: Knots, C to B]

A technique jumping. Techniques show no level, so the window marks a jump by the name. It comes when the skill under the technique has gained a letter and a fight or a scene has pushed the technique past its old limit, and it gives one thing: power, reach, speed or a lower cost, sometimes with a condition attached. The new name arrives in capitals, and the old name stays on the sheet and in use.

[Skill: Brawling, C to B]
[Short Right Hand is now THE RIGHT HAND OF THE RIVER WARD]
[Gained: reach · Condition: both feet planted]
[Short Right Hand: kept]

Waking. The commoner’s little sheet opens out. Most souls that wake are taught to, as The Gate explains, and the rest get there by crisis or out of a bottle. A Waking Draft is very dear and often kills or warps a child, and most of the rich buy one for their children anyway, while commoners sell themselves into guild debt for it. Those who come through read something like this:

[Crystal: open]
[Stage I, Murmuring]
[Reserve: 100% ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓]
[Vitality F · Ardency Hollow · Gnosis F · Dexterity F]

A Class. The soul’s offer, with its names, at Glory and each time the leading ladder climbs a rung after that. The offer comes through the lead layer, so the names lean the way the road does: a Body soul is mostly offered names of work and matter, a Spirit soul names of souls and meanings, a Fate soul names of bonds, debts and odds, and every so often the soul offers something from clean off the road and enjoys the silence. A Fate-led soul, whose Thread rung waits on grip and decades, gets its offer when whichever of its three ladders stands highest climbs. The other ladders move without ceremony, as one numeral changing on the line. See Classes and the Three Ladders.

[Threshold reached: Essence III, Confessor]
[Your life has earned:]
  Auditor
  Rope-Walk Clerk
[Choose.]

A breakthrough. A lived one is loud and transforms you with the window lighting up, and a bought one is a Draft in a quiet room with a physician standing by. A lived one passes the Great Work in its own road’s terms, nigredo, albedo, citrinitas and rubedo: the soul broken down, purified, woken and fixed. Crisis, seclusion and tribulation all still happen, each in the form of the soul’s own road: the Catalyst is met in the layer the soul leans on, seclusion is kept on that Path’s own ground, and the bought kind is poured by Path, on Salt for Body, Core stock for Spirit and the Layer reagent for Fate. The Sixteen Stages gives every crossing three ways under The Climb by Path. The sheet shows the same Stage in both cases, and that has started more fights than any other line on it.

[STAGE VIII: TRANSCENDENCE]
[Crystal: melted and rebuilt, layer by layer]
[Ceiling lifted · Points to place: ▓▓░░░░░░░░]
[Trait: Banked Coal, Level 41 to 47]

[Stage VIII, Transcendence]
[Ceiling lifted · Points to place: ▓▓░░░░░░░░]
[Physician: present]

Taverns tell the second block as a joke, and the last line is the joke.

A Release or a Domain. Calling a Release fills the window with its name and what changed, and a timer bar starts to drain. The same goes for a Domain, the space where a soul’s own law outranks the world’s settled habits. Both are big and loud on the window for the owner alone, whatever the room is doing, and the two-Grade cap stands inside them. What the room is doing depends on the road, and Releases and Domains by Path has that.

[FIRST RELEASE: THE LONG YOKE]
[Ardency: top of band]
[Charge: 47% ▓▓▓▓▓░░░░░]
[Holds: ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░]

[DOMAIN: THE WHOLE SHIFT]
[Changed: within the yard the bell decides who may rest]
[Ardency: two Grades up · cap reached]
[Held: ██████░░░░]

How a Blessing Lies

A blessing grants a power out of a person’s natural reach, even in Level. It shows on the sheet as its own line, and that line can lie, because the giver wrote it. Every other line on a window is the soul reporting on itself. This is the one line on it with an outside author.

King Godric Fenn of Pennycrown, on the night his cousin’s army reached the walls, prayed at a spring he had been told to leave alone, and woke with this:

[Blessing: Favor of the Spring]
[You will not be unseated.]

He wept for joy and rode out, and he was not unseated. Lances broke on him, and he stayed in the saddle with the horse going down under him twice. He rode home victorious and found he could not get off. The horse died in the courtyard eleven days later, and when his men had cut him free of it and set him on the throne to recover, he found he could not get off that either. He has held it for twenty-three years, on a great many cushions, and he has outlived four sons who tried to take it from him. His window still reads Favor.

A curse is often a blessing with terms you did not read, or from a giver who does not care what you want. Nobody can tell at first, and some are both. The sailor’s Favor of the Tide-Mother says You will not drown, and every sailor who has had that line will tell you it is true. Read it again.

Those who hold Hataraki, the Moto’s governing power, can take a blessing back or remove it, which is one reason people are so afraid of them. It takes two Works or two Moto, and the line simply goes:

[Blessing: removed]

When Your Own Window Lies

The soul does not lie to its owner of its own accord, with the one exception above. A strong mind-worker can. The arts that do it run on the Wellsprings of memory, sleep and reflection (Letheveil, Hypnather, Mirithane and their neighbors), and a strong enough hand there can reach the report itself: show false numbers, hide a line, tell a man at nine percent that he has plenty.

It stays a violation with a cost, on the victim and on the user both, and every realm’s law around it is an argument. Gnosis gives a fair counter. A stronger Perception notices that the report has stopped matching the soul, and everyone has the plain counter of their own body, since a window can say eighty percent and your lungs will still tell you the truth. Veterans who have fought mind-workers count their breath against their reserve line, and anyone who has lost a friend that way does it for life.

Reading Other People

A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward, by Grade, and everything else is an estimate said aloud. Your own soul gives you the result as a window, with its gaps showing:

[Read: Dagny Thorn · Level 188 · Stage VII]
[Charge: 3% · Vitality 44%]

And the same read from a weaker eye:

[Read: ??? · Level ??? · Stage ?]
  • The trained talk about it constantly. Fighters and onlookers rank each other aloud, read shoulders and breath, and argue over it through the whole fight. Stage IX? Look at his feet. That’s a VII in a good coat. All of it is estimate, and a confident wrong one has won as many fights as a right one.
  • People hide and fake it. Holding Pressure in, thinning the coat, walking like a tired man and flaring an aura brighter than the reserve behind it can pay for are all arts. Suppression and Feint are real Sub-Stats, and a person with a high Ward can stand in a crowded room and be nobody.
  • Instruments read everyone. Stand in the coil and the clerk writes down your Stage, Level, Grade, ladders and yield, which is more than your own window will tell you. It takes the fee and your standing in the coil, and it will not read your blessings or your class name.
  • Monsters sit on the same scale. A monster has a Level, Grades and a Threat Tier (the Bestiary’s danger grade, which has nothing to do with an item’s Tier or a guild’s), T1 to T9, each tier standing for a band of Stages. It has Pressure, a Path set by how it lives and rungs on the ladders, and it never takes a class. A weak reader gets question marks, usually at the moment it matters most. A famous one carries a name, an epithet and a bounty on top of it. See The Bestiary.
  • Items take an Appraisal skill. An Appraisal skill and an assay instrument read a piece of gear, and a good forger beats a weak appraiser. True relics of the Great Spirit War read as question marks to anyone, and some have opinions about who holds them.

How People Brag and Lie

Since nobody can see your window, your window is whatever you say it is, until somebody with the right to ask makes you pay for it.

In the street, say what you like. Everyone shouts their Level at the brawl and rounds up. A bare claim costs nothing, and the Bad Debt won a Sweepstakes on one.

On the slate, you pay the bookies’ rate. Odds-men chalk what they can get, and heralds cry their own numbers. The Accord keeps a roll, each realm and sect keeps a list, minstrels get the details wrong in rhyme, and these rankings all disagree. Getting on any of them makes you a target.

At a hiring, they ask for proof. A crew or a lord wants more than your word. They will want an assay slip with a seal on it, which costs a silver mark at the house and can be forged, and forging a token is a great crime, which keeps the price of a good forgery high. A sensible employer also hires a reader, and has you lift something heavy.

At court, you swear. A proper court makes you swear to what your sheet shows. Lying under a proper oath costs you, because an oath is small Fate, and the cost lands on your own window, where only you can see it. Only an assay can check what you said.

[Oath: "Level 140, as my sheet shows." Sworn false.]
[Terms: every chance goes against you until it is unsaid.]

Lawyers fight over the exact wording, and they eat well on it. As my sheet shows and as I am are different oaths. A man with a lying blessing can swear to it in perfect safety, and so can a man whose window has been tampered with. A Bench in the Concord spent four years on whether a false Level sworn on a mind-touched sheet was perjury, and gave up when both advocates were found to have been touched.

At home, you push. Everybody reads their own sheet, and that shapes a society to its roots. Parents push their children from the first Grade. A six-year-old in a Hammerford market will tell you her Sums before her name, because her mother made her practice saying it. Whole villages know whose son has which skill, and matches are made on it, on the parents’ word.

Who Built It

The oldest quarrel in the Measurewrights’ hall, carried on at weddings, in long letters and on two occasions with knives.

The Null School holds that nobody built it. The Crystal is the soul, the Continuum measures it, and a thing measured and a thing aware of being measured will report. The window is what it feels like to be a soul with a bill. This is the position of most working Measurewrights, chiefly because it requires no further funding.

The Author School points at the tongues. Bridge-slang from Kingsbridge turns up on a Kingsbridge docker’s window within the year. A report that keeps up with slang, they say, has someone doing the translating, and it uses complaining for a back. They have also noticed that blessings prove a window can be written on from outside, and they would like to know who wrote the rest. They have no candidate they will sign their names to, and three they will mention after dark.

The Mirror School answers that the slang is the owner’s own. The soul speaks to itself in the only words it has, and nothing on the window has ever told anybody something they could not, in principle, have felt. This view is strong among healers. It is weak among anyone who has read a skill they did not know they had.

The old beings have been no help. A Measurewright’s expedition once asked a very old thing in a very old battlefield who had built the window. After some thought it asked what a window was. Nobody present could show it one.

Every faith has its own answer, and real wars come of it. Moto speak to their dead, and many hold that the numbers are their ancestors’ reckoning of them, kept as carefully as the family altar. The Marrowkin tally their dead, and they see no mystery here: it is the hold’s Tally, entered by the one in whom it is kept. Kharven price theirs, and a Kharven priest will tell you that somebody holds your note, and that you will meet him. Sancta Lux, which claims jurisdiction over oath and status, treats a window as a sworn witness inside you, and will ask you what it said. The Verdant Orcs regard the sheet as rings in a tree, and think it strange that anyone needs it read. And the river towns of Hallenfeld fought the towns of the Golden Hollow for six summers over whether a priest could lawfully require a child to read her window aloud at confirmation, in a war that killed some eleven thousand people and ended with both sides still doing what they had been doing.

The Archives take no position. They note that the window has been asked, by a great many people, in a great many tongues, and it reports only that their reserve is down.
