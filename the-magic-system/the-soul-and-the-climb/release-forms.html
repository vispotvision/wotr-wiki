Release Forms

Release Forms

Nov 28, 202634 min read

How a practitioner turns their own Trait loose, in a form the whole room can see. For the whole system in brief, see How Magic Works.

The Widow Ansa Brack kept the Three Keys at Sallow Ferry for thirty-one years, and the song says she never once raised her voice before the night the Tollgate Free Company came in without paying. The song is wrong, as songs usually are, because the whole town knew that voice. A hundred and forty men in red-and-yellow quarters filled her yard with their horses and their opinions, Harry Stott leaned on her doorpost and told her what he thought the ale was worth, and she came out onto the step with a slop pail and said what she had said at closing every night of her married life.

"EVERYBODY OUT."

Her hands went first, black as old door iron to the wrist. The ring of keys at her belt rose and stood straight out on the air, jangling, and her aura slammed to its full color, a silver shot through with deep violet that lit every face in the yard from underneath. The crowd has always called that one Door-Iron. Then one of the keys turned, by itself, in nothing.

The light went violet from wall to wall, and every man in that yard felt the ground decide he had no business standing on it. They left. They left through the gate, through the hedge and through the side of the stable, in one long bright spill of armor and horses that came down across the river meadow and broke four dozen collarbones. Harry Stott, who had a good grip, went with the rest and left his hand on the doorpost, where it stayed, and it hung over the bar for the next sixty years. That was the Second, the Outgang, a field that ruled the ground of her yard and the weight of everything standing on it.

The bill came with the law, as the big ones do. A landlady is the last one out of a house she closes, and Ansa Brack closed that one with herself on the step. She never set foot inside the Three Keys again. She stood on that step for nineteen years and served through the window. She walked the Body road, which can hold three releases, and the minstrels gave her the Third she never had, an error that has cost several people money.

A child can say the whole of it: she tells you to leave, and you leave. What follows is how a Guild Measurewright says it, at somewhat greater length.

What it is

A release is a practitioner’s Primary Trait (the deepest standing law in their soul) let off the safety limit of the Soul Crystal (the soul itself, in its working form) on purpose, in a shape the Crystal has learned to hold: the same force as an overchannel (forcing the Crystal past its safe ceiling), rehearsed until it keeps a form, a price and a name. A technique is something the practitioner makes with the Trait; a release changes the practitioner, and every technique fired inside it runs through the released Trait.

One idea, three sizes. Trait, Release and Domain are a single concept escalating. The Trait is the idea, the Release is the idea worn on a transformed body, and the Domain is the world obeying it. A kit that reads as three unrelated powers has been filed wrong, and any Measurewright will tell you so for free. See The Trait System for where the idea comes from.

The road sets the count. The Path a soul leads on decides how many releases it can ever have, and each is deeper than the one before. The Body has three, at IV, VII and IX. The Spirit has two, at IV and VII. Fate has one, the One, at Refraction, deeper than any First, and it counts as a Second: it enlarges the claim and the Pressure as a First would, and it lays the field that opens and powers the Domain, as a Second does. Below Refraction a Fate fighter has no release at all, and fights with Range, Aura Pressure, presence, bonds and vows, which grow with the Stage and are quite enough to be going on with. A soul on two roads has its lead road’s count and no more. The road behind colors those releases and brings none of its own.

Each road also spends its own thing and tires in its own way, and Releases and Domains by Path lays the three side by side with a famous case of each.

A bloodline art does not change the road. A Moto’s release follows the layer that Moto leans on, and a Work used inside it is still a pull, paying a pull’s price and leaving a pull’s knot on top of the release’s clock. Neither the release nor the field ever pulls a thread.

The form is free. A release takes whatever shape the person’s soul would take: wings, plate, a changed blade, a beast’s outline, a crown of something that should not burn. A practitioner tempered on Ascensio (the Ladder of Ascent, a Wild Wellspring of rising) may release as a white crane. What the form does still comes from the Trait and its Wellspring (the lens it works through), so the crane’s wings strike as weight and muscle strike, by the world’s old habits, and the rising is the lens’s own business.

The aura. A release drives the aura to the practitioner’s full color at once: the hue of the Wellspring they have tempered on longest, at their Stage’s clarity. At Stage IV that is a smoky flare, charcoal with the hue clouded through it; at Stage VIII it is clear enough to throw light. Where that light falls is the road’s affair. A Body fighter’s flare sits on the skin and lights the room in its own color. A Spirit fighter’s stands a half-step off the body, lights the holder and the struck, and bends the shadows more than it brightens the walls. A Fate fighter’s is a thread of hue at the edge of the eye, and what the room gets is stillness: straight flames and settled dust.

The Domain. The Second Release opens and powers the Domain (a space where the practitioner’s inner law outranks the world’s), and on the Fate road the One does. The flicker of a seed that carries a soul into Stage VII is a one-time burst of the Trait and needs no Release, and it follows the road as the field later will: a Body flicker rules matter, a Spirit one meanings, a Fate one conditions. The Second, or the One, is what lets a seed be called and held. At Stage VII the field is a seed: it lasts minutes and collapses if attention breaks. From Stage VIII it is a true Domain, grown by living and called aloud, held for hours and steady under small disruptions, and anyone at Stage VIII can have one, their own concept made real, with the fame, rivalries and bounties that follow. The Stage grants it, and a Grade never does. The crossing into Stage IX carries a first flicker of a Realm on the same terms, once, on every road, with no release behind it. The Realm itself, a field that can hold other Domains inside it, is the far end of the climb and opens no earlier than Stage X, by each road’s own door (The Realm, below). From Stage XI the first of a soul’s releases no longer needs calling: it is simply how they are.

When fields meet. Stage comes first: a field two Stages up simply overwrites the lower one. Otherwise two release fields, or a field and another’s Pressure (the weight a strong soul puts on a room), contest the ground by Dominion (the stat of authority over ground and presence) before a blow lands. The border shows in the room as light changing color across the floor, bystanders feel both, and the weaker side starts the fight already paying. A read can flip it: find what the field rests on, the owner’s focus, its edge, or the thing they are guarding, press there, and the border moves back.

The window. The Crystal is the soul, and it reports to its owner. When a release fires, the owner’s window (the soul’s report of its own numbers, seen by nobody else) fills with the release’s name and what changed, and a timer bar starts to drain. Percentages, bars and the Grade of what the Trait throws, and never a letter on the owner: the exact figures stay with the assayers.

Nobody else sees it, so the man across from you is left to read the skin, the shimmer and the breath, and he has been known to read them wrong.

The bonded weapon. A weapon its owner has carried for years can carry part of the release: the blade burns with the owner’s heat, the staff holds the edge of the field. Its part is written on the owner’s card, and is lost with the weapon.

Called or silent. The owner chooses. Called, the release’s name goes out as it fires, in its owner’s own tongue, with the meaning landing in the same beat through a witness or a knowing second. A showman bawls it in capitals and a cold killer may barely breathe it, and either way the call costs a beat, buys the full rise, and is a tell everyone within earshot can use. Silent, it costs no beat and gives no spoken tell, but it still flares and still sounds, and it comes in weaker: the form without the call’s rise. The card says exactly what silence costs each release.

Who carries them. Any practitioner with a woken Crystal and an armed Primary Trait; an unwoken soul (the sealed Shell) carries Traits but cannot release them. How many a person can have is the road’s, three on the Body, two on the Spirit and one on Fate, and having the right to one is a long way from having it. Most who fight their way to Stage IV on the Body or the Spirit road earn a First in time. Far fewer earn a Second, or the One. A Third is the mark of a Body fighter the ballads already know. A Stage bought in a Draft or granted in a blessing opens the door and nothing past it, so a rich Stage IX may have no release at all while a poor Body fighter at the same Stage has all three, and there is steady money for bookies in knowing which is which.

What they are called. The Archives say First, Second and Third Release, and on the Fate road the One. Every people has its own street words for the three, as it does for the Stages: bracket crowds in the Common tongue call them going big, going loud and going home, the last because a certain number do not. A person’s own art may count its tiers in its own word, Acts, Verses, Knots, Entries, so that the count itself becomes a threat, and there is no more unpleasant sentence in a duel than “that was the First Knot.” Bloodline faculties release too, by the bloodline’s own law and stages, kept as a family secret and written on the bloodline’s page.

How one is learned. A release must be found and mastered by fighting, and no sum of money has ever changed that. A bought Stage comes without one and no purse opens one. A sect can teach the road to it, and that is much of what a sect is for: the drills, the rites for its Wellspring, the sparring ladder, and elders who will hurt you in the right places. Like the Trait, the shape itself is survived into being, and the shape that comes is the fighter’s own. Each release has its own Catalyst (What Opens Each One, below), and it is reached by one of three roads. Crisis is the commonest road to a First Release: the Trait breaks its limit in a fight the practitioner should have lost, and the Crystal keeps the shape. Seclusion is the commonest road to a Second and to the One: months working the Trait alone on what the last fights showed, until it claims the ground around them, and then back out to prove it against someone who means it, because a field that has never been contested will not hold. The ground is chosen by road. A Body fighter secludes on Aetheric Density, a mine head or a wild valley, where the Shell can be packed and emptied all day. A Spirit fighter goes into Echo Depth, a charnel house, an old temple or the home she grew up in. A Fate fighter sits under Thread Weight, on a battlefield or at a crossroads, and the sects that own such ground charge for the bench. Tribulation is how every Third arrives: the Wellspring’s own Aether comes down on the flesh of a Body fighter and tests the claim before it agrees to share the law, and not everyone survives the test. The first time a release holds, it prints on the Crystal, and the transformation is the kind people remember seeing. Command of it comes after that, in bout after bout, as the time on the bar lengthens and the price shrinks toward what the card says.

It deepens with the person. A release keeps its core for life but grows new aspects as its owner advances, survives and changes, and a great turn in a life can reshape it. Each new aspect is added to the card when it first shows.

What Opens Each One

A Stage has its Catalyst, and so does every release, and the benches spend a good deal of breath keeping the two apart. Five things hold for all of them.

  • The release’s own Stage first. A release Catalyst counts only once the soul stands on the release’s Stage: IV for a First, VII for a Second and for the One, IX for a Third. The same deed done a year earlier is a story and opens nothing.
  • One deed, one door. A deed meets one Catalyst and no more. Where it would answer both a release and the next Stage’s crossing, the crossing takes it, because the crossing is the louder demand, and the release waits for another day. Every Expert who hopes to buy two doors with one bad night is told this, and most of them try anyway.
  • Met under contest. Every release Catalyst is met against something that means it: a fighter, a beast, a flood, a court, another field. The deed is the trial, and the shape that prints is the fighter’s own.
  • Never bought. No Draft and no blessing has ever supplied a release Catalyst. A purse buys a Stage’s door and nothing past it.
  • Overgrown waits. A release Catalyst met while the Crystal is Overgrown prints on the stone and will not let the release out until Level reaches the Stage’s hold. The fighter knows it is there and cannot reach it, which is its own kind of education.

The Body’s First: the body broken, refusing to fall

The body breaks in a fight, truly breaks, bone or joint or the Shell itself, and the fighter stays on their feet and goes on acting with what is broken, by their own will and nobody else’s, until the fight ends one way or the other.

A quarry foreman at Adept takes a mace across the forearm, hears the bone go, and keeps the gate with the other hand and the broken one tucked against his ribs until the raiders give it up. That meets it. A caravan guard has her knee smashed by a drake’s tail, fights the rest of the bout on one leg and a spear butt, and loses, and that meets it too, because winning was never asked. A duelist with a cracked collarbone who is held up by his second while he finishes the bout fails: somebody else refused for him. So does the fighter whom a vow holds on his feet, since the Crystal reads the vow and not the body.

The room sees the break, a limb wrong or a rib line gone, and a fighter who does not sit. The Charge comes up through the break as a smoky flare of the base hue packed tight against the skin, the heat over the broken place plain to see, and the people watching go quiet, because they have all seen a broken man fall and this one is not doing it. Ever after, the First very often comes up first in the limb that broke.

The Body’s Second: a material known so well it obeys at range

One material the fighter knows to the grain, stone or iron, timber, bone, river water or the salt in blood, obeys them across open ground with no hand or weapon on it, in a contested fight, at their word and on purpose.

A smith of thirty years, a Journeyman with the sword and a Master at the anvil, makes an opponent’s mail go heavy and tight across the yard and fights him inside it. A mason at Refraction, in a siege breach, tells the fallen stones of the wall twenty paces off to stand, and they stand while the storming party is still coming over them. Both meet it. The tavern fighter whose pints fell upward for four seconds when he was pressed past holding does not: that was the seed that carried him into Refraction, unaimed and unwanted, and it opens nothing else. Nor does the smith who moves the mail with her hand on it, or through her hammer’s contact, which is an older rung and asks nothing new. A trade at Journeyman or better on the material makes the deed easier and is never required.

The material moves first, before the field: the stone stands, the mail cinches, the water in the ditch goes still and hard. Then the Charge leaves the skin and runs out along the ground in the fighter’s hue, and the Measurewrights in the stands start writing, because the line it draws is where the Domain will one day stand.

The Body’s Third: the body remade around a wound

In the tribulation, when the Wellspring’s own Aether comes down on the flesh to test the claim, the fighter takes a new wound from it and does not heal back to the body they had. The body is remade with the wound as part of its plan, the Wellspring sharing the work, and the fighter fights in that body before the day is out.

A Fractura fighter’s tribulation opens a seam of light down his sword arm that never closes; the arm is rebuilt along it, and his Third comes up through that seam every time. A Pyraeon hunter’s burns her hands to the bone. She lets no physician touch them, and when the burning stops her hands are forge-colored and hold heat as a crucible does, and she takes a contract the next morning with them. Both meet it. The Master whose tribulation wound a priced mender closed clean, the body left as it was, survived the test and kept nothing of it, and the Crystal has nothing to build a Third around. The fighter who offers up an old wound to be remade has mistaken the floor: an old wound made to bear weight is Transcendence’s work, done already or still owed, and the Third asks a new one, and asks to share it with the Wellspring.

The room sees weather that has found its target, the ground around one body taking the lens’s look, and then a body that should have been destroyed standing up changed, the wound lit at its edges in the Wellspring’s second color, the color that will rim the Third’s field from then on.

The Spirit’s First: the self said aloud, and true

The soul says what it is, aloud, in its own words, to someone or something that denies it and has the power to make the saying false, and in the same contest it proves the saying true by what it does.

A temple scribe at Adept, told by a Shepherd of the dead that she is a thief of other people’s grief, answers that she is a keeper of it, and then keeps a dead child’s mother from the thing that came for her. A sellsword called a coward for nine years tells the man who said so that he is a man who runs to save his own and will not run today, and does not. Both meet it. The young thrower who declares herself the finest in the province before a bout and is beaten by an honest Journeyman fails, because the saying was a boast and the fight proved it untrue. So does a true saying with nobody in the room able to deny it: a self said to friends has met no contest.

The benches keep it apart from two neighbors. Flourishing’s own Catalyst for a Spirit soul is being known, another soul seeing this one whole, shame and all, and not putting it down, and that is received; the First is given, declared outward at risk. Refraction’s seed comes when a soul faces aloud a truth about itself it did not want to know, which is a confession of what it found, where the First is a declaration of what it is. One deed meets one of them, never two.

Very little shows in the room and a great deal in the people. The aura rises a half-step off the body in its full hue, the one who denied finds the words have gone into him past any armor he wore, and bystanders remember the sentence for years and quote it slightly wrong.

The Spirit’s Second: a meaning borne for someone else

The soul takes up a meaning that belongs to someone else, a grief, a name, a last word, a guilt, the sense of an oath, which that person cannot carry, and carries it whole in their name, in its true sense and not its own, while something contests it: a power that wants the meaning broken, changed, buried or taken.

A sin-eater’s daughter at Refraction eats the bread over a murdered magistrate and takes his last ruling into her Core, and when his killers come to the burial to have it unsaid, she says it, his words and not hers, and it stands. A soldier carries the meaning of his dead captain’s surrender, given to save the company, through a court that wants it called treason, and holds it as mercy, which is what it was. Both meet it. A widow who carries her own husband’s death as her own grief and makes it bear weight has done Transcendence’s work, because the meaning was hers. The bearer who carries another’s meaning and tells it as she would have liked it to be fails too: the Crystal reads the bending and counts nothing.

This is the deed that opens the Domain on the Spirit road, and the benches say why: a field of meanings is a soul holding what things mean for everyone who stands in it. Nothing moves when it is met. The bearer’s aura takes something of the other person’s coloring at its edge, people who knew the one whose meaning is carried feel for a moment that they are in a room with them, and then the line of the field runs out along the floor, and inside it the meaning is simply so.

Fate’s One: a claim staked on your life, and kept

The soul names aloud a claim over something outside itself, a ground, a gate, a people’s standing, what may happen in a place, with its own life as the forfeit, against a contestant who has the power to take the claim and the life together, and keeps the claim, unyielded, until the contest ends.

A toll-reeve at Refraction stands at the middle of her bridge and says that nothing crosses it armed while she lives, to a company that can kill her, and holds it through the night. She meets it whether they turn back or she is carried off the bridge alive at dawn with the claim still standing. A bonded guardian claims a child’s right to her mother’s seat before a house that wants the child dead, stakes his life on it in their own hall, and keeps it through the season they spend trying to break it. A Fate fighter who holds the same bridge by pulling a thread, so that the company’s horses go lame on the approach, has made a pull and not a claim: it leaves a knot and counts for nothing. So does the claim staked and then yielded at the last hour to save the claimant’s life, however wise that was.

A vow is a word about one’s own act, and Refraction’s own Fate seed is a vow held for a few seconds at the moment it ought to break. The One is a claim over something outside the self, held for the whole of a contest, and it has exactly the shape of a Domain, which is why it opens and powers one. When it is met the dust lies flat from the claimant’s feet to the edge of what was claimed, flames stand straight, and the people inside it find they have stepped back to give it room before anyone told them to. When the contest ends with the claim still standing, the stillness stays on that ground for a day, and animals will not cross it.

The Realm

The crossing into Invocation carries a first flicker of a Realm, once, needing no release, on every road. The Realm itself is the far end of the climb: a field that can hold other fields inside it, still running, with their owners in them. No release makes one. Every road reaches it late, by its own door, from Realization at the earliest: the Body through the Third, the Spirit through its Second grown whole, held as a true Domain for years, and Fate through the One. Each door has its own Catalyst, met under contest and never bought, and a bought Stage X has no door at all. Releases and Domains by Path gives the three in full, with what each Realm is, where it breaks and what it costs.

The shape spreads the strain. Because the release is a learned shape, the Crystal carries it without structural damage, provided it is held inside its limit. Past the limit it is a raw overchannel again.

  • The call costs a beat every time.
  • Its own price. A big release also charges in the coin of its own idea, on top of reserve and body tells, and the card says how. A landlady ends up on the step. A heat release blisters the hands that carry it.
  • Outside a fight a release may be held at the same cost: a First Release worn through a court audience to make a point, a Second spread over a winter camp to keep the snow off. The clock runs the same.
  • On the stack a release counts toward the cap of two steps on the hidden scale over the sustainable figure on the card, which is read with the gear off, and a Third already stands at it for its one effect, so nothing more stacks there. Only outside help goes past the cap, and a blessing counts as outside help, a point Measurewrights have settled several times.

How it is beaten

Most releases can be answered by someone at their own Stage, before, during and after, though none is owed such an answer: a release keeps the flaw its idea gives it. Under each way below lie the four answers the benches teach: the contrary idea, a soul further refined in the same idea, ground that holds another idea, and a refusal of the claim.

  • Before. A called release costs a beat, and the aura flares and the sound comes before the power rises. A fighter who reads the tell can strike into that beat, or get out of reach. A silent release skips the beat but still flares, still sounds, and comes in weaker.
  • During. The clock is the counter, and it runs on a bar only the owner can see. A release spends reserve fast and ends at a tenth, so outlasting it is a real plan: keep distance, make them chase, and fight on ground that raises their cost: cold and wet against heat, shallow ground with no memory in it against a Spirit release, light ground where nothing was ever decided against a Fate one, and salted, bled or thin ground (ground with little Aether to pull) against anyone, since a reserve refills only from what the ground can give. Rich ground cuts one way that surprises people: it fills a Fate fighter as it fills anyone, and it crowds her Range and the reach of her field while it does. A Second Release or the One at Stage VII collapses when attention breaks: a wound, a shout, a name that matters to them. Every field has an edge; step out of it. Contest the ground with a stronger Dominion, or read what holds it up. Take the bonded weapon and its part of the release goes with it.
  • After. The aftermath is a window. A First Release leaves them spent; a Second, the One and a Third leave the Crystal spilling and easy to read.

This is how a lower-Stage fighter beats a higher one across one Stage: survive the release, read it, and strike in the aftermath.

Example

Corra Vell is a Stage VII hired hunter on the Body Path, harmonized to Pyraeon (the Ember Crown, a Wild Wellspring and so at home on her road), with a heat Primary Trait. Her color is Pyraeon’s banked ember-orange at Refraction’s clarity: gray and orange sharing one field without mixing. She has fought eleven years with the same forge hammer, and it is bonded. She found Kiln Skin in a bad fight in a culvert in her fifth year, and Smelter’s Yard over one winter of hard contracts and a hard teacher. Her card carries two releases:

KILN SKIN (First Release). She says “Kiln Skin” and her skin goes the dull cherry-orange of iron at forging heat, from the hands up over the forearms to the collarbones, and the air around her shimmers like the air over a forge bed. There is a sound like a bellows drawn once, hard. Every blow she lands carries forge heat into the wound, and her heat workings strike at the top of their Grade. Her bonded hammer takes the heat with her: its head glows the same cherry-orange and keeps it, and the forge heat in her hammer blows is the hammer’s share, lost if the hammer is. From a full reserve it holds about six minutes, and it ends on its own when her reserve falls to a tenth; afterwards her palms are blistered and she cannot pull heat for an hour. Released silently, it takes the color and the shimmer but not the call’s rise, and her heat workings strike at the middle of their Grade, not the top. The counter is in the beat of the call, which can be struck into, in cold and wet ground, which makes every second cost more, and in the blistered hands afterwards, which cannot hold a weapon properly.

SMELTER’S YARD (Second Release). She says “Smelter’s Yard” and the heat leaves her skin and fills the ground around her, about the size of a courtyard: the air goes forge-hot and dry, the light turns orange, and everyone inside feels the color change as a pressure behind the eyes. The bellows sound becomes a low, steady roar for as long as it holds. This is her Domain seed, and inside it her heat workings strike one full Grade higher. From a full reserve it holds about three minutes, and it collapses at once if her attention breaks; afterwards her Crystal runs Overgrown until she sleeps, and heat leaks off her with her temper. Released silently, the yard forms without its first roar and her workings strike at the top of their own Grade inside it, not one higher. The counter is to break her focus with a wound, a shout or a name that matters, to step out past the edge of the yard, to contest its ground with a stronger Dominion, and to strike while her spilling Crystal is easy to read.

On a dockside job Corra meets a Stage VII duelist tempered on Judicium (the Wellspring of Truth, which runs arc-blue) who opens his own field, a ring of arc-blue that makes every hair in the yard stand up. She answers with Smelter’s Yard, and the two fields meet in a seam across the cobbles where orange light and blue light grind against each other. His Dominion is higher, and the seam starts walking toward her. Then she reads him: his field is centered on his left hand, which never moves from his belt. She throws the hammer at it. He flinches to guard the hand, his attention breaks, and his field goes out like a doused lamp, with the slap of hot air and flying cobbles that every collapse pays, a Resonance Shockwave of the small kind, which puts him on his back. The orange floods the yard to the water’s edge. Only she can see the rest of it:

[SECOND RELEASE: SMELTER'S YARD]
[Heat workings: one Grade up]
[Field: holding · his has collapsed]
[Time: ████░░░░░░ about a minute left]
[Reserve: 47%]

She is going to need her hammer back.
