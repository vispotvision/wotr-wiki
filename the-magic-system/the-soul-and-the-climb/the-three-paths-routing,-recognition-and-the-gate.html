The Three Paths: Routing, Recognition and the Gate

The Three Paths: Routing, Recognition and the Gate

Nov 17, 202648 min read

You choose what you spend your life on. A Path is the name for that choice, said back to you by your own Crystal (the Soul Crystal, which is the soul itself), some years after it stopped being reversible.

The Door Wager of Kell Harbor has been lost and won again in every bracket tavern in the four quarters. Lord Ormund Rusk, drunk on his own birthday, staked four thousand silver marks that no Stage VI fighter alive could get through his four new sea-gates, nine inches of banded bronze apiece with his arms on them in blue and gold, and the harbor turned out in its thousands to watch four people try.

Big Una Ostrick of the Red Anvils walked up to the first gate and hit it, and every gull on the harbor went up at once, and hit it again, and on the ninth blow the bronze bowed in like a kicked pail and the hand she did it with came apart in five places, and she held the hand up to the crowd and roared. Master Bai Lüxian of the Nine Inkstones stood sixty paces off the second gate in a green robe and wrote on it from there, a line of characters burning across the metal until the hinges forgot what they were for. He shifted not an ounce of the bronze and could not have: the gate came open under its own weight, and he was the only one of the four who never had to touch it. Sweet Robin Bellamy spoke to the keeper of the third gate for the length of a cup of wine, and the man opened up, wept, and later named a daughter after him. The fourth was Hyeon Do-sun, a thin porter from the Mahuo country whom nobody had bet on, who had sworn at nineteen that he would walk through that harbor’s gate on the day his brother’s ship came home. He sat down in front of the gate and did nothing at all. The crowd laughed until dusk and then went to bed. On the third morning the watch hauled that gate open for a battered cog with a man at the rail that everyone had believed drowned for seven years, and Hyeon got up and walked through. He had let his mother die without him to keep that vow. One-Ear Pask, who held the book, paid out on three and refused the fourth, and was still refusing it in front of a magistrate twenty years later.

Four gates and three roads. The bracket crowds have four words, hitters, throwers, talkers and stayers. The Crystal has three: Body, Spirit and Fate. A talker and a stayer are on one road, and the whole difference between Sweet Robin and Hyeon Do-sun is how far down it they had walked.

Anyone who takes from this that Fate is the road for sitting still should ask the levies who marched on Akagawa, twenty thousand spears against a Moto host of three thousand. The bridge they were counting on had a rotten pile that nobody had looked at. The general’s gray threw a shoe on the one morning it mattered. The wind came round in the hour the archers loosed, the third company heard a rumor that was very nearly true, and by noon an army that had not yet lost a single fight was running exactly where an old woman under a plain banner had spent two days arranging for it to run. Nobody on her side had raised their voice. Then they went down the riverbank and killed them, and four of them carried her home, and she did not walk again or know her own daughters. She had given eleven years to being able to do that once.

I. What a Path Is

A soul meets the world through three layers, and every soul has all three, the unwoken included. Each layer sits on its own plane, and each is a different way of touching what is there.

LayerPlaneWhat it doesThe Path that leans on it
Aether ShellThe Physical WorldKnows and works physical reality: matter, force, heat, the blow and the blockBody
Essence CoreThe Soul PlaneHolds identity, memory and Traits, and works spiritual reality: souls, spirits, the dead, what a thing meansSpirit
Attraction LayerThe Plane of FateA soul’s own share of fate, and its reach into the outer part of reality: what has to be so before anything can happenFate

There are two realities and one thing that is not. The three Epochs stand over the three roads. Aegor built Physical Reality, and Ashura is Spiritual Reality, and those are the lower realities: everything that is, is in one or both. Bara stands over the third, and a careful scholar stops calling it a reality at this point. Fate is everything that flows: every possibility, every idea, every concept, anything and everything that can happen in the lower realities, before it has. The Guild says “Plane of Fate” as a courtesy, the way one says “the Crown” of a very old and dangerous man. So the Body Path works on what is, the Spirit Path works on who and what it means, and the Fate Path works on what could be.

A Path is the layer a Crystal has learned to lean on, hardened by repetition into the way it meets the world best and, eventually, the way it does so without thinking.

A Path is a route and never a class. The bracket slang says hitter and thrower the way it says tall or left-handed, and crews get built around the mix, but what the soul offers its owner as a named class is a separate thing altogether, the Class, and each Path’s layer has a ladder of its own beside it on that page: Aether Class for the Shell, Essence Class for the Core, Thread Class for the Layer. A Path says how the power meets the world. A class says what the owner has made of their life. Plenty of drunks will argue the matter with you regardless.

What a person calls their temperament has nothing to do with it, and neither does the school on their token. Two practitioners with identical Wellsprings (the types of Aether each has tuned to, each a governing idea that changes how a soul uses its power), identical training and identical Stage will produce different results on the same problem, and the difference is which layer the working went out through, and what that layer spends.

What is being routed

Each road spends a different thing, and that is the first fact about it. Aether (mana, in plain words) is the ocean, thick in some places and thin in others, and it belongs to nobody. Only a body can draw it in, and nobody, in any art or any legend worth believing, has ever steered it in the world without drawing it in first. What happens to it after that is the road.

On the Body Path the Aether is never refined. It is packed into the Shell as it came, the lens shapes it right there, and the Shell drives it out. A Body fighter’s fire is Aether burning in the Wellspring’s shape, and there is no Essence in it. The Core still authors the blow, since the will and the intent are the soul’s, but the Core decides and the Shell does the work. So a hitter spending Charge is spending the world, and what she pays is the price of handling it: the Shell is the athanor, the furnace that never goes cold, and its burning comes off her as heat, the fibers tear, and every command to matter comes back up the arm.

On the Spirit Path the Aether is refined into Essence (spiritual energy), and Essence is the soul’s own substance. Drawn into the Crystal and refined there, it takes on the stamp of whoever refined it, and from then on it is a little more of you than there was. The Essence Core is the center of all identity, and the Crystal is the soul, in so many words, so a Spirit practitioner holding a full reserve is holding herself and one spending it is spending herself. What she loses on the way is paid in the Core, as a hollowing, and none of it is heat. This is why a thrower running on empty stops being entirely the person you knew, and why nobody who has been there calls the reserve a fuel.

On the Fate Path no Thread is stored at all. Thread is Attraction Force pulled down, the pull between things taken hold of where it runs above the world, and it will not keep. A Fate soul’s reserve is Essence, refined in the Crystal exactly as a Spirit soul’s is. A thread is taken hold of, pulled for the one working, and paid for from that reserve, the soul’s own share of fate spent out through the Layer, and what is lost on the way stays on the Layer as a knot the world collects later. A vow is the other thing that spends the soul’s share. It drains the reserve like any working and refills the same way, and it pulls nothing and leaves no knot. So a Fate fighter tops up on Core stock like a thrower, and no bench anywhere pours a Draft of Thread.

One reserve, one number. A card still carries a single figure for what a soul can hold, in EU, and what fills it follows the lead Path: a Body-led soul holds most of it as Charge, and a Spirit-led or a Fate-led soul holds it as Essence. A fighter on both roads refines part of what she draws and packs the rest, and the card gives the split as a share (Charge 70% · Essence 30%), set by how she practices. Charge once packed never becomes Essence, and Essence never goes back to raw. A sealed Shell holds no Charge, which is one more thing waking changes. Measurewrights will tell you at length that the U in EU ought to stand for something else now.

Running dry differs by road. The Guild’s word for it is Starvation, and it has a form for each coin. Below a tenth of the reserve a Body fighter is in Charge Starvation, and the Shell begins drawing on the flesh itself. A Spirit fighter is in Essence Starvation, and the Core begins to fray: a name goes missing, then a year, then whose idea this was. A Fate fighter’s reserve is Essence too, so her Core frays the same way underneath, and on top of it come the tells of her own road: the luck around her curdles, then her name sits wrong in other mouths, and at the very end every knot she owes falls due at once. Section XI sets out how each road flags, so that you can tell them apart from across a yard.

Older books sort souls by an inborn bias toward one element or another. The trade has given that up. What gives a blow its character is the Wellspring a soul has tempered itself on and the Path it comes out by, and both of those can be read off a life.

The lens on each Path

A Wellspring is the lens a soul bases its tempering on, and it shapes what each Path spends. The idea stays the same and the target changes. Take Pyreveil, the fire of renewal. On the Body Path it shapes raw Aether in the Shell: the heat lives in the tissue and the blow burns on contact, and what it burns is matter. On the Spirit Path it shapes Essence in the Core and is thrown, and it burns what a soul means and holds, a grief, a grudge, an attachment, and leaves the coat unscorched. On the Fate Path it is a claim that burns its way through: it burns chances, what a thing could have been, and rivalries and oaths catch around the person who holds it.

Every Wellspring has a home: the Wild ones in the Physical World, the Spirit ones on the Soul Plane, the Wellsprings of Fate on the Plane of Fate, and a few, called Medium, at home on all three. A home is a qualifier and never a lock. Any Path can use any Wellspring. At home it harmonizes cleanly and can reach double its effect after years of mastery. Away from home the resonance is poorer, the cost is higher, and half again is the most anyone has got out of it. The cost is exact: off home, the yield (η: the share of a spend that arrives) falls, that fall multiplies with whatever the ground and the ladder are already taking, and the lost share is paid in the road’s own coin, as heat in a Body fighter’s furnace, as hollowing in a Spirit one, and as a heavier knot on Fate. On two Paths the lens is read working by working, by the layer that working goes out through, so one person can be home with a blow and abroad with a line.

Harmonization, said once, since the trade says it four ways. The lock happens at the Shell, where Aether enters. The tie is held on the Layer, among the soul’s other bonds. The lens does its shaping in the lead layer, whichever that is: raw Aether in the Shell for a Body fighter, Essence in the Core for a Spirit fighter, and for a Fate fighter the claim on the Layer, so that any Thread pulled through that claim takes its shape. A sealed Shell therefore cannot harmonize at all, and a Spirit fighter’s lens does its work in the Core though the tie sits on the Layer and the Aether came in at the skin. A Clouded Crystal, Stages I to IV, can take a lens, at a real risk of tearing the lattice, and from the Faceted tier it is safe, which is why the careful halls make their students wait and the hungry ones do not.

II. Recognition, Not Declaration

A Crystal recognizes Path commitment, and nobody gets to declare it.

The Crystal reads behavioral pattern: the consistent routing of power through one plane’s logic over time. A practitioner who has fought exclusively through Body Path mechanics for three full Stages (rungs of the soul’s sixteen-Stage climb) has genuine Body Path alignment regardless of what they claim, what they were trained as, or what their house registered them under.

And the reverse holds, which is where careers stall. A practitioner who has occasionally used Spirit techniques while routing primarily through physical expression will find Spirit-aligned Sub-Stats (the finer stats beneath each main stat) quietly resisting advancement beyond C-Grade (a middling rank on the F to EX+ scale) until the actual pattern of use reflects commitment. Nothing announces this. There is no refusal, no failure, no diagnosis. There is simply a ceiling that arrives earlier than it should and does not move.

This is the single most misdiagnosed condition in the trade. A practitioner stalled at C-Grade on a Sub-Stat they use constantly assumes insufficient training, insufficient Essence, insufficient talent, and buys accordingly.

What they have is a Crystal that has been watching them for a decade and does not agree with their account of themselves. The instructors who can identify this on sight charge what they charge for exactly one reason.

III. The Three

Body Path. The shortest road and the plainest, and the literal understanding and manipulation of physical reality: a Body master knows what stone will do under load and what heat is doing in a joint the way a mason does, and then makes it do something else. It works raw Aether, packed in the Shell as the Charge and never refined, so its price is the body’s: heat, torn fiber and recoil. A direct multiplicative bonus to Strike Force, Durability and Lifting Strength, running from five percent at early Stages to twenty percent at Stage XII and above. Body Path practitioners at equal Grade and Stage consistently out-hit, out-tank and out-lift their counterparts, and the margin widens with advancement. The trade is Range and Aura Pressure (the felt weight of a practitioner’s power on those near), which is why a Body Path practitioner has to close and everybody who fights them knows it.

That is the road at its start, and Matla, the Zettari’s instrument for addressing why matter remains matter, shows where it goes, though only a Zettari can carry Matla itself and the rest of the world climbs the same ladder by other names. The novice commands matter as an object, the adept through contact, the master through recognition, the sovereign through law, and the rare Totality Architect as a complete system. Those five are rungs of Aether Class, the Shell’s ladder (I object, II contact, III recognition, IV and V law, Ω system), and the one table says which Stage allows which. High enough up, which on that ladder means the rungs of law, a Body fighter’s reach is as far as there is matter to read: your armor gets too heavy to lift, the blade rusts in your hand, the distance between you refuses to close, and proximity alone feels as if the world has got heavier. They still cannot make matter from nothing, and every command comes back as recoil through their own bones and Crystal.

Spirit Path. Everything to do with spiritual reality: souls, spirits, the dead, the Essence in a person and the meaning in a thing. It spends Essence, through the Core. A moderate bonus to Strike Force delivered through projection, five to twenty-five percent, and no bonus at all to force delivered by hand. Some configurations take a slight penalty there. That force at range is force on souls, and on souls only. It knocks a soul out of true, lays dread on a mind or cuts a Core, it goes past plate and Durability as if they were not there, and it leaves no mark on matter: the soul wound is the hurt it deals, and Integrity and Anchoring are what answer it. The Shell is the only door into matter. A Spirit fighter who wants a stone moved moves it through her own Shell, as a Body working at whatever her Aether Class will bear, or does not move it. What the Core can do to a thing is slower and stranger: a line written on it carries the writer’s say on what the thing is taken to be, and that is why Bai Lüxian’s hinges forgot, with every ounce of their bronze where it had been. They hit hardest at range and their force holds coherence across distance where Body Path force does not. Durability bonus is negligible and replaced by precision in defensive deployment, which means they survive through correct response rather than absorption. A Spirit Path practitioner who is caught out of position is in more trouble than anyone else in the same situation.

The road has a climb and a far end, as the others do. The climb is Essence Class: a Listener hears, an Answerer is answered, a Confessor reads a soul whole, a Shepherd holds one, and a Ferryman holds many. The far end is Null-Light, Ashura’s own way of seeing, which the Yukari carry as the Zettari carry Matla and the Moto the Works. Its law is that to be known is to be held. A Regard can hold a dying man where he is, or run a wound through its whole course in a breath, and it gives no fire at all.

Fate Path. The hardest road there is. Fate sits above the two lower realities, and it is what has to be so before anything can happen: the law and the construction of the world itself. Before a blow lands, a strap has to hold or give. Before a wall stands, somebody has to have built it right. Before an army wins, it has to arrive. A Fate Path fighter can pull a thread of that down and move what the thing depends on, so that the result turns up without having been forced, and almost nobody can do it twice in a day. Thread is what this road spends. It is never held, it is paid for from the soul’s own share, and every pull leaves a knot the world collects afterward, on the puller, in its own time. Most of what a Fate fighter does in most fights is plainer, and it is described in the next paragraph.

In plain force they take the smallest direct bonus to Strike Force and Durability and the largest to Range, Aura Pressure, and the interaction between force and Domain (a region where the world runs on the practitioner’s terms) authority. Their projected force degrades least over distance and their presence extends across the largest volume. The ground has a say in how large: rich Aetheric ground crowds a Fate fighter’s Range and the radius of a Domain, however well it fills the reserve, and heavy Thread Weight (ground where great things were decided) extends both. Inside a formed Domain, Fate Path amplification can exceed the Body Path’s raw advantage outright. Outside it, Body Path remains king of the straight exchange, which is why no Fate fighter who wants to live agrees to one. The far reaches of the Path are gated by permission, and output never enters into it: Persistence, Continuity, Ceiling, Sovereignty, the Fate Sub-Stat itself, the far reaches of Ward, Gravity, Sense and a bloodline’s Maturity, and at the end of everything Throne, which requires Stage XIII, cannot be advanced below EX-Grade, and is the only Sub-Stat in the system with no practical upper boundary on this side of Apotheosis.

Old registers and old gate tables list a fourth Path, Attraction. They were looking at the shallow end of Fate and calling it a road of its own, as anyone might who had never met someone from the deep end. Read an Attraction gate in an old table as a Fate gate.

How each beats each

There is no better Path. Every tavern has a man who will prove otherwise for the price of a drink, and every one of them has lost money on it. Each road is good, each has plain limits, and each of the three has a way of killing each of the other two.

Its limits
BodyNeeds existing substance. Must close until very high up. Every command comes back as recoil. Has no purchase on a soul
SpiritNeeds a soul or a meaning to work on, and its thrown force marks no matter. A fragile body. Dies out of position

Body beats Spirit by getting there. A spear does not care what you mean. A working takes time to author, the distance is shorter than the Spirit fighter would like, and a dense Shell outlasts a thrown one. Spirit beats Body by going past the Shell. Armor and Durability do not cover the soul, a matter-reader has nothing to read on one, and the Spirit fighter has seen the charge from sixty paces and is somewhere else.

Body beats Fate with force and soundness. Nothing moves the odds on an avalanche. A well-kept fighter with good gear and no bad habits offers no strap to give, and a straight exchange ends before the pull can set. Fate beats Body through what closing depends on: the ground, the gear, the hour, the footing. The hardest hitter alive is no use if the blow never arrives.

Spirit beats Fate by reaching the hand on the thread. Every pull is made by a soul, it shows on the Core before it lands, and a wounded soul loses its grip. Fate beats Spirit because Spirit survives by being right. One wrong read or one spoiled stroke is enough, and the meanings a Spirit fighter works with came down from Fate in the first place.

Stage, ground and preparation decide which of these holds on the day. A wide enough Stage gap beats all of it, and a good fighter of any road walks onto a field already knowing who they do not want to meet.

This is why some Paths are combined, whatever it costs (see section V). Body and Spirit is a hitter who cannot be gone around. Body and Fate is a blow that always arrives. Spirit and Fate reads the thread and moves it. Each pair is somebody who got tired of losing to the same person.

Which layer reads what

The Core authors everything, and the layer it lands through sets the rung. Every working starts in the Core, because the Core is who is saying it, and that holds for a Body fighter’s raw Aether as much as for a thrower’s Essence: the Core decides and the Shell does the work. What it arrives on decides which of the three ladders the clerk reads: matter reads Aether, a soul or a meaning reads Essence, a condition reads Thread. An art’s Path line picks the ladder, and a true pull always takes Thread on top of whatever else is being paid.

A ladder sets the band a working’s yield falls in, and Tempering Clarity sets the place inside that band, which is why a Clarity held at C by an unopened gate costs a Body fighter a few parts in a hundred and never a rung. A Ø on the line means nothing gets out of that layer on purpose, apart from the exceptions in this table. It does not mean the layer is empty, as every soul has all three.

IV. The Gates

Every Sub-Stat carries a Path Alignment requirement, and the requirement is a pair: a Path, and a Temperance Stage (a rung of the soul’s sixteen-Stage climb) by which commitment to it must be demonstrated. Below that threshold the Sub-Stat advances normally. Above it, it does not advance at all.

Fortitude needs Body at Stage III to pass C. Alacrity needs Spirit at Stage III to pass C. Projection needs Fate at Stage III to pass C. Persistence needs Fate at Stage VII to pass S. The Stage in each pair is the Path Stage of that road (section V), and the Fate Path’s climbs with the soul like any other. Roughly a third of the register is open advancement with no gate, which is what makes a broadly competent practitioner possible and what makes an exceptional one so narrow. Attunement is among the open ones, since everybody tunes to a Wellspring, while Empathy, Breadth, Radius, Sovereignty and Gravity stay behind Fate gates. Working quietly is open too: a Body fighter hides a working through Compression with no gate at all, and the Fate gate at Stage VII covers only the hiding of a pull or a claim.

The points follow the road as well. Life spends most of a soul’s stat points by what it does, and what it does is mostly what its lead layer does, so a hitter’s sheet fills in Ardency, Vitality, Dexterity and Tempering, a thrower’s in Gnosis and Harmonics, and a Fate walker’s in Dominion and Resilience, and the grant a Threshold hands over leans the same way.

A character sheet read properly is a biography. The gated Sub-Stats a practitioner has cleared say what they have actually spent their life doing, and the ones they have not cleared say what they told themselves they were doing instead.

V. One Path, Two or Three

A soul has one life’s time to spend, and a Path is paid for in years. There are three ways of holding one. Studied is what anyone may do with any Path: read it, learn it, gain real knowledge and small uses of it, and open no gates by doing so. Walked means at least three parts in ten of a person’s practice have gone down that road for long enough that the Crystal counts it. Mastered means the gates on that road have stopped being an obstacle at the person’s Stage.

Each road carries a Path Stage on the card: the highest Stage whose gates that Path has opened. Mastered means the Path Stage equals the soul’s Stage, and it is read again at every Threshold, so a master of VII who crosses into VIII stands Walked on her own road until the new gates open, and a bought Stage leaves it exactly where it was. Walked means it stands behind that, with three parts in ten of practice or more. The clerk writes it Body VII Mastered · Fate VI Walked 35%. Practice on the Fate Path is counted in vows kept and bonds held, and in very little else. Its Path Stage climbs with the soul’s Stage like any road’s, gate by gate. Only the Thread rung waits on a grip and decades, so Fate VIII Mastered over Thread Ø is an ordinary line on a card. The base time below is what a soul on one road takes to open each Stage’s gates.

The force bonus and the lens are both applied after the Tier is read, so neither ever moves a Grade’s letter and neither counts on the two-Grade cap. They work inside the letter.

One Path is where almost everyone ends up, and the trade thinks it is correct. You can still read a Domain’s terms if you are a hitter, and most good hitters can. The other roads simply are not the goal.

Two Paths cost more than twice as much, and they leave holes. The soul is learning two things at once, and one is always ahead. A two-Path fighter on a given day may have the hands and not yet the reach, or the reach and not yet the hands, and how bad that is depends entirely on who they are and how many years they have put in. Time closes the gap in the gates. It never closes the gap in force, because a lattice holding two arguments does not set as hard as a lattice holding one. On their own ground a master of one road beats a master of two at the same Stage. Off it they may never find their opponent’s weak side at all, because the thing they read in the first exchange is only half the person.

Three Paths is the thing people say when they mean a waste of a life. It is accepted very nearly everywhere that all three cannot be mastered in one mortal span, and there is simply not the time to perfect three crafts. Anyone trying is looked down on by hall, sect and tavern alike. The Kharven say three roads, no house. Anselm Threeways of the Sallow Reach gave eighty-one years to it, died at C-Grade in everything, and is remembered as a fool by everyone except his students, of whom four reached Stage IX. Whether a being with a good deal more than a mortal span can do it is the sort of question scholars enjoy, because almost nobody can check.

VI. Paths and Traits Are the Same Recognition, Read at Two Scales

This is the part the teaching literature has never joined up, and everything below follows from it.

A Trait forms when lived experience hardens into a statement the Continuum (the lawful fabric of reality) accepts as reliable. It is survived into existence rather than chosen, and the Crystal recognizes it rather than being told.

A Path is recognized the same way, off the same evidence. Consistent routing over time, read as pattern, accepted as commitment.

So a Path is what a soul’s Traits have in common, and no separate thing standing beside them.

Traits form one at a time, each out of its own survived experience. The Path is the direction they turn out to be pointing when there are enough of them to have a direction. It runs the other way too: a Trait levels fastest when it is used through the lead layer, and the mark it leaves on the body follows the road. Nobody notices it happening. It is noticed afterward, in the shape of a gate that opens easily or a gate that will not open at all.

Which is why Path commitment cannot be faked and cannot be bought. A Draft can seed a lattice and carry a soul over a Threshold. It cannot supply ten years of having been the sort of person who does that, and a bought Stage comes with its gated Sub-Stats exactly as shut as they were.

The consequences are immediate and they explain several things the registers record without explaining.

A practitioner’s Path is legible from their Traits before it is legible from their record. A physician reading Trait tissue is reading Path alignment a decade before the Crystal has finished recognizing it, which is why house physicians are paid what they are paid and why their assessments are not shown to the subject.

Traits that point one way produce a specialist. Traits that point three ways produce someone genuinely capable at everything and gated out of the high grades on all of it. This is the mechanism of Tempering Ceiling, stated in the vocabulary of behavior rather than of stats.

VII. Paths and the Seat

A Threshold is a melt, and a melt cools into order only around Traits, because a law the Continuum has accepted does not stop being reliable at temperature. The melt takes the lead layer first and hardest, and so everything about a crossing arrives in that layer’s terms. The Catalyst is one demand to a Stage, met in the flesh by a Body soul, in a soul or a meaning by a Spirit one and in a bond, an oath or the odds by a Fate one, and a soul on two roads meets its lead road’s. Seclusion before it is kept on the road’s own ground: Aetheric Density for the Body, Echo Depth for the Spirit, Thread Weight for Fate. A bought breakthrough is poured on the road’s own base as well. The Sixteen Stages gives every floor three ways.

Seeds that share a Path cool clean. They agree about which plane’s logic the new lattice should favor, and the lattice sets one way throughout. That is the clean seat, and it is why a soul that has only ever done one thing acts before it has finished thinking, narrowly and very deep.

Seeds that pull across Paths cool rough. The lattice records the disagreement permanently and the practitioner comes out with genuine capability on two routes and the whole of their force on neither.

A rough seat is cross-Path Trait architecture, and this is why rough-seated practitioners are unpredictable rather than weak. Nothing in them is damaged: they are two arguments held in one lattice, and an opponent reading them for a Body Path pattern is reading a man who is also, honestly and simultaneously, something else.

The halls cap a rough seat at Tier Four on certification. The halls are protecting the certificate rather than the practitioner, and several of the most feared individuals in the four quarters are catastrophically rough-seated.

And the failure case follows. A melt with no adequate seed cools amorphous, which is a Dead Set. A soul that has never committed to a route has nothing for the new lattice to grow around, which is why the practitioners most likely to come out slag are the ones who spent their whole careers keeping their options open.

VIII. Fate in a Fight, Fate at War, and How It Is Recognized

The Plane of Fate is the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force, the pull between things and the grip that will not let go, and through those two the workings of reality itself: the manipulation of probabilities to work outside what reality already knows, so as to control the fate or predestiny of a thing. All spirits and all physical things have fates, which is why the plane reaches both worlds. The two forces are one substance held two ways. Thread is Attraction Force pulled down: a pull takes hold of the current, moves what it must and lets go. Obsession is Thread held by force, gripped past the working and kept. A held thread will sustain a working that ought to have ended, which is how the corrupted ones are fed, and it leaves a heavier knot for every hour it is held. Most of the Path spends those forces the way they already run. The far end changes how they are going to run, and the difference is the difference between swimming a river and moving it. Scholars keep a long and furious literature on whether this counts as a force at all, and the people it has been used on do not attend.

In a fight

Most of the time a Fate fighter fights with their presence. The room is heavy before they arrive, their reach is long, and everyone sworn to them fights better for it. When they pull, they fight the conditions of the fight. Across a yard you see them set: a stillness in the eyes, the breath held on a count that is not the count of the exchange, the air around them gone slightly too tidy, the way a room feels when everything in it is about to line up. Then the other fighter’s back foot comes down on the one wet stone, and the blade is already there. The cut is an ordinary cut and the wound is an ordinary wound. What was moved was everything the cut depended on, and the one who moved it is bleeding from the nose and eyes, and has perhaps half of what they walked in with. That is the first bill. The second is the knot, which arrives some days later as an accident of the puller’s own. The tell, the length of the warning and the knot have a page to themselves: The Tell and the Knot.

Nobody finds this easy, for a reason worth sitting with. Everything you understand is made of the thing you are trying to move. Your notions of cause, of likely, of what a blow is, all came out of Fate, and they cannot be reasoned into a tool to use against it. A Measurewright can describe a thread for a lifetime and never lift one. Nobody grips it bare, which is why there are different workings at all: each Work of Hataraki is a distinct, inherited way of taking hold of one function, and a Moto child gets generations of somebody else’s grip before they ever have to find their own. A Work lowers the chance of failure and never the price or the knot, and the grips do not trade. Blood gives the grip and never the road: a Moto who reads a field and then hits it is on the Body Path with a Work beside it, paying at Thread whenever the Work takes hold, and gets none of the Fate Path’s Range and Pressure unless that Moto walks it too, at the two-Path price. The card keeps the grip on a line of its own, Fate Art: Work · grip · Thread. The rare soul with no house behind it, like Pell Oakshot at the Saltgate Fair, can win a personal grip once, and “once” is the operative word.

And Fate is not empty up there. Most if not all of the Archons are Sovereign across all three planes, and on the Plane of Fate they are the most powerful things that still act there, with Bara standing somewhere above them, and their reach is greater still because they are not merely strong there: they are the laws, and the plane is the law and construction of the world. A thread may turn out to be somebody’s, and it is possible to be noticed. Some of them are deemed greater than others, and Uurgath and Urion are unparalleled in Fate, because what they are cannot be conceived. Everyone has an idea of a balance and an idea of an ending. Neither of those is what is up there.

Three more things hold it to the rules, and veterans plan their whole fight around them.

  • It only moves what already has a way of happening. Young Moto Naoki pronounced collapse on a sound gate at Tobishima in front of nine thousand people, and the gate stood. A strap that is wearing can give. A strap that is not wearing will not. A fighter with good gear, good footing and no bad habits gives a Fate fighter almost nothing to work with, which is one more reason everyone looks at your kit.
  • It costs ruinously. Low down, one lean on the odds can take a large share of the reserve and leave the body bleeding, and it often fails anyway. The arithmetic is plain: at Thread I about a tenth of what is spent arrives, and at Thread II about a fifth, so the pull works and nearly all of the price goes into the knot. The farther a result is from likely, the worse it gets (the price table sets rung against distance), and a working that asks for something nearly impossible will empty a Stage XII soul and still fail. Then the world collects: every pull, landed or failed, leaves a knot sized by the same distance, due within days for a lean and before the business is finished for a prerequisite removed.
  • It can be contested. Dominion’s Fate Sub-Stat pushes and the target’s Persistence and Continuity (the soul’s hold on going on being true) push back. A soul well enough bound simply refuses the result.

The counters follow. Bring another Fate user, who can read the weights and lean the other way. Be too sound or too well bound to move. Or get to the fighter before the result arrives, because a body at the end of a straight exchange with a Body Path veteran is an ordinary body, and they know it. The pull gives warning while it sets, a breath for a lean, several heartbeats for a prerequisite removed and far longer for a field, and reaching the puller inside that warning is the counter.

At war

A Fate commander works the whole field, on the rare day one can. Weather, supply, panic, the hour a column arrives, the company that breaks first and which way it runs. Every one of those already has a way of happening, and an army is thousands of straps wearing at once. It is a life’s masterwork at high Stage or a bloodline’s art, it may be done once in a war and it may cost the person doing it, and it is why each side’s first job is to find the other’s Fate users, ahead of even their champions. Nothing on the field looks like magic until the afterward, when the survivors sit down and count how many small things went wrong.

The Moto are what this looks like with a thousand years behind it. Their Hataraki is the deadliest lineage art on record, because it treats the world as a set of jobs that can be read, assigned and taken away, and the house’s Hataraki no Sho carries that onto the battlefield, down to the art of moving a frightened army with banners and footwork alone. Their children learn to rank a field the way other children learn sums.

How it is recognized

Body Path is proved by hitting things and being hit. Spirit Path by projecting and inscribing. The shallow water of Fate is proved in the same open way, by results that keep going your way past what luck allows, by the sworn company that keeps its word to you and the room that turns when you speak.

Read what the far end actually gates and force drops out of the pattern altogether: Persistence. Continuity. Ceiling. Sovereignty. Fate. Throne. Every one of them concerns what goes on being true when the odds say it should have stopped.

Deep Fate commitment is recognized by what a soul refuses to let end. A vow kept past the point where keeping it costs everything. A bond maintained across a death. A claim held for a century by somebody who could have put it down. Each of those is a small daily argument with what is likely, and Hyeon Do-sun, sitting in front of the fourth gate at Kell Harbor, had been winning that argument for most of his life. The Crystal reads continuity under pressure, and there is no drill that produces it and no instructor who can assign it.

This is why an army with a deep Fate practitioner in it is feared out of all proportion to its spears, why the great houses cannot manufacture one however much they spend, and why the vow is the most reliable instrument in the world for producing Fate alignment: Fate can attach to an oath, and a vow kept is continuity, performed daily, for years, in front of a Crystal that is counting.

It is also why no Choir of the Celestial Host walks far down the Fate Path. A Celestial’s Crystal is externally anchored, pulling its power from another’s Throne rather than growing its own, and a being wired that way can bond for ever and has never once had to hold anything by itself. The Grand Angel threshold and the deep Fate gate are the same gate, and Faisal spent eleven thousand years walking through it.

Fate Arts and blessings

Real Fate Arts are rare and run mostly by bloodline. Hataraki and a few other bloodlines hold them, some high-Stage masters reach them late along this Path, and the Path stays very hard all the way up. They are considered incredibly overpowered by everyone who has ever been on the wrong side of one, and the charge is fair. Most people only ever touch Fate from the outside, through a blessing or a Master Work Draft: a Fate Essence Draft lends the touch for a price few can name, and it no more makes a Fate Path practitioner than a borrowed horse makes a cavalryman.

Fate is more tied to blessings than to anything else. A blessing need not go through Fate, but most do, because the Plane of Fate represents the possibility of all things. Hataraki can take a blessing back or remove it, which is a large part of why that name is said quietly. It takes two Works, or two Moto, because one grip does one job. The Ladder and the Draft has the rest.

IX. The Unawakened Have Paths Too

An unwoken soul (Aether Ø, the sealed Shell) has Traits, refines by the Weathering (the slow hardening hard living gives a soul, without magic), and hardens along the direction it was already being used. Grain is single-axis by definition, which makes a weathered soul functionally single-Path with no waking Crystal to recognize it and no gate for the recognition to open.

A forty-year mason is Body Path. A forty-year midwife is Fate Path. Neither will ever be told, neither would know what the words meant, and the alignment is as real as any registered practitioner’s and considerably more consistent.

The screening apparatus reads Crystals. The coil (the assay instrument that reads a Crystal), the Refinement Spectrum and the Tiered Path all measure a Path by measuring output, and a soul with no output returns nothing on any of them. This is the same blind spot three separate registers carry, and it is one blind spot rather than three.

X. Drift

Tempering Coherence is the alignment between a practitioner’s Path and their lived behavior, and on the lived road it is the structural prerequisite for Threshold eligibility. A soul whose routing has drifted from its recognized Path receives no punishment and no authorization.

The presentation is slow and unmistakable to anyone who has seen it before. Output falls with no injury behind it. Gated Sub-Stats stop moving. A Threshold that should have come does not come, and the practitioner is quantitatively ready for years. Traits begin to argue with their bearer, because a Trait is a standing law and the bearer has started living against it.

Nothing corrects this except changing what the person actually does. Instruction does not touch it. A breakthrough Draft will carry a drifted soul over the Threshold regardless, and the drift goes over with it. A Draft seeds a lattice and cannot seed a decade of conduct.

The orders understand this and the great houses find it insulting, which is most of the difference between the two institutions and all of the difference in what comes out of their Thresholds.

XI. How It Reads

Across a yard, a Path is a habit of body before it is anything else. The Body Path practitioner closes without deciding to. The Spirit Path practitioner backs off half a step and is already reading. The Fate Path practitioner speaks first and the room reorganizes, and the one from the far end does not move at all, and whatever they said last week is still true. A two-Path fighter does one of those first and then, usually at the worst moment for you, the other.

And a Path is read again by how its walker flags. Everyone shows the shared tells as the reserve falls: the breath shortens and the aura dims at half, tremor and thinning color at a quarter, a flickering aura and a stuttering Trait at a tenth, and heat, blood and a cracking aura at the end. On top of those each road tires in its own way, because each is spending a different thing.

A hitter who is nearly done is red, wet and steaming. A thrower who is nearly done is gray, cold and calling her second by the wrong name. A Fate fighter who is nearly done looks much as she did, and the bench behind you has just lost a leg. Her reserve is Essence, so under the bad luck her Core is fraying like a thrower’s, and the careful opponent listens for the slipped name as well as watching the furniture. Soul Wounds and the Menders has the Spirit road’s bill in full, and The Tell and the Knot has Fate’s.

An opponent who has correctly identified a Path knows what the other man cannot do. That is the whole of the read, and it is worth more than knowing any technique he has.
