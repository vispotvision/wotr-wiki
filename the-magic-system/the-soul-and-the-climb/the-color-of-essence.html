The Color of Essence

The Color of Essence

Nov 17, 202649 min read

A Visual Taxonomy of Essence Hue, Temperance Clarity, and Wellspring Color Across the Continuum. Compiled for the Archives. Companion to The Sixty Wellsprings, The Sixteen Stages, the Provenance Doctrine of The Bench of Attribution, and The Standing Index.

On the last night of the Boat Fair at Tarn Quay, the lamps along Fishmonger’s Row went ochre one after another from the harbor end, a full ten minutes before there was anybody to see. The paper lanterns went first, then the oil in the glass, then the pale evening over the net lofts, and a fine dust of harbor silt began to settle on the eels. Nobody on that quay had ever read a line of the Refinement Spectrum, and every one of them knew exactly what it meant. The Red Crane school and the Tanners’ Boys, who had been brawling up and down the Row all week in scarlet and blue, stopped in mid-punch and straightened each other’s collars. Every stall on the quay began taking in its awning. Then Grandmother Seok Bo-ok came up the Row in wooden clogs, Stage XII, Basilithe to the bone, carrying the color of a quarry at sunset on everything she passed, and it was already bleaching toward white at its edges. She had come for a pair of boots a tanner had owed her since they were both twelve. He paid, in both hands, in front of everybody, and handed over his front teeth along with them without being asked. It cost the Row three weeks of sweeping, and he told the story for the rest of his life.

Ochre for the Wellspring, the white edge for the Stage, and nobody on the quay needed the Archives for either. The Archives, however, has opinions.

Preface

On the question of whether Essence (spiritual energy, the soul’s own stuff of power) has color, the Archives’ position has always been that it does, that the color is not decorative, and that anyone who dismisses it as merely aesthetic has never been in a room with a Zenith practitioner.

Essence color is determined by two independent variables. The Wellspring (one of the Sixty, the governing idea that changes how a soul uses its power) a soul has tempered on longest assigns the hue. The Temperance Stage (how far the soul has climbed) of the Soul Crystal (the soul itself, in its working form) assigns the clarity. Hue without clarity is noise. Clarity without hue is an empty vessel. Together they produce the practitioner’s Essence Color: the visible expression of who they are and what they have survived.

The hue comes from the Wellspring a soul has tempered on, and from the person before that. A soul that has yet to temper on any Wellspring shows a color of its own, which belongs to no lens and answers to nothing but the person wearing it. Once it has tempered on one, that Wellspring sets the hue, and a soul with several takes its hue from the lens it has tempered on longest. Either way the hue is there from the first Stage, clouded low down. Older books have the color coming from an inborn bias of the soul, and the trade has given that up: a soul’s color changes when what it has tempered on changes, and an inborn bias would have no business doing that.

An aura is built in three coats, and a good eye reads all of them. The Wellspring a soul has tempered on longest sets the base color. Every lens adds texture and sound, the grain and the voice of the thing: a tint laid over the base, the way it moves, the note it holds. A class or a big Trait adds a shape, a set of antlers in the glare, a door-frame standing around a landlady, a coil of smoke that will not leave a man’s left shoulder. Three people on one Wellspring in one Stage will never be mistaken for one another, and no two people have ever agreed on who looked best.

Hue follows the idea. Every hue is what its idea looks like held up to the light, the Wild ones as surely as the rest. A Pyreveil practitioner reads ember-orange because fire that renews stays in the soul where it was lit and never flares off into smoke, so what shows is the steady orange of iron holding its heat. A Judicium practitioner reads arc-blue with colored fringes because a thing truly told apart shows its parts at the edge, each in its own color. Penance is dull iron, darkening, because the soul is hardest where it has gone back most, and Benediction warm red-gold because a bond freely given is warm in the hand and weighed like gold. The Measurewrights will tell you, with a chart, that Pyreveil is orange because that is the color heat takes in iron and Judicium blue because that is the color current makes in air, and the chart has the shade exactly right and the reason backward. The Wellsprings did not choose their colors. The colors chose the Wellsprings, because a color is what a law looks like from outside.

Clarity follows refinement. Every woken aura shows its own hue from the first Stage, clouded and smoky low down and clearing as the Stage rises. A Murmuring Crystal reads nearly black because it is full of noise, contradiction and unresolved identity, and its color comes through that like a coal through ash. A Zenith Crystal passes light like spring water because almost everything that was not essential has been burned away, and the eye looking through it reports mostly brightness. Temperance is subtraction, and the Refinement Spectrum is what subtraction looks like from outside. There is one color in a soul, the lens’s, and the Spectrum says only how much cloud still stands between it and the eye. Color says what. Clarity says how high.

Part One · The Refinement Spectrum

Temperance clarity across the Sixteen Stages. The hue is present in every row, smothered at the bottom and clearing as you read down. Nothing in this table is a color of its own: the black at the head of it is cloud, and the white at the foot of it is daylight coming through.

The metaphor is smelting. Raw ore is dark, dense, full of impurity. Heat and pressure drive the impurities out, and what remains brightens with each pass of the forge. The Soul Crystal is the ore. Temperance is the forge. The Refinement Spectrum is the color of what comes out.

The assay houses prefer a jeweler’s bench to a smelter’s floor, and they grade a soul the way they would grade a stone: by its color for kind, and by its clarity and depth for worth. Clarity is how little stands in the light’s way. Depth is how far into the color the eye can go before it stops. A Spectrum reading is those two things and nothing else, and a clerk who writes “gray” on a slip for a Glory fighter has described the cloud and forgotten the man. A student who was taught that the high Stages go white was taught by someone who has only met the bright lenses, and the white in the rows above is what a clear stone does with daylight when its own color has light in it.

The aura, Stage by Stage

The aura a person wears is the same reading at arm’s length, and it changes its manners as it clears.

Low down it is smoke. From Murmuring to Flourishing an aura has no edge. It shows only during a working, clings to the hands, drifts off the shoulders in tatters like breath on a cold morning, and loses itself within a handspan at first and an arm’s length by the end. It stains what it touches and lights nothing.

In the middle Stages it clears and takes a shape. Splintering runs clear veins through the smoke, and from Glory the aura keeps an outline when its owner stands still, which is why Glory is where a civilian first sees one. Glory is also where the soul first offers a class, and the third coat, the antlers or the door-frame or the coil of smoke at the left shoulder, arrives with it. By Refraction the shape holds at rest and in a wind, and from Transcendence it is clear enough to throw light. On a Body fighter, whose aura is Charge showing through the Shell, that light lands on the wall behind. On a Spirit fighter it stays with the figure a half-step off the body, and what the wall gets is a shadow leaning the wrong way. On a Fate walker there is no light for anyone but a reader, and the wall is merely free of dust.

At the top it bends light. From Emanation the edges bleach, and from Principality the aura does to light what a full glass does to a spoon. A doorpost seen past the shoulder stands a finger off where it is, a lamp flame beyond them leans without a draft, and their shadow carries a bright rim. Nobody needs a Mancer to read that, and nobody who has seen it once mistakes anything else for it.

The unwoken show nothing. A sealed Crystal puts out no color at all, and a reader with the Gnosis for it finds only the dark stone of Part Three.

A kind of neighborhood shows, since fire tends to orange, stone to ochre and water to teal, but the hue belongs to the Wellspring and nothing above it.

Part Three · The Crystal, the Aura and the Body

The Crystal is the soul itself, as literal as a liver, standing where the Material, Soul and Fate planes overlap, and it has observable physical properties. One of those is color.

The Crystal’s color tracks the Refinement Spectrum but is not identical to the Essence’s color. Essence is what the Crystal produces. The Crystal is what produces it, and the distinction matters at the extremes.

The Crystal by tier

The Crystal passes through eight named tiers, each what the whole stone has grown into, all three layers together. The names are a jeweler’s, and each one describes what a reader with the Gnosis for it actually sees in the chest.

  • Seed (the unwoken) · The grower’s word for the small first crystal a stone is raised from. No Essence output, but the Crystal exists and can be perceived: dark, inert, dimensionally flat. A black stone in the chest with no luminance and no depth
  • Clouded (Stages I–IV) · Cloud is the bench word for the milk inside an unworked stone. The Crystal has opened and is circulating, the color is in it, and the eye cannot get past the surface. The aura over it is smoke for the same reason
  • Faceted (Stages V–VI) · The stone has taken faces. Splintering cuts them, and each one returns the light the same way twice, which is the recurring rhythm the Continuum can at last read consistently. Turned in a reader’s sight it flashes and goes dark and flashes again
  • Clear (Stage VII) · A reader sees into it and through it. The last cloud lies in the heart of the stone like breath on a pane, and power moves in it without being pushed
  • Fire (Stage VIII) · The jeweler’s word for light split into color and thrown back out. The Crystal stops only passing light and begins to give it, in the lens’s hue, and the first true Domain stands on that
  • Flawless (Stages IX–XII) · Nothing in it under any glass: no cloud, no feather, no seam between what the person is and what the person’s law does. The color runs so deep that a reader looking in reports no back to it
  • Starred (Stages XIII–XIV) · A star caught inside the stone, with its rays running out to the faces. A reader sees the person in it, whole and exact, and has usually looked away by then
  • Absolute (Stages XV–XVI) · Output virtually indistinguishable from Continuum-law. The Crystal reads as a source of light that does not behave the way light should, because what shines there is identity, rendering itself visible by sheer force of coherence

The progression is not uniform:

Seed to Clouded is the smallest color change and the largest qualitative change: dead stone to a bellows breathing on its own, and the visual difference is barely perceptible.

Clear to Fire is the first moment the Crystal produces enough light to be seen without mantic assistance.

Flawless to Starred is where the Crystal stops reading as an object inside a person and shows what it has been all along: the person. The color is the soul, expressed.

The old names. Old books, the north-road songs and any assay slip written before the renaming call the same eight Dormant, Awakened, Harmonic, Resonant, Radiant, Sovereign, the Crystallized Soul and Absolute, in that order. The Measurewrights retired the first seven because three of them were also rungs of the Aether Class and two were also Bands, and a clerk who writes Resonant on a slip ought to mean one thing by it.

A tier is grown into, and a Stage can be bought. The tier is read against the Stage and lags a bought Stage until Level fills it. A Draft clears the aura on schedule and leaves the stone a tier behind, so a trained eye sees a color that is clear and shallow, bright at the surface with cloud still standing a little way in. Depth is the part of the reading that money has never moved, and it is why the coil is asked for a tier as well as a Stage. Tier Grade, Bands and the Aether Shell has what each tier can structurally do.

The aura by Path

The Stage sets how clear an aura is. The Path sets where it sits, because each road works a different thing through a different layer, and the coat is that thing showing. The books go on saying Essence color for all three, though only one of the three is Essence, and a Measurewright will explain why this is wrong for as long as anyone stands still.

A soul on two roads shows both, in the share it practices them: a Body-and-Spirit fighter wears haze on the skin and a second outline a half-step off, and a tailor fitting one has been known to pin the wrong figure.

The three at the Gull. Tarn Quay still talks about the evening three Masters, all at Transcendence and none acquainted, took the one free table at the Gull and Anchor. Brannoch Vey, a Pyreveil shipwright off the slips, wore his ember-orange close as a shirt, and when he set his mug down the handle glowed the color of his hand for a moment after. Sister Ilse of the Rose Door, Catharsis, sat with her rose-gold standing a half-step behind her chair like a second, taller woman, and her shadow on the plaster leaned toward the lamp. The house cat stopped washing and watched her for an hour. The third was a small gray carter named Tam Oddy, Benediction, and the pot-boy swore afterward there was nothing on him at all. The dust in the lamp beam over his head hung where it was, though, and when the same pot-boy dropped the till, every coin in it lay face up on the boards. The bookie by the door, who knew his trade, gave odds on the shipwright in a brawl, the Sister in a quarrel and the carter over the season, and collected on all three.

What the climb does to the body

The same three roads show in the flesh, slowly, and a Stage or two behind the aura.

The Body Path grows dense. The Shell is the layer that lives in matter, and a soul that leans on it lays the leaning down in bone and fiber. A Body fighter is heavier than they look from the first Stages, heavier than they have any right to be by Glory, and warm to the touch in any weather, a hand like a loaf just out of the oven. Ferrymen charge them double and are right to. High up, from the Flawless tier, the eyes catch light like a cat’s, a flat coin of the lens’s color in a dark doorway, and snow does not lie long where they have been sitting.

The Spirit Path grows light. A soul that leans on the Core wears its flesh more loosely. The tread goes quiet, the weight goes off the stair, and in strong light, from about the same tier, there is a faint translucence at the edges of them: the rim of an ear, the web of a hand held up against noon. What every animal notices long before that is the stillness. Horses stop fidgeting, a hall full of dogs goes quiet, and birds do not get up when they walk through.

The Fate Path shows almost nothing. A Fate walker at Emanation can look like a clerk on half pay. What changes is the world around them: the weather they walk under, which is theirs more often than chance allows, fair or foul by the person; the crowd that parts without knowing it has; the queue that happens to be short. The body keeps no record at all, and that is why the careful reader watches the street and not the man.

How each road looks when it tires is a different picture, heat and split skin for the Body, flat color and slipped names for the Spirit, curdled luck for Fate, and it is set out with the three roads.

Part Four · The Essence Matrix

The full Essence Color of any practitioner is the intersection of hue and Temperance clarity: sixty Wellsprings across sixteen Stages. Left edge of each row: the color at its most clouded and least refined. Right edge: clearest, deepest and most refined, with daylight coming through it. Every practitioner in the Continuum sits somewhere on this grid, and their position can be estimated by anyone who looks and read outright by anyone whose Gnosis Perception outranks their Resilience Ward.

The applications are diagnostic. A field Mancer assessing an unknown practitioner reads two things at once: the hue tells them what the practitioner works, the clarity tells them how long they have been working it.

A bright saturated Pyreveil reading is a high-Stage fire practitioner, and a bright rose-gold looks nearly as warm and is one of the Spirit kind (Part Five). A dark murky Anima Spirare or Mortalis reading is an early-Stage healer or necromancer who has not refined past the noise. Taken together, the single most reliable field-assessment tool available to a Mancer who does not have time for a full Gnosis workup and needs to know, in the next three seconds, whether the person in front of them is dangerous.

The answer, for the record, is nearly always yes.

Schools know all this and dress to it. A Pyreveil sect’s scarves are ember-orange and its members will fight you over the shade, a Thaloré hall sings about teal, and half the brawls at any Stage bracket start with somebody’s cousin wearing the wrong color in the wrong stand.

Part Five · How Each Wellspring Reads in the Room

What a soul works does what its Wellspring’s law would do if that law were visible, and when a practitioner pushes it, it shows. The table gives each Wellspring as it reads on its home road, and the room’s report follows the road in use: worked through the Shell a lens does these things to the air and the furniture, worked through the Core it does them to the people in the room and leaves the furniture alone, and worked through the Layer it does them to what happens next. The aura flares in their own color, the lens’s hue at their Stage’s clarity, sitting where their Path puts it (Part Three): a smoky smear of it on the hands at Welling, a flare a civilian can see from Glory, a light that colors the walls from Transcendence. The harder they push, the brighter it burns, and a release drives it to its full color at once. A good eye reads the physics first and the color with it.

The Spirit lenses report less. Fifteen of the Sixty are of the Spirit kind, and they keep a color without any of these manners. The hue comes up on schedule, Catharsis rose-gold and Penanceflare red going to white, and the griddle stays cold, because those two give a fighter no fire to throw and usually do little or nothing to the room. What they change is what the person can bear, know, keep or let go. Mancers call this reading a furnace with nothing in it, and the ones who have rushed such a person on the strength of that reading will tell you the nothing was the least of their troubles.

The Fate lenses report later. Six of the Sixty are of Fate, and the hue comes up on them too, Benediction’s red-gold and Somnalis’s teal with its ghost outlines. The room reports nothing at all. What it reports, over the next several days, is that luck has gone strange, that an oath has started to bite and that strangers keep meeting, and the only instrument anyone has found for it is a written log.

The road in use decides, and the kind only says where a lens is at home. A Wild lens on a Spirit worker reports like a Spirit lens. Sister Maud of the Ash Door threw Pyreveil through the Core for thirty years and never warmed a griddle: the people in the room felt a grudge they had carried since childhood go to cinders, and the candles burned on as they were. The other way about holds too. A Spirit lens on a hitter still gives no fire to throw, and it reports through the flesh, in what that body can bear and let go, at the poorer resonance any lens has away from home. A Mancer who reads the hue and then assumes the manners has made the commonest mistake in the trade.

Part Six · The Coat, the Weight and the Nose

Three arts so famous that no two peoples agree what to call them and no two taverns agree how they work.

The color a practitioner wears is also the first art anyone learns to use on purpose, and two more go everywhere with it. A Guild roll says reinforcement for the aura coat (the constant visible coat of a practitioner’s own aura, bought with reserve), Pressure for the weight a strong soul lays on a room, and sensing for the trained read of another person’s power. Hardly anybody else on the continent says any of that, and the words they do say carry their arguments with them.

The Moto hold that a coat anyone can see is badly worn and that showing color at table insults the household. The Kharven hold that a man who hides his hide is lying about his price. The Marrowkin enter the whole affair in the Tally, a shift worked and a load carried, and argue about who pays, and they will read a Moto lord’s quiet matoi off his color like the heat of a pour and tell him to his face that it runs cold. The lineage halls teach that a junior lowers the eyes before wēiyā, and a fair number of the feuds between the halls and everyone else began with somebody who did not. Every grandmother among the Mahuo teaches nunchi before letters, on the sound reasoning that a child without it gets hit. Measurewrights maintain that all of this describes one phenomenon, and have been wrong about the details in every generation so far.

How the coat works. Stats sit in the body as a passive base, and the coat is the active layer over it, surging at the point of contact and glowing brighter as its owner pushes. It can be focused to one place at the cost of a thin rest and extended into held gear, and it is lost asleep, drugged or surprised, which is why so many famous people have died at dinner. What the coat covers follows the road. A Shell coat, worn at the wearer’s Aether Class, is the only one that hardens a body. A Spirit fighter’s coat is Essence and wards the soul, so a thrower who wants her ribs kept as well wears both and pays for both. A Fate fighter’s coat is the claim worn as presence: Pressure, and the room settling around the wearer, and nothing a blade would notice.

How the weight shows. It shows in the room first, in smoke, frost, lamp flames and animals, and then in bodies. One Stage up bends attention. Two brings sweat and nausea. Three or more drops people where they stand, and a Band gap changes the weather. The unwoken take it by Grade: dread from F to D, a weight in the room from C, knees bending from A. Only after all that does the window (the soul’s report of its own numbers to its owner, seen by nobody else) catch up and report it as a line, by which time the knees have usually formed their own opinion:

[Pressure: far above you · Focus down a Grade]

A title lends no weight. A Grandmaster merchant with no fighting Stage has no Pressure at all, whatever his manners suggest, and many a fighter has been embarrassed in a counting house by assuming otherwise.

How the nose reads. A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward: Level, Stage and Grades, on the reader’s own window, as plain as a price. A stronger Ward leaves the reader with question marks. Everything else in the trade is an estimate said aloud, and that is why fighters talk about each other so much and so loudly. People can hide it or fake it. A Moto lord banks his coat down until a Stage XI looks like a tired Stage VI, and a Free Company sergeant with a bad hand and a loud mouth will run his aura hot enough to pass for two Stages over what he is. Trained noses know both tricks, and both tricks still work on someone.

How the spending shows. The color tells on its owner at fixed marks, whatever the words used for them. At half the reserve the breath shortens and the aura dims. At a quarter come a tremor and a thinning of the color. At a tenth the aura flickers and the Trait stutters, which the Guild calls Starvation, Charge Starvation on a Body fighter and Essence Starvation on a Spirit or a Fate one, whose reserve is Essence. Past that lie heat, blood and a cracking aura. Each road adds its own tells on top of those (Part Three): the Body runs hot, the Spirit runs hollow and Fate runs unlucky. Waste is loud and hot, mastery is quiet, and a good nose reads all of it from across a market. The owner has the same news sooner and more exactly, as [Reserve: 41%] on a window nobody else can see, which is the whole reason a practiced fighter can fake a tremor at half.
