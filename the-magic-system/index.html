The Magic System

The Magic System

Nov 17, 202635 min read

At the midsummer fair of Hallingsgate, in front of four thousand people and the entire fish guild, Gudrun Halfdane stepped into the Accord’s brass coil to prove what the minstrels had been singing about her for three years. They called her Gudrun Infinite. She stood six and a half feet in a coat of red and gold, she had a move called INFINITE FIST and a purse with every coin she owned on it riding at the bookies’ tables, and she had told the whole of the Fishgate that the coil would read Grade S. The clerk, a small man with a clear voice, read out Stage III and a Flux of D.

The fair howled, the fish guild loudest of all. Bookies ran for the boats. Gudrun looked at the coil for a while, and said something the clerk declined to enter, and hit it.

It rang for two days. Dogs left town. Her hand broke in three places, and the clerk, when he had climbed out from under his table, read her again, because procedure requires it, and the reading was the same.

She went north to the wars. Eleven years later she came back with one arm and no red coat, stood in the same coil at the same fair in front of a crowd who had come specifically to laugh, and it read Stage VII. She said nothing to anyone. She walked out along the Hallingsgate breakwater, set her feet, turned her one fist on the gray-green sea and called the name, the same stupid name, in a voice you could have stood a spoon in. There is a gap in the breakwater sixty feet wide. The harbor still charges her heirs for it, and no child in Hallingsgate has ever laughed at the word Infinite, though they have all tried it on each other.

Everything in this section is in that story. She could not argue with the number, and it went up when she had survived something, and it cost her an arm. The Measurewrights will tell you it was the Stage that made the name true. Her family says the name came first, and I have stopped having this conversation at weddings. The heir of the fish guild, who laughed loudest that first midsummer, read Stage VII himself by the age of thirty, at the cost of most of his father’s fleet in Drafts, and has never put a hole in anything. The harbor does not charge him. He comes to the fair each year and stands a long while looking at that gap.

The Continuum grants no power. It measures a soul, reports what the measurement found to the soul’s owner, and permits exactly as much as the architecture will bear. Everything in this section is an account of that measurement: what it reads, what it refuses to read, and what it costs to move the reading.

“You call Temperance a buff. It is a confession of how much you have survived.”

Where the numbers live. Everything on this page is the account. The arithmetic underneath it is Fracture of Worlds — The Living System, filed at the foot of this section in twenty-four Parts across ten pages. Bands and Temperance Gates, the Tier Grade thresholds, the Sub-Stat ceiling per Stage, all sixty-four Path gates, Threshold Catalysts, Resonant Pairs, Strike Force and Durability benchmarks, the yield (η) of the three class ladders, the yield table, Domain costs.

Read it when a value is in question, not before. This page tells you what the system is. Fracture of Worlds tells you what the number has to be.

The Six Things That Carry the System

Everything after these is detail.

NumberThe rule
OneReality is spoken of as three planes. Matter, soul, and fate: matter and soul are the two lower realities, and fate, a plane by courtesy, stands above them and is everything that can happen in them. Every act of magic crosses all three at once. Remove one and the working fails, without exception and without appeal.
TwoAether is the ocean. Essence is your water. Foreigners say mana and spiritual energy, and they mean the same two things. Aether is infinite and belongs to no one. You pull it from the world around you, and nobody has ever worked a drop of it they did not first pull inside. What you do with it then is your road, and the three roads spend three different things. On the Body Path you pack it raw into your Shell as the Charge, shape it there and drive it out unrefined, and what you lose burns in the Shell’s furnace and comes off you as heat. On the Spirit Path you refine it in your Crystal into Essence, stamp it with your identity and spend it through your Core, and what you lose is paid as a hollowing there. On the Fate Path you hold nothing: you pull Thread down for the one working, and what you lose stays behind as a knot the world collects later. There is only ever as much as you have earned or paid for. Essence is your soul’s own substance, so spending it is spending yourself. Charge is the world’s, so spending it is spending the world, and your body pays for the handling. Rich ground fills you faster; thin ground starves you. A Wellspring represents an alchemical and a spiritual transformation and concept. You can see it as a source of power to pull from, and it is more the governing idea that transforms how your soul uses Aether: a lens on magic already potent, which shapes whatever your Path works (raw Aether in the Shell, Essence in the Core, the claim on the Layer) and in time can double its effect on its home Path, half again at most away from it. There are sixty, and the scholars explain them as sixty types of Aether with one law of physics apiece.
ThreeYou cast through a Soul Crystal, and the Soul Crystal is your soul, literally. A wand, a word and a will are decoration. The Crystal has three layers, one per plane, and its architecture decides what you are capable of long before your ambition gets a vote.
FourPower is refined. Nothing accumulates, because Temperance is subtraction. Sixteen Stages, each one a structural reorganization of the Crystal: Tempering melts it down and rebuilds each layer, refines your Essence and makes it stronger, and the body gains Traits. Training gets you to the door, and a Catalyst opens it. A crisis, a seclusion or a tribulation can supply one, and so can a Draft or a blessing, so Stages can simply be bought and the wealthy are stronger because they are wealthy. A bought Stage opens the door and no more: a Release must still be found and mastered by fighting.
FiveEverything costs. Power is spent, and nobody has ever summoned any. Charge bills the body, Essence bills the self, and a pulled thread leaves a knot. Domains bleed. Traits fatigue. Overchannel scars. Drafts cost money, and the best of them cost someone else’s life. A practitioner who tells you a technique is free is either lying or has not yet paid the invoice.
SixThe system does not lie and it does not forgive. Your numbers are the Continuum’s honest assessment of the soul carrying them, and your soul reports them to you in windows that no one else can see. They can be improved. They cannot be argued with. The one line on the sheet that can lie is a blessing’s, because the giver wrote it.

The Four Axes

One number has never described a practitioner. Four independent measurements run alongside each other, and the gaps between them are as diagnostic as the values themselves.

AxisWhat it tells youWhere it is read
StageThe ceiling. How far the Crystal has been remade: every stat’s cap, what can open, and how high a ladder may go. It can be boughtThe Sixteen Stages
GradeThe magnitude. How much a stat delivers, letter by letter. The Grade of the stat in use decides a contestTier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell
Path, with its laddersThe road. Which layer of the Crystal the soul leans on, what that opens and shuts, and how much of the magnitude arrives: the ladder of the layer in use, Aether, Essence or Thread Class, sets the share. It cannot be boughtThe Three Paths, Classes and the Three Ladders
The lensThe meaning. The Wellspring a soul has tempered on, and it changes what the magnitude does without changing the number by a single point: over years of mastery on its home Path it can double the effect, half again at most away from it, while the reading stands where it wasThe Sixty Wellsprings

The four do not move together, and that is the whole of the diagnosis. Stage opens a ladder’s ceiling and practice moves the rung up to it, so a rich heir can stand under a very high roof on a very low rung. Path and lens both apply after the Grade is read, so neither has ever moved a letter. A Class, the name a soul offers from Glory on, sits beside all four and says what it has made of your life.

Two practitioners at identical stat values and identical Stage can produce wildly different results if one is Aether III Resonant and the other is Aether I Muridic. The stats are the same. The delivery is not.

The Grammar of a Working

Newcomers ask how to learn a spell. The question is malformed. Workings are built, and the building starts with a person: what they refuse, what they survived, and the one wild concept they can explain in a breath, soup or debt or doors. The lens comes after that, the idea’s operation, its price and its flaw, and the Codex (the Accord’s master register of laws) is only asked at the end what to file it under. Once it is filed, every named art in the Continuum answers the same four lines, and its owner’s card answers a fifth.

LineWhat it says
PathThe layer the art leans on, one or a named pair, and so the ladder that reads it: matter reads Aether, a soul or a meaning reads Essence, a condition reads Thread. A true pull takes Thread on top of whatever else is being paid.
LensThe Wellspring it is worked through, and that Wellspring’s kind, which names a home and locks nobody out.
FormWhat the hands do. Strike and Shape are at home on the Body; Read, Write, Rite and Make on the Spirit; Sing on Spirit and Fate; Brew on Body and Spirit; Call, Bind, Claim and Pull on Fate. A home here is a qualifier too.
GatesThe Sub-Stats it needs opened, each with its Path and Stage.
SourceOn the card and not the art: Lived, Taught, Inherited, Bought or Blessed. The same art can be any of the five in different hands.

Under those lines sit three old words the trade still uses every day. The Affinity is the particular face of a Wellspring’s idea a person works, fire within Pyreveil or weight within Petralon. The Ability is the internalized governing system a Crystal has developed for it, Pyrokinesis for instance, which does nothing visible on its own: it is the platform. A Technique is a specific, repeatable application on that platform, with defined inputs, outputs and limitations, and it is what you actually do.

And every working, whatever its Form, is at bottom one sentence of Parun, the language of law, with four roles: Authorization says by what right a limit is set, Boundary sets it, Direction says which way the law runs across it, and Sealing ends it. The Wellspring supplies the law and the glyph tells it where to stop, and the stopping lands on the layer the working leaves through: a physical limit on the Shell, what a thing is taken to be on the Core, a term or a prerequisite on the Attraction Layer. So the same Exuroth in the same hand, with the same boundary, cracks a stone on the Body and proves a liar on the Spirit.

The Scholars’ Nine Methods

Older books sort every working by a Method, encoded in the suffix of its name, and the Guild’s clerks still name disciplines that way. A Method is no tier of the grammar above. It is a scholar’s name for a road and a trade taken together: Corporis is the Body speaking, Scriptia is cutting, Alchemia is pouring. The name itself is built on the Affinity: fire, not Pyreveil, gives Pyromancy its root.

The Eight Primaries

Eight Primary Stats, eight Sub-Stats beneath each, sixty-four values in total. A Primary is the combined total of its eight Sub-Stats, with one exception: Dominion’s Throne sits outside the total, so Dominion is read over seven. Points are spent on the grain, most of them by the life a person leads and a few placed by choice at each Stage; the category is the arithmetic afterward. The Temperance ceiling and the Path gates bind the Sub-Stat, and a Primary’s Grade is read off the mean. Two characters at identical Primary totals can be radically different practitioners because their distributions tell different stories, and a specialist can read modest on an assessor’s ledger while three Sub-Stats at the ceiling do the actual work.

VitalityThe body’s capacity to carry power without breaking. Fortitude, constitution, tolerance, regeneration.Material

Two Sub-Stats gate everything else. Tempering Coherence is the structural prerequisite for Threshold eligibility, the Crystal’s internal plumb line. Tempering Maturity is the Sub-Stat the Crystal consults first when evaluating whether you are ready.

A practitioner obsessed with Ardency and negligent of Tempering mounts a very impressive trebuchet on a hay cart, and the first shot settles which of them was the better idea.

The Paths

Sub-Stats are gated behind Path commitment. Every soul has all three layers of the Crystal, and a Path is the one it has learned to lean on: the direction advancement actually took, assessed retroactively and recorded whether the practitioner agrees or not. Nobody picks one at registration.

Body Path · the Aether Shell, in the Physical World, and the literal understanding and manipulation of physical reality. It works raw Aether: drawn in, packed unrefined in the Shell as the Charge, shaped there by the lens and driven out through the Shell, with the lost share burned in the Shell’s furnace and shed as heat, and the body paying for the handling. The shortest road and the most reliable. Direct multiplicative bonus to Strike Force, Durability, and Lifting Strength, from roughly five percent early to twenty percent at Stage XII and above. The trade is reduced Range and Aura Pressure, so low down a Body Path practitioner must close distance to spend their advantage. The road climbs a ladder, whose best-known teaching is Matla: matter commanded as an object, then through contact, through recognition, through law, and at last as a complete system. High up a Body fighter’s reach runs as far as there is matter to read, still needing existing substance and paying in recoil through body and Crystal.

Spirit Path · the Essence Core, on the Soul Plane, and everything to do with spiritual reality: souls, spirits, the dead, and what a thing means. It spends Essence, refined and stamped, through the Core, and its loss is a hollowing in the Core and never heat. Perception, inscription, clarity, processing, and force at range, which is force on souls: a soul knocked out of true, dread laid on a mind, a Core wounded (see Soul Wounds and the Menders). The Shell is the only door into matter, so a Spirit practitioner who wants a stone moved moves it through their own Shell, at their own Aether Class, or leaves it where it is. Gates most of Gnosis and much of Tempering’s refinement architecture.

Fate Path · the Attraction Layer, on the Plane of Fate, and the hardest road there is. Fate sits above the two lower realities and is what has to be so before anything can happen, the law and construction of the world itself, and “plane” is a courtesy. A Fate fighter’s everyday weapons are Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds. On the rare day they can pull a thread of Fate down, they move what a blow, a wall or a march depends on, and the result arrives without having been forced, at a ruinous price and often not at all. What they pull is Thread, which is never held: it comes down through the Attraction Layer for the one working, is paid for from the soul’s own share, and leaves a knot the world collects later. The Path’s shallow water is bond, recognition, reach, command: it gates nearly all of Harmonics and the majority of Dominion. Its far end holds the high gates: Persistence, Continuity, Ceiling, Sovereignty, Fate, Throne. Almost nothing at the far end unlocks below Stage V, and its terminal Sub-Stats do not open below Stage XIII.

No road is better. Body needs existing substance, must close until very high, pays in recoil and has no purchase on a soul. Spirit needs a soul or a meaning to work on, has a fragile body and dies out of position. Fate is ruinously hard, slow to set, moves only what already has a way of happening and loses a straight exchange, and being above the lower realities makes it harder and never better. So Body beats Spirit by getting there, and Spirit beats Body by going past the Shell. Body beats Fate with force and soundness, and Fate beats Body through what closing depends on. Spirit beats Fate by reaching the hand on the thread, and Fate beats Spirit because one wrong read is enough. Stage, ground and preparation decide which of these holds on the day, and this is why some people combine Paths.

Mastered, Walked, Studied. A Path stands on a sheet in one of three ways. Mastered means its gates are no obstacle at the person’s Stage. Walked means three parts in ten of their practice or more have gone down it. Studied is knowledge and small uses, open to anyone, and it opens no gates. A card carries each road’s Path Stage, the highest Stage whose gates it has opened, so that Mastered means the Path Stage equals the soul’s, and the clerk writes Body VII Mastered · Fate VI Walked 35%. One Path masters fastest, at the full force bonus and on a clean seat. Two take about two and a half times as long, at three-quarters of the bonus on each for life, on a rough seat. Three take about six times as long, past a mortal span, at half the bonus, with the high Grades shut, and the world looks down on anyone trying.

The three-and-four question, settled. Older registers and gate tables name a fourth Path, Attraction. They were looking at the shallow end of Fate and calling it a road of its own, and an Attraction gate in an old table is a Fate gate at the same Stage and Grade. Fate takes the smallest line in a table of force modifiers because a real pull goes around force: the cut that lands is an ordinary cut, and what moved was everything it depended on. Its small line there is a fact about force rather than a fact about Paths.

The deeper difference is how commitment is recognized. Body and Spirit are demonstrated by what a soul routes, Charge through the Shell or Essence through the Core, and the shallow water of Fate by results that keep going your way past what luck allows, by the sworn company that keeps its word and the room that turns. The far end gates Persistence, Continuity, Ceiling, Sovereignty past A, Stability on Wellspring-absent ground, Fate and Throne, none of which concern output at all, so deep Fate commitment is recognized by what a soul refuses to let end. No drill produces it and no instructor assigns it.

Which is why the Measurewrights read the far end as a late-tier extension. It arrives on time, and it stays invisible until somebody has held something long enough for the holding to count. Full treatment in The Three Paths.

The Wellsprings on the Paths. A Wellspring’s kind names its home plane and locks nobody out. Thirty-three are Wild, transformations of matter, things you can point at, which a soul shapes what it spends around: raw Charge on the Body road, Essence on the Spirit. Fifteen are Spirit, purely spiritual, and they usually do little or nothing to the physical world and give a fighter no fire on any Path, showing instead in what the person can bear, know, keep or let go. Six are Wellsprings of Fate, working on bonds, debts, names and odds. Six are Medium, at home on all three. Any Path can use any Wellspring: at home it can reach double with years of mastery, and away from home it runs at poorer resonance and higher cost, and half again is the most anyone has got out of it. Off its home plane a Wellspring changes target and never idea: on the Body it acts on matter, on the Spirit on what a thing means and holds, on Fate on what a thing could have been.

How each road tires. The shared tells stand for everyone, at half, a quarter and a tenth of the reserve, and each Path adds its own on top. The Body runs hot and cracks: heat-haze on the skin, then steam and cramps, then a Shell drawing on the flesh. The Spirit runs hollow: flat colors, a cold and a slipped word, then names and memories. Fate runs unlucky: small luck curdling, things going wrong nearby, a name that sits wrong in other mouths. You can read a Path by watching its walker flag.

What Your Soul Shows You

A window is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in the owner’s own tongue, and no one else can see it. Scholars argue about who built it. It comes in short blocks, fights included, for damage, skill gains and reserve, and it shows Grades, percentages, bars and levels, while EU, AU/s and yield stay with the instruments and the notes. A normal look gives the sheet:

[Corra Vell · Human · Level 196 · Stage VII, Refraction · Path: Body]
[Class: Yard Breaker]
[Aether I · Essence Ø · Thread Ø]
[Vitality B · Ardency B · Gnosis D · Dexterity C]
[Harmonics E · Resilience D · Tempering D · Dominion E]
[Reserve: 100% ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓]
[Trait: Bank the Fire, Level 41]
[Skills: Hammerwork A · Tracking B · Bargaining D]
[Blessing: none]

The sheet. Race and lineage show on it and shape base stats and what comes easily. The Eight Primaries show with Grades, read off the mean of their Sub-Stats, so a working fighter’s sheet reads lower than her fists: Corra’s Flux and Fortitude stand well above that B. A Sub-Stat appears when it is used or when it moves; the full set remains to study.

Skills rise with use and show as Grades. Drafts, runes, tags and chants are skills and trades like any other. Traits stay as filed in their Four Reflections and their laws, and each one carries a level that rises with use and grows with overall Level and skill. Techniques are named things done with a skill. They show no level and take a bigger name when they jump.

Class. At thresholds the soul is offered a named class fitting how the person has lived. It gives abilities, shapes growth and evolves by how it is lived, each time the leading ladder rises. It has no ladder of its own. The one wild concept stays the person’s own.

The three ladders, one to each layer of the Crystal, read from Glory. Aether Class is the Shell and influence on the physical world (Ø Dormant, I Muridic, II Harmonic, III Resonant, IV Luminous, V Radiant, VI Voidic, Ω Absolute), and the yield tables were drawn up for it first. Essence Class is the Core and influence on spiritual reality (Ø Silent, I Listener, II Answerer, III Confessor, IV Shepherd, V Ferryman, VI Sin-Eater, Ω Absolute). Thread Class is the Attraction Layer and how much of Fate a soul can pull down and hold (Ø Unthreaded, I Touched, II Drawn, III Spinner, IV Knotwright, V Weaver, VI Severer, Ω Absolute). It is the steep one: most souls stay Ø for life, and its numeral gives yield as two rungs below the same numeral elsewhere. Each ladder sets the yield for workings through its own layer. The Stage sets a ladder’s ceiling, and practice moves you up to it, so a bought Stage opens the ceiling and not the rung. A Walked second road’s ladder runs a rung behind until mastered, and a Studied one runs two behind the lead and stops at II, or at I for Thread. Thread has no Stage climb at all: it moves by a grip, from blood or a blessing, plus decades. Classes and the Three Ladders has the one table.

Level moves by danger overcome. Killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills, and crews argue over who got the kill.

Reading someone else. A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward, and everything else is an estimate said aloud, which people can hide or fake.

What Money Buys

The Catalyst was for a long time the Accord’s favorite argument that the ladder was fair. Drafts and blessings can supply one, and everybody with a vault has always known it. Stages can be bought, the great houses hoard the top Drafts, and a coil at a noble’s wedding reads like a ledger of the family’s holdings. The poor rise by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings, and the great houses lose more sleep over one commoner with a monster core than over any rival.

Money stops at the door it opens. A purse can buy the Catalyst for any Stage, and at Zenith a Hollow Throne must still recognize the buyer, so there money buys the attempt and not the seat. Sects teach what it cannot buy: Releases, Domains, techniques, skills and the rites for their Wellspring. A rich Stage IX may have no Release at all while a poor fighter at the same Stage has all three. Anyone at Stage VIII can have a Domain, their own concept made real, once the Second Release that opens it has been found. The two-Grade boost cap stays, and only outside help goes past it.

A monster’s core is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster, and an alchemist renders it into the Essence Drafts used for tempering. The core’s road decides the Draft: Body cores render to Salts, Spirit cores to Core stock, and the rare Fate core to a reagent for the Layer that only the black market sells. Swallowed raw it gives everything at once, and most who try die or come out wrong. A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner makes the strongest Draft of all, the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable. Monsters themselves level by killing, evolve and share the Level and Grade scale, and dungeons grow down in floors around a Core, alive and learning, with something guarding the heart. The Bestiary has the rest.

Fate and Blessings

The Plane of Fate is the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force and the workings of reality itself: manipulating probabilities to work outside what reality already knows, to control the fate or predestiny of something. All spirits and all physical things have fates. It can attach to oaths, where it is small and anyone’s to use (see Vows and Oaths), and it is more tied to blessings. Fate Arts are rare, mostly by bloodline, reached late by some high-Stage masters along the Fate Path, and considered incredibly overpowered. Hataraki can take back or remove a blessing, which is a good part of why that bloodline is feared, though it takes two Works or two Moto, because each Work is an inherited grip on one function, which lowers the chance of failure and never the price.

Vows and blessings are its small uses. People also fight and wage wars with Fate, and the Moto are the model of how deadly a lineage with it is, though it is the hardest thing in the system. Fate is the law and construction of the world itself, most if not all of the Archons and the great principles they embody are Sovereign across all three planes, this one included, and understanding gets no purchase on it, because understanding is made of the thing it would be used against. Some Archons are deemed greater than others, and Uurgath and Urion are unparalleled in Fate, because what they are cannot be conceived. An ordinary Fate Path fighter mostly fights with Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds. A real pull moves what a blow depends on, so a guard slips and an ordinary cut is already there, and it is rare, ruinously expensive and often fails, and landed or failed it leaves a knot, a debt the world collects from the puller later. Moving a whole field is a life’s masterwork at high Stage or a bloodline’s art. It moves only what already has a way of happening, it has a charge and a tell, and it is contested: Dominion’s Fate Sub-Stat against the target’s Resilience Persistence and Continuity. See The Three Paths, The Tell and the Knot for how a pull is seen coming and what it leaves owing, and Counterplay for how such a fighter is beaten.

A blessing grants a power out of a person’s natural reach, even in Level. It need not go through Fate, but most do. Higher spirits, Archons, Epochs and people of higher Temperance give them, because they are spiritually stronger, and a great giver spends almost nothing. Epochs and Archons act rarely, and it shakes the world. A blessing is listed on the sheet as its own line and can lie, because the giver wrote the line. A curse is often a blessing with terms you did not read. See Blessings and Curses.

II · The Foundations
