The Sixty Wellsprings

The Sixty Wellsprings

Nov 17, 202655 min read

“What burns in the crucible burns in the one who tends it.”

The oldest saying of the bench, older than any register.

Fourth edition. The first edition gave every one of the sixty a physical analogue, a mechanism in that analogue’s own terms, and a failure mode following from the mechanism rather than from the mood. The second added what the trade has always known and the Register was too proud to set down on the same page: what each of the sixty means at the bench and what it does to the soul. The third turned the page right way up, so that every entry opens on the idea, the transformation and the principle, and the physics follows as the scholars’ explanation of them. This one files the sixty by where each is at home, names the Titan whose law each one is, and gives each its own color, its own ground, its own contraries and its own operation at the bench, with each failure read from the flaw in the idea. No figure has moved. The physics now sits where it belongs, last on each entry, as the Measurewrights’ account. The Measurewrights have lodged their objection in the usual place.

Nobody on the Cold Tun bridge needs the Sixty explained, because their grandmothers saw Sanne Ruddock and Ond Gravell. Same Stage (a rung of the soul’s sixteen-step climb), same Grade (the letter an assay gives a stat) on the sheet, and both banks packed to the reeds with the green sashes of the Hot Tun Ladies and the slate caps of the Quarrymen, with a man selling fried dough off a pole between them. The bridge stands where a hot spring comes up through old bedrock, so two kinds of Aether (mana, the raw stuff of magic in the world around you) pool under that one span, and each fighter stood in the same air and pulled a different one. Sanne breathed in Pyreveil and came up deep ember-orange from the ribs outward, skin flushed and bone-dry, heat-shimmer climbing both arms until the spit boiled off her lip. Ond breathed in Petralon and went the gray of a wet anvil, and the dust on the deck sagged down around his boots with a long settling groan, and everyone on the bridge felt him get heavier without being able to say how. Her fire broke over him, and he stood in it. His fist went into her guard like a dropped lintel, and she laughed with blood on her teeth, because her body had stopped minding. For the length of a long sermon neither of them could do one thing about the other, and the crowd sang all through it.

Then both bills came due inside a single breath. Sanne got both hands round his wrist and hauled, straight outward, in tension, the one direction Petralon does not answer, and the man who had just stood in a bath of fire parted at the shoulder.

He did not bend first. Stone does not. And the heat she had kept banked in her own tissue to do it had to leave by whatever was nearest, which was her palms, and she stood there on the bridge with her hands cooking and her mouth wide open while Ond Gravell, one-armed and roaring, fell on her and took them both over the rail into the Tun. Draws keep the stakes at Cold Tun. The bookies bought new roofs.

Two fighters, one bridge, one breath of the same air. The whole of the difference was which idea each of them had let govern their Aether, what it did to the soul that took it, and what it would eventually charge. Every child in the Four Quarters already half knows this, being a Fixatio child or a Tarturon child by the day of the sixty they were born on, which is stated before their age is. The rest of this page is the long version, and it is worth the lamp oil.

Four Things to Know First

Every one of the sixty answers four questions, and anyone who keeps hold of them can read any entry in this register.

Its kind: where is it at home? Each of the sixty belongs to a plane. A Wild Wellspring is at home in the Physical World, Aegor’s reality, and it is a thing you can point at: fire in the ground, stone that moves. A Spirit Wellspring is at home on the Soul Plane, Ashura’s reality, and it is a change in a soul: forgiveness, forgetting, a failure owned. A Wellspring of Fate is at home on the Plane of Fate, Bara’s, and it is a change in what holds: a bond, a debt, a name, the odds. A Medium Wellspring is at home on all three and works across the Veil between them. That makes thirty-three, fifteen, six and six, and each kind has its own page. A kind names a home and never a lock.

Its lens: what does it do to you? A soul’s power was there before any Wellspring touched it. A Wellspring is the idea that soul bases its tempering on (tempering being the slow melting down and rebuilding of the soul’s layers), and it brings that power to a point: a little at first, and with years of mastery up to double. The Grade on the sheet does not move. What grows is how much of the burn, or the ending, or the stubborn weight is in the thing that hits you.

Its Titan: whose law is it? Each of the sixty is one way a Titan’s law shows up in the world. Thirteen Titans hold the sixty between them, and every entry names its own.

Your Path: what are you doing with it? A soul has three layers, and a Path is the one it has learned to lean on: the Body Path on the Aether Shell, the Spirit Path on the Essence Core, the Fate Path on the Attraction Layer. Any Path can take any Wellspring. On its home Path a lens can reach double, and away from home half again at most, and the same Wellspring down three Paths makes three different fighters.

What follows is those four answers at length, then what the scholars make of them, and then the register itself.

What a Wellspring Is

A Wellspring is an idea that changes a soul. Each of the sixty stands for one transformation, alchemical and spiritual at once: fire that renews, the breaking point, forgiveness, the honest mirror, the cup of forgetting, the first substance. Aether comes in sixty types because there are sixty of these ideas for it to carry.

You can see a Wellspring as a source of power you pull from, and on the street everybody does. They have reason to, since the ember really is in the ground at Cold Tun and you really can fill up on it. The better description, the one the benches and the confessors held long before there were registers, is the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether. Your soul was already potent before any Wellspring touched it. A Wellspring puts a lens on that magic: it takes what is already there, brings it to a point, and turns the way the power moves in you.

That is why the Sixty matter more than almost anything else on a sheet. A Stage says how far up a soul has climbed. A Wellspring says what the climb is doing to it, and two fighters of one Stage and one Grade can stand on one bridge and be two entirely different weather systems. The Wellspring also sets the bill, because a law taken up as your way of living goes on charging you in its own coin.

The Four Kinds

Surveyors call these the four encounter types, because the classification tells them which direction the danger arrives from. It is also the kind on every entry below, which names the plane a Wellspring is at home on and locks nobody out of it.

The classification rests on a single question: where does the effect originate, and where does it arrive?

More than half of the Sixty are physical, and most of the rest are not physical at all. A Wild Wellspring is a thing in the world: fire in the ground, stone that moves, lightning, a solution crystallizing out of itself on the bench. You can point at it, and a practitioner who takes it up shapes what their soul spends around it. A Spirit Wellspring is a change in a soul: forgiveness, forgetting, a failure owned. There is nothing to point at, and it gives its bearer no fire to throw. It usually does little or nothing to the physical world, though an edge case will always find a Measurewright: a Thaloré grief can be felt across a room, and the argument over whether that counts is older than the Guild. A Wellspring of Fate is a change in what holds: a bond, a debt, a name, the odds. There is nothing to point at there either, only what keeps happening afterward. A Medium Wellspring is at home on all three planes and crosses the Veil between them as it works. Every one of the sixty changes the soul that takes it up; the difference is whether the world shows it too, and how soon. A spiritual entry below still carries an analogue, which is the scholars’ picture of what is happening in the soul, and no one has yet watched a forgiveness anneal.

The nested depth model

The three planes are nested, not stacked.

The Plane of Fate stands above the lower realities, the lattice upon which all possibility rests. A surveyor measures from the ground he stands on and calls that depth, and a theologian points the other way, and they mean the same place. The Soul Plane lies between, the stratum through which abstract law becomes a soul’s felt truth. The Physical Plane sits at the surface (the Material Plane is the same place under its other name), where truth becomes event, body, ruin, and mountain range. Only the last two are realities in any careful sense: what stands above them is everything that flows, every possibility, idea and concept, and “plane” is a courtesy.

Fate is a precursor. A tide that falls. What is decided at Fate-depth cascades through the Soul Plane and manifests on the Physical Plane as event.

This is why the four types produce different threat profiles.

Wild operates at the surface. The danger is physical and legible.

Spirit operates from the middle layer, and the cascade runs outward: the Crystal fires, the Shell expresses, and the body, where it is reached at all, manifests what the Soul Plane authored. What reaches the surveyor comes from no outside blow. They are being authored from inside.

Fate operates from above both lower realities, and the cascade has to come through both of the others. By the time the surveyor can see what was decided, it has already happened to them, and it looks like luck.

Medium operates at the Veil itself, the threshold where one plane’s law softens into another’s. Every layer moves at once, and the movement is reciprocal. The Veil is the skin of the lower realities, with an inner fold running between Physical and Soul and an outer face where both meet Fate, and a Medium lens works along the whole of it.

Whose Law It Is: The Titans

Follow any Wellspring upward and you arrive at someone. Bara wrote the Works, the laws the world is built from. An Archon is a Work crowned, a law enthroned. Titans descend from Archons: an Archon is a principle enthroned and a Titan is the same principle bearing weight, though the pairing is ragged and the descent is argued by everyone with a chair to argue from. And the Titans are the Wellsprings themselves, and the Sixty are their manifestations. Each of the sixty is one way a Titan’s law shows up, carried by Aether (mana), which is what remains of Omnara undivided and can therefore carry any idea at all. A Titan’s Ground, on the Fourteen Titans pages, is that law at full scale. An entry in this register is the same law at the size a person can take in.

Titans bear all three planes, so the Sixty make up all three. Dazkar shows up Wild in Judicium, Spirit in Penance and Fate in Tarturon, and the Prometheus who roars in Pyreveil is the same one who will not let you hide in Penanceflare. A kind says where a showing is at home, and a Titan says whose it is. The Master Codex and the Titans’ own pages have the arguments, and this register files each of the sixty under one name.

Thirteen Titans hold the sixty. The fourteenth, Vohrin, holds none: its frost is a Titan’s own power worked at Wellspring level, with nothing standing between it and its Titan, and the Wild page carries a marked note on it as the register’s one exception. A Titan walks no Path. A Path is something a soul does, and a Titan is the law itself, so nobody fights on the Fate Path with Sylorin. A soul takes up one showing of a law and tempers on that, and harmonizing with Pyreveil has never yet got anyone an audience.

The Lens by Path

A soul meets the world through three layers, and a Path is the one it has learned to lean on. The Body Path leans on the Aether Shell and the Physical World, the Spirit Path on the Essence Core and the Soul Plane, and the Fate Path on the Attraction Layer and the Plane of Fate, what has to be so before anything can happen. The Three Paths has the whole account. A Wellspring is the lens a soul bases its tempering on, and it shapes what each Path spends, in the terms of whichever layer is doing the work: raw Aether packed in the Shell (the Charge) on the Body Path, Essence in the Core on the Spirit Path, the claim on the Attraction Layer on the Fate Path. The idea stays the same and the target changes, from matter, to what a thing means and holds, to what a thing could have been. One Aether in three people makes three different fighters, and every entry in this register says how.

A blow’s character comes from Wellspring and Path. Give two hitters the same Flux at the same Grade, one under Pyreveil and one under Nihiloth, and the first blow burns and ruptures while the second ends and quiets things. Hand that Pyreveil to a thrower on the Spirit Path and it is a third blow again, arriving from sixty paces on the soul and nowhere else: it burns the grudge a man walked in with and leaves his coat unscorched. A Fate fighter’s Pyreveil is mostly reach and a room that catches, and on the one day in a long while that they pull a thread it is a fourth: an ordinary cut, arriving because a strap has already burned through. The older books put all of this down to an inborn bias in the soul toward one Wellspring or another. The trade has given that up, having never once found the bias in anyone who had not already spent ten years under the Wellspring it was supposed to explain.

Every Wellspring has a home, and a home is never a lock. A Wellspring’s kind is a qualifier naming the plane it belongs to, so the home Path of a Wild Wellspring is the Body, of a Spirit Wellspring the Spirit, and of a Wellspring of Fate the Fate Path, while a Medium Wellspring is at home on all three (the four kinds are set out above). Any Path can use any Wellspring. At home it harmonizes cleanly and can reach double the effect with years of mastery. Away from home it works in that layer’s terms, at lower-quality resonance and higher cost, and reaches half again at most. The higher cost has a plain form. Off its home the lens lowers efficiency (η, the share of what is spent that arrives as effect), and that loss multiplies with the two every working already carries, the η of the ground and the η of the worker’s class ladder, so an away lens on bad ground in an untrained layer is three losses stacked. The lost share is paid in the Path’s own coin: a Body hand sheds it as heat, a Spirit hand pays it in the Core as hollowing, and a Fate hand finds it in a heavier knot. Such a lens still changes how that soul uses Aether, and plenty of famous fighters are famous for exactly that clash. A Spirit Wellspring on the Body Path still gives no fire: it changes what the body can bear. The benches keep a standing bet on the first soul to pass half again away from home, and it has never paid out.

The lens brings forward what is already there. More of the idea arrives in every blow and every working. The Grade on the sheet does not move and no stat points appear, because a lens never added any. What grows is how much of the burn, or the ending, or the stubborn weight is in the thing that hits you.

It starts small, and only years reach double. Wenna Corry of Tallow Row, a lamp-lighter’s girl who went down the Body Path with her fists, paid a year’s wages for a bottle of Ignivale at sixteen, and for her first year the lens gave her about what a good pair of bellows gives a smith: her wicks caught a shade sooner and a shade hotter than her arm alone could have managed. She lived with it for twenty-four years. By forty she could break a man’s jaw on the Tallow Row steps and his beard would be alight before his teeth landed, and the bookies stopped asking whether she had been drinking and started asking what she charged. Her cousin Perrin took the same Ignivale down the Spirit Path, putting the spark into other people’s nerve from across the Row for a living (a shy bidder, a bride at the door, a fighter between rounds, and never once a wick), and has been stuck at half again since before she caught up with him, paying more for each spark than she pays for a jaw. Each soul has said so throughout, in the window nobody else can see (the soul’s report of its own numbers to its owner, in square brackets on the page), and this is how it reads:

[Ignivale: harmonized. Lens on the Body, at home: +8%]
[Ignivale: harmonized, 24 years. Lens on the Body, at home: +100%]
[Ignivale: harmonized, 31 years. Lens on the Spirit, away: +50%]

Those figures are a sample from two sheets, every soul’s road runs at its own speed, and only the two ceilings are law. The rule under them is plain: a lens grows the way a Trait grows, by being lived with. A Draft can hand you the lens. The years are yours to live.

Each lens grows on its own. A soul with three Wellsprings has three lenses at three stages of mastery, all under the Capacity rules further down this page, and a lens compounds only with its own years.

You can see it. The Wellspring a soul has tempered on longest sets the base color of its aura. Every lens adds its texture and its own sound and smell, and a Class or a big Trait adds a shape. A trained eye at fifty paces can tell you the base lens from the hue, make a good guess at the others from the grain, and tell you from how steadily the color sits whether the lens is two years old or twenty. The Color of Essence has every hue, and each entry below carries its own.

Measurewrights at Sealhall have spent a long time arguing about what exactly gets doubled, since the Grade holds still and no fork rings a lens. Their working answer is to ask the person who got hit.

How you come by one

There are four roads, and most people who have more than one Wellspring have walked more than one of them.

At a Core, in a dungeon. Most Wellspring Cores lie in dungeons, the living underground places that grow down in floors around a Core with something guarding the heart. On a held site a person can sit at the edge of a known Wellspring, with a keeper and a baseline taken first, and come away harmonized, at a price by the hour. In a place nobody holds, a crew that goes too deep takes Wellspring Baptism whether it wanted one or not. This is the road dungeon luck runs on, and the reason a nobody with a good rope can come home changed. See Dungeons.

From a school. A sect teaches the rites for its Wellspring, along with the Releases, Domains, techniques and skills that money cannot buy, and in return you wear its colors, sing its songs and inherit its grudges. A school’s style is that lens worn by a hundred people at once, which is why you can spot it across a square.

From a Draft or a blessing. A Draft puts the lens in a bottle, and the great houses hoard the best ones. A blessing is given by a higher spirit, an Archon, an Epoch or a person of higher Temperance, for whom it costs next to nothing. Either way it can land in a soul that could never have reached it alone. A blessing shows as its own line on the sheet, and because the giver wrote that line it can lie, so what reads as a gift for years may be a curse with terms you did not read.

By birth. Some souls arrive already harmonized, by bloodline or by generations of living downwind of a concentration. The birth-day name is another matter entirely: a Fixatio child is any child born on Fixatio’s day, and being called one has never once sealed anything.

One Aether, three readings

Every one of the sixty can be read three ways, and each reading is a whole profession that thinks the other two are amateurs. These are readings and not Paths: a confessor, a bench hand and a Measurewright can all stand around one Body Path hitter and describe three different things, none of which is her Path.

As a spiritual transformation. The confessor’s reading, and the oldest: what taking this Aether into the soul does to the person who survives it. Temperance is the climb of a soul from Stage to Stage, and every Wellspring is a road on that climb, so the Fracture register names Pyreveil Temperance through burning and Absolution Temperance through forgiveness, and it gives all sixty such a name. Where the confessors read a road otherwise, the entry sets their reading beside the name, marked as such and argued over at every deathbed. To pass through a Wellspring is to be unmade just enough to keep going. Every Wellspring touches all three layers of the Crystal, rarely evenly, whichever Path the soul is on. The Essence Core decides how the soul burns, heals or empties under that law, the Aether Shell how the law moves through and around the body, and the Attraction Layer how reality, memory and people bind to the soul once the law has taken hold.

As an alchemical principle. The bench’s reading: what that same idea does to matter in a vessel. Each of the sixty is a living process, a law of transformation, and the discipline of Alchemetrica (the working of Wellspring law upon matter) is built on that fact. The reagents are the nouns and the Wellspring is the verb. Six of the sixty are at once Wellspring, cosmic principle and named bench operation, and four more lend their law to a stage of the Great Work (the seven-stage discipline an alchemist lays on the matter and on himself at once). The rest have no operation filed under their name at the Bench, though every working alchemist has an opinion about what each of them is the principle of, and those opinions appear in the entries, marked as the trade’s reading and open to a fight.

As a law of the world. The Measurewright’s reading, and the youngest: the physical analogue, the mechanism, the failure. Scholars have an account of everything, this is theirs, and it has the great merit of predicting the bill. The rest of this page gives it in full.

The correspondence is architecture rather than poetry, which is what Alchemetrica means when it says that what burns in the crucible burns in the one who tends it.

How the Scholars Explain It

“The Wellspring is neither gift nor spirit. It is a law that was already running before you noticed it, and harmonization is the act of agreeing to be measured by it.”

From the preface to the First Physical Register.

Everything below this line is the account the Measurewrights (the guild’s certified assessors of practitioners and workings) give, and every figure on this page lives in it. It may be wrong about what a Wellspring ultimately is, as the confessors will tell you, and it has never yet been wrong about what one will charge.

Why the Sixty Are Sorted by Home

The older registers arranged the Wellsprings by feeling. Grief stood with grief. Fire stood with fire because both were warm. It produced a beautiful taxonomy and a useless one, because a practitioner who knew that Cinerion and Pyreveil were both House of the Ember still had no idea why one of them refilled a reserve and the other emptied it faster.

The guild’s physics stays on each entry, last, under The Measurewrights’ account, where the analogue gives their picture of how a Wellspring behaves. The entry itself opens on the idea, which is what the Wellspring is. The kind says where it is at home, which is the one sort that has never yet misled anyone walking onto the ground.

A warning to the devotional reader. Setting a law from natural philosophy beside the Wellspring does not demote it. The Measurewrights have written down how much it will do and what it will charge; why it does anything was never theirs to write.

A warning to the Measurewright. Writing the law down does not explain why a soul that takes in Absolution stops hating its father. The physics stays on every entry, last, because its figures work. The entries have stopped pretending the physics came first, or that it was ever the reason.

What a Wellspring Physically Is

In the scholars’ terms a Wellspring is a type of Aether that carries a fixed law. Aether is what practitioners pull from the environment around them, and it comes in sixty types, fixed and counted, and each type affects the Soul Crystal differently. Take in Pyreveil and your reserve burns hotter while your body stops minding the heat. Take in Hypnather and you sleep like the dead and wake structurally different. Take in Fractura and you will never again look at a bridge, a friend or your own left wrist without seeing where it is going to go. A type of Aether is not stored anywhere and it does not belong to anyone. It runs whether observed or not, in the way that a pressure gradient runs whether or not something is standing in it.

The old registers call each type a current, and the trade still does, because Aether moves over ground the way wind moves over a field. Keep the word and lose any picture of a pipe. Nobody has ever laid a Wellspring on or shut one off at a valve, and the Measurewright who tells you otherwise is about to send you a bill.

The road into the soul is the same for all sixty: Aether is pulled in from the world through the Aether Shell (the Crystal’s outer layer, and the only door into matter), and nobody has ever worked it without pulling it in first. Older editions of this register ran the road straight on from there for everyone, refined into Essence and out through the Shell again. That is one road of three, and half of it. On the Body Path the Aether is packed in the Shell unrefined, as the Charge, and driven out through the Shell as it came. On the Spirit Path it is refined in the Soul Crystal into Essence (spiritual energy: Aether stamped with identity, the soul’s own substance) and spent through the Essence Core. On the Fate Path the Aether is refined into Essence as on the Spirit Path, and a thread is pulled down through the Attraction Layer and paid for from the soul’s own share, which is a part of that reserve and no separate purse; Thread itself is never stored. The Three Paths sets the three out with their prices. What differs among the sixty is the type. Where you stand therefore matters twice: rich ground fills you faster and thin ground starves you, and the ground decides which of the sixty is on offer. On the street this is plain sense. A brawler will say she has the ember in her, or the stone, or that she took the water at Saint Orrin’s pond, and she will say he only fights like that on hot ground. On a Guild roll all of that is harmonization, which comes next.

Harmonization is phase-locking. The Aether Shell adopts the characteristic behavior of one type of Aether so that whatever the soul works afterward is shaped by that law before it reaches the world. Nothing is added to the practitioner. The conduit is retuned, and everything that passes through it afterward carries the retuning. This is the lens, in the scholars’ words.

Said once and for all: the lock happens at the Shell, where Aether enters, the tie is held on the Attraction Layer, and the lens shapes what the lead layer works: raw Aether in the Shell for a Body fighter, Essence in the Core for a Spirit fighter, the claim on the Layer for a Fate fighter, where the Thread pulled through the claim takes its shape. The Shell is where the instruments catch the lock, since all Aether enters that way, whatever road it leaves by. On the Body Path the shaping is done right there, on Aether that never sees the refining, so a hitter’s fire is Aether burning in the Wellspring’s shape with none of the hitter in it. On the Spirit Path and the Fate Path the law seats hardest in the Essence Core or the Attraction Layer, which is the lens working in that layer’s terms.

This is why harmonization changes what a number means without changing the number. A Fortitude of B-Grade harmonized to Monolithion does not become a larger Fortitude. It becomes a Fortitude that behaves like a load-bearing mass rather than like tissue, with the failure curve of stone rather than the failure curve of flesh. The magnitude stays where it was and the constitutive law changes.

Most practitioners awaken three to five Wellsprings in a lifetime. Serious adepts harmonize with twelve or more. Those who embody all sixty are called Principals of Temperance, and there are not many of them, and you will not meet one.

Harmonization, Traits, and the glyph

Three things bias a Shell and they are constantly mistaken for one another.

A harmonization retunes the conduit. It is reversible in principle, it degrades if Capacity (the limit on how many Wellsprings a soul can hold at once) is exceeded, and it shapes everything passing outward for as long as it holds. The law belongs to the Aether and the practitioner has it on loan.

A Trait biases the conduit permanently. It is a law the soul itself carries, held in the Essence Core (the Crystal’s inner layer, holding identity, memory and Traits), and it writes into the Shell’s conductivity map rather than borrowing a law from outside. It cannot be dropped to make room, and it has a level of its own that rises with use and with the bearer’s overall Level and skill. This is why two practitioners harmonized to the same Wellspring produce recognizably different output: the Aether supplies one law and the Core supplies another, and the working is what comes out of both.

A glyph is a boundary condition. It creates nothing. The Wellspring’s law was already running; the glyph moves a boundary and the law does what it has always done on the far side of the new boundary. Every entry below should be read with that in mind. Nobody in this register makes fire. They establish conditions under which the ordinary behavior of heat produces fire where it previously did not, and the fire that results obeys every rule fire has ever obeyed, including the ones nobody thought about.

Sites do not check for a Crystal. Aether pooling in a valley acts on everything standing in the valley, and an unwoken soul’s Shell is sealed and cannot phase-lock, but it has an Essence Core like anyone else. Sustained residence at Active Concentration or above therefore does to a shepherd what it does to an adept, minus the harmonization and minus the vocabulary.

What it leaves behind is a Trait, formed by Wellspring Convergence, in a body that will never be screened and a life that has no framework for it. Every quarter has villages downwind of a concentration where one family has been strange for six generations, and the strangeness is lawful, inheritable, and entirely undocumented.

Where a Wellspring Pools

A Wellspring is a type of Aether and no kind of place. But a Wellspring pools.

The way groundwater is not stored in a specific container but absolutely collects in aquifers, a type of Aether concentrates where the substrate is Aether-conductive, where competing types are absent, and where enough time has passed for it to saturate the local field until the physical environment begins expressing the law passively.

A Wellspring site is the aquifer. The Aether is the water table.

Cores, keepers and closed ground

Common speech calls any site where a Wellspring’s Aether pools a Core, and most Cores lie in dungeons: places a Core has made around itself, or that have grown up around one, going down in floors with something guarding the heart. A dungeon is alive and learning, and scholars argue whether it thinks. The rest of the Cores are said to lie in vast territories, with the weight on “said”, and Dungeons has the whole account. Surveyors keep Wellspring Core for the top line of the Density Scale below, an Essence Core is a layer of your own Crystal, and a Measurewright who hears a ferryman call the warm pond behind the tannery the Pyreveil Core will explain all of this at length until the ferryman charges him double. This register uses the surveyor’s word, and you should use the ferryman’s if you want to get across the river.

A worked Core is a dungeon someone cleared and now holds. It recharges and it has keepers. A site people have pulled from and tended for generations refills at the rate its ground replaces what has been taken, which the numbers below will give you, and somebody always stands at the gate of it: a sect, a village, a Guild house, three brothers and a fence. Keepers charge by the hour or by the bottle. They strike, they overcharge, they play favorites, and they can close the site, turning everyone off it and working the ground shut, which no amount of Stage talks them out of unless the Stage is prepared to do it by force in front of witnesses.

The counter this hands you is the oldest in the book. Take the fight off rich ground. A fighter fat on a Core loses the fat a league down the road, on salted ground (worked by ward-cutters and hedge-workers so that Aether will not pool on it, and it gives back little for a season), on bled ground (pulled empty by a long fight or a greedy crew, just before you arrive if somebody has planned it), or on ground that was simply thin to begin with. Half the famous upsets in the brackets were won in the week before the fight, by someone quietly paying off the people who choose the field.

The second mode · Object Wellsprings

Aether of one type captured, bound, or naturally accumulated inside a vessel produces a contained domain, carried in a sword, a relic, or a formation old enough that the material and the Aether are indistinguishable.

Crevice Shale, T9, the Crevice itself: forbidden to move or hold or trade. A law about a rock, and the only such law the Accord has ever written. The most extreme Object Wellspring in the register.

The critical distinction between the two modes is interface geometry.

A site has a perimeter. The density gradient gives warning. The surveyor feels the Aether thickening as they approach the core.

An object is binary. You are holding it or you are not, and its internal domain contacts the Shell at zero distance. If the Shell is incompatible, the resulting conversation was not authorized and cannot be escaped without severing the causal relationship at the Plane of Fate (above the lower realities, the control of Attraction and Obsession and the workings of reality itself) level.

The colloquial term for an Object Wellspring whose Attraction Layer (the layer that holds bonds and claims) will not accept a termination event is cursed.

The Density Scale in Numbers

The condition is what a surveyor writes down. The number is what the ground will actually give.

The Measurewrights reckon one EU at one megajoule, so by their arithmetic a density in EU per cubic meter is an energy density, and an energy density is a pressure. In their books a density in EU/m³ is the same figure in megapascals. That one identity is why the scale is a ceiling and not a mood. A boundary cannot be pushed into ground at a pressure the ground does not carry, and what is standing in the ground has a strength measured in the same unit.

What a site will actually give. Aether crosses a site the size of a hall in about a thousand seconds. That is the interval in which the ground replaces what has been taken from it. So a site yields at most a thousandth of its density, per second. The supply rate in AU/s (Aether Units per second, a rate of output) per cubic meter is the density in EU/m³ divided by a thousand, and the ceiling on a practitioner’s sustained output and refill is that rate across the volume his field reaches. A burst is spent out of the reserve he walked in with and owes the ground nothing until he wants it back.

In a Silence (ground where no lawful working can be authorized) the figure is zero. That is the whole of why a Silence is something other than shielding. There is nothing to pull and nothing to push into, which is also why a soul held in one reports the sensation as loneliness rather than as weakness.

Ambient Saturation is the reference. Its midpoint, one EU per cubic meter, is the ground every laddered reach is quoted against. Denser ground fills a practitioner faster and lifts his ceiling on sustained output, as the supply column shows, which is why fighters will pay a keeper for it. It does nothing for his arm’s length, and a Fate fighter, whose Range and Domain radius are most of his fighting, is crowded by rich ground worst of all. It raises the floor his own field has to stand above, and addressing range, authority radius and anchor reach all fall as the square root of the local density. Concentrated ground cuts all three to an eighteenth. A Nexus cuts them to a thousandth. A Core cuts them to a five-millionth. That last figure is the arithmetic behind the sentence in the table above: inside a Core the law the Wellspring carries is the only law operating, and an S-Grade’s kilometer of authority is a fifth of a millimeter wide. One measure pushes the other way for one road. Thread Weight, the count of what has been decided on a piece of ground, extends a Fate fighter’s Range and the radius of his Domain, so heavy ground gives back some of what dense ground took, and the two are read together on any field a Fate lord means to hold (The Three Grounds).

Why residence changes a body. A person at rest dissipates about a hundred watts through about a fifteenth of a cubic meter, which is one and a half kilowatts per cubic meter. Active Concentration supplies ten to ten thousand kilowatts per cubic meter. The field is putting seven to seven thousand times the subject’s own metabolic power density through them, continuously, and a body has no mechanism for declining a power density. That is why Baptism at that condition is involuntary, and why it reaches the shepherd as readily as the adept.

Saturation, stated once. Saturation begins at ten thousand EU per cubic meter, the stress at which a crystal lattice fails. It is reached by leaving that much above the ground’s own density in each cubic meter of a region (a Nexus or a Core runs past the figure natively, and what those do to a visitor is the Density Scale’s business), or by sustaining more than a hundred AU/s through each square meter of the region’s boundary. The two are one statement. The second is the first carried out at the Aether’s own speed. The threshold grows with the surface while output does not, so a kilometer of open ground swallows what a hall would not. A Master’s field (Stages VIII to X, at the benchmark rate) a kilometer across puts about a fifth of one AU/s through each square meter of its edge, a five-hundredth of threshold. The same output inside a twenty-meter hall puts five hundred and fifty through each square meter, five times over, and everyone in the hall is a casualty of it. Open ground stops being the answer near the top of the ladder: an Archmaster’s benchmark through that same kilometer is eighty-odd per square meter, and the top of the Archmaster band runs past threshold. A drawn circle is the other half of the same arithmetic. A rite bleeding sixty EU a second into two meters of floor reaches Active Concentration within the hour and stays there, which is what the Circle step is for and what an improvised one costs.

The Attraction and Obsession Axis

A site operating under clean Attraction Force recognizes practitioners who enter it, reads the Crystal, assesses compatibility, and either permits harmonization or ignores the visitor. The experience is intense but orderly.

A site operating under Obsession Force has been corrupted. Pulled on without reciprocity. Exposed to sustained Mechanica (forbidden working that takes power without lawful consent) extraction. Saturated by wartime discharge never processed.

The Aether there does not recognize. It takes. It pulls at the Crystal whether the visitor is compatible or not.

Ground

Each Wellspring that acts on the world answers to the places where its idea is already at work. Aetheric Density sets the ceiling on sustained output everywhere, but a Wild or Medium lens’s own idea sets a second, narrower gate on top of it, and veterans choose ground accordingly. The Ground line on each entry says which: Pyreveil favors burned ground coming back green, kilns, forges and hot springs and hates still cold water and air that will not feed a flame, Petralon wants great masses of stone and heights under it, places that have long borne everything stood on them, and hates silt, loose sand and a drop with nothing under it, and Aurevane wants dry air and a clear line of sight and hates iron-walled rooms and dense fog, where the light is shut in or smeared before it can arrive.

These conditions bind what acts on the world, the Wild and the Medium. A Spirit Wellspring or a Wellspring of Fate has nothing in the world for the ground to favor or smother. It fills from Aetheric Density like every other lens, since every reserve is pulled Aether, and it fights on a measure of its own: a Spirit lens on Echo Depth, how thickly souls and memory lie on a place, and a lens of Fate on Thread Weight, how much has been decided there. Each band of Depth raises the yield (η) of Spirit work by a tenth, and each band of Weight does the same for a pull, exactly as each band of Density does for what acts on matter (The Three Grounds has the rolls). Its Ground line says where its Aether pools and thins, and those are nearly always the same places the Depth or the Weight is found: Catharsis gathers at bathhouses and confessionals, and Somnalis at crossroads, ferries and betting yards, and neither will light your way across a bad floor.

None of this is a prohibition. A Pyreveil practitioner in saturated cold still works. They simply pay a larger fraction of their reserve to work on ground that holds nothing of their idea, and their yield falls accordingly. Terrain is a modifier on yield (η: the share of spent power that arrives as effect), and anyone who calls it flavor has not yet paid for it.

Harmonization, Stacking, and Interference

What happens when a Crystal is asked to obey two laws at once.

A lens compounds only with its own years. Two Wellsprings never add up to one larger lens, and a second lens starts small however old the first one is. What they can do is agree. Coagula with Petralon is the bench’s standard example, since matter given form through force is one problem described twice: the two laws reinforce along the same axes, and such a soul’s Vitality and Resilience behave denser than an identical stat spread carrying neither. The numbers on the Ledger are identical. The qualitative expression is not, and anyone who has fought both can tell them apart in one exchange.

Contrary laws interfere, and the entries say which. Every further harmonization introduces resonance complexity, and each entry’s Contrary line names the pairs that will not behave and why. Contrary laws produce tension the Crystal must resolve, either by Temperance advancement or by developing a Compound Trait that holds the contradiction as a single law. Monolithion against Nihiloth is the canonical hard case, form set against the erosion of all shape. A contrary is no bar to holding both, and plenty of famous fighters are famous for exactly that, and it is the first thing a rival’s second looks for.

The ceiling is Tempering Capacity and it is not negotiable. Exceeding it does not fail gracefully by dropping the newest harmonization. It degrades all active harmonizations equally, which is how practitioners lose a decade of work by reaching for one more Wellspring in a bad hour.

Where the Registers Disagree

Attested, not reconciled.

The count · The Fracture register lists Sublimare among the Sixty. The consolidated Magic System register omits it entirely. With Sublimare restored and Phreatis counted once, the register reconciles exactly to sixty unique types. The Measurewrights record the consolidated table as the party in error and this register follows the Fracture.

Phreatis · Older registers list Phreatis twice, once as memory and once as water, which made sixty-one listings against sixty types. The idea supports the double assignment rather than resolving it: deep water and long memory are one keeping, read twice. The Measurewrights’ account says the same in its own terms, synaptic potentiation and aquifer residence as two physics describing one behavior. The entry is written once, on the Medium page, with both readings on it.

Sublimatio and Sublimare · The two have been treated as near-duplicates in prior registers on the strength of their names. Their operations separate them cleanly: Sublimatio’s is sublimation, the soul leaving its lower state in one clean step, and Sublimare’s is distillation, what is light in the soul driven up and parted from what stays. They are different operations with different residues and different failure modes, and the duplication was linguistic rather than real. (The Measurewrights put it as sublimation skipping a phase and fractional distillation separating a mixture.)

Sublimare in the Codex · Three Codex rows, the glyph [Prc] Pressure, the spell Pressure Veil and one alchemical row, still file Sublimare as cross-listed and read it as tracking phase-transition behavior. The Fracture register reads it otherwise and its bench law is distillation, and this register follows both. The Codex’s wording is recorded here and left as it stands.

Vohrin · Frost arts and cold galleries are entered all over the registers as if Vohrin were one of the sixty, and Vohrin is a Titan’s power and not one of the Sixty. The count stays at sixty. The power can be worked at Wellspring level, a cold site is filed as a Titan-touched site, and the Wild page carries a marked note with its analogue, failure and signature as the register’s one exception. Several Codex rows still give frost to Cymorath, which is the Air of Ascent and no frost Wellspring.

Petralon’s two Titans · Atla’zon’s line and Kaldrion’s both name Petralon. The register files it under Kaldrion, and for any ruling at an assay table the Titan read is Kaldrion and the table above is the authority. Atla’zon’s art stays written in Petralon, which nobody has yet had the nerve to tell either of them, and the argument is left standing in the halls, where it is a good deal older than this register and in better health.

The eleven with no form · Pyreveil, Cinerion, Manganthra, Aurevane, Nyxial, Tarturon, Ascensio, Abython, Penance, Redemption and Contrition appear in no row of the Glyph Index, primary or attested. The Index is fixed at 136 and nobody has shown whether the gap is in the record or in the world. A working under one of the eleven is written in a form seated elsewhere or in one of the unclassified Roots, which name a kind of limit and will condition any law.

Old sorting names in older sources · House of the Green Pulse, House of the Veil, House of the Black Gate and similar appear throughout the Wellspring codices and the Veil literature. These are the pre-correction feeling-first Houses and do not map cleanly onto the kinds.
