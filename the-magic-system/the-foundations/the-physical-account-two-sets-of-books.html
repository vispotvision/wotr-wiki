The Physical Account: Two Sets of Books

The Physical Account: Two Sets of Books

Nov 28, 202622 min read

The Continuum (the whole lawful order of reality) keeps two sets of books on every working ever performed, and they balance. Anyone who tells you only one of them is real is selling something.

A saying of the Guild of Measurewrights. The confessors’ answer, chalked under it on the Guild’s own door: “Both books balance. Only one of them says why.”

Hamo Thackeray, whom the song calls the Bishop of Cinders, burned the blue barge at the Kharven regatta in front of nine thousand people, and he did it in a white robe with gold on the shoulders, standing on the prow of his own boat with his arms out and his aura pouring off him the color of iron at cherry heat. He told the whole river that the Wellspring (one of the sixty great ideas that govern how a soul uses Aether, the raw stuff of magic in the world around you) had chosen him and that the fire was its blessing. The barge replied with a sheet of flame that blew the hats off half the quay. The Scarlet Oars went mad on the east bank. On the quay, up on a fish crate with a brass assay fork in one hand and a small brass water clock in the other, stood Ysolde Garth, a Measurewright (a certified assessor of workings) whose own Crystal had never woken, with forty silver riding on the afternoon, and she began shouting the fire’s schedule at him across the water. The tar would catch in a count of ninety. The mast would go at three hundred, to the left, because of the wind. It went to the left. The sail would throw sparks on the Bishop’s own boat at about four hundred and ten, and those sparks would burn exactly as sparks burn, whatever he had chosen to believe about them, and at four hundred and six the white robe went up with the Bishop in it. He had stopped working the fire some time earlier. The fire had not been told. He went into the river screaming and came out with no eyebrows and a new name, and Garth collected from three bookies and bought a round for the Scarlet Oars.

Thackeray’s account of that afternoon and Garth’s account were both complete, and that is the awkward part. He really had let an idea of fire take hold of his soul and established a boundary with it, at a real cost, and she really had read every step of what followed with a fork, a clock and a wet finger. This page is about why a working can be told both ways and has to be, and about which way to tell it first.

Whose book this is. This is the Measurewrights’ account, set down by their own guild under its own title, and it is filed here as their theory. The canon reading of a working is the lens: the idea that took hold of the soul, the operation it performed there, what it changed in the worker, how far the world gave way to that change, the glyph’s limit and the price. Thackeray’s idea is why there was fire on the river at all. Garth’s fork and clock are how much fire, for how long, and which way the mast would fall, and once the fire stood in the world it kept the world’s old habits, which is why she could call its schedule. The guild is often right about how much and never about why, and the benches will tell you so at length, whether or not you asked.

I. The Two Accounts

Every working admits two complete descriptions. The soul’s comes first, because it is what the person did and why it happened. The physics follows, because it is how the Measurewrights explain what the person did, and how they tell you what it will charge.

The stratal account is the lens, read across the three strata: which Wellspring’s idea governed the soul that did it and what operation it performed there, what lens that laid on the magic already in that soul and which Path it went out by, what the Aether in that room was doing, where the glyph set the law’s limit, and what it cost the Soul Crystal that spent it. This account requires a coil (the brass assay instrument a practitioner stands inside), a trained reader, and a practitioner willing to be read.

The physical account describes, in natural philosophy’s words, what happened to matter and energy in the room. Heat moved. Pressure changed. A structure failed along a plane of weakness. Air of a given humidity carried a given charge until it did not. It is a record of the world’s old habits, the ones settled in the Physical Epochs, and the measures describe those habits and never cause them. This account uses ordinary measurement, it is reproducible, and a competent Measurewright can take it from the outside with instruments and arithmetic, even one whose Soul Crystal (the soul itself, which stores and channels power) has never woken.

Both accounts are complete, and neither is a translation of the other, but they are not equals: the stratal book says why, and the physical book says how much. A working that has a good physical account and no stratal account was no working at all, and a working with a stratal account whose physics does not close was recorded by someone who did not check, since the world keeps its habits even while a soul is arguing with them.

How much each book has to say depends on the road. A Body Path blow gives the physical account nearly everything to write: the force, the tissue, the ground it went into. A working on the Spirit Path, and above all one under a Spirit Wellspring, may leave the room almost untouched, and then the physical book runs short: the reserve gone down, a skin that never warmed, a pulse taken, a man who sleeps differently afterward. It is a thin entry, and it still has to close, and the stratal book carries the rest. A pull on the Fate Path gives the opposite trouble. There the physical book closes perfectly and has nothing unusual in it, a worn strap parted at its worn stitch and a blade arriving where a blade could go, and the only line out of place is a person across the yard who has started to bleed from the nose. That nosebleed is no part of the guild’s energy, as section III explains. Everything that makes it a working is in the stratal book, which is how a Measurewright tells a pull from a bad morning, on the days she can tell at all.

The Accord’s Register asks for both from any hall that wants a working entered in it, and has since its second edition. Entries that carry only the mystic half are conventionally marked as devotional literature and shelved accordingly, which is the politest insult the Accord’s archivists have ever agreed on. The confessors return the courtesy and file the physics-only entries under plumbing.

II. What the Strata Actually Measure

The Wellspring stratum. Aether comes in sixty types, and each is first an idea: an alchemical principle and a spiritual transformation, a governing concept that changes how a soul uses what it pulls. A Wellspring is a lens on magic already potent, and it shapes what each Path spends (raw Aether in the Shell, Essence in the Core, the claim on the Attraction Layer), in time even doubling its effect on its home Path and reaching half again at most away from it; the Sixty have the full account. Each of them is one way a Titan’s law shows up, carried by Aether (mana) because Aether will carry anything. The stratal reading names which lens was in use and how long it has been lived with.

Scholars write each of those ideas down as a Core Law, a regularity of the world as their instruments see it, and the Measurewrights then set a law of natural philosophy beside it as their account of how much it will do. The glyph does not create the law. The glyph sets where a law that was already running stops, its limit at the layer the working lands through, and the working is what the idea does once that limit has moved.

This is the single most misunderstood sentence in practical instruction. Nobody makes fire out of nothing. A practitioner lets an idea of fire take hold of their own soul, the world gives way as far as that soul has truly changed, and fire stands where it did not; and once it stands in the world, the fire keeps every habit fire has ever kept, including the ones the practitioner has not thought about. Practitioners are burned by their own workings constantly, and the reason is always the same: the world’s habits kept running after the intent stopped. Ask the Bishop of Cinders.

The Aether stratum. Practitioners pull Aether from the environment around them, so where you stand matters: rich ground fills you faster and thin ground starves you. Aetheric Density is the scholar’s term for it, the local concentration available to be pulled. Residue is what a prior working left behind and has not yet dispersed. Saturation is the ceiling past which a room or a stretch of ground stops accepting further expression and begins returning it. These are field quantities. They vary with terrain, weather, season, the proximity of a site where a Wellspring’s Aether pools, and, in any settled town, with what the last forty people to fight there have done to the ground, whether anyone has salted or bled it, and whether the keepers of the nearest worked site have closed their gate. Fighters on the street call the two ends fat ground and lean ground, and they will walk an opponent half a league to get onto the one they like.

A practitioner who pulls in a saturated room is working against a gradient and pays for it in Harmonics (the stat that governs pulling Aether from the ground). A practitioner who pulls in a depleted one is drinking from a dry cup. Neither has anything to do with how strong they are.

The Essence stratum. The Soul Crystal is the soul, read here as a structure: its three layers, its look (how far the whole Crystal has matured, from Clouded to Absolute, which older books called its Developmental Tier), its Crystal State (Fractured, Refined or Overgrown), its yield (η, the share of a spend that reaches the world, as a distillation has its yield) through each layer, its reserve. This stratum is where the invoice arrives. Everything the other two strata do lawfully and impersonally, this stratum pays for personally. How personally depends on the road. Essence (spiritual energy) is Aether stamped with the identity of whoever refined it, the soul’s own substance, so on the Spirit Path the coin of that invoice is the person. The Charge a Body fighter packs in the Shell is raw Aether with nobody’s stamp on it, so there the coin is the world’s and the invoice is for handling: Shell strain, the heat of the Shell’s furnace burning, torn fiber. Either way it is the one reserve that goes down.

The Fate account lives on this stratum too, since there is nowhere else for it to live. Attraction Force, the third of the framework’s forces beside Aether and Essence, is what gives a working its endurance, and the Fracture treats it at length. Thread is that same force pulled down: what a Fate worker takes hold of and spends is Attraction Force brought through her own Layer, for the length of one working and no longer. Obsession Force is Thread held by force, kept in the hand after the working that called it ought to have let go. It is what sustains a corrupted working, and it leaves heavier knots than a clean pull of the same size. The coin that pays for either is Essence. A Fate soul refines what it draws exactly as a Spirit soul does, its share of fate is a part of that reserve, and a vow drains it and refills like any working. A reading of the Essence stratum therefore asks about the Attraction Layer as well as the reserve: what was pulled, how far from likely it was, and whose it may have been.

III. Where the Energy Comes From

The road in is short to say, and it is the same for everyone: Aether is pulled in from the world around the practitioner, through the Shell (the Crystal’s outer layer, and the only door into matter there is). Earlier editions of this page ran one road out again, for every working. There are three, and The Three Paths prices each.

The honest answer to the harder question is that what a working spends was Aether in a room once, which is to say a little of what the Division left over (Omnara has that story). A Body fighter spends them as they came, and a Spirit worker spends them refined, as does a Fate worker, who pays for her Thread in Essence. A fight Draft follows the drinker’s road. Core stock comes in from the bottle as unstamped Essence, and the crash that follows is the soul stamping it after the fact, at the soul’s expense. A Salt packs raw Aether straight into the Shell, and its crash is cramps and fever. There is no third bottle: a Fate worker drinks Core stock. What the practitioner spends was pulled from the world, refined and held before the working began, which is why a reserve runs down at all and why a fighter carried off rich ground has only what he walked in with. The Ledger converts it for the guild: what is spent is so many EU, which the Measurewrights reckon at a megajoule apiece, and the yielded share of it arrives. Only above a yield of one does the stratum pay directly, when the Continuum recognizes the expression as law and supplements it from the room around the practitioner, and the room shows it afterward.

This is why a Crystal can produce an output that no metabolism could sustain, and why the practitioner is nevertheless destroyed by producing it. The power came out of the room, by way of the reserve, and never out of the body’s own meals. The structure that let it move in that particular direction was in the person, and structure is what fails.

Yield is the ratio of effect that arrives to what was spent. Each of the three layers has its own figure, set by that road’s rung of Mastery (Body for the Shell, Spirit for the Core, Fate for the Attraction Layer), read on one table, so one soul can be thrifty through one layer and ruinous through another. The remainder goes where the layer sends it, and only the Shell sends it into the room. What the Shell loses burns in the Shell, which is the athanor, the furnace that never goes cold, and leaves as heat, sound, and structural bleed, which is why a low-yield practitioner is loud, warm, and easy to find, and why the Grandmasters and Archmasters leak so little: what a room sees and hears of them is their own aura and the working itself, never the furnace.

Core and Layer loss are paid in that layer. A low-yield Spirit working takes its loss out of the Core, as fray and a name gone missing, and a low pull that loses nearly all it spent takes the loss out of the soul’s own share and leaves it standing on the Layer as a knot, which the world collects from the puller some days later and which no fork has ever rung. Neither warms the room by a degree. The blood and the body tells that come with them are the report of that payment, and they carry none of the energy. A first-year will always work out what half a Glory reserve is in the guild’s megajoules, enough to boil a fair-sized millpond, set that against one nosebleed across a yard, and bring the sum to her instructor as proof that the books do not close. They close. The physical book was never owed that money, the stratal book took the whole debit on the day, and the nosebleed is the receipt.

IV. The Four Theories

The Accord’s halls hold to one account of why a Wellspring answers and do not pretend the other three have been refuted. None of the four disputes what a lens does to a soul, since every confessor and every bench has watched it happen, and none of them is physics. They dispute why the idea answers at all. Measurewrights will fight about this at weddings, and at least one of the four is wrong, and possibly all of them.

The Consent Doctrine, the one the halls keep. The Wellspring recognizes a lawful boundary condition and behaves accordingly, in the same sense that a river recognizes a channel. No intention is attributed to the Wellspring. This is the position that permits licensing, because a process can be licensed and a relationship cannot.

The Correspondence Position. The practitioner and the Wellspring are the same law expressed at two scales, and the working is the two scales briefly agreeing. Ecclesiastically respectable, administratively useless.

The Debt Position. The Wellspring answers on credit and the account is settled somewhere the practitioner cannot see. Held mostly in the eastern quarter and by the older rendering families, and impossible to test, and disturbingly good at predicting which practitioners come apart.

The Residue Position. There is no answering at all. Every Wellspring is the standing residue of something that already happened at Epoch scale, and practitioners are moving through the wreckage of a working performed before there were people. The Archives has never formally engaged with it. Two Prime Instructors hold it privately.

The Accord’s preference for the Consent Doctrine is a licensing decision rather than a finding, and the Registers say so in a footnote that has been recopied without comment for four hundred years.

V. The Body Is the Readout

Nothing in a soul improves without strain, and strain is a physical event before it is anything else.

Tempering melts the Crystal down and rebuilds it, one layer at a time. Each layer is rebuilt in its own coin, the lead layer first: the Shell comes out able to pack and hold more raw Charge, the Core’s Essence comes out refined and stronger, and the Layer’s claim firms, though no rung of Fate Mastery moves for it, since Mastery is read from Level. The body gains Traits from all of it. A Crystal densifies when it is driven to the edge of its structural tolerance and held there. In practice that means long breathing and pulling in dense Aether or at a site where a Wellspring pools, a seclusion held past comfort on the Path’s own ground (Aetheric Density for a Body soul, Echo Depth for a Spirit one, Thread Weight for a Fate one), an Essence Draft rendered from a monster’s core, a real fight, injury past safety, or genuine proximity to death. The Crystal does not distinguish between a Threshold (the crisis that lifts a soul to its next Stage) approached deliberately in a seclusion chamber or a training yard and one arrived at by accident in a ditch, except that the ditch has a better record. All of this is tempering and pressure on the next Stage. Level is another matter and moves by danger overcome, barring a blessing, so the real fight and the ditch count twice and the seclusion once. Neither does the Crystal ask where the Threshold’s Catalyst (the event a Stage demands before it will open) came from. Drafts and blessings can supply one, so a Stage can simply be bought, and the melt is every bit as real in a house with good carpets. A lived Threshold shows what a bought one hides: it passes the Great Work in its Path’s own terms, nigredo, albedo, citrinitas and rubedo, the soul broken down, purified, woken and fixed, and the benches can tell the two seats apart at a glance. What that buys and what it does not is on The Ladder and the Draft, and the ditch still has the better record.

Because the Aether Shell is the layer that sits in the Physical World, everything a Crystal does eventually shows in tissue. Traits (standing laws a soul carries, formed by what it survived, each working as deep as its bearer’s Mastery) deposit mineral, rebuild vasculature, alter myelination, shift resting core temperature. Overchannel (forcing output past the Crystal’s safe ceiling) scars. Starvation (running below a tenth of reserve; Charge Starvation in a Body fighter, Essence Starvation in a Spirit or a Fate worker, both of whom hold Essence) flickers a Trait the way a bad wick flickers a lamp, and after that each road sends its own bill: a Body fighter’s Shell starts drawing its Charge out of the flesh and the skin splits in fine lines, and a Spirit worker’s Core starts to fray, names and memory first. A physician can tell which road a patient walked by how they ran dry, hot and cracked or cold and hollow, and a Fate worker’s ward-mates will tell her without being asked, since their luck went first. A practitioner’s history can be taken off their living body by a competent physician and off their dead one by a competent renderer, and the second reading is better.

This is why the physical account matters at all. A system that only described itself in its own vocabulary could not be checked. The body is where the two sets of books are reconciled, and the reconciliation is involuntary. The physical book checks how much, and it is very good at it; it has never once said why.

The private report. A soul also keeps a third account, for its owner alone. Since the Soul Crystal is the soul and the Continuum measures it, the soul reports its numbers to its bearer in the bearer’s own tongue, and nobody else can see it. People call it a window. Who built it is one more thing Measurewrights will fight about at weddings. A window shows numbers, ranks, percentages, bars and Level, and it keeps none of the Measurewright’s own quantities, so EU (the unit of the reserve), AU/s (the rate of output) and η (yield) stay on her side of the ledger:

[Reserve: 41% ▓▓▓▓░░░░░░]
[Mastery: Body II, up from I]
[Skill: Long Breathing, Apprentice to Journeyman]

A window balances neither book and proves nothing in a hearing, since its only witness is the person it flatters. It reports what the body has already said, and a line a giver wrote into the sheet with a blessing can lie.

VI. The Form of a Proper Entry

The Registry’s standard, reordered in this edition to run in the order the thing actually happens, and honored about half the time.

The concept, in one breath and ordinary words, with the Wellspring whose idea it wears, the transformation that Wellspring works and the Path it goes out by.

The operation, the bench operation the idea performs in the soul (calcination, dissolution, separation, conjunction, fermentation, distillation, coagulation or sublimation), what it changes in the worker and how that change reaches the world. Beside it, for the guild’s own file, the phenomenon a Measurewright would name, labeled as theirs.

The Wellspring and its law, with its kind and its Titan, the Core Law stated as a regularity of the idea, and the glyph identified as the limit it sets.

The Path, Lens, Form and Gates, which say where the cost sits and where the counter sits.

The Essence account, at all three strata, in diagnostic voice.

The fault, which falls out of the flaw in the idea, the thing it cannot hold, and never out of the practitioner’s stamina. Every working has one. A working recorded without a fault has been recorded by someone who did not look, and the Registry’s back catalog is full of them.

VII. The Four Faults

Every documented failure mode, the guild says, reduces to one of these. The benches agree, and add that each is the same thing seen from a different side: an idea’s own flaw meeting the world.

Boundary fault. The condition was established imprecisely and the law ran somewhere unintended. Burns, structural collapse in the wrong wall, a working that continued after the practitioner stopped.

Field fault. The room could not supply what the working assumed. Saturation, depletion, salted or bled ground, a worked site whose keepers have closed it, a Silence Realm (ground where no lawful working can be authorized). The working is correct and produces nothing. This is why the cheapest counter to a fighter fat on rich ground is to take the fight somewhere else.

Structural fault. The Crystal could not hold what the boundary permitted. Overchannel, Backlash, Fracture. The working succeeds and the practitioner does not.

Coherence fault. The practitioner’s lived behavior and their stated law have drifted apart, and the Continuum reads the drift. Output degrades, Thresholds refuse to authorize, and Traits begin to argue with their bearer. This is the slowest fault and the one that ends careers.
