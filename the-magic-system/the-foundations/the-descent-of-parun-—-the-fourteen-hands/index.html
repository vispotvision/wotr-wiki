The Descent of Parun — The Fourteen Hands

The Descent of Parun — The Fourteen Hands

Nov 17, 202620 min read

A Reader’s Grammar of the Written Tongues · compiled for the Guild Accord Division of Alchemetrica

Fourteen hands · one lattice · one root

“There is one script. There have always been fourteen ways to be wrong about that.”

Attributed to a Measurewright (a certified assessor of practitioners and workings) of the Vaulted Ring, name struck from the record.

Ambrose Pinchbeck, a Concord wine factor of good family, learned Barkstone Tally in one afternoon at the Cooper’s Fair, read four hundred cask ledgers by the evening, and bought what he took for forty-year pitch-oak at a price that made the Cask Line of the Long Rack go very quiet and very polite. The letters had said forty. The slant of the barbs had said four. He tapped the first cask in front of a paying crowd, and got a spray of young green beer that went up like a whale’s breath and hit the banner of his own house, and then the whole rack began to go, bung after bung blowing out across the yard, four hundred casks working in the summer heat, with the coopers singing the tally aloud in harmony and the foam running gold and stinking down Rack Street as far as the river. Pinchbeck was still paying for it nine years later. Every clerk in the Four Quarters knows the song, and hardly one of them can tell you which barb he misread, so here is the whole business from the root.

The Root and the Six Drivers

Nothing in this record is a separate invention. Every hand traces the same Parun (the root script of law that reality reads) path across the same lattice (the fixed grid every glyph is traced on, which from above is Bara’s Loom; the Glyph Index joins the two), and every difference between them is the residue of a decision that was never about writing at all.

DriverWhat it does to the hand
The toolA chisel makes straight segments. A punch makes points and no line at all. A knife on a stave cuts with the grain or splits it. A reed on wet bark cannot be lifted. Three of these produce scripts a scholar would not guess were related
The surfaceGranite holds a curve badly and holds a straight cut for four thousand years. Soft iron takes an impact and refuses a stroke. Wet bark bleeds. Living tissue grows, which is a problem no other surface poses
The speedA trade hand and a liturgical hand cannot be the same hand. Every script here that got faster did so by joining what had been separate, and every one of them was accused of dilution while doing it
The purposeLaw wants a hand that cannot be varied. Ritual wants a hand that cannot be casually produced. Correspondence wants a hand that can be produced at all. These pull in different directions and no culture has ever resolved them into one script
The prohibitionTwo hands were shaped mostly by what was refused. The Varrisak refused the rail. The Ironblood refused, and still refuse, to write Gravetongue down at all
The bodyOnly the Fleshshapers write on something that changes size. Their solution, nesting the same glyph at three scales, is the single most original piece of orthography in the world and is universally read by outsiders as scarring

Every plate renders the same five roots in every hand: BALANCE of Urion, MOTION of Wyther, RECALL of Maelor, DISRUPTION of Uurgath, and VASTNESS of Zhaeren. Read down the plates and the family resemblance surfaces on its own.

What a Hand Changes and What It Does Not

The question every apprentice asks in the second week, answered badly by most instructors.

A glyph creates nothing. The law of the Wellspring (one of the sixty governing ideas that change how a soul uses Aether, which is mana, each written down by scholars as a fixed law) was already running, and the glyph is a boundary set on it: it says where the law stops. The boundary is set by the traced path, and the path is the same path in all fourteen hands, because all fourteen trace one lattice. Where the limit lands is a separate question, settled by the layer of the soul the maker’s Essence (spiritual energy) leaves through and answered in the Index. The hand has no say in that.

So a hand does not change the working. A ward cut in Old High Runic and the same ward struck in Old Vaross impose the same condition on the same law and produce the same effect. This is why the Accord can enforce a writ across eleven hundred leagues and why a Chancery notary and a Cask Line cooper are arguing about the same instrument even when neither can read the other’s page.

What a hand changes is everything around the working.

What the hand decidesWhy
How long the boundary can holdThe Seal sets the term and the substrate caps it. A boundary cannot persist longer than its substrate does. Granite outlives the mason. Wet bark outlives the afternoon. The hand and the surface are one decision and the ceiling on duration follows from it
Who can find itMirror-Sign leaves no signature because nothing was inscribed to leave one. The boundary is real and the evidence of it is not, which is a legal problem rather than a metaphysical one and the Accord has never solved it
Who can check itInverted Parunic is an attack on the reader rather than on the working. The outsider reads fluently, arrives at a boundary that was not set, and acts on a condition that does not exist
Whether it can be validated at allCold Parunic engineers ambiguity out so that two readers cannot set two boundaries from one page. Sylvaaric refuses the question. Both positions are coherent and they cannot share a treaty
Whether it survives its own authorSkin-Records grow. The nested scales exist because a boundary written on something that changes size stops describing the same limit within a decade

Gravetongue is the exception that proves the rule, and it is not a hand at all. Every other entry here is rune-cutting: a boundary committed to substrate. Gravetongue carries its condition in a physical resonance produced by a body that knows what it is made from, which makes it a chant (a working carried by the voice) with no notation and explains the inert transcriptions completely. No chant is Parun, for that matter: a chant holds its condition in resonance, and its words carry a sentence only when someone trained in the writing of law has put one there.

A written Gravetongue phrase produces nothing because the writing was never where the boundary lived. The Crimson Order’s pulse-code works for the same reason: they kept the resonance and discarded the part that was never load-bearing.

Cutting a rune is a skill and a trade, and anyone with a woken Crystal can level it. It rises with use and shows as a Grade on the sheet, one line for each hand the cutter has really worked, and a guild’s title says how far she has gone in it and how large a job a stranger may trust her with. The Temple of Inscription and the Accord’s scribes have been telling each other for centuries that the other side does not count, and that is the whole of the rank in it.

Parun

The Lawful Root

Not a language in the sense that a mother sings to a child in it. It is the grammar the Continuum (the whole lawful order of reality) reads, and it has no register below the legal one.

A Parun sentence names who is speaking, what ground the speech covers, which way the working runs, and how it is to end, and it does this whether it is chalked on a barn door or held internally by an Archmagus who has not moved his hands.

Every hand that follows is Parun with one thing done to it. Change the tool, change the surface, change what the writing is for, and the same traced path comes out the other side unrecognizable to anyone but a scholar.

The scholars are the ones who insist all of this is one script. Nobody else believes them until it is shown side by side.

Legibility · Spoken, gestured, inscribed. No mother tongue anywhere.

The Fourteen Hands

The count is a librarian’s. It takes Parun itself as the first of fourteen and the thirteen below it as the rest, Gravetongue included by courtesy, since it has no hand to count. The hands are not one per Archon, whatever the lecture halls of the Vaulted Ring would like: the two fourteens share a number and no history.

Old High Runic

The High Line · the sacred hand

A chisel driven into granite will not make a curve. It makes a straight segment and then another one, and the mason’s wrist has eight bearings available to it and no more. Old High Runic is Lattice Tongue put through that constraint for four thousand years: every arc squared off, every terminal blocked, every glyph resolved onto the compass.

The Temple of Inscription runs seventy years on a single theorem cut in this hand, and the discipline is not the theorem. It is the cutting.

The High Line will tell you the hand is slow because truth is slow. What they will not tell you is that they have never found a way to make it faster and have stopped considering that a failure.

Legibility · Read by any trained Parunist. Cut correctly by almost no one.

Barkstone Tally

The Cask Line · the working hand · plate 03

A knife on a cask stave cuts with the grain or it splits the stave, so the Barkstone hand gave up on the second dimension entirely. The whole path collapses onto one stem and every turn in it becomes a barb cut off that stem: above it, below it, or straight across. It reads downward, the way a stave stands.

The trap is that the barb angles are not decoration. They carry duration, which in a brewing hand is the load-bearing quantity, and a Grand Brewmaster reading a forty-year cask ledger is reading time off the slant of the cuts.

Outsiders learn the letters in an afternoon and then misprice barrels for a decade.

Legibility · Learned in an afternoon. Read properly after a decade.

Mirror-Sign

Deepkin · the hand that is not there · plate 04, the Hollow

Everything else in this record is a stroke laid on a surface. Mirror-Sign is the surface laid around a stroke that was never made. The field is worked and the path is left bare, so what a reader follows is the absence, and the absence produces no Aetheric signature because nothing was ever inscribed to produce one.

This is why no ward in the world detects Deepkin correspondence and why no Concord auditor has ever successfully audited a Deepkin holding.

The High Line calls it a broken geometry. The Deepkin have not sent a reply, and the Mirror-Sign record of their position has never been translated for anyone outside the lineage.

Legibility · Read by fingertip and by reflection. Undetectable to Harmonics (the trained sense that reads Aether in the field).

Old Vaross

Ironblood · the ancestral hand · plate 05, the Wedge

A punch cannot draw a line. It can only strike a point and leave a wedge behind, so Old Vaross kept the nodes of the Parun path and lost every connecting stroke between them. What sits on the iron is a constellation of impacts, and the reader supplies the line that runs between them out of their own head.

Ironblood theology takes this as the correct arrangement rather than a limitation.

The First Impact did not draw anything either. It struck once and left the world to work out what shape the strike had been, and a people who read their own script by reconstructing the missing stroke are practicing that every time they open a war-oath.

Legibility · Read across every goblinoid people. Written now only in ritual.

Low Goblintongue

Za’tarch · the trade hand

Trade will not wait for a punch and a hammer. Somewhere in the Za’tarch markets a clerk shrank the wedges down to nail-marks and ran a single quick stroke between them, and what had been a constellation became a line again with the impacts still hanging off it.

Faster, and legible to a Verdant Orc and a Tinker and a Hobgoblin quartermaster alike, and every Ironblood elder alive considers it a thinned thing.

Gorrum Ashentusk writes in it deliberately and in front of them, which is a position taken rather than a convenience.

Legibility · The most widely read goblinoid hand. Universal in council.

Gravetongue

Ironblood · never written · plate 14, the Silent

Gravetongue has no hand and has refused every attempt to give it one. Transcriptions exist. They are inert. What is on the page produces nothing when read aloud by someone who was not there, because the meaning of a Gravetongue phrase is not carried in the phonemes but in a specific physical resonance produced by a body that knows what it is made from.

The Crimson Order stripped the chant element out and kept the three-beat rhythm as pulse-code, which works, and which Ironblood traditionalists regard as grave-robbery.

The empty frame on the plate is drawn from a Concord ethnographer’s field notes. He ruled the box, was refused, and filed it empty rather than fill it with an approximation.

Legibility · No script exists. The frame is what a scribe leaves behind.

Swamp Parunic

Verdant Orc · the flowing hand

Reed on wet bark bleeds. Lift the reed and the ink spreads into the fiber and eats the next stroke before you have made it, so the Verdant hand never lifts. One continuous line, every glyph joined to the next, terminals drawn long so the bleed has somewhere to go that does not matter.

Held near saturated ground, certain sequences in this hand have catalyzed Spoor-Meld states in readers who were not attempting one. Whether that is a property of the script or of the language it carries is disputed. The Verdant position is that the question is badly posed.

Legibility · Read with effort by other goblinoids. Written by few outsiders.

Florwyndic Songscript

Eressean · the binding hand · plate 08

The rail is drawn first. One unbroken horizontal stroke across the whole line, and every glyph after it hangs beneath that stroke rather than standing on a baseline.

The rail is the Authorization clause (the part of a working that names its lawful channel) for the entire sentence, which is why nothing written under a broken rail carries force, and why an Eressean scribe who lifts the pen mid-rail begins the page again.

The hand of law and consecration and nothing else. Ordinary correspondence uses a plain unrailed cursive that no one bothers to name.

A private letter written in Songscript is either a legal instrument or a threat, and the recipient is expected to know which before opening it.

Legibility · Read by every educated elf. Written only under license.

Sylvaaric

Varrisak · the unruled hand

The Varrisak took the Eressean forms and refused the rail. What is left runs as a single meandering line with the nodes swelled into blooms and small tendrils thrown off wherever the hand felt like throwing one. No two Varrisak scribes produce the same glyph twice and none of them regard this as a defect.

An Eressean jurist will tell you Sylvaaric cannot be a script because it cannot be validated. A Varrisak reading a hand they know will tell you they can identify the writer, the season and the mood from the tendrils alone, which is a great deal more information than a validated glyph carries.

Legibility · Read within the wandering. Illegible to Eressean eyes by design.

Inverted Parunic

Drow · the hand built to be misread

Every Drow glyph is its Parun ancestor mirrored, run right to left, with the opening and sealing terminals exchanged.

Opacity would be kinder. An outsider does not stall on Inverted Parunic.

They read it easily, confidently, at speed, and they arrive at a meaning that is not the one on the page.

The inversion is constructed rather than drifted, which means it is applied with a consistency no natural hand has ever managed. Its one failure mode is under pressure, when Eressean structure surfaces underneath, and a Drow scribe who lets that happen in front of a witness has done something they will not be forgiven for.

Legibility · Read fluently by any Parunist, and read wrong.

Cold Parunic

Echo Elf · the hand without a hand · plate 11

Quantizes the Parun path onto a fixed grid of cells. Every glyph occupies the same count of cells. No cursive form, no ligature, no personal hand, no flourish, and no permitted variation of any kind between one scribe and the next.

A treaty that can be written two ways can be recited two ways, and the Echo Elf position is that a peace which has to be interpreted will eventually have to be refought.

Their diplomacy is exhausting and their treaties have never once needed renegotiation. They regard the second fact as sufficient answer to the first.

Legibility · Read identically by everyone. That is the entire specification.

Skin-Records

Fleshshaper · the growing hand

A glyph cut into living tissue does not stay the size it was cut. The body grows, the scar migrates, and within a decade a single-scale inscription has stretched past legibility. Most of what a Fleshshaper cuts is record and carries no working; a cut that sets a live boundary in the flesh is a rare and dangerous high art, and its failures are buried quietly.

The answer is to cut the same glyph three times, nested inside itself at diminishing scale, so that when the outer form has distorted the middle one is still readable and the innermost has not begun to move.

An elder’s back is a stack of decades that can be read downward through the scales, and a Fleshshaper reading another Fleshshaper is reading age directly off the ratio between the rings.

Outside their own communities this is invariably mistaken for mutilation, and they have long since stopped correcting the mistake.

Legibility · Read by Fleshshapers. Read as disfigurement by everyone else.

Accord Chancery Hand

Guild Accord · the ruled hand

It did not descend from anything. Assembled by committee in the post-Calamity regulation era out of whatever fragments of the older hands could be made to sit inside a ruled band of fixed height, and then boxed, so that a writ issued in one kingdom could be read without dispute in another eleven hundred leagues away.

It is characterless on purpose. No lineage, no region, no hand, no mood, and every scribe trained in it produces output indistinguishable from every other.

Practitioners of the older hands describe it as a script with nothing behind it. Accord notaries consider this the highest compliment their office has ever received.

Legibility · Read by everyone from Marrowhold to Kharven. That is the product.

A Hand Outside the Count

The Valorin write Vaeloric Script, and it is a further hand in every sense the six drivers can measure. Its surface is the soul itself, a Scripted Soul Lattice that never crystallized, and it traces the same lattice as everything on this page, so nothing in this record is a separate invention even here. A soul with no Crystal still has the three faces of one, and when the Continuum reads a Valorin’s Authorization it reads the Attraction Layer of that written soul exactly as it reads anyone’s.

The odd things the house does are ordinary grammar on an odd surface. The lines in its Cistern are formulae left one word short, which is a skipped slot, and a skipped slot fires nothing and bills nothing, which is why a master can carry a lifetime of them and sleep soundly. Errata is an amendment to a sentence somebody else is already saying, so it adds no third form to the two that obstruct. Their writing on Fate stays Fate: they cannot read it, and can only write a sentence and wait to see whether it is recognized, which takes generations.

Who Reads What

Cross-legibility is not symmetrical and is not a function of how closely two hands are related. A Parunist can read Inverted Parunic fluently and will be wrong about all of it. A Concord clerk can read the Chancery Hand and nothing else. The practical map is narrower than the family tree suggests.

The Method

Workbook. How the Division rules its plates, so that no form has to be drawn from nothing.

The lattice is the 5×5 kamea of Mars, constant 65. Each of the fourteen Archons reads that lattice from a different station: one of the eight dihedral symmetries plus a cyclic value-shift.

A glyph name reduces letter by letter to lattice cells, and the path traced between those cells is the glyph. Class and Wellspring are inflections laid on that path rather than redrawings of it.

No glyph is drawn by eye. Which means the method never runs out: name a new spell and its written form already exists in every one of the fourteen hands.

Any path can be traced, then, and what stays fixed is attestation: a hundred and thirty-six forms have a station’s reading on record, and a fresh trace has none. The Seven Works of Hataraki trace nothing at all, being grips on Fate and not paths on the lattice, and so they appear in no hand.

Load-bearing cross-reference. The Long Reading holds that the Moto ancestral seat’s own name is recorded in a glyph the Continuum cannot classify, one of the thirty unclassified forms in the Master Glyph Index. It is the oldest proper noun in the record and it is deliberately blank.
