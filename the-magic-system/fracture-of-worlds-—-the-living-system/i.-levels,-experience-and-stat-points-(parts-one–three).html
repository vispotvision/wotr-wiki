I. Levels, Experience and Stat Points (Parts One–Three)

I. Levels, Experience and Stat Points (Parts One–Three)

Nov 17, 202615 min read

Jory Eel-Hand of the Slackwater fens, below Altherion, holds a record nobody has tried to take from him. Over thirteen years he killed the same kind of marsh eel, violet and thick as a thigh, with the same stroke of the same gaff, somewhere past forty thousand times, in the firm belief that a man who killed enough of anything must eventually climb. He gained two Levels (a Level is one count of the Soul Crystal growing denser), both in the first year. The children on the Eel Steps sang a counting song about whether he would ever make a third, and they had reached the rude verse on the morning a Well-spawn eel (a creature born where a Wellspring’s Aether pools) the length of a tithe barge came up through the fen with a mask of shining gold across its face. Jory put the gaff through the mask because it was the only stroke he knew, and then spent an hour in the thing’s coils being broken in ways no eel had ever broken him. He crawled out at sunset minus an ear and three fingers, nine Levels higher, weeping and laughing, and the whole of the Steps carried him to the taverns on a door. His gaff hangs in the fish guild’s hall, with a brass plate. Young fools come to touch it, and the Crystal counts that for about as much as it counted the eels.

The Guild has a word for what forty thousand eels were worth, Familiar, and you will find it in Part Ten on the next page. This page is the arithmetic of that afternoon.

Part One — The Level Structure

The Hard Cap

The system operates on a maximum of Level 500 for every practitioner below Stage XV. This number is not arbitrary. It represents the structural limit of what a mortal Soul Crystal (literally the soul itself, where a practitioner’s power is held and shaped) can contain before the distinction between practitioner and Principle collapses entirely. Characters below Stage XV who reach Level 500 and continue to accumulate sufficient Soul Crystal pressure do not advance in level. They begin the Apotheosis approach, a qualitative event outside the scope of numbered progression. Paragons exceed Level 500. At Stages XV–XVI, Level keeps rising past 500 with no ceiling and no sixth Band; the Codex does not number it.

Levels divide into five Bands of one hundred levels each. Every Band corresponds to a Temperance Stage (one of the sixteen rungs of a soul’s refinement) cluster. No character can breach a Band ceiling without first passing the Temperance Threshold (the breakthrough that lifts a soul to its next Stage) that cluster demands. The System will simply halt the level counter and build pressure, called Residual Strain, until the Threshold is passed or the character fractures under the weight.

The Five Bands

Band I: Mortal Foundation, Levels 1 through 100. The era of first contact. The Soul Crystal is porous, receptive, and relatively unformed. Growth here is the fastest in a character’s life because the Crystal is still learning the shape of Essence. Temperance cluster: Stage I through Stage IV, Murmuring through Flourishing. Practitioners of genuine but bounded power, dangerous to mortals, irrelevant to Archons. Gate: Stage IV before Level 100 can be surpassed.

Band II: Awakened, Levels 101 through 200. The character has survived their first genuine encounters with forces larger than themselves. The Soul Crystal has begun to show its character, the specific resonance signature that will define this practitioner’s identity across every future stage. Techniques begin to cohere. A Domain seed (the first root of a space where the practitioner’s law overrides local law) becomes possible. Temperance cluster: Stage V through Stage VII, Splintering through Refraction. Gate: Stage VII before Level 200.

Band III: Sovereign, Levels 201 through 300. Genuine metaphysical authority. Their Aura suppresses weaker resonances without effort. Their techniques carry the weight of doctrine rather than improvisation. The world responds to their presence before they act. Temperance cluster: Stage VIII through Stage X, Transcendence through Realization. Gate: Stage X before Level 300.

Band IV: Mythic, Levels 301 through 400. Power is no longer measured in individual techniques. It is measured in how significantly a practitioner’s existence reshapes the metaphysical landscape of the space they occupy. Domain becomes law. Soul Crystal density generates passive environmental effects. Temperance cluster: Stage XI through Stage XII, Dissonance through Emanation. Gate: Stage XII before Level 400.

Band V: Absolute, Levels 401 through 500. Cosmological presences. Metaphysical output measured against Archon and Titan-scale phenomena. Individual combat becomes a secondary concern; the primary arena is the contest of Domains, Principles, and accumulated doctrine pressing against the laws of the Continuum itself. Temperance cluster: Stage XIII through Stage XIV, Principality and Zenith, with Stage XV Revelation and Stage XVI Apex lying beyond canonical mortal progression.

Zenith, Stage XIV, is not a stage that ends. It is the state of a soul that has reached the upper boundary of mortal architecture and remains there, pressing against what it has not yet become. Gate: none. There is no Band beyond Band V. The pressure of Stage XIV has no ceiling to break through, only the Apotheosis approach, which is not a level event.

Part Two — Experience and Resonant Accumulation

What Experience Actually Is

Experience is Resonant Accumulation, the compression residue left inside the Soul Crystal when a living being makes genuine contact with forces, choices, and consequences that exceed their current metaphysical comfort. Level moves by danger overcome, so killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills, and crews argue over who got the kill, loudly and for years. The window settles nothing, since it reports only to its owner: [Level 12, up 9].

The Soul Crystal is not impressed by repetition. It measures growth pressure. The question it asks of every event is: did this stretch the soul’s architecture toward something it has not yet become? If yes, the Crystal records it. If no, the event passes through without deposit.

This is why experience cannot be farmed indefinitely. A threat that demanded everything from you at Level 12 demands nothing of you at Level 47 against the same opponent type with the same techniques. The Crystal has already solved that shape.

How Experience Is Earned

Combat and Genuine Threat. The System does not reward the number of enemies defeated. It rewards the degree of genuine pressure sustained, the novelty of response demanded, and the scale of force engaged relative to the practitioner’s current ceiling.

Same Tier, previously used technique against the same opponent type: base XP at diminishing rates beginning at the second repetition, reaching ten percent of base value by the fifth engagement with no meaningful variation.

Same Tier, novel adaptive response: one hundred percent of base XP regardless of repetition history. The Crystal rewards the new sentence, not the repeated word.

Fighting above Tier is the single most efficient form of experience generation in the system. One Tier above: 1.8x. Two Tiers above: 3.2x. Three Tiers above: 6.0x. Four or more Tiers above, survived: minimum 12.0x, scaling further with the degree to which survival required genuine soul-edge engagement.

Fighting at zero personal risk, no Essence burned, no wound sustained, no moment of genuine threat registered: thirty percent of base combat XP. The Crystal is not impressed by efficiency. It rewards contact with consequence.

Wellspring Contact Events. Direct first contact with the active Aether of a Wellspring not previously encountered produces a Resonant Surge, a significant one-time XP event. Minor Wellspring contact at Band I: roughly 500 XP. First contact with the raw, untamed Aether of a primary Wellspring at Band III: upward of 40,000 XP. Non-repeatable with the same Wellspring. Deepening existing attunement through sustained channeling, by long breathing and pulling at a Wellspring site or in dense Aether, and by seclusion before a Threshold, moves no Level. It tempers the Crystal and builds the slow, persistent pressure toward the next Threshold that Part Eight asks for.

Gnosis Events. Deciphering an unknown Parunic (written in Parun, the language of magical law) glyph-construct independently. Reading a Wellspring’s structural composition without prior instruction. Diagnosing a Soul Crystal fracture through Aetheric Diagnostics alone. At a desk these build pressure toward the next Threshold and move no Level, because conceptual growth registers as structural deepening in the Crystal rather than as danger overcome. Done with a life riding on the answer, they count as the danger they were.

Soul-Cost Events. Using Overchannel (forcing more output than the Crystal can safely sustain) and sustaining structural Aether Shell (the Crystal’s outer layer, which turns Essence into force) damage to protect something irreplaceable. Breaking a technique before completion at personal mechanical cost to prevent harm to another. Not counted: theatrical self-harm, deliberate damage with no real stakes, cost manufactured strategically for accumulation. The Crystal reads intent, not form.

Oath and Covenant Formation. Swearing a genuine oath, before a Witness Stone or on nothing but your own breath, builds pressure toward the next Threshold commensurate with the weight of what is risked, and Level moves only when keeping it walks the swearer through real danger. An oath that costs nothing builds nothing. A significant pulse on swearing, and again on fulfillment. A broken vow lands at once by its own terms, and catastrophic violation registers as a Temperance strain event that builds its own painful pressure. See Vows and Oaths.

Temperance Threshold Completion. The largest single XP event available. It generates a retroactive surge that partially pre-fills the next Band. Temperance advancement is not a product of experience. It is a generator of it. The Crystal that survives a Threshold is a larger vessel than it was before. A Threshold crossed on a bought Catalyst pays no surge, since nothing was overcome: the vessel is larger and sits empty until danger fills it.

XP requirements are not linear. The early Bands move swiftly because the Crystal is soft. The later Bands require cosmological-scale pressure because the Crystal at that point is nearly stone.

Part Three — Stat Points

Allocation Per Level

Every level grants a fixed allotment of Stat Points. Points are spent on Sub-Stats only. A Primary Stat is never purchased and holds no value of its own. It is the combined total of its eight Sub-Stats, recalculated the moment any one of them moves. Vitality is what Fortitude, Absorption, Constitution and the other five add up to. Dominion is what Radius, Sovereignty, Sense and the rest add up to. The Crystal does not record a category. It records where the pressure went, and the category is the arithmetic afterward.

This is the model every sheet is built on. A sheet that records a Primary as a figure bought in its own right, a second layer of spend laid over the Sub-Stats, is wrong by this page and is re-costed to this shape: points on the Sub-Stats only, the Primary as the total of its eight, the Grade read off the mean.

Life spends most of these points. A level’s allotment lands where the pressure went, by what the person actually does, and a farrier’s points do not wander into Feint because he would have liked them to. A few are the owner’s to place, at each Stage, on whatever Sub-Stats they choose. Both obey the concentration cap below, which applies per Sub-Stat per level-up.

Temperance Threshold events grant bonus Stat Points equal to the Stage number multiplied by 100, landing in Sub-Stats on the same terms: most where the life that reached the Threshold has been spending itself, and a few wherever the owner sets them. A Stage VI Threshold awards 600 points. A Stage XIII Threshold awards 1,300.

Stage I counts as a Threshold worth 100 points. Every practitioner starts with those 100 Threshold points, and the totals below count them: 1,000 from Thresholds I through IV, 9,100 through Stage XIII.

The Full Point Economy

Band I contributes 1,200 points. Band II, 1,500. Band III, 1,800. Band IV, 2,100. Band V, 2,400. Total from leveling at Level 500: 9,000 points. Threshold bonuses through Stage XIII total 9,100 points. Full lifetime pool through Stage XIII: approximately 18,100 points, spendable across sixty-three Sub-Stats, Throne excluded.

The allotment was cut by two fifths from its original figures when the Sub-Stat count was consolidated from a hundred and twenty to sixty-four. The old pool was sized against a phantom count of twenty stats and produced an overabundance the ceiling could not absorb. The cut restores scarcity without making versatility a luxury.

A sheet costed on the old figures, twenty, twenty-five and thirty a Level in Bands I through III, is re-costed to the table above: allocations trimmed to fit the current pool, Grade letters re-derived from the trimmed values, the Path gates of Part Seven applied in the same pass. The Threshold grants are unchanged and stay in the pool. A Temperance Stage ratified on such a sheet stands. Only the numbers move.

Breadth is affordable. Mastery is not. A character who spreads the lifetime pool evenly carries roughly 287 in every Sub-Stat, which is A-Grade across the board at Stage XIII against an X-Grade ceiling. That is the versatile build, and it is a legitimate one: competent everywhere, dominant nowhere, and its Primary reads A. The specialist pushes a handful of Sub-Stats toward the 1,200 ceiling and pays for it with entries left near the floor. Both are viable, which is the point of the cut.

Worked examples. A character finishing Band I at Level 100 and Stage IV holds 1,200 from leveling and 1,000 from Thresholds I through IV. Spread evenly that is 35 a Sub-Stat, E-Grade. Concentrated into fifteen entries it is 147, C-Grade, against a Stage IV ceiling of 275.

A character finishing Band II at Level 200 and Stage VII holds roughly 5,500 cumulative. Spread evenly, 87 a Sub-Stat, D-Grade. Concentrated into twenty entries, 275, B-Grade, against a Stage VII allocation cap of 400, with 401 to 475 reachable only under strain. A Band II practitioner reading B with an A ceiling above him is the correct shape, not an anomaly requiring justification.

Temperance Ceilings

The ceiling binds the Sub-Stat. No Sub-Stat may exceed the ceiling imposed by the character’s current Temperance Stage, regardless of how many points are available to spend. The Stage’s Max Grade letter binds allocation: a Sub-Stat may not be allocated past the top of that Grade’s bracket. Where the Stage’s numeric ceiling sits above the bracket, at Stages V, VII, IX and XI, the band between them is the instability zone, reachable only under strain, never by allocation. Stage V caps allocation at 275, B-Grade, and 276 to 350 is its instability zone. Stage XIII caps a Sub-Stat at 1,200. The pool generates the points. The Crystal determines whether those points can be held. Points earned and refused by the ceiling are not lost; they sit as pressure until a Threshold enlarges the vessel.

Throne is the single exception. It carries no ceiling and is excluded from the Dominion total. Dominion’s mean is therefore the total divided by seven, the seven counted Sub-Stats; Throne sits outside both the total and the mean.

Tier Grade is read off the mean. A Primary’s total divided by eight, or by seven for Dominion, gives the value measured against the Grade table, which is the number a character sheet has always carried on the Primary line. A practitioner whose eight Ardency Sub-Stats total 2,400 has a mean of 300 and is A-Grade in Ardency. The total is what the sheet records. The mean is what an assessor reads and what a Measurewright says aloud; a contested exchange is decided by the Grade of the Sub-Stat actually in use.

Because the ceiling sits on the Sub-Stat rather than the mean, the original rule holds and is restored: a character’s Sub-Stats and their Primary Grade sit in the same bracket. If they are B-Grade, their stats are broadly B-Grade. Spread shows what they are good at, and a specialist’s favorites may stand a bracket or more above the mean.

What the mean does to specialists. A Primary is dragged down by its neglected entries as hard as it is lifted by its favorites. A practitioner with three Dominion Sub-Stats at the ceiling and five near nothing reads as a modest Dominion on any assessor’s ledger while legislating local reality with the three. The institutions measure the mean. The fight is decided by the peak. That gap is not a defect in the accounting; it is the reason a register is worth lying to.

The Sheet in the Window

The owner sees all of this in letters. A normal look at the window shows the Eight Primaries with their Grades. A Sub-Stat appears when it is used or when it moves, and the full set remains there to study. Race and lineage show on the sheet and shape base stats and what comes easily, and skills sit beside them, rising with use and shown as Grades. Jory’s window, on the evening they carried him to the taverns, ran something like this:

[Level 12, up 9]
[Fortitude: F, rising]
[Threshold: F, rising]
[Skill: Gaff, D]
[Points to place: none until your next Stage]

The raw values above are what a sheet costs out and what a Measurewright reads. Nobody’s own window has ever shown them, and Jory could not have told you what a Sub-Stat was on the best day of his life, which this one was.
