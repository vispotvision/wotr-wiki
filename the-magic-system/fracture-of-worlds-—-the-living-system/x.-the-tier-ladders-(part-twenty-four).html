X. The Tier Ladders (Part Twenty-Four)

X. The Tier Ladders (Part Twenty-Four)

Nov 17, 202674 min read

The Wyvern of Anner Moss had killed forty carters and a magistrate’s mule before anyone troubled to read its row. The Reeve posted three hundred silver marks in hand-copied bills on every door in the hundred, the heralds cried it as far as the coast, and the Accord’s Commission (its licensed hunting contract, rated by danger) stamped the job T6 under a Gold Sigil, which the taverns of the hundred took as their cue to lay twenty to one against the Merry Gallows: fourteen hedge-hunters, one borrowed Sigil, and a cook named Bettris Two-Spoons, who had read nothing in her life but recipes and the Bestiary ladder. On that ladder a Wyvern throws a bolt the guild reckons at about a gigajoule, which is a fine way to lose a barn. It stands two rungs above that, on its reach and on the storm that feeds it, and the row says plainly that cold and water break a storm’s hold. So Bettris left the Moss alone. She drove a red brewer’s wagon full of screaming goats out onto the ice of Hob’s Tarn, four leagues from the crag where the thing kept its weather, with a stewpot strapped on her head, and she sat up there singing until it came.

The first bolt went violet through the wagon and took her left ear, four goats and a Gallows boy called Little Perkin. Then the crew on the far bank hauled on the ropes they had sawn through the ice all night, the floe tipped, and the whole Wyvern went shoulder-deep into black water with its sky unraveling above it, the violet running out of the clouds like dye out of a cheap shirt. It still killed five of them before the boar spears went in under the wing. The minstrels say she finished it with a ladle, and the ladle has its own song now and a shrine at Anner Moss that charges admission. The spear was hers, and the ladle was for afterward, and the whole plan cost a penny, which is what a copied row goes for at any Guild Hall door.

Every ladder in the Continuum (the whole fabric of reality) hangs on one spine: peak output in EU delivered (the EU spent times the yield, η, the share of a spend that reaches the world), read to a Grade (a letter rank of output, Hollow up to EX+), read to a rung from one to nine. The Essence Ledger sets the spine. Every joule, kilojoule, terajoule and ton of TNT on this page is the Measurewrights’ conversion of the same figure, at a megajoule to the EU, kept because the frontier has learned to talk in them and never the reading. This Part names the rungs for each kind of thing that can be measured on it (beasts, draughts, artifacts, summons, Domains (a space where a soul’s inner law becomes local reality), Wellspring (one of the sixty governing ideas that transform how a soul uses Aether, each with its own type of Aether and its own law) sites, weapons and armor) and says what each ladder adds that an output figure does not show. A rung is a fact anyone can look up. What it means in the room is for whoever is standing in it to work out.

The Bestiary ladder

A beast goes on the spine (the spine of the Essence Ledger) the way a practitioner does: by its peak output in EU delivered, its hardest blow, breath, dive or discharge. That figure finds the row, and the row is the rung. The Bestiary ladder gives the nine rungs the names the frontier already uses for them, and then adds the three things a beast has that an output figure does not show: how far its harm reaches, how hard it is to end, and what ground feeds it.

The rung says what the thing can break. The modifiers say how long you will be in the room with it, and whose room it is.

Monsters climb. They move and live across the whole Inner World, beasts and beast-like animals more widely still, and the Scourge of Hell is only the one area known for extremely dangerous ones. A monster gains Levels by killing and changes form at its own thresholds, and it stands on the same Level and Grade scale as a person. A strong enough Gnosis reads it like anyone else, and a weak reader gets question marks in the window, [Wyvern · Level ??? · Grade ?], and is well advised to take the hint. Commissions grade monsters T1 to T9, and a famous one earns a name, an epithet and a bounty, at about which point the odds boards take an interest.

The core and the parts. A monster’s core is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster. It is the lawful Crystal Draw of The Standing Index: an alchemist renders it into Essence Drafts used for tempering, with Creature-Sourced potency and the risk of Identity Contamination (the source’s nature coming through in the drinker). Swallowed raw it gives everything at once, and most who try die or come out wrong. Monster parts make gear. A stone and its parts are read from the beast’s own Grade and Level on the coil; T stays the shorthand for danger and price, and most beasts line up with it. A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner makes the strongest Draft of all, the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable, and it is why Grade IV of the Index has never balanced. Some of what is sold as monster core came out of people.

Any rung kills a man. A crossbow bolt carries a few hundred joules. The ladder does not measure whether a creature can kill you. It measures what it can break when you are not the only thing in front of it.

Hearth-pest. Below 0.015 EU delivered, fifteen kilojoules to the guild. Its hardest blow is a man’s or a dog’s. A door holds against it, a householder with a spade answers it, and it is dangerous by being where somebody is rather than by what it does when it arrives. Vermin, the single walking corpse, the trickster in the hedge.

Field-bane. Up to 20.9 EU delivered, twenty-one megajoules to the guild, Wall level at the ceiling. It kills stock, breaks a fence, stoves in a door, puts a horse and rider down in one exchange. The great natural predators live here: the Greater Wolf’s run, the lion’s charge, the griffon’s dive, a grown Droval’s pounce across thirty meters. A Field-bane costs a fraction of an EU at the floor and twenty at the top.

Road-bane. Up to 1,046 EU delivered, a gigajoule to the guild. It closes a road: overturns a wagon, breaks a gate, capsizes a boat, brings down a bridge span or a small house. This is the Cyclops’s wall-breaking blow, the Goliath’s stomp, a hellhound’s breath across a camp. Its hardest working costs between 21 and 1,046 EU.

Hamlet-bane. Up to 46,024 EU delivered, forty-six gigajoules to the guild, Building to Large Building. A lightning strike lands here, and so does the petrifying gaze, because remaking a body into stone asks the whole of it to become something else, which the guild reckons on the order of a gigajoule for a grown man. What stands here can take down the buildings of a hamlet one at a time.

Town-bane. Up to 4.184 million EU delivered, four terajoules to the guild, City Block to Multi-City Block. The wyrm’s breath, the phoenix’s burst, the gorgon’s gaze swept across a battalion, the hydra’s heads all breathing at once. What stands here can empty a town in an afternoon if it is let.

March-bane. Up to 418 million EU delivered, four hundred terajoules and one to a hundred kilotons to the guild: Town to Large Town. A coast-shattering surge, a trench throwing up a sea, a Titan’s pulse flash-freezing a battlefield. This is the highest rung any creature in the register reaches by its own output. The Leviathan’s surge and the Kraken’s upheaval both sit here.

Realm-bane. Large Town to Small Country. No creature in the register stands here by output. Every creature that stands here stands on a Town-bane or March-bane output and the modifiers below.

World-bane. Country to Large Planet. No creature in the register stands here by output. The Voidwyrm, the Cerberus and the high Titan Droval stand here by standing, and the difference between those two words is the whole of this ladder.

Sky-eater. Brown Dwarf and beyond. No creature in the register reaches it by output. Those that stand here stand on a March-bane’s output with all three modifiers held, and the name is the frontier’s, not the Ledger’s.

What this ladder adds

The spine gives every ladder its own modifiers. The Bestiary’s are three, and each lifts the rung by one. A creature’s standing is its output rung plus the modifiers it holds: never more than three above its output, never past nine.

Reach. Its harm lands beyond the ground a party can hold. Lightning at three kilometers, a song across open water, a hunt that runs across realms on an Essence signature, harm that outlasts the creature’s presence: a storm curse on a valley, ground frozen meters deep after the Titan has gone, currents altered for a season. A creature with reach cannot be answered by standing still and holding a line, because the line is inside its range.

Persistence. It is hard to kill. It regenerates, returns, stores its life somewhere other than its body, or keeps fighting through wounds that end a body: the hydra’s heads, the phoenix’s ashes, the lich’s phylactery, the Goliath’s stoneform. Hard to hit does not lift the rung. A thing that phases, teleports, flies or vanishes into snow lengthens the fight and does not raise its ceiling; the first blow that lands on it lands on its rung. The Ledger prices what lands, not what misses.

The tie. It is fed by a Wellspring, a vein, a Crevice or a plane boundary: stronger at the site, weaker off it, and in the worst cases a living anchor of the site itself. The tie is the only modifier that can be taken away in the middle of a fight. Draw the creature off its ground, or deny the ground, and the rung the tie gave it is gone.

Workings that do not strike. A gaze, a song, a drain or a curse is read by the work it does to what it lands on. A body turned to stone is priced at remaking that body’s matter. A song that walks sailors onto a reef is priced at the tide that wrecks them. A death is not a quantity of work. A sting that kills, or a severed Essence thread, stands on the blow that carried it, and the difference between that blow and the body count is the Commission’s to price, not the Ledger’s.

Numbers are not a modifier. The Ledger reads the animal. A pack, a herd, a horde or a coven is a party’s problem and a Commission’s figure; it is never a rung.

The Bestiary’s own observation that almost everything above T6 is a Wellspring event rather than an animal is what the ladder shows in figures. Nothing in the register reaches Realm-bane by output, and every creature the Ledger stands at seven or above holds the tie. The Lich holds it through the person it was.

A Commission Tier is not a Grade. A Threat Tier names the title a Commission requires, because an adventurer may take commissions up to the Threat Tier of their title: T4 goes to Adepts and above, T7 goes to Grandmasters under a Gold Sigil and nobody engages lawfully without one. A Grade is a band of EU delivered. On a fighting guild’s table, a Tier of Standing is a set of Grades. So the Commission’s T is a figure for the answer, and the Ledger’s rung is a figure for the thing, and the Ledger’s standing, output plus modifiers, is the Ledger’s figure for the same question the Commission asks. Where the Commission weighs the same three things the Ledger weighs, the two agree, and across most of the register they do.

The Leviathan and the Kraken show why a T cannot simply be a Grade. Both are T9, and neither eats worlds. The Leviathan’s hardest working is a surge that shatters a coast, on the order of 10⁸ EU delivered (10¹⁴ joules to the guild), March-bane. What carries it three rungs higher is that it reaches (a maelstrom that pulls fleets under and currents altered for a season), it persists (hundreds of meters of abyss-black plate and a gaze that severs the soul’s tether before the body is touched), and it is tied (a living anchor of Abython, the Abyssal Pulse, on a plane boundary). The Kraken is the same arithmetic on a smaller surge. A T9 beast is a March-bane holding all three. An EX+ Grade would be something else entirely, and the register has none.

T1 is empty because nobody files it. A Hearth-pest is a householder’s matter. The rat in the grain store and the fox at the hens are T1 on the Commission’s scale and no Guild Hall has ever opened a Commission for one, so the register asks its eleven questions of nothing there. The rung exists; the paperwork does not.

T2 and T3 share the word Moderate because a crowd changes the word. T2 is Minor for one and Moderate for many: one walking corpse is a Hearth-pest with a grip, a battlefield of them is a Moderate Threat, and the Commission issues the same tier for both because it sends the same rank to either. T3 is Moderate by itself. The Ledger reads the body in both cases and lets the Commission read the crowd.

Where the two differ, both stand. The Ledger never overrules a Commission; it enters its reading beside it. In the register as it stands the Ledger never reads above the Commission. Where it reads below, the Commission is pricing something the ladder does not: numbers, a rider, what a thing does to a place rather than a body, what it does loose, or a death without output. Those gaps are where a hunter can choose the fight the Ledger describes instead of the one the Commission does.

The Lich is read as a person first. Its output is the Grade the necromancer held, placed on the spine like any practitioner’s, and the three modifiers stand on top of it. The Commission enters every lich at T9. The Ledger enters a lich at nine only when the necromancer stood at Master before the phylactery; a lesser one who made the same bargain stands lower, and the Commission’s tier is the tier of the worst of them.

The Elemental is the one entry with no fixed rung, because it has no fixed size. It always holds the tie, since an Elemental is concentrated Wellspring energy and collapses when the site is spent. The small ones stand one rung above their output; the ones that reshape a landscape reach and stand two.

Summoned things are read loose, as the Commission reads them. The leash is a fact about the summoner. The Ledger’s rung is a fact about the beast.

What the rung tells a hunter

The output rung is what you must be able to survive: armor, ward and Grade are chosen against it, and one Grade above a creature’s output wins the exchange the way it wins any exchange. The modifiers tell you where and how to fight. Persistence means bring the thing that ends it (fire for the hydra’s stumps, the phylactery before the lich, the ashes before the phoenix rises), and a creature that is only hard to hit needs patience, noise and mirrors, not a higher rank. Reach means close the distance or leave its ground, and Raga is the one entry where closing is the mistake. The tie is the best counter in the register: draw the thing off its Wellspring, deny it the site, or fight it where the vein is thin, and every rung the tie gave it is gone. And where the Commission’s tier sits above the Ledger’s standing, the Commission is pricing a pack, a rider, a place or a death. Break the pack, unseat the rider, change the ground, or refuse the death, and you are fighting the creature the Ledger describes.

The Drafts ladder

Alchemical works and Drafts, on the spine. Every Draft, salt, oil, powder and charge the trade produces is a working stabilized into a substance, and a substance holds a definite quantity of Essence. The ladder reads that quantity. A work is placed by the EU one dose holds, read on the spine like any other peak output, with the guild’s joules beside it at a megajoule to the unit. Two modifiers, Carry and Fidelity, then move it.

What is read is what the dose holds, not what arrives. The spine is read on the held figure at the constant, before anybody’s yield (η: the share of spent Essence that arrives as effect) touches it. What reaches the drinker is the held figure times the drinker’s own yield, and it is spent where the formula sends it: on the body, on the Shell (the soul’s outer layer, which passes power out into the world), or on the pressure a Crystal builds toward its next Threshold (the breakthrough that opens the next Stage), or, in a work made for it, on the Catalyst itself. Only a fight Draft refills a reserve. Otherwise a practitioner is refilled by rest, the ground and the Wellsprings, and an ordinary Draft drunk on an empty reserve mends the body and steadies the Shell and leaves the reserve as empty as it found it. A fight Draft, poured for the purpose, gives the reserve back at once and takes its price in the crash after.

Tincture. A work measured in thousandths of a unit. It cleans a cut, holds a fall, lights a room, and needs no circle and no understanding. The whole first rung together costs less than the smallest reserve carries in its lowest percent, which is why a household can own a shelf of it and never think of it as alchemy.

Tonic. A working day’s steadiness in a bottle, a lamp for a season, a thunderclap in a flask. A careless hand can ruin a Tonic without dying of it. This is the rung of the ration and the field kit.

Draught. The trade standard. A Draught holds enough to matter against a Welling reserve and to carry a Welling practitioner toward the Ascension floor across a course of doses. The bulk of what the four quarters drink for their Crystal is here.

Philter. A dose that holds a Journeyman’s whole working range in one vessel. This is the rung at which a Draft can carry a practitioner to a quantitative floor in one issue, and the rung at which it is sold against a term of service rather than a price.

Elixir. Threshold work in earnest. An Elixir holds as much as an Expert’s reserve spends in a long engagement and supports a Crystal through a reorganization. An Elixir can supply the floor and it can supply a seed, and a work made for the purpose, here or on a higher rung, can supply the Catalyst (the event that sets off a Stage breakthrough) as well. The Stage has then simply been bought, the door is open and no more, and the great houses hoard the top of this ladder for exactly that reason. The low Stages are bought smaller. Below Splintering a Threshold holds little, so a work made to supply its Catalyst stands on a Tonic, a Draught or a Philter, dear for its recipe rather than for what it holds, and a house child drinks through them on an allowance. A purse can buy the Catalyst for any Stage; at Zenith a Hollow Throne must still recognize the buyer, so money buys the attempt and not the seat.

Quintessence. A dose at the scale of a Master’s Domain. Works on this rung anchor, stabilize and hold at the size of a live, law-bearing field, and the question of what the dose was rendered from stops being hypothetical.

Magisterium. A work that enforces. It holds energy on the scale of a regional event, and what it does to a blade, a wound or a Domain behaves as a ruling rather than a reaction.

Panacea. Realm-scale. What a Panacea is laid into registers in the Continuum as terrain. Works on this rung are used a handful of times in the record, and what they are laid into outlasts whoever laid it.

Stone. The rung with no ceiling. It holds what a Paragon’s working holds, and the Stone is the rung the trade keeps open because the ladder required a top, not because the trade has filled it.

Master Works. The trade has its own: a Draft that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named. A Fate Essence Draft is one, bottling the manipulation of possibility itself, extremely hard to make and very coveted. Its rung is whatever its energy says, and that is the least interesting thing about it.

The Index and the ladder

The Standing Index and the ladder are two readings of the same lot, and neither corrects the other. The Index ranks an entered lot from T1 to T9 by provenance and standing: its Provenance Grade, its rank gate, who may lawfully hold it and what the Bench is prepared to certify about it. The ladder ranks the same lot by the energy one dose holds. The Index prices provenance and standing. The ladder prices energy held.

The two agree most often through the middle of the trade, where a Threshold ration’s rank gate and its dose climb together. They part wherever the Index is pricing law, provenance or danger rather than what a dose holds. A lot can hold almost nothing and be reserved to Masters, because what it does is dangerous out of all proportion to what it holds; and a lot can hold a Domain’s worth and be entered one rank lower than its rung, because its Carry lifts it on the ladder and not in the ledger. A buyer reads the Index to learn who may hold a thing. A drinker, and anyone facing one, reads the rung to learn what it will do.

Counterplay

The rung says how much a dosed opponent took in, and none of it is reserve. A Philter or an Elixir drunk before a fight has gone into the body and the Shell, and the opponent’s reserve stands where it stood, spending at the same rate toward the same ten-percent floor. The answer is findable in the arithmetic, and it is the answer it was before the vial: make them spend it. A Draft supplies energy and it can supply a seed, so a practitioner on an ordinary dose is mended and steadied, holds no more to spend than they did, and is not a Stage higher than they were. One made to supply a Catalyst does leave them a Stage higher, and a Stage bought that week has no Release behind it, since a Release is found and mastered by fighting. Where the Carry is Heavy, the origin arrived with the energy, and the source’s own weaknesses, appetites and habits of mind are in the drinker now and can be found there. Where the Fidelity is C or worse, the dose did not do the same thing twice, and the part that went into the adjacent process is a tell.

The Artifacts ladder

Artifacts, on the spine. An artifact is a working given a body that outlasts the working. Some hold a store of their own, charge laid down against a later need. Most hold nothing and pass what their bearer spends through a glyph (a sigil written into a working) chain that shapes it. The ladder reads both the same way. An artifact is placed by the EU it stores or the EU one act of it discharges, whichever is greater, read on the spine like any other peak output, with the guild’s joules beside it at a megajoule to the unit. One modifier, how bound the thing is to its bearer, then decides whose rung it is.

Stored and discharged are read differently. A store is read as it stands: what the thing holds, in EU. A discharge is read as delivered, the EU spent times the yield of whoever spends it; where a record gives only the drain on the bearer, the drain is read at the constant and the rung is a ceiling. A drain given as a share of the bearer’s reserve is as large as the reserve makes it. The share decides how long the bearer lasts; the absolute size of the reserve decides how large each act is, and an artifact that asks a Master for five percent of a Master’s reserve is discharging on a Master’s scale.

Counterplay

The rung says the most one act of the thing can deliver, and the binding says where the answer lives. A Free artifact’s rung goes wherever the object goes, so taking it, burying it or starving what it feeds on removes the rung whole. A Keyed artifact stands one rung lower the moment its key is contested: challenge the Writ, break the vow’s terms, deny the harmonization, and the thing works in its crudest form or not at all. A Soulbound artifact can never outrun its bearer’s Tier, and stolen it is inert, so there is nothing to gain by taking it and everything to gain by making the bearer pay; every act crosses the bearer’s Shell and is spent out of the bearer’s reserve toward the bearer’s floor. A Grown-in artifact cannot be separated from its bearer at all, and the fight is with the ground or the conditions it depends on. A store empties. An artifact placed by what it holds steps down the ladder with every discharge, and a patient opponent can count the rings.

The Summons ladder

Summons, on the spine. A called thing is a working that stands up and walks. Whatever it does once it is standing, the one figure every entry in the register carries is what it cost to call, and that figure is the ladder’s. A summon is placed by its call cost read through its yield: the EU spent to call it, times the yield of the crossing. The EU delivered finds the row, and the row is the rung; the guild multiplies by a megajoule and writes joules.

Whose yield. The crossing is the summoner’s Shell, so the summoner’s own yield is read wherever it is recorded. Where it is not, the crossing is read at the summon’s own Aether Class, because Aether Class governs how much of the donated Essence survives the crossing into form: Class I loses thirty to forty percent (η 0.60–0.70), Class II fifteen to twenty-five (η 0.75–0.85), Class III five to ten (η 0.90–0.95), and Class V delivers one hundred percent or more (η 1.00 and above). A call cost given as a share of reserve is as large as the summoner’s reserve makes it, and where the reserve is not recorded it is set by the law of reserves at the summoner’s Level.

The rung says what was paid to put the thing on the field. It does not say what the thing will do once it is there, and it does not say what it costs to keep it there. The modifiers say those.

The Domains ladder

Domains, on the spine. A Domain is inner law made local reality, and it is held, not thrown. What the spine reads is what the Domain holds: the EU standing in its territory at one time. Two figures fix that, and the ladder reads them together: the radius the law is held over, and the EU held across it. The EU held finds the row and the row is the rung, and the guild writes it in joules. The radius says how far it is spread, and the two multiplied back together are the size and density that Part Twenty-Two makes the measure of a Domain’s collapse.

How what is held is read. A Domain is paid for as a rate, and the Ledger turns a rate into a quantity over its own unit of time, the six-second turn. What is held is the maintenance drain across one turn, delivered through the holder’s yield: drain in EU/s × 6 × η, and the guild’s joules held are that times a megajoule. A Domain expression paid per activation rather than per second is read at its activation cost through the same yield. A Domain spent whole, detonated or collapsed on purpose, is read at what the spending cost, and stands on its own row beside the held one.

Density is the tell. The Measurewrights divide the joules held by the area of the disc the radius draws, π r², for the charge on each square meter of held ground, in J/m². It does not move the rung. It is what an opponent standing at the edge can estimate, and it is the figure a collapse is felt in.

The Wellspring and vein sites ladder

Sites, on the spine. A Wellspring is an alchemical and a spiritual transformation, the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether, and its own type of Aether pools: it concentrates where the substrate conducts, where no competing Aether crowds it, and where time has let it saturate the field until the ground expresses the law on its own. A site is that pool, and a vein is the channel that feeds it. Common speech calls any such site a Core. Cores are mostly found in dungeons, which grow down in floors around them with something guarding the heart, and they are said to lie in vast territories too. A worked Core is a dungeon someone cleared and now holds: it recharges and has keepers, who can close it. The survey keeps the words Wellspring Core for the seventh rung alone and has stopped correcting people at the door. A site is placed by hazard: the output a practitioner must be able to command to keep their own law inside the site at its worst ground. That figure is read in EU delivered like every other peak, the row it lands in is the rung, and the rung says how strong you must be to stand there and remain yourself.

Why hazard is read this way. A site does not strike. It retunes. Harmonization is a soul taking a Wellspring’s idea as its own, and Aether holding an idea strongly enough to press it on a Shell against its bearer’s will is Aether the bearer’s own output has failed to hold off. The survey calls it phase-locking, which is the Measurewrights’ word for it. So the ladder asks the question the survey asks, from the other side: what Grade of output holds a Crystal’s own architecture against this Aether. The density scale answers it in words, and the spine answers it in EU delivered. The two meet at one fixed point: Domain law at SSS can override the local behavior of a Wellspring, and a Wellspring Core is the ground where the Wellspring’s law is the only law operating. A Core therefore stands on SSS, the seventh rung, and the density scale is laid down the spine from there.

The Weapons and armor ladder

Weapons and armor, on the spine. Plain arms in the four quarters are Bearing-grade material with no Essence in them, and they perform as their physics; cheap runed pieces are nearly as common, and the great pieces are old and rare. The ladder reads the ones that carry a working, and it reads them the way the spine reads everything: in EU delivered, with the guild’s joules beside it. A weapon is placed by the hit it delivers: the attack output of the working in it, at the Grade of whoever put the Essence there. A piece of armor, a ward or a shield is placed by what it stops: the attack output band of the strongest strike it holds against. The EU delivered finds the row, and the row is the rung.

Whose Grade. An enhanced weapon performs no higher than the Grade of whoever put the Essence into it. Channeled through the weapon in the moment, that is the wielder’s Grade; enchanted, glyphed or worked beforehand, it is the maker’s Grade at the time of making, and a relic keeps its maker’s ceiling in any hand. A weapon’s rung travels with its maker’s work and not with its current owner, except where the working is channeled, and then it travels with the hand.

Beside the Bearing and the Holding

The ladder does not replace the grades the trade already stamps. It reads a different quantity from the same object, and the two readings are held side by side.

The Bearing and Holding grades say what the material is. Bearing is its physical work: what it stops, carries and survives as steel, stone or hide. Holding is its Essence work: what working it can carry and for how long. Beside its Grade and Rarity, a material’s virtue decides which workings it holds well and how it fails: stone remembers, salt fixes, gold will not corrupt, quicksilver will not keep a shape. Every material has both Bearing and Holding, they run independently, and the material’s tier, T0 to T9, is where the Master Material Ledger enters it.

The item Tier says what the object may hold, and the rung says what it does. An item Tier is the lower of what its material can hold and the Tier of Standing its maker stood at, and material tier n holds a working up to item Tier n, level with Tier of Standing n. So the material tier is the rung’s ceiling for any standing working: a weapon or armor placed above its material’s tier is a working the material cannot carry, and the material fails first. A plain item with no working in it has no Tier and no rung; its Bearing is the whole of it.

Each has a rung, and the rung waits on its Grade.

Counterplay

The rung tells an opponent what the piece can do and, just as plainly, what it cannot. A weapon’s rung is its maker’s ceiling or its wielder’s, and the piece says which: a channeled blade is answered by the hand, a made one by its proof, and a relic in a stranger’s grip still hits at its maker’s Grade. Armor tells its own limit on its mark: one rung above the proof breaks it, and two go through it as if it were not there. Every enhancement radiates its working’s signature, and a Harmonist reads the rung without touching the piece. The seam, the joint and the material’s failure are where the answer lives; which one is the fight’s business.
