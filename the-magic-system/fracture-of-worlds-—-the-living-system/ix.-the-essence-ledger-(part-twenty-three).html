IX. The Essence Ledger (Part Twenty-Three)

IX. The Essence Ledger (Part Twenty-Three)

Nov 17, 202640 min read

The fire at Gull Strand took nine seconds, and a clerk had it on her slate before it started. Wu Jinbao of the Vermilion Lamp Hall, Stage III, twenty-two years old, in a gold sash and with teeth like a lord’s plate, stood up on a beached fishing smack at the Strand brackets and announced to the whole beach that he would hold his full output for one whole minute and let any comer try him while he did it. The heralds cried it, the odds went up on a board, and Marget Pinch, who kept tallies for the Strand’s net-menders and had never thrown a punch, asked the Measurewright (an assayer of souls and workings) in the judges’ box for two figures off Wu’s assay sheet, forty-two thousand and four thousand two hundred. She did a short sum and put her sister’s dowry on nine seconds.

He opened on the noon bell.

His aura went up scarlet and gold, a column of it, the prettiest thing anyone on that strand had ever seen, and three parts in five of what he spent came off him as raw heat. Every stick of timber within a hundred and twenty-seven meters took light together: booths, bunting, the judges’ box, the bookies’ board with the odds still chalked on it. People on the sea wall three hundred meters off were slapping at their own faces. Seventeen died of it, and for the rest of their lives some hundreds carried marks across the nose and cheeks that the Strand still calls Wu’s freckles. At nine seconds by the water clock the column stuttered and his color went thin as weak tea, and a net-mender called Big Ned, who had been standing in the shallows with an oar, waded up the burning beach and knocked him off the boat. The Hall paid for the fair, and Wu paid the Hall for thirty years. Marget Pinch bought four boats, and when anyone asked how she had known she told them she could divide.

Her sum is the first line of this Part. A reserve divided by a rate is the time to empty, nine tenths of that is the time to Starvation (the floor at a tenth of the reserve, where the Traits begin to flicker), and the heat is the Shell’s furnace burning everything the yield (η, the share of a spend that arrives as effect, as a distillation has its yield) lets go of, when the spend goes out through the Shell as Wu’s did. Every one of those figures can be had from a sheet, and any one of them can be used by whoever is standing across from the person it describes. Wu’s own window, for what it was worth, had shown him a bar and a percentage the whole nine seconds, [Reserve: 10%] at the last, because a window leaves EU, AU/s and yield to the assay sheet. Marget Pinch had the better numbers.

Four quantities run through every practitioner in the Continuum (the whole fabric of reality): a reserve in EU, a compression in EU/g, an output rate in AU/s, a yield η. The Grade ladder reads destruction in EU delivered, and the Measurewrights keep a second set of books in joules. This Part is the join. The guild reckons one Essence Unit at one megajoule, and that conversion is theirs, never the reading. What follows is what that buys on the ladder, what a reserve costs to spend, how long it lasts at full output, how fast it comes back, and what can be done about all of it from the other side of a fight.

The four quantities, and the fifth

EU (Essence Units) is the raw reserve: the total volume of usable power the Crystal (the Soul Crystal, which is the soul itself) holds at any moment. It is set by Level and capped by Temperance Stage (one of sixteen rungs of the soul’s refinement), and governed primarily by Tempering Yield. Maximum EU is the structural ceiling the Crystal refills to.

What an EU counts. The unit was named when the books held that every soul spent Essence. It now counts either of two things, at the same rate. On the Body Path it counts Charge: raw Aether packed in the Shell, never refined, shaped there by the lens and driven out through the Shell. On the Spirit Path it counts Essence: Aether refined in the Crystal and stamped with identity, the soul’s own substance, spent through the Core. A soul’s one figure is held mostly as the one or the other by its lead Path, a soul on both roads carries the split on its card (Charge 70% · Essence 30%), and the two never trade back. So a Spirit worker’s reserve is a quantity of the person, and for her this Part is the book kept on somebody spending themselves. A Body fighter’s reserve is a quantity of the world, held, and for him it is the book kept on what the handling costs a body. A Fate worker’s pull is priced in EU through the Thread rung, though the Thread itself is never held. The arithmetic does not care which, and none of it has changed. A Measurewright will tell you the U ought to stand for something else now, and will go on writing EU. Tier Grade, Bands and the Aether Shell sets out the three routes.

Operating below ten percent EU produces Essence Starvation: reduced stat expression, Shell (the soul’s outer layer, which passes power out into the world) degradation, Trait (a belief hardened into a personal law of the soul) flickering.

Flux Density (EU/g) is the compression ratio, how much power is packed per unit of Crystal mass. Higher means greater output from the same reserve, and it is the same number on every Path. It is governed by Tempering Clarity and Ardency Compression, and it is what separates a practitioner who burns through their reserve in three techniques from one who fights for an hour on the same pool.

AU/s (Aether Units per second) is the output rate, how much of the reserve is spent into active expression per second at full output. It is governed by Ardency Flux, Ardency Alacrity and Tempering Yield, and it follows the formula AU/s = Flux Density × η.

Yield (η) is the share of a spend that arrives as intended effect; the Measurewrights’ older word for it is efficiency. At η 0.50 through the Shell, half of every expenditure burns in the Shell’s furnace as heat, with noise and structural bleed; the same share lost through the Core is paid in the Core as a hollowing and warms nothing, and through the Attraction Layer it stands as a knot. At η 0.90, ninety percent converts to clean output. It is the single most important variable in sustained combat, because for the same delivered effect it determines how long the reserve lasts. It is governed by the class ladder of the layer in use (Aether, Essence or Thread Class), Tempering Clarity and Ardency Compression.

The fifth quantity is the one Part Eleven already measures, and it is the one that lets the other four be priced at all. Strike Force is the EU a single attack delivers, which the Measurewrights record besides in newtons for peak contact force and in joules for the whole of it. The Continuum keeps its books in what arrives; the guild keeps theirs in joules. The Ledger is not a new currency; it is the exchange rate between the two sets of books.

The direction of the conversion. EU is potential and AU is actual: what the Crystal holds against what leaves it. One AU is one EU leaving the reserve, across the Shell for a Body spend and through the Core for a Spirit one. The two are never the same number in practice, because one is the store, one is the flow, and the yield says how much of it crosses the boundary between them.

The Aristotelian reading, in-world. The Collegium tradition that descends from the old faculty of Causes reads the Ledger as the doctrine of potency and act: EU is what a Crystal is able to become, AU is what it has become, the yield is the fraction of the becoming that arrives. Their term for Essence Starvation is the exhausted potency, and their objection to the whole Aether-Flow apparatus is that it measures the act and infers the potency, which they hold is backwards. They are a school, not a mechanic. The numbers below are what happens; the Causes faculty is one of several arguments about what the numbers mean, carried the way the Four Theories are carried.

The constant: what one EU is worth

The constant

EU delivered = EU spent × η. The guild’s conversion: 1 EU = 1 MJ of potential, so 1 AU/s = 1 MW.

A Grade reads off EU delivered, at the old thresholds the guild writes in joules divided by a million, so their joules and the Grade always agree. The Ledger converts at one megajoule everywhere. Every joule, watt, ton of TNT and megajoule in this Part is the guild’s figure at that constant, and there is no second rate for any register, Path or Stage.

How to use it. Take the thing’s peak output in EU delivered, or in the guild’s joules, which land on the same row: a beast’s hardest blow, what a draught releases, an artifact’s discharge, a summon’s call cost read through its yield, what a Domain holds. Find the row. That row’s number is the thing’s tier on every ladder. Each ladder then adds its own modifiers: a beast that is hard to kill rather than hard to be hit by, a draught that works slowly, an artifact that only fires once.

The lowest rungs are bought in fractions of a unit. An Initiate’s whole ceiling, the top of Hollow, F and E together, 15 kJ to the guild, costs 0.015 EU delivered; the smallest reserve a practitioner carries pays for more than a hundred thousand of them. The Ledger counts reserves in whole EU and the bottom of the Grade ladder in thousandths of one, and both are correct.

The spine runs without a break. The Part Four bands meet edge to edge: S ends at 2.42672 × 10⁷ EU delivered, which the guild writes as 2.42672 × 10¹³ J and 5.8 kilotons, and SS begins there, so every peak output has exactly one Grade. Both S and SS sit in Master.

Reserve bands by Stage and by Tier of Standing

The law of reserves

A reserve is set by Level and capped by Stage. Level sets it along a log-linear law; the Band (a span of a hundred Levels) gates, which a Stage must pass before its Level can climb, cap it.

The law runs from waking. A sealed Crystal holds no Essence, so a commoner’s Level sits on the window and buys no reserve at all, and the day the Crystal opens the law takes up at whatever Level the life has reached. A bought Stage lifts the cap and leaves the reserve where Level put it: an heir who drinks through to Stage VII at Level 20 holds about 4,750 EU, an eighth of a Stage III brawler’s 37,400 at Level 90, with the ceiling and the color and nothing underneath.

The law runs through three fixed reference points: a Stage VIII Master at Level 250 with 4,200,000 EU, a Stage XII Grandmaster at Level 378 with 180,000,000 EU, and a Stage XII Grandmaster at Level 430 with 850,000,000 EU. Between the first two the slope is 0.012750 decades per Level; between the second two it is 0.012964. The two segments agree to 1.7 percent, and across the whole span:

log₁₀ EU = 3.4202 + 0.012812 × Level. A reserve multiplies by ten every 78.1 Levels, and by about 19 across each hundred-Level Band.

A Stage X Realization practitioner at Level 355, Band IV, holds 90,000,000 EU, within four percent of the 93,000,000 this law gives at that Level.

The Pythagorean reading, in-world. The Measurewrights’ (the assayers who measure souls and workings) old Harmonic faculty never accepted the Level axis and reads the same regularity as a ratio: each Band multiplies the reserve by a fixed interval, so the five Bands are five octaves of one string and the Crystal is a monochord being stopped shorter. They tune their assay forks to intervals rather than to numbers and they are, in practice, no worse at it. A school, not a mechanic. The fork reads what the arithmetic above says it reads, and the Harmonic faculty’s error is in what they think it proves.

The bands

Two things bind a Stage’s reserve, and they are different in kind.

The gate ceiling, which is hard. Part One sets four gates and states them as absolutes: no character can breach a Band ceiling without first passing the Temperance Threshold (the breakthrough that opens the next Stage) that cluster demands, and the System halts the level counter and builds Residual Strain (pressure that keeps arriving after the counter halts) until the Threshold is passed or the character fractures. Stage IV must be reached before Level 100 can be surpassed, Stage VII before Level 200, Stage X before Level 300, Stage XII before Level 400. Read the other way round: a Stage III practitioner cannot be above Level 100, a Stage VI cannot be above 200, a Stage IX cannot be above 300, a Stage XI cannot be above 400. Through the law of reserves that is a hard ceiling on the reserve.

The cluster band, which is typical. Each Band has its Temperance cluster: Band I (Levels 1–100) is Stages I–IV, Band II (101–200) Stages V–VII, Band III (201–300) VIII–X, Band IV (301–400) XI–XII, Band V (401–500) XIII–XIV. That is where a Stage’s practitioners usually sit, not where they must, and it gives the working range.

AU/s as power: how long the output holds

The drain

AU/s is a rate: how much of the reserve is spent into active expression per second at full output, and the variable that determines how long the reserve lasts when it is held there. One AU is one EU leaving the reserve, so a second at full AU/s costs exactly AU/s worth of EU.

One combat turn is six seconds. Divide either clock by six and it reads in turns: a practitioner whose reserve holds twenty-one seconds at full output has three and a half turns of it.

The drain times are a property of the two figures a practitioner carries, reserve and AU/s, and of one AU being one EU. The power and the lost share are the guild’s figures, at their conversion.

Yield counts twice, on purpose. AU/s = Flux Density × η says how much the layer in use can move (the Shell, in the example that follows), and AU/s × η says what survives the trip, so delivered power is Flux Density × η². Take the small practitioner below, 3,100 EU at a Flux Density of 290 EU/g. At η 0.89 the Shell moves 258.1 AU/s and delivers 229.7 MW; at η 0.50 the same Crystal would move 145 AU/s and deliver 72.5 MW. At full output the cleaner Shell empties the reserve faster, 12.0 seconds against 21.4. For the same delivered effect it lasts longer: 72.5 MW at η 0.89 costs 81.5 AU/s and holds for 38.1 seconds, against the 21.4 at η 0.50.

What AU/s is not

AU/s is a ceiling on the rate, not a duty cycle. No practitioner runs at their AU/s continuously, and sustained work runs at a fraction of it. The small practitioner’s broadcast suppression costs 390 EU, is a full-broadcast sympathetic suppression across twenty-five feet, and lasts eight seconds: 390 ÷ 8 = 48.75 AU/s, about a fifth of the ceiling. The ceiling is what the practitioner can do for a heartbeat; the fifth is what they do for eight seconds. Read a practitioner’s AU/s as the number that caps a burst, and read a working’s EU ÷ duration as the number that runs a scene.

Costs are a share of the reserve

A working is priced as a share of the reserve it spends from, and the shares are large enough that a real fight reaches the ten-percent floor. The small practitioner’s workings run 4.5 to 12.6 percent of the reserve apiece. The middle practitioner’s branches run 3.0 to 9.7 percent, and the apex strike 8.0. The large practitioner’s constructs run 5 to 15 percent. A real pull of Fate is priced the same way, through the Thread rung, and it is the worst of them: low down one lean on the odds can take a large share of the reserve and still fail, and landed or failed it leaves a knot on top of the share, a debt no reserve pays and the world collects later. A practitioner who commits to a fight spends in whole percents per exchange, and ten to twenty committed exchanges bring anyone to Starvation. The absolute size of the reserve decides how large each working is; the share decides how long the practitioner lasts, and the share is what a scene tracks.

The cost can be permanent. A working may forfeit a permanent portion of the practitioner’s own maximum reserve each time it is performed. Performed seven times, it takes seven portions that do not grow back, and the maximum reserve was higher before the first.

Flux Density and yield

The equation, read per working gram

AU/s = Flux Density × η. The equation governs: a practitioner’s output rate is their Flux Density multiplied by their yield.

AU/s is Essence per second and Flux Density is Essence per gram, so the equation is read per working gram per second: the gram of Crystal engaged at the working aperture in each second of output. The gram goes unwritten because it is always one. It is not the whole Crystal. A Crystal of nine kilograms of Crystal and slag that reads as one structure, at a Flux Density of 670 million EU/g and η 0.61, outputs 408,700,000 AU/s, passed through one gram of it at a time.

Where the heat goes, and why it cannot go through the body

This section and the next are the Shell’s, and so they are the Body Path’s bill and the bill of anyone else who sends a working into matter. The missing fraction of every expenditure through the Shell burns off as heat, noise and structural bleed, because the Shell is the athanor, the furnace that never goes cold, and what it does not drive into the work it burns. The Measurewrights’ account says the same in their own terms, and their list is right about how much. Their first law says the missing fraction cannot vanish; the second says it cannot come back as work; the Gouy–Stodola relation (Ẇlost = T₀ · Ṡgen) says it reappears as low-grade heat at the place the loss happened. Light and sound are ways out of that place, not alternatives to it, and each becomes heat where it is absorbed.

It cannot burn inside the body and be carried out. The Measurewrights prove it by Fourier’s law, q = −k∇T. The small practitioner’s 28.4 MW of furnace heat through 1.8 m² of skin is 15.8 MW/m²; across 3 mm of skin at k ≈ 0.4 W·m⁻¹·K⁻¹ that would need

ΔT = qL/k = 1.58 × 10⁷ × 0.003 / 0.4 = 1.2 × 10⁵ K.

across three millimeters, and that is the small practitioner. At the middle practitioner’s 39.0 MW it is 1.63 × 10⁵ K; at the large practitioner’s 204 GW it is 8.5 × 10⁸ K. Skin chars at about 200 °C.

So the furnace is not in the chest. The Soul Crystal is an aperture, the Shell is the furnace, and its heat burns at or outside the body’s surface. The body does not carry the heat of its own workings; the Shell does, and the Shell sheds it into the room. A practitioner at a yield above one is marked by exactly this: no furnace heat, aura warmth or harmonic noise leaks outward from them, which says that from everyone below one it does. Their aura still shows in its own color, and their strength can still be felt; it simply sheds nothing.

Only the Shell’s loss is heat. A spend through the Core loses its share in the Core, as a hollowing: a color gone flat, a cold, a slipped word, and at the end a name. A pull through the Attraction Layer leaves its lost share standing on the Layer as a knot. Neither warms the room by a degree, and the blood and the shaking that come with them are the report of the payment and carry none of it. The guild’s megajoules are the same megajoules, and the stratal book takes the whole debit. So the table below prices a hitter. A Spirit worker at full output lights no timber at any radius, and a cool, quiet aura may be a master of the Shell or may be a thrower spending herself, which is a different thing to be afraid of.

This is the tell, and it is also the cost. A practitioner at full output is a lamp, a furnace and a hazard to everyone within a stated radius, and the radius is on this table. A low-yield practitioner throws more heat and glare for the same work. A high-yield one sheds almost none: their aura still burns in its own color and brightens as they push, but it gives off no heat, which is why a cool, quiet aura is remarked on, and why one working in silence and in the cold should frighten an onlooker more, not less. The ladder of who is brighter than whom depends on (1 − η) × output and on nothing else. The tell does not end when the working does: scorched ground, a burned ring and a room still warm are the aftermath, and the aftermath is read like the working was.

Yield above one

Above 1.0 the Continuum recognizes the expression as law and supplements it with ambient flow; the environment becomes a co-author. A yield above one is real. The soul has become so wholly the law it works that the world joins the work, and what the room adds was never the worker’s to pay. The Measurewrights, who say it breaks none of their laws because the practitioner is not a closed system, compare it to a heat pump, a device they can reckon on a slate and nobody has ever built, delivering three to five times its input by moving energy it did not make.

The surplus is pulled from the Aether stratum, the room, not from the Crystal. Of every 1.05 delivered at η 1.05, 0.05 is pulled in. At 9,870 AU/s and η 1.05 that is 4.76% of the 10.36 GW delivered, 494 MW taken from the stratum around the practitioner, every second of full output, while their own Crystal pays only the 9,870 EU/s it would pay at η 1.00. Their reserve drains at the same rate as anyone’s with the same AU/s; what the high yield buys is delivered effect and a working that sheds nothing.

The room pays, and the room shows it. The stratum a practitioner above one pulls on is measurably depleted, and it stays thinner after the working ends; passive recovery in that ground runs slower (see “How fast it comes back, and on what it depends”), for them and for anyone else who stands in it. That is the tell and it is also the counter: starve the reservoir. Aether-dead ground, a depleted site or a Silence Realm (a place where the Aether is dead) leaves nothing to supplement the expression with, and the yield falls back below one by definition.

What it costs

Traits come and go without pattern under Essence Starvation. A Trait is a standing bias maintained across the Shell, and a Shell below its working reserve cannot maintain a bias, so the law drops out and returns as the reserve moves. That is the Flicker.

Starvation has a direction, not only a line. A store doing the same work through a falling density loses yield: a millpond sinking behind its dam turns the wheel weaker, and a slackening bowstring throws the bolt shorter. So the yield falls as the reserve falls, and the lost share (1 − η) rises at exactly the moment the practitioner can least afford it. The Starved practitioner is brighter for the same work, sheds more, delivers less. Starvation does not begin at ten percent; it becomes visible there.

Each road runs dry in its own way. The shared tells stand for everyone: the breath shortens and the aura dims at half, tremor and thinning color at a quarter, the Flicker at a tenth. On top of them a Body fighter runs hot and then cracks, since the furnace burns more of every spend: haze on the skin at half, steam off the shoulders and cramps at a quarter, and below the floor the Shell draws its Charge out of the flesh and the skin splits in fine lines. A Spirit worker runs hollow, since the rising loss is paid in the Core: the colors near her go flat, then a cold and a slipped word, and below the floor names and memories go. A Fate worker runs unlucky: small luck curdles nearby, then things go wrong around her, then her own name sits wrong in other mouths, and at the very end the knots all come due at once. The Three Paths has the table.

The reserve clock is the clock a fight runs on. Because the heat burns at the Shell and is not carried by the body, a practitioner can spend down to the floor and past it, and a real fight does: at the shares of “Costs are a share of the reserve”, ten to twenty committed exchanges bring a practitioner to Starvation, and at full output the table in “The clock across the ladder” does it in turns.

How fast it comes back, and on what it depends

Recovery runs by three routes.

Passive Recovery is the baseline pull of ambient Aether without active effort, packed raw in the Shell as Charge or refined in the Crystal into Essence in the shares the soul’s Paths have set, governed by Tempering Yield and Harmonics Synergy. It is faster near active Wellspring veins, slower in depleted or hostile environments, and negligible in Silence Realms. Rich ground fills a Crystal faster and thin ground starves it.

Active Recovery is deliberate absorption through meditation, Wellspring communion or alchemical treatment. It is significantly faster, but requires the character to stop fighting and concentrate. An alchemical treatment quickens the pull from the ground and the Wellspring, and a fight Draft (a dose brewed for battle) pours power straight back into a reserve, taking its price in the crash that follows. The Draft follows the drinker’s road: Core stock feeds the Core with unstamped Essence and the crash is the stamping, and a Body fighter’s Salt packs raw Aether into the Shell and leaves cramps and fever.

Crisis Recovery is a surge triggered by a soul-cost event, a Threshold completion or a genuine emotional breakthrough during combat. It is not reliable and cannot be manufactured.

Long enough to be a decision in a fight, short enough not to end one, and slow enough that a siege still works: a percent a turn does not refill anybody mid-exchange.

Energy taken in

Energy a practitioner absorbs is banked in their own Crystal. Whatever reaches the Crystal from outside (a working turned and taken rather than blocked, a Wellspring drunk from, a discharge caught) enters the reserve at the same rate it leaves it: one EU banked for every EU’s worth taken in, which the guild books as a megajoule, as Charge in a Body fighter’s Shell and as Essence, once stamped, in a Spirit worker’s Core.

Absorption can overfill the Crystal. Recovery stops at maximum; absorption does not. A practitioner can be driven above their own maximum reserve by what they take in, and what they hold above it is reserve like any other, spent at the same rate and counted toward the same floor.

What Starvation is not

It is not unconsciousness and it is not a hit-point track. A Starved practitioner is still a person with a sword. What they have lost is the standing part of what they were: Traits that were law become weather, the Shell that was a wall becomes a sheet, stats read one Grade lower than they stand at full reserve. Below ten percent a practitioner suffers Essence Starvation, and a defender who can afford to spend nothing wins a long engagement against an attacker who cannot afford to spend nothing. Sieges are decided by this more often than by any working.

What the Ledger gives the other side of the fight

Every figure in this Part is a figure someone can use against the person it describes.

It costs something that hurts. Seven broadcast suppressions and the small practitioner is Starved; six heavy constructs and the large practitioner is (see “Worked, at three scales”). A committed exchange costs whole percents of a reserve (see “Costs are a share of the reserve”). A working that forfeits reserve leaves the maximum permanently smaller each time it is performed. The furnace’s heat is a burn radius around the practitioner’s own position (see “The tell, priced”).

It has stated limits. The Stage caps the reserve (see “The bands”). Reserve ÷ AU/s caps the duration (see “The drain”). The Stage’s Max Grade caps the output (see “The spine: EU delivered → Grade → a tier from one to nine”). The AU/s a practitioner carries is a burst ceiling, not a duty cycle (see “What AU/s is not”).

Something beats it, and it is one of the four routes. Deny the field. Break the boundary. Break the body. Break the man. The Ledger arms the first and the third:

  • Deny the field (see “Yield above one”). A practitioner at a yield above 1.0 is pulling on the room. Aether-dead ground, a depleted site or a Silence Realm takes the supplement away and the yield falls below one by definition. The same ground slows or stops the refill (see “How fast it comes back, and on what it depends”).
  • Break the body (see “The clock across the ladder”). Most reserves empty in under two minutes at full drain. The whole column is a clock, and making an enemy spend is making the clock run. Starvation lowers every stat a Grade and makes the Traits flicker; the practitioner who has spent is the practitioner who can be broken. It has a tell. Surface temperature, burn radius and the aftermath of scorched ground mark a practitioner whose furnace is burning (see “The tell, priced”). A thinned stratum marks one pulling above one (see “Yield above one”). The Flicker is visible before the practitioner admits to it (see “What it costs”), and a Starved practitioner burns brighter for the same work. Heat is the Shell’s tell. A Spirit worker running down goes flat, cold and forgetful, and a Fate worker’s bad luck arrives before she does.

Its numbers stay in band. Every reserve stands against its Stage’s band (see “Where a reserve sits against its band”), including those that sit under it and those that stand past the gate.
