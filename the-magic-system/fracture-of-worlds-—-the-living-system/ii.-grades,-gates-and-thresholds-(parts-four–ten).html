II. Grades, Gates and Thresholds (Parts Four–Ten)

II. Grades, Gates and Thresholds (Parts Four–Ten)

Nov 17, 202629 min read

Gudmund Never-Hit worked the ice fairs of Kharven for nine winters with one act. He stood on a square of swept ice in a blue cloak and a hat with a bell on it, and for a copper a bolt anyone with a crossbow could try him at twenty paces. His Reflex read D-Grade on a Guild roll, which is the very letter for the trick, and his Vitality read F, because nobody had ever hit him. He watched the finger, he watched the tiller kick, he leaned, the bolt went past his ear into a wall of straw bales, the wind of it set the bell on his hat ringing, and the crowd bawled for more. At the Sunmeet Fair he slipped nineteen in a row from a line of grinning sergeants, with the odds on a hit drifting out to forty to one. The twentieth came from behind him. A chandler’s widow to whom he owed nine silver marks had paid her copper, climbed into the hayloft over the mead tent and rested the stock on a beam.

The bolt went in under the hat and out through his teeth, and the bell went on ringing a good while after he had stopped moving. His coat (the aura a practitioner wears as armor) did nothing for him, since a coat is a thing a man holds and a man taken by surprise is holding nothing. The widow had put one silver mark on a hit before she climbed the ladder, which at forty to one covered the debt four times over and bought a mourning band for the hat. Every line of that afternoon is a matter of letters, and the letters are on this page.

Part Four — The Tier Grade System

Tier Grade is the qualitative bracket that determines what scale of force, phenomenon, or entity a practitioner can meaningfully engage, resist, or overcome. It is derived from a Sub-Stat’s raw numeric value and confirmed by Temperance Stage. A stat cannot hold its Tier Grade if the Soul Crystal has not undergone the Threshold required to sustain that density.

An attack Grade reads off EU delivered, the EU spent times the yield (η), the share of what is spent that reaches the world. The thresholds are the trade’s old ones, and the Measurewrights still reckon them in joules and tons of TNT at one EU to the megajoule; that column is their conversion and never the reading. The speed column is speed, measured. When two characters clash, who wins a contested exchange is answered first by Tier Grade differential, then by technique and tactical intelligence, then by Soul Crystal architecture.

One full Tier Grade above an opponent in the relevant stat wins a direct exchange of that stat category without meaningful contest. Two Grades above is overwhelming to the point of one-sided. Three Grades above means the lower-grade character is not engaging in a battle, they are surviving an event.

Where the Grade Sits

The table below measures a Sub-Stat’s raw value, and the Temperance ceiling in Part Five binds the Sub-Stat. Every Grade letter in this document, in the Path gates of Part Seven and the Resonant Pairs of Part Nine, means what it has always meant: a threshold on that Sub-Stat’s own number.

A Primary is the combined total of its eight Sub-Stats, and its Grade is read off the mean, the total divided by eight, or by seven for Dominion, whose Throne is not counted. That mean is the figure a character sheet has always carried on the Primary line. Eight Ardency Sub-Stats totaling 2,400 give a mean of 300 and an A-Grade Ardency. The total is what the sheet records; the mean is what an assessor reads, and a contested exchange is decided by the Grade of the Sub-Stat actually in use. See I. Levels, Experience and Stat Points for the point economy.

Because the ceiling sits on the Sub-Stat, a character’s Sub-Stats and their Primary Grade sit in the same bracket. A B-Grade practitioner has broadly B-Grade stats. Spread shows what they are good at, and a specialist’s favorites may stand a bracket or more above the mean. The owner’s window reads in these letters, and the raw value stays for study.

A Primary is dragged down by its neglected entries as hard as it is lifted by its favorites. A practitioner with three Dominion Sub-Stats at the ceiling and five near nothing reads as a modest Dominion on any assessor’s ledger while legislating local reality with the three. The institutions measure the mean. The fight is decided by the peak.

Throne is the exception. It carries no Temperance ceiling, its floor sits at EX-Grade, and it is excluded from the Dominion total. It is carried as its own line on the character sheet. Dominion’s mean is the total divided by seven, the seven counted Sub-Stats; Throne sits outside both the total and the mean.

In the notes below, the scale names (Wall, Building, Town) say what a blow at that Grade can bring down, and the tons of TNT beside them are the Measurewrights’ reckoning of the same thresholds, a conversion for the trade.

Hollow. The unawakened baseline. No genuine contact with Essence. Output indistinguishable from ordinary mortal capacity. The System counts it all the same: a baker at Hollow keeps a Level, a Baking skill and a little sheet, with no Essence on it until waking.

F. First resonance contact has occurred. The Soul Crystal has opened. Technique nascent, Essence output barely controlled, but no longer fully mortal in potential.

E. Essence is deliberately shaped. Wellspring attunement fragile but genuine. Techniques constructed rather than erupted. Street to low Wall level.

D. Technique coherence established. Multi-step Aetheric expressions without collapse. Wall level, 0.005 tons TNT at the ceiling.

C. The journeyman tier. Intermediate-complexity techniques supported. Dominion at this Grade has the density a Domain (a space where the practitioner’s law overrides local law) will one day stand on, though the Stage grants a Domain and a Grade never does: a seed flickers at the Threshold into Refraction, a true Domain comes at Transcendence. Small Building to low Building, 0.005 to 0.25 tons TNT.

B. Presence felt before techniques land. An unwoken crowd has felt the weight in the room since C; between the woken, Pressure runs on the Stage gap and Dominion, as Part Eleven sets out. Building to Large Building, 0.25 to 11 tons TNT.

A. Sovereign practitioner tier. A Domain held at this Grade, from Stage VIII on, is live and law-bearing. City Block to Multi-City Block, 11 to 1,000 tons TNT.

S. Legend-class entry. Full-output techniques register at natural-disaster level. Aura suppresses B-grade and below passively. Small Town to Town, 1 to 5.8 kilotons TNT.

SS. Continental threat entry. Full-output events measured against major geological disruptions. A Domain at this level begins reshaping its local environment rather than merely existing within it. Town to Large Town, 5.8 to 100 kilotons TNT.

SSS. Small-country scale destructive equivalence. Domain law can override localized Wellspring behavior within its territory. Full-power expression is a regional metaphysical event. Large Town to Small Country, 100 kilotons to 1 teraton TNT.

X. Archon-adjacent (near the power of the fourteen Archons, the beings who hold conceptual law). Existence exerts passive influence on the metaphysical laws of the surrounding environment. Domain produces Archon-echo effects. Country to Moon level, 1 teraton to 29.6 exatons TNT.

EX. Archon-equivalent. The distinction between an EX practitioner and a functioning Archon is qualitative, a matter of metaphysical doctrine and the Principle they embody, not raw output. Moon to Large Planet, 29.6 exatons to 16.506 ronnatons TNT.

EX+. No formal upper ceiling beneath the Apotheosis threshold (where the line between practitioner and living Principle collapses). Titan-adjacent space. Characters here are not participants in ordinary conflict. They are metaphysical events that other characters survive or fail to survive. Brown Dwarf level and beyond, scaling toward stellar and solar-system scale.

At Zenith, Stage XIV, speed and attack output are no longer assessed by conventional metrics. The character exists in a state where the Continuum’s ordinary measuring systems require metaphysical recalibration to even register the practitioner’s presence. Zenith characters do not move faster than light. They exist within frameworks where the question of movement speed has ceased to be the relevant variable.

Part Five — Temperance Gates and Stat Ceilings

Temperance Stage functions as a hard ceiling on every Sub-Stat in the system, and a Primary’s mean therefore cannot exceed it either. A character cannot allocate their way past a ceiling their Soul Crystal cannot structurally sustain. Throne alone carries no ceiling. The Crystal must have undergone the corresponding Threshold Event before it can hold a stat at the grade above. The ceiling binds what a Crystal holds to stay. A transient boost (a vow, a Release, a Draft, a strain lift) passes it lawfully inside the two-Grade cap, which is read from the sustainable Grade on the sheet, and outside help adds no risk unless it is forced on someone; a blessing counts as outside help.

The Max Grade column binds allocation: a Sub-Stat may not be allocated past the top of the Stage’s Max Grade bracket. At Stages V, VII, IX and XI the Sub-Stat ceiling sits above that bracket, and the band between them, 276 to 350, 401 to 475, 551 to 625, 726 to 750, is the instability zone, reachable only under strain, never by allocation.

Forced advancement past Temperance gates is not impossible, but it is catastrophic. Stats pushed above their sustainable grade to stay, into the instability zone or past the ceiling itself, by alchemical augmentation, bloodline exploitation, or external metaphysical imposition generate Crystal Fracture Events at an exponentially increasing rate until the stat collapses or the Threshold is passed. This is a stat dragged past a Threshold nobody has crossed. A Draft or a blessing that supplies the Catalyst crosses the Threshold itself, and Part Eight sets out the terms.

The Tier of Standing column is the fighting guilds’ yardstick. The Guild Accord is a coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations, and its nine titles, Initiate through Paragon, are the same in all of them, grading trust in that trade: a merchant may deal up to his title, a craftsman make up to hers, an adventurer take commissions up to the same Threat Tier. Fighting guilds, adventurers’ guilds included, read the title off this table, so a fighter’s title also says how dangerous they are, and a Grandmaster merchant may not be able to throw a punch. The column replaces the former lettered Coherence Bands, which shared their letters with the Grade table and stopped at Zenith. The nine Tiers reach Revelation and Apex. Dissonance, Stage XI, carries unique behavior. Characters who pass through Dissonance rather than becoming trapped within it reach Stage XII with stats that are fractured but denser than the clean advancement path would produce. Certain Sub-Stats, particularly Overflow, Overchannel and Persistence, can only reach their true ceiling in a character who has passed through Dissonance. The fractures are not damage. They are architectural features. Emanation, Stage XII, is where those fractures become features that radiate outward.

Part Six — Speed by Grade and Level

Speed divides into three components: Attack Speed, the velocity of technique execution; Reaction Speed, the capacity to perceive and respond to incoming force; and Travel Speed, movement velocity across space. All three are influenced by Dexterity’s Sub-Stats, particularly Celerity, Reflex and Sequence, but each responds differently to Temperance advancement.

These values are the natural ceiling achievable at each Tier Grade through pure stat advancement, without technique amplification. Techniques built around Alacrity and Celerity can push further within overchannel risk parameters.

At D-Grade the practitioner can read a crossbow bolt loosed across a room and slip it, if they see the loose. At B-Grade they can stand in a volley of bolts and arrows without significant reactive disadvantage. Every missile carries a fixed threat Grade by its kind, sling stone, hunting arrow, war-bow shaft, crossbow bolt or ballista bolt, a worked missile taking its maker’s Grade instead, and the Speed Grade meets it. A bolt stays the cheap thing that can kill a high-Stage fighter in a room, which is why veterans sit with their backs to walls. At A-Grade movement generates significant environmental effects, heat buildup from air friction, pressure waves, surface disruption, without deliberate technique activation. At S-Grade the air itself becomes a weapon; characters without S-Grade Resilience or higher sustain structural damage simply from proximity to a moving S-Grade practitioner at full speed. At SS-Grade biological neural processes are insufficient and Essence-mediated sensory routing becomes the primary perceptual mechanism, and the practitioner’s passage through space at full speed is perceived as instantaneous by anyone below S-Grade. Dodging a full-speed SSS attack requires SSS Reaction Speed at minimum to even register the attack as having occurred. Below SSS-Grade nobody can perceive, let alone react to, X-Grade full speed. EX movement generates relativistic effects that interact with the surrounding Aether field and can cause localized temporal distortion as a byproduct rather than an intention. EX+ movement is not faster travel between two points; it is Essence-mediated spatial restructuring where distance is subject to Domain authority.

Zenith, Stage XIV: Immeasurable Speed classification applies at full expression. The practitioner does not move through space. In the limit case, they exist in all relevant positions simultaneously until they choose otherwise.

Part Seven — Individual Stat Gates

Every Sub-Stat carries a Path Alignment requirement. Certain subs cannot exceed a given Tier Grade without demonstrated Path commitment. Path commitment is not declared. It is recognized by the Crystal based on behavioral pattern, the consistent routing of Essence through a specific plane’s logic over time.

Where a gate below names a component of a Sub-Stat, the inscription and glyph-chain durability under Density, the gravitational component under Tolerance, and the rest, the gate caps only that component. Part Twelve’s Merge Ledger folded those components into the entries that carry them, but the gate still reads the part it names: a sheet without the named Path commitment runs that component capped at its Grade, or without it, while the rest of the Sub-Stat runs at full. A healer without Body Path heals fully and cannot reinforce.

A character who has fought exclusively through Body Path mechanics for three full Bands has genuine Body Path alignment regardless of what they claim. A character who has occasionally used Spirit techniques while routing primarily through physical expression will find Spirit-aligned Sub-Stats quietly resisting advancement beyond C-Grade until the actual pattern of Soul Crystal use reflects commitment.

The gates below are written for one Path. A soul on one Path opens them at the Stage written. A soul on two Paths opens the gates of the Path behind a Stage late until that Path is mastered. A soul on three opens every gate two Stages late, and the high Grades (SSS and up) stay shut. The highest Stage whose gates a Path has opened is its Path Stage on the card, and Mastered means it equals the soul’s Stage, read again at each Threshold. Studying a Path, which anyone may do, opens no gates at all. A gate asks for the road and never for a rung on a class ladder: Fate gates open from Stage II though Thread Class is not read before Glory, and a Fate Path fighter clears them on Range, presence, bonds and oaths without ever having pulled a thread.

Older registers list Attraction as a road of its own. They were looking at the shallow end of Fate, and an Attraction gate in an old table is a Fate gate at the same Stage and Grade. Obsession Force satisfies a Fate gate the same way clean Attraction Force does, every corrupted practitioner in the four quarters included.

The Fate Path gate at Stage VII to exceed S stands for one thing only, hiding a pull or a claim, which is done on the Attraction Layer and by nobody who has not walked that road.

Part Eight — Temperance Threshold Events

Thresholds are not achieved by accumulating enough experience. They are triggered by two factors: the Soul Crystal must have reached sufficient pressure within the current stage, a quantitative floor; and a Catalyst Condition must have been met, a specific quality of experience the Crystal requires before it will authorize the transition, or else the Catalyst must be supplied from outside.

A character can be quantitatively eligible for months of in-world time and never pass a Threshold because the Catalyst has not occurred. The road to the Catalyst differs: some meet it in crisis, some work toward it in seclusion, some are driven to it by tribulation, and most by a mixture of the three.

The fourth road is money. A Draft or a blessing can supply the Catalyst for any Stage, and it simply works, so Stages can be bought, and the wealthy are stronger because they are wealthy. The great houses hoard the top Drafts. The poor rise by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings, and a blessing may carry a person further than any of those, since it grants a power out of their natural reach, even in Level. A bought Stage opens the door and no more. The ceiling lifts, and nothing the events below would have taught comes with it. A Release must still be found and mastered by fighting, and sects teach what money cannot buy: Releases, Domains, techniques, skills and the rites for their Wellspring. Veterans will tell you they can feel a bought Stage inside two exchanges, and they say so at the top of their lungs, usually to the person who bought it.

Stage I counts as a Threshold worth 100 points. Every practitioner starts with it. The events below are the transitions as a soul lives them, and each grants the Stage number multiplied by 100 on the terms of Part Three, whichever way the Catalyst arrived.

I to II, Welling. First genuine contact with a Wellspring’s Aether under personal pressure, not observed, not instructed, but touched and survived.

II to III, Ascension. First technique developed independently from instruction, built from the character’s own understanding of their Essence rather than received doctrine.

III to IV, Flourishing. Formation of the first genuine Spirit Axis, a bond with another soul that carries mechanical weight in the Harmonics system.

IV to V, Splintering. Surviving contact with an entity or force more than two full Tier Grades above the character’s current ceiling. Not defeating it, surviving it. The Crystal fractures along emotional lines.

V to VI, Glory. The self burns bright and is noticed. Willingly accepting genuine soul-cost to preserve something the character values above their own structural integrity. The peak of the inner phase.

VI to VII, Refraction. Dual sight. The soul sees itself from outside. First Domain seed activation, however fragile or small: a one-time flicker of the person’s own Trait at the Threshold, which needs no Release. The Second Release, open from this Stage, is what later lets a seed be called and held. Projected fields become coherent.

VII to VIII, Transcendence. The world is read as symbol. Resolving a core internal contradiction documented in the Soul Crystal’s fracture record, a wound carried as structural damage and consciously processed into architecture. From this Stage a true Domain can be grown by living and called aloud.

VIII to IX, Invocation. Calling on Wellsprings, Titans, or Archonic law as co-authors of expression. The practitioner’s Domain throws a first flicker of a Realm capable of holding other Domains, once, at the Threshold, on the same terms as the seed. The Third Release, open from this Stage, is what makes a Realm.

IX to X, Realization. Truth as lived consequence. The world answers back. Pacts, artifacts and law imprint on terrain. The Domain becomes a reality the Continuum must accommodate.

X to XI, Dissonance. Deliberately entering contact with a force or truth capable of ending the character’s existence and remaining coherent long enough to choose how they meet it. Power and identity clash. The Crystal fractures or breaks through.

XI to XII, Emanation. Emerging from Dissonance with a Soul Crystal denser and cleaner than before entry. The fractures must have become features. The practitioner radiates outward. Traits become local law. Passive miracles leak.

XII to XIII, Principality. Achieving Domain coherence sufficient for the Continuum to formally register a law the character embodies. A local force of nature. First World Spirit threshold. Biomes carry doctrine.

XIII to XIV, Zenith. The character’s accumulated doctrine becomes recognizable to a Hollow Throne (the empty seat of a dead Archon). The Throne resonates with them. The transition is not a breakthrough. It is recognition, and a Throne must recognize a buyer too, so here money buys the attempt and not the seat. Full World Spirit. Reality acclimates to the character. Story becomes law.

XIV to XV, Revelation. Genuine exchange with an Archon or Titan at a level where the character is a participant rather than a witness or a casualty. The first step of the Apotheosis approach.

XV to XVI, Apex. Holding a Principle in the Soul Crystal without fracturing, even momentarily, even imperfectly. Apex is perpetual. It is the state of a soul pressing against what it has not yet become.

Part Ten — Stagnation and Diminishing Returns

The System has two structural enemies: grinding and stagnation. Both are penalized in the architecture of the Crystal itself.

Grinding is countered by the Crystal Adaptation Rule. Any threat or stimulus type engaged more than five times without meaningful variation to the character’s technique or the threat’s composition is reclassified internally as Familiar. Familiar stimuli yield ten percent of their original XP value, permanently, for that character. The only way to restore value is to approach it with a genuinely novel technique or under genuinely novel conditions the Crystal registers as a different category of experience.

Stagnation is countered by Residual Strain accumulation. Every fifty levels’ worth of XP accrued at a halted Band ceiling without passing the required Temperance Threshold imposes a five percent penalty to all XP gains and a two percent increase to all Overchannel and Backlash risk values. This pressure builds continuously.

It dissolves entirely the moment the Threshold is passed. On dissolution the character receives a Strain Release Bonus equal to thirty percent of all XP accumulated during the stagnation period, delivered as a single Resonant Surge. The System is not punishing patience. It is representing the physical truth of a soul carrying the weight of what it has already earned but not yet integrated. That weight has texture. That texture eventually becomes pain. And then, when the character meets the Catalyst Condition or can afford someone who bottled it, it becomes power.
