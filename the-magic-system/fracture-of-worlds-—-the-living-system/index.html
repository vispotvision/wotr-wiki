Fracture of Worlds — The Living System

Fracture of Worlds — The Living System

Nov 28, 202614 min read

The System does not reward survival. It rewards contact with things larger than you.

Every scar the Soul Crystal carries is a record of how far you reached beyond what you were.

The most expensive sentence ever spoken at the Haymarket Fair in Altherion cost forty silver marks, the good name of a councilman’s son and most of a pie stall. Aldous Thwaite had hired a Measurewright (a Guild scholar who assays souls for a fee) to read his Soul Crystal from a scarlet-draped stage, with trumpets. The man set a brass assay fork against Thwaite’s breastbone, listened to it sing, and then, the fork still ringing and himself an honest fool, turned it toward the groom holding the horse. “Your groom reads higher, young sir.” Thwaite called the groom out on the spot, and the groom hit him once, apologizing, and six hundred people watched a gold-stitched coat leave through the pies. What follows is the ledger that fork was reading. Measurewrights who read it aloud to paying customers learn to stand near a door.

What the System Is

The Fracture of Worlds progression framework is the metaphysical skeleton beneath every character in War of the Realms. It is not a game overlay imposed on the world from outside. It is a natural consequence of how the Continuum observes, measures, and responds to the maturation of living souls. When a practitioner grows, the Continuum recognizes it. When they stagnate, the Continuum applies pressure. When they fracture, the Continuum waits to see what re-forms in the break.

The System tracks eight dimensions of a soul’s development, each called a Primary Stat, and beneath each of those, eight finer measurements called Sub-Stats. These numbers are not just mechanical data. They are the Continuum’s honest accounting of who you actually are, not who you claim to be.

Level is simply the count of how many times your Soul Crystal (literally the soul itself, where a practitioner’s power is held and shaped) has meaningfully densified. It is not a measure of age. It is not a measure of training hours. It is the sediment of genuine contact with consequence, counted, compressed, and expressed as a number. Level moves by any real work a life does: danger overcome above all, so killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills and crews argue over who got the kill, but also a flood night held, a plague nursed through, fifty years at a trade. The baker can stand at Level 140 and never have been in danger. Level does not open a Stage by itself. A Stage opens when its Catalyst is met and the soul’s Level can hold it; How Power Grows has the short road.

The ceiling is 500 for everyone below Stage XV. Paragons (practitioners at Stages XV and XVI) exceed it, and the Codex does not number what they reach.

The Window

Everyone carries a private copy of this ledger, the unwoken included: a baker has a Baking skill and a little sheet. The Soul Crystal is the soul and the Continuum measures it, so the soul reports its numbers to its owner in their own tongue, and nobody else can see the report. That report is the window. It comes up often and in detail, in a fight as readily as on a quiet morning, for damage, for skill gains and for reserve, and it sits in the owner’s sight as a plain block of text: [Reserve: 41%]. It shows numbers, percentages, bars and levels, and it never gives its owner a letter: Grades are for things. The EU, AU/s and yield (η) of the later Parts stay out of it and live on a Measurewright’s slate. The groom at the Haymarket had been reading his own every morning for years, and a normal look at it went something like this:

[Stage IV Flourishing · Adept (Clouded)]
[Level 64]
[Race: Human · Lineage: none of note]
[Vitality 552 · Ardency 310 · Gnosis 141 · Dexterity 604]
[Harmonics 120 · Resilience 133 · Tempering 150 · Dominion 112]
[Reserve: 100% ██████████]
[Skills: Horse-handling, Journeyman · Short Right Hand, Apprentice]
[Blessings: none]

A normal look shows the Eight Primaries with their totals. A Sub-Stat surfaces when it is used or when it moves, and the full set stays there for anyone with the patience to study it. Race and lineage show, and they shape base stats and what comes easily. Skills rise with use and show on the nine ranks, Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster and Paragon; techniques are named things done with them, show no level, and take a bigger name when they jump. A Trait has no level: it works at its base until Glory and at the lead Path’s Mastery after. A blessing sits on its own line, and since the giver wrote that line, it can lie. To read anyone else you need an assay fork or a Gnosis Perception stronger than their Resilience Ward; everything short of that is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide it or fake it, which keeps the Measurewrights in pie.

The Short Version

Ringside, nobody says any of this. They ask what rank a fighter is and what he is good for, and the Measurewright in the judges’ box winces and writes down the real terms, which are these.

Three things climb. The soul climbs Stages, and each Stage asks one thing of it. The life climbs Level, by everything hard and real it has done. The hands climb skills, by use. Everything else on the sheet is read from those three.

Stage, said as rank. Sixteen Stages, and everyone says the rank: I Initiate, II Apprentice, III Journeyman, IV Adept, V to VII Expert, VIII to X Master, XI and XII Grandmaster, XIII and XIV Archmaster, XV and XVI Paragon. The Stage name is the precise term (“a Journeyman, Ascension, and close to Flourishing”). The Crystal shows it in its look, which is how the stone looks and not a track of its own: Clouded (I to IV), Faceted (V to VII), Fire (VIII to X), Flawless (XI and XII), Starred (XIII and XIV), Absolute (XV and XVI). The old reckoning of five Bands of a hundred Levels, Mortal Foundation through Absolute, survives only in old books.

Level cap 500. A Stage opens when its Catalyst is met and the soul’s Level can hold it: Stage II holds at Level 5, IV at 30, V at 101, VIII at 201, XI at 301, XIII at 401, XV at 500, and Level keeps rising past 500 at Paragon with no number the Codex will give. A Stage held above its Level, bought or met early, leaves the Crystal Overgrown until Level catches up. Level that runs past the top of its rank’s group with no Catalyst to the next (past 100 below Expert, 200 below Master, 300 below Grandmaster, 400 below Archmaster) builds Residual Strain, and the veteran stuck at that gate is the strange and dangerous one.

The hidden scale is a fixed threshold on the raw Sub-Stat value, universal across every stat: 1–10, 11–25, 26–50, 51–100, 101–175, 176–275, 276–400, 401–550, 551–725, 726–950, 951–1,200, 1,201–1,500, 1,501 and above. On a thing it is read aloud as a Grade, Hollow and F through EX+, and a sword, a bolt, a beast or a bounty carries its letter openly. On a person it is never named: the sheet shows the number, and a stat a full step ahead on the scale wins its exchange without a contest. A Primary is the combined total of its eight Sub-Stats; Dominion counts seven, since Throne stands outside it. A Measurewright may still say a woman hits like a Grade A beast, and she will not thank him.

Same words, two worlds. The nine ranks are also the Guild Accord’s nine titles, Initiate through Paragon, and the Accord grades trust in a trade with them: a merchant may deal up to his title, a craftsman make up to hers, an adventurer take commissions up to the Grade of bounty the Accord allows that title. Titles are earned by commissions; fighting guilds also ask the title’s own rank in Stage, and trades ask none. So “Master Venn” at the forge may be an Apprentice by Stage on the road, and a Grandmaster merchant may still be unable to throw a punch. People mix the two up, and lose money doing it.

Temperance Stage caps the value. A character cannot allocate past a ceiling their Crystal cannot structurally sustain. A transient boost passes that ceiling lawfully inside two steps on the hidden scale, read from the sustainable figure on the sheet; only a stat forced there to stay produces Crystal Fracture Events at an exponentially increasing rate. A Catalyst can be met by crisis, seclusion or tribulation, or handed over in a breakthrough Draft or a blessing, so a Stage can simply be bought. A bought Stage opens the door and no more: Level, Mastery and every Release must still be lived.

A Class is a name the soul offers for how the person has lived, first at Glory, Stage VI, with Mastery I. It is a name and never a number, and it evolves when the lead Path’s Mastery rises, each evolution giving one move that fits it. Mastery is one rung per Path, I to V and Ω, written on the card as Body IV or Spirit III. It is never tracked: the Stage sets the ceiling and Level places the soul under it, and it is unread below Glory, where the Stage’s typical figure serves. From Glory it sets the yield of workings through its Path’s layer: matter reads Body, a soul or a meaning reads Spirit, a condition reads Fate. Fate climbs with Level like the others, but without a grip, a Work of a bloodline or a blessing, it stops at II. Each road keeps its own folk words for the rungs (Muridic, Harmonic and the rest on the Body road; Listener, Answerer on the Spirit; Touched, Drawn on the Fate), and a rung held in the feared wrong way is called Inverted. The old Aether, Essence and Thread Classes, each running Ø to Ω, are the scholars’ old name for it. Classes and Mastery has the one table.

The reserve is one figure, and what fills it follows the road. Essence is Aether refined in the Crystal and stamped with identity. The Essence Core is the center of that identity and the Crystal is the soul, so Essence is the soul’s own substance, and spending it is spending yourself: it is what the Spirit Path spends, through the Core, and what it loses on the way is paid there as a hollowing and never as heat. The Charge is Aether packed raw in the Shell and never refined. It is what the Body Path spends, shaped by the lens right there and driven out through the Shell; spending it is spending the world, the body pays for the handling, and the lost share burns in the Shell’s furnace as heat. Thread is what the Fate Path pulls: never held, paid from the soul’s own share, and every pull leaves a knot the world collects later. Below a tenth of the reserve a Spirit worker’s Core frays, names first and then memory, and a Body fighter’s Shell starts drawing on the flesh. The Three Paths has the three routes and their prices.

A Wellspring is an alchemical and a spiritual transformation, the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether: a lens on magic already potent, which a soul bases its tempering on. The Titans are the Wellsprings themselves, and each of the Sixty is one way a Titan’s law shows up. It is got at a Core in a dungeon, from a school, from a Draft or blessing, or by birth. Each of the Sixty has a kind naming its home, Wild (thirty-three), Spirit (fifteen), Fate (six) or Medium (six, at home on all three), and a kind is a qualifier and never a lock: any Path can use any Wellspring.

A Path is the layer of the Crystal a soul leans on, and there are three: Body (the Physical World, through the Aether Shell, spending Charge), Spirit (the Soul Plane, through the Essence Core, spending Essence) and Fate (the Plane of Fate, through the Attraction Layer, pulling Thread). The Shell is the only door into matter, so a Spirit worker’s thrown force lands on souls and on nothing else. One Path masters fastest. Two take about two and a half times as long and three about six. A card gives each road a Path Stage, the highest Stage whose gates it has opened, and Mastered means that equals the soul’s Stage. No Path is the better one. Each has plain limits and each beats each of the others, and the Stage, the ground and the preparation decide which of those holds on the day.

The Plane of Fate is the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force and the workings of reality itself. Physical Reality under Aegor and Spiritual Reality under Ashura are the two lower realities; Fate, under Bara, sits above them and is no reality at all, being everything that flows, every possibility, idea and concept. People fight and wage wars with it, and it is the hardest thing they do. Real Fate Arts are rare and mostly by bloodline, and most people only touch it through a blessing.

Eight Primary Stats: Vitality, Ardency, Gnosis, Dexterity, Harmonics, Resilience, Tempering, Dominion. Eight Sub-Stats each, sixty-four in total.

The Crystal does not lie. It only counts what you actually are. The line a blessing’s giver wrote on it is another matter.
