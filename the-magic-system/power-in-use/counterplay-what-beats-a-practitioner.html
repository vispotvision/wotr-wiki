Counterplay: What Beats a Practitioner

Counterplay: What Beats a Practitioner

Nov 17, 202627 min read

Leave the working alone. Choose which of the four things you are taking away from him, and count how long you have before he notices which one you chose.

Every washing-house north of the Reach sings about Thorvald Eikhagen of the Red Anvil school, and every one of them has the soap wrong. Eikhagen stood at Stage IX (ninth of the soul’s sixteen steps, high enough that weather takes an interest), and that spring he had walked through the whole Blackwater levy at a stroll, four hundred spears, with his aura standing a hand high off his shoulders in the deep ochre of a quarry at sunset, while the bookies at Skarrow stopped taking money against him because nobody could be found to hold the other end of the bet. Then he went to the Three Herons bathhouse to soak. Behind the towel heap sat Wenna Sprat, a laundress of sixty-one with a bad hip and two sons who had been in that levy, and across her knees lay a secondhand crossbow that had cost her eight silver, spanned with a goat’s-foot lever an hour before, because she could not trust her back to do it in a hurry. Nobody reinforces in the tub. He was humming.

The bolt went in at the hollow of the throat and stopped against the neck bone. He stood up, and that part the song has right: the water ran arterial red to the rim of the tub, the ochre came roaring up over him in the one beat a coat takes to rebuild, around a bolt already in, and he crossed four steps of wet tile with the whole house shaking and the steam going gold around him. He got as far as the towels. The song gives him a vow there. The bath-boy, who was under a bench, gives him the word “Who?” He was dead by the ninth breath. The Red Anvil posted two thousand gold on her head, and it was never collected, because every washerwoman in Skarrow went before the bailiffs that month and swore she was Wenna Sprat.

Four hundred spears, then, and eight silver. I keep this register for the Guild as a Measurewright (one of the Guild’s sworn measurers of Aether, and of anything else that holds still), and I have assayed that corpse’s records at length. My colleague at Altherion maintains that Eikhagen’s reaction speed would have slipped the bolt with room to spare. He is correct, and he has spent thirty years failing to see why it does not matter. The man did not see the loose.

The Four Routes

Everything in this register reduces to one of four. Deny the field. Break the boundary. Break the body. Break the man. They cost different amounts, they take different lengths of time, and a competent commander picks by what is available rather than by preference.

The Four Answers

The routes are a soldier’s list, and they go after the man. The confessors and the benches keep a second list of four, older than mine, and it goes after the working itself. Its contrary idea. A soul further refined in its own idea. Ground that already holds another idea. A refusal of its claim. A frost lens laid against a fire lens is the first. The second is the old master who has lived the same idea longer and truer than the boy throwing it at her, so that it comes to her as a thing she already owns. The third is a graveyard thick with Echo, a battlefield heavy with Thread Weight, or somebody else’s Domain, ground where the world has already agreed to a different law. The fourth is a soul that will not consent to what the working says about it, and it is the one the Fate section below calls being too well bound to move. The two lists sit side by side and do not quarrel. Choosing the ground by his Wellspring is the third answer in a soldier’s coat, and the routes against the body and the man take the speaker away before any of the four has to be argued.

None of the four is promised to be within reach. The register lists what answers an art, and for some arts that is a soul two Stages up, a bloodline nobody can hire, or nothing yet found, and I write it down as it is rather than as a commander would like it. When a working does give way, under any of these, it gives way along its own idea’s flaw, at the place its idea never asked to hold, and never by some general collapse. My guild’s names for those failures (thermal debt, quench cracking and the rest) are good bookkeeping and wrong about the cause, as the entries in the Sixty say plainly.

I. Deny the Field

Take away what he pulls from. The working is correct and produces nothing.

Take him off rich ground. A practitioner pulls Aether out of the environment around him before he can do anything at all, on every road. A Body fighter packs it raw in the Shell (the outer layer of the Soul Crystal, which is the soul itself) as his Charge; a Spirit worker refines it in the Crystal into Essence (the soul’s own stamped substance) and spends that through the Core; and a Fate fighter fills his reserve off the same ground, refining it into Essence exactly as the Spirit worker does, before he reaches for anything higher, since Thread is pulled per working and never stored. Nobody works a grain he has not first pulled inside, so where he stands matters: rich ground fills him faster and thin ground starves him. Wherever there is a yard to choose, this is the first move and the cheapest, and it is settled with purses as often as with swords. Fight him on salted ground, worked the night before by hedge-workers (the hired hands of the petty arts, paid by the job) so that Aether will not pool on it, on bled ground, pulled empty by a long fight or a greedy crew, or on plain thin ground, and he has what he walked in with. A worked Wellspring site, which common speech calls a Core and which is a dungeon somebody cleared and now holds, recharges and has keepers, and a keeper with the right seal can close it in under an hour, if he has been paid, if he has not been paid more by the other side, and if he has not simply refused. The practitioner is not weakened; the room is. Every serious action inside a Concord city begins with somebody deciding what ground it will happen on, and that decision is made by people who will not be present at the fighting.

Choose the ground by his plane. Ground is measured three ways, and each road cares most about one. Aetheric Density (wild ground, mines, Wells) fills every reserve, so thin ground starves all three. Echo Depth is how thick a place is with memory, graveyards, temples and old homes, and it cheapens Spirit workings: meet a Spirit worker in a barn built last spring or a rented hall nobody has ever wept in, and every stroke costs him the full price. Thread Weight is how much has been decided on a piece of ground, battlefields, coronation halls and crossroads, and it cheapens Fate pulls: meet a Fate fighter in a turnip field where nothing has ever been settled. Where a fight comes down to the ground, the measure that counts is the one for his road, so read that one and deny it. The Three Grounds has the famous ground and what was done to hold it.

Choose the ground by his Wellspring (the lens he has tempered on; each entry in the Sixty lists the ground it favors and hates). Terrain is a modifier on yield (η, the share of what he spends that reaches the world) and the modifiers are large. For the Wild kind the ground the Wellspring’s idea cannot abide tells you most of it, and I give the cases below in my guild’s terms, which are right about how much and say nothing of why. A heat lens fails in thermal equilibrium, saturated cold, and oxygen-poor air. A lens of current fails against boiled leather, fired clay, dense fog and a shielded interior. A stone lens has opinions and no tools on open water. A lens of water cannot act without a medium, so a dry vault is a prison. The counter-lightning kit has been leather and ceramic for four centuries and everyone knows it, and it stays cheaper to ignore until somebody dies of it. A lens of absence needs a gradient, and saturated ground has none left to offer. For a mind-worker a crowd is noise. Light him, and fill the room with people. A lens of distance needs a survey it can trust to say where here is, so unmapped ground, warp-scarred ground and ground two Domains (regions where the world runs on one practitioner’s terms) are arguing over all leave the transform nothing to solve against. A lens of force needs something to push back. Loose sediment, scree, mud, deep water and free fall take the third law away from him. A lens of growth is rate-limited chemistry. Rates roughly halve for every ten degrees you take out of the air, so winter, bare stone and salted earth are a sentence on it. The Spirit kind cares nothing for weather: its ground looks ordinary, and what it hates is a place where nobody is carrying anything, or where everybody is carrying the wrong thing. The Fate kind hates a clean ledger and a room of strangers with no history between them.

Saturate it. Flood the area with released power faster than it disperses and everyone present takes Fracture (damage to the Soul Crystal itself) risk, including whoever did it. A weapon of last resort and a real one, and its practical use is the threat rather than the act. The threshold is a density and not a total: ten thousand EU (Essence Units) in each cubic meter, or a hundred AU/s (Aether Units per second, a rate of output) through each square meter of the region’s edge. It grows with the surface while output does not, so below Archmaster you cannot flood a field, only a building, and you have to be inside it. A Master’s full output a kilometer wide is about a five-hundredth of threshold. The same output in a hall is five times over it. A Zenith’s full output across that same kilometer stands close to the threshold, and at the top of his band well over it, which is one reason nobody invites a Zenith to a siege twice.

Shrink his authority. The radius in which a practitioner’s own law is the operating law is his field standing above the ground’s, and the ground wins on a square root. Addressing range, anchor reach and authority radius all fall as the root of the local density. The square root is my guild’s rule of thumb, and the bench’s reason for it is older and plainer: a voice is harder to hear in a crowded room, and rich ground is a room crowded with the world’s own Aether. Fight him on concentrated ground and all three drop to an eighteenth. At a Veil-Thin Nexus, a thousandth. Inside a Wellspring Core (the site where a Wellspring’s Aether pools and its law runs strongest) an S-Grade Domain is a fraction of a millimeter wide, because there the only law operating is the one the Wellspring carries. This is why a commander who cannot starve him takes the fight to the vein instead. It runs the other way too: Aether-thin ground gives him ten times the reach and almost nothing to pull, which is the whole of the bargain the lenses of absence strike. A Fate fighter feels this rule hardest, because Range and the radius of his Domain are most of what he fights with. Rich Aetheric ground crowds both, however well it fills him. What gives them back is Thread Weight: on ground where a great deal has been decided his Range and his Domain’s radius run longer than the Density alone would allow, so a Fate lord on an old battlefield holds more of the yard than the same man in a mine, and the cheapest answer to him is rich young ground, a new-sunk shaft or a fresh Well, where there is Aether in plenty and nothing has ever been settled.

Silence. The removal of magical possibility from a region, a stranger thing than any shield or counterspell. Lawful transformation cannot be authorized at all inside one. Animatria constructs fail completely because their continuity runs back to a source the Silence has severed. A soul held in one reports the sensation as loneliness rather than weakness, and that distinction is the most reliable field diagnostic for whether a quiet place is Silence or merely thin.

II. Break the Boundary

Attack the sentence and leave the speaker for later.

Interrupt the chain. Below Refraction (Stage VII) a working requires an external anchor: voice, hand, ink or blood. Take the anchor and the sentence does not complete. Break his wrist and a gestured chain is finished. Put a hand over his mouth and a spoken one is. Above Refraction the chain is internal and none of this applies, which is the practical reason Stage VII is the line every soldier is taught to fear rather than the reason the Codex (the Accord’s master register of laws) gives.

Force the Seal early. The Seal closes an operation against the warrant that opened it, and a warrant overstating standing seals anyway, with the difference deducted from the speaker at the moment of closing. A third party who can compel an early close has stopped defending and started billing him.

Knot and Lock. The only two Counter forms in the index. Laws cannot be opposed, so these two condition the conditioning. Knot entangles a path so it cannot be traced cleanly. Lock occupies a limit so no other limit can be set there. Every counter-working ever recorded is one of those two moves.

Wards. A cut working standing on a substrate, and therefore beaten the way any cut work is beaten: damage the substrate. Nobody argues with a ward. It is chiseled off, or the wall it sits on is brought down, or the third layer is scraped through by somebody who knows which layer to scrape. An inscription with a defective Sealing role has been failing continuously for two centuries, and whoever owns it owns a liability.

Countersigning. Some Seals end when countersigned in writing, and some end when the sum is spoken back correctly by another. A working that carries either of those closing conditions can be ended by an opponent who is literate enough, which is why Accord field practice avoids both and why they persist in the old inscriptions everywhere.

III. Break the Body

The Crystal is inside a person and the person is flesh: harder than common flesh, never proof. Taken asleep, drugged or by surprise, he keeps only the base his stats give the body and loses the coat of reinforcement he pushes over it, which is why ambush is the oldest answer on this list.

The bolt. Cheap, mechanically simple, and it does not care about Stage below the point where reaction speed outruns it. A crossbow costs about what a trooper’s saber costs, a child can be taught the lever in an afternoon, and it is the cheap thing that can kill a high-Stage fighter in a room. It is read by Speed Grade like any missile: every missile carries a fixed threat Grade by its kind, sling stone, hunting arrow, war-bow shaft, crossbow bolt and ballista bolt, a worked one taking its maker’s Grade, and the fighter’s Speed Grade meets it. A D-Grade (a low rank on the F to EX+ scale of raw strength) practitioner can read a crossbow bolt loosed across a room and slip it, if they see the loose. At B-Grade they can stand in a volley of bolts and arrows without significant reactive disadvantage. Everything below that line is arithmetic, and everything above it is still a body in a tub. A volley is a volley. This is the entire reason armor is still worth a lord’s rents and the entire reason ranks still stand in them. Bows, thrown things and siege engines belong on this line too, at their own threat Grades and their own prices.

Numbers, correctly used. One practitioner beats twenty men. Twenty men who have been drilled to spread, to make him choose, and to keep a lane open for the crossbows at all times will take casualties and kill him, and the doctrine that produces this is a hundred and forty years old and taught badly everywhere.

Distance. Current, by my guild’s reckoning, falls off with the square. Force has to close. A fighter working through his own body is a body, and a body has reach. Most practitioners are a threat about closing range and are written as though they were not. A Body Path fighter has to close until very high up, when his reach runs as far as there is matter to read. A Spirit Path fighter wants the reverse and survives by being right, and once you are inside his reach he is in more trouble than anyone else on the field.

Exhaustion. Below ten percent reserve a practitioner suffers Starvation (Charge Starvation in a Body fighter, Essence Starvation in a Spirit or a Fate one, whose reserves are both Essence): reduced stat expression, Shell degradation, Traits flickering. A defender who can afford to spend nothing wins a long engagement against an attacker who cannot afford to spend nothing, and sieges are decided by this more often than by any working. What starving does to him depends on his road, and so does the way to hurry it. Make a Body fighter spend. His Charge is raw Aether, and every share he spends for nothing burns in his Shell’s furnace and comes off him as heat, so make him swing at air, hold his coat through a long approach and hit things that do not matter. At a quarter he is steaming and cramping, and at a tenth the Shell cracks, the skin splitting in fine lines as it draws the Charge out of his own flesh. A Spirit worker run down goes hollow and cold and starts losing words and then names. A Fate fighter run down frays like the Spirit worker, since it is Essence he has run out of, and goes unlucky besides, which is worst for whoever is standing nearest. Stacking and Spending has the table.

Overfill him. A practitioner who absorbs banks the load in his own Crystal, and what bounds the bank is its rated Flux Density (how densely power packs into the Crystal) rather than his reserve. To half again over rating he holds it and bleeds. Past half again the lattice takes a permanent set and he is Overgrown. At two and a quarter times rating it bursts, and the Fracture is his, from power you handed him. Feeding an absorber is cheaper than out-hitting him. And the bank is short. It leaks at one minus his yield per second, three seconds for a poor Crystal and twenty for a fine one, so a defender who declines to be hit for ten seconds has taken it off him without touching him. The bleed is the tell, and it lasts exactly as long as the bank does.

Armor tiers. Padded takes bruising and not gore. Mail is immune to cuts, vulnerable to thrusts and blunt trauma, and vibrates under Essence shock in a way the wearer feels in the teeth. Brigandine hides its plates and the gaps are the openings. Proofed plate turns a bolt at range and turns most edges entirely, leaving joints, grapples and the close end of a room. Ceramic and shard are sacrificial and fail on the second hit in the same place. None of it covers the soul. A Spirit Path working goes past the Shell and past whatever is buckled over it, and the answer to that man is to get there, because a spear does not care what he means. The second answer is to ward the soul: a temple charm, a reliquary or a warded band, worn, the worked ones lifting Resilience Ward and Harmonics by a real margin for a little draw on the wearer, at five silver for a charm and two to thirty gold for a worked piece. The fair-stall kind lifts nothing. Know also what his stroke cannot do. It lands on souls only, a soul knocked out of true, a dread laid on a mind, a Core wounded, and it will not move a stone or break a jaw; a Spirit worker who wants matter moved has to do it through his own Shell, at his own Aether Class, like anyone. What does get through leaves a soul wound, and a second stroke on the same place sets it for life, so do not let him find the pale place twice (Soul Wounds and the Menders).

IV. Break the Man

The most reliable and the least discussed.

Check whether he paid for it. Stages can be bought with Drafts and given by blessings (The Ladder and the Draft has the price list). He has the ceiling, the door and the color, thin underneath, and none of the fighting that normally comes with them, and his Release, if it is not yet found and mastered, is not there to be feared. A veteran two rungs below him who has been hit before is a better bet than the ladder says, and the bookies at the better bout-rings know which heirs to lay against. Learn it by making him fight past his first plan.

Read him. Start with his road: an opponent who has identified a Path knows what the other man cannot do, and The Three Paths sets out how each beats each. Taught techniques are documented and counterable by anyone who studied. Self-derived techniques must be read live, in the two or three exchanges before they kill you. Every fighter whose Crystal acts before conscious thought has a pattern, because one road walked long enough produces one, and a pattern learned is a pattern interruptible.

Make him hold Pressure (the felt weight of his power on everyone near him) in. Suppression is a skill and it is tiring. A practitioner who cannot afford to be identified is paying continuously for the privilege, and a long approach through populated ground costs him more than the fight will. It cuts both ways: people hide Pressure and people fake it, and unless your Gnosis Perception outranks his Resilience Ward by Grade, what you think you are facing is an estimate said aloud.

Read the blessing, if you can. A blessing grants power out of a person’s natural reach, sits on the sheet as its own line, and can lie, because the giver wrote the line. A man who has built his plan on what his own window says about a gift has handed a stranger the wording. The answer almost nobody has is Hataraki, the Fate Art that can take a blessing back or remove it outright, with two Works or two Moto, which is one reason its holders are spoken of the way they are.

Rigidity. A Trait’s law is not negotiable by its bearer, and the more absolute it is the less he can contradict it. Present him with the situation his own law handles worst and he will handle it that way regardless, because he has no other way available. This is the cruelest counter in the register and it requires knowing the man rather than the technique.

The vow. A vow kept buys Coherence (the match between a practitioner’s stated law and his conduct) with conduct. A vow broken lands at once, by its own terms, and repeated denial of the truth behind it makes a Shear Break (a Trait turning on the bearer who denied it), so an opponent who knows the terms of a vow holds a weapon that does not require touching him. Maneuvering a sworn man into breaking his own oath is legal everywhere, universally despised, and has ended more careers than any duel.

Coherence drift. A practitioner whose lived behavior has drifted from his stated law is degrading before anyone lays a hand on him: output falls, Thresholds refuse to authorize, and his Traits begin to argue with him. Nobody has to cause this. Somebody has to notice it and wait.

Beating a Fate Fighter

His blade had not moved. Your strap gave, your foot found the one wet stone, and the blade was already there. Now look at his nose.

The four routes were written against people who push, and a Fate Path fighter (one whose Crystal leans on the Attraction Layer, the soul’s own share of the Plane of Fate) is mostly fought the way anyone is. On most days and in most fights his weapons are long Range, heavy Aura Pressure, presence and the people sworn to him, and every route above applies to all of those. What earns him a section of his own is the rare day he pulls: he reaches for what has to be so before anything can happen and moves what the blow depends on, so that the result arrives without having been forced. The strap wears through, the back foot lands wrong, and the cut is an ordinary cut.

Do not be frightened of it in the wrong way. This is the hardest thing in the register. Nobody reasons their way into it, since reasoning is made of the thing it would be used against, and I have corresponded for twenty years with a colleague who can describe a thread to the last decimal and has never lifted one. One lean on the odds can take a large share of a man’s reserve and leave him bleeding from the nose and eyes, and it often fails. He knows that, and it decides when he tries. If he tries, you have a charge to read: the eyes gone still, the breath held on a count that is not the count of the exchange, the air around him gone slightly too tidy. Between that and the wet stone is all the warning you get: a breath for a lean on the odds, several heartbeats for a prerequisite removed, longer for a field.

There are three answers, and they are routes I, II and III wearing his name.

Bring another Fate user. This is the boundary route. A second reader of the weights can see what he is leaning on and lean the other way, and the contest then runs soul against soul, Fate Sub-Stat against Fate Sub-Stat. It is the one case in this register where answering a practitioner with a practitioner counts as a decision, and the two of them will pay for it about equally. Mind that you can find one, since the road is hard enough that most armies have none to spare.

Be too sound, or too well bound, to move. This is the field route, and it is by far the cheapest. It moves only what already has a way of happening. A strap that is wearing can give and a strap that is not wearing will not, so the answer is a good strap, good footing and no bad habits, inspected that morning by somebody who is not you. Young Moto Naoki pronounced collapse on a sound gate at Tobishima in front of nine thousand people and the gate stood, and a Fate Art is no exception to this: his aunt’s stand folded because a carpenter owed money. The soul has its own version. Dominion’s Fate Sub-Stat pushes and the target’s Resilience Persistence and Continuity (the soul’s hold on going on being true) push back, and a soul bound well enough simply refuses the result. Nobody has ever written a song about a well-kept strap, and that is a pity, as it has outlasted several people who have one.

Reach him before the result arrives. This is the body route, and it is the one every Fate fighter fears. In plain force he takes the smallest bonus in the table, and outside his Domain, at the end of a straight exchange with a Body Path veteran, he is an ordinary body, one that may have spent half of itself on the last pull. So close. Keeping your distance is a present to him, because his force degrades least across it and his presence fills the volume you are standing in, and a long wary circling is the time he needs to set. The tell is the window, and reaching the puller inside it is the counter: three crossbowmen who loose a heartbeat late have only watched. Everything under Break the Body applies, the tub included. A Spirit Path fighter has the shortest way of doing it, because every pull is made by a soul, it shows on the Core before it lands, and a wounded soul loses its grip.

A true Fate Art is the same three answers at a worse price. Real Fate Arts are rare and mostly by bloodline, and their reputation as incredibly overpowered is the opinion of the survivors. Nobody grips Fate bare, and a Work of Hataraki is an inherited way of taking hold of one function, which is why that house can do on purpose what others do by accident. It still needs something to exploit, and it still costs its holder. You will not meet this in a tavern. On a field, turn to The Practitioner Problem, which says to find him first.

And then collect. Every pull he makes, landed or failed, leaves a knot, and the world collects it later, as an ordinary accident, on him or on what is his: within days for a lean, within the arc of the business for a removed prerequisite. A Work makes his hand surer and has never lowered the price or the knot. So a Fate fighter who pulled last week is a man waiting for a strap to go, and a patient enemy makes him pull often, counts the days, and offers battle in the week the bills fall due. Ask what he wears. A knot-cutter will take one knot’s debt onto itself and break, half of those sold are fakes, and a cracked ring on his hand tells you he has already used his. The Tell and the Knot has the law of it.

What Does Not Work

Matching him. Two practitioners at the same Grade resolve by technique, positioning and Sub-Stat (the finer stats beneath each main stat) architecture, and the exchange is a coin toss dressed as a plan. A commander who answers a practitioner with a practitioner has declined to make a decision.

Shielding. Nothing in the register stops a working by standing in front of it. Armor changes what the working does to a body. Wards condition what may be authorized in a space. Neither is a wall and both are written as one constantly.

Waiting for the cost. The reserve’s tells show inside a fight, and a fighter who can read them will spend to the last tenth anyway; the deep costs are rarely due until afterward. A plan that consists of surviving until he tires is a plan to be killed by a tired man.

The doctrine, stated once. A practitioner is beaten by taking away one of four things: the field, the sentence, the body, or the man. Pick before contact. Everything that goes wrong in the record went wrong because somebody tried to decide during.
