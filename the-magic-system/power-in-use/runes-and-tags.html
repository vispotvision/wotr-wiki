Runes and Tags

Runes and Tags

Nov 17, 202611 min read

Runes in the hand and on the field: runed gear, prepared tags, bonded weapons, and runes cut into living bodies. For the whole system in brief, see How Magic Works; for the cutting beside the other working trades, see The Working Trades.

“Check the seal, check the date, check who sold it to you, and then stand well back.” Painted over the door of the tag-sellers’ row at Skelby, since the events described below.

Nobody in Skelby calls it anything but the Long Noon. A back-alley tag seller known as Cheap Perrin sold a drover named Dob Lumley an unstamped flash tag for one silver, and Dob slapped it on the door of his brother-in-law’s privy for a laugh. It lit up white, as advertised, and then, because the seal had been cut by somebody’s nephew, it did not stop.

It burned for thirteen days and thirteen nights. There was noon over Tanner’s Row at midnight, hard and white and throwing shadows the wrong way down the street. The cocks crowed until they lost their voices, the bees came out at the second bell and worked till they dropped, the burglars of the quarter had a ruinous season, and pilgrims walked in from three valleys to pray at a privy. The lamp-wardens (the living craftsmen who keep a town’s runed lamps cut, paid by the street) demanded triple to walk the round, on the grounds that nobody could prove they had done anything. It took that long because the first Engraver refused, the second wanted the Guild’s fee in advance, and the third, Mistress Alys Thorne of the chartered cutters, finally went up to it with a chisel and a smoked glass and cut across the line. It cost her the sight of the left eye. Cheap Perrin sold the rest of his book the following week at double the price, as the man whose tags really work.

The door is in the Guild’s strongroom at Altherion and is still warm, and for a copper the porter will let you hold your hand near it. Every word below is what Dob Lumley ought to have known.

What it is

The cutting (a working written in Parun, the root script of law, into a substance so that it holds without its maker) is a skill anyone with a woken Crystal can level and a trade its guilds rank above every other, loudly, and it is what a fighter carries most. A cut sentence sets a limit on what a Wellspring is doing, at the layer of the soul the cutter’s working leaves through: a Body cutter’s ward stops the bottle, a Spirit cutter’s is taken for a wall, and a Fate cutter’s sets a term on the cutter’s own share of fate. The Glyph Index works one sentence through all three. Whichever it is, a cut reads its maker’s Essence Class, and even a hand on the bottom rung can do small stamped work. It reaches the field in four forms, from the common to the very rare.

FormWhat it isHow common
Runed gearA standing working cut into an everyday objectEverywhere in the cities, cheap
Prepared tagsA short working cut on paper, hide, bark or thin foil, carried and set off by touchCarried by most fighting practitioners

How it looks

Runed gear. Lamps that burn without oil, stove plates, door wards in a clerk’s house, a knife that holds its edge, a coat that sheds rain, a lock that knows its key. The cuts are fine and shallow, stamped with a cutter’s mark and an excise seal, and often repaired, worse each time. All of it was cut by a living hand and is kept up by one: lamp-wardens walk their paid rounds re-cutting worn lamps, ward-cutters charge by the doorframe, and when either of them goes on strike or decides your street owes them money, your street finds out what the dark looks like. The great pieces are another matter: a gate-array, a sword that has killed in nine wars, a ward under a fortress. They are all old, relics of the Great Spirit War and the days of the Physical Epochs that people still kill for, several can no longer be fully read, and nobody living can make another, because what was lost was the condition the old cutters worked in, and no amount of skill brings that back.

How a piece is rated. Everything cut carries two ratings: a Grade for power, on the fighters’ letters, and a Rarity for scarcity, Common up through Mythic and Unique. Beside both sits what the stuff itself is, because a material holds a working by its virtue: stone remembers, salt fixes, gold will not corrupt, and quicksilver will not keep a shape. Which workings a piece holds well, and how it fails when it fails, follow from that, which is why a ward is cut into a wall and a promise sealed in gold. A cutter’s guild title caps the Grade she may lawfully cut, and a merchant’s caps the Rarity he may sell. The Accord’s old stamps (Marked, Proofed, Tempered and so on up) still go on the steel beside the maker’s mark. Gear stays what it was made; only a bonded weapon grows. An Appraisal skill and an assay instrument read a piece, and a good forger beats a weak appraiser every market day of the year.

Prepared tags. A strip a finger long, cut with a short sentence and its seal: a flash, a smoke, a binding, a ward on a doorway, a spark to light a torch. The user presses it to a surface or slaps it onto a body and pushes their own power into it with the thumb, whichever kind their road holds: a Spirit or a Fate hand pushes Essence (spiritual energy), and a Body fighter fires it with his own Charge, raw Aether out of the Shell, which the strip takes just as readily. It flares in the user’s color, makes its own small sound, and works. Fighters carry them in belt-books, sleeve-cuffs and hat-bands. Every tag runs on one of three fuels, and the fuel is written on the strip for anyone who can read it:

FuelHow it worksIts weakness
AmbientDrinks the Aether (mana, the world’s medium of power) of the place it is set. Cheap and quiet to carryDies on thin, salted or bled ground (Aether-poor by nature, worked against pooling, or pulled empty), beside a Core (a Wellspring site, in common speech) whose keepers have closed it, or in Silence (a region where magic cannot be worked)
The user’s reserveTakes its force from the one who sets it off. The stronger the user, the stronger the tagCosts the user, mid-fight
The maker’s chargeCarries power the maker stored in it, Essence from most cutters and Charge from a Body-led one. Works anywhere short of a Silence, at the maker’s strengthDear, and goes stale, as an enhanced bolt does

Only practitioners can use a tag. The working waits for a woken Crystal (the Soul Crystal, the soul itself, opened and taught) to close it, and a sealed Crystal cannot: the Authorization is read at the firing, off the soul behind the thumb, and a sealed one has no harmonization to show. That is why the commoner’s magic is the bottle and not the strip. Licensed cutters, Engravers under a hall’s mark, make them, and a black market sells unstamped ones beside every hall.

Bonded weapons. A practitioner who carries one weapon through years of hard fighting leaves their own power in it the way a hand leaves a shine on a hilt: Essence from a Spirit or a Fate hand, and from a Body fighter his own Charge, which is how most bonded steel in the world was bonded, since it is Body fighters who carry one weapon for thirty years. In time the weapon answers them: it takes their color when they reinforce, and when they release (let their Trait loose in its learned form), it carries part of the release. A blade may burn with its owner’s heat, a staff hold the edge of their field. The weapon grows as its owner grows, the one kind of gear that does, and its part of the release is written on the owner’s card.

Body runes. At the far end of the skill, a cutter writes into a living Aether Shell rather than stone. The lines show as pale scar-tracks, or as nothing at all until the working runs, when they light in the bearer’s color under the skin. A body rune can do what nothing else can: a ward that is part of the person, a working that never needs carrying.

Time and the cut. A tag takes a cutter an hour or a day; a ward on a wall, a season. A bad cut misfires every time it is used, and a bad seal makes a working that does not stop: the most dangerous thing on any black-market tag. A strip missing its seal altogether does nothing at all, and it is the seal cut wrong that lights Tanner’s Row for thirteen days. Duration comes from three places: the seal sets the term, the paper or the stone caps it, and the anchoring laid down as the sentence closes holds it. A working left unsealed has nothing holding it and nothing stopping it, so it drinks the Aether of the place until the place runs out or someone cuts across the line.

Bonding. Years of the same weapon in the hand, and the loss of part of a release if it is broken or taken.

Body runes. Months of cutting into a living person, pain the whole way, and no undoing. An error misfires on the bearer continuously, for as long as they live, and a faulty seal is a working that runs inside them until it kills them or is cut out.

How it is beaten

  • Damage the substrate. A tag is paper or foil: tear it, wet it, burn it, smear the ink. A ward is beaten by chiseling it, or by bringing down the wall it sits on.
  • Starve the ambient tag. Fight on thin, salted or bled ground, beside a closed Core, or in a Silence, and every ambient tag on the belt is a strip of paper.
  • Make them pay. A reserve tag costs its user reserve, Charge or Essence as the road has it. A fighter who sets off three of them has spent what he needed for his coat.
  • Strike the beat. Pressing a tag takes a hand and a moment. Pin the hand.
  • Check the date. Charged tags go stale. A raider with an old book is carrying blanks and does not know which.
  • Take the bonded weapon. Disarm the owner and the weapon’s part of the release goes with it.
  • Cut across the line. A body rune is a sentence in the skin, and a deep cut across it breaks the sentence. It is a brutal answer, and sometimes the only one.

Example

Elsie Marrow, a Stage II hired hunter, goes into a flooded mill after a Well-spawn (a creature born out of a Well) with a belt-book of six tags. Two are ambient flash tags at 2 silver each; two are reserve binding tags at 5 silver; two are charged ward tags, 1 gold apiece, cut by a Journeyman she trusts.

The mill’s ground was bled a decade ago, when a sect ran its novices there for a season, and the Aether in the wet stone is thin: her first flash tag sputters and dies on the wall. She knew it might. She slaps a reserve binding on the spawn’s foreleg, it takes a long breath of her own reserve and flares a dull, smoky orange, and the leg locks. The second binding she cannot afford; her reserve is near half and she still needs her coat. She sets the two charged wards on the doorway behind her instead, and they run at her Journeyman’s strength, not hers, which is the whole reason she paid a gold mark for each. The spawn hits the ward and stops.
