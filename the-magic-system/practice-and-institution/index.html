Practice and Institution

Practice and Institution

Nov 17, 20262 min read

Who may practice, where their capacity came from, what their guild will trust them with, what money can and cannot buy them, how they are trained, and what the practice is set against.

Any stallholder in any market will sing you the school song she hates most, badly, with the words changed, and then charge you for the noodles. Practitioners are common and everybody has opinions. These pages are the machinery behind the opinions, set down by the archivists who keep having to explain them.

  • The Gate: Who May Learn Magic Why nearly everyone carries a Crystal, why about one in ten is taught to wake it, who holds the door on the rest of the lesson, what a purse does once you are through it, and why a Magister and a Natural cannot agree about the girl who burned the Bureau’s tent.
  • The Standing and the Title: Guild Titles as Trust The nine titles every guild shares, what each one lets a stranger hand you, and the two other names you carry.
  • Training and Sects Schools, disciple ranks, seclusion, the tournament ladder, and what a sect teaches that money cannot buy.
  • Blessings and Curses What a thumb grants, who can press one on you, how the line on your window lies, and who can take it off again.
  • Vows and Oaths Small Fate, anyone’s to make: what the window shows, what lands when you break one, what your own denial does after, and what you risk when a court hands you the book.
  • Who Pays for the Lamp: The Living Crafts The lamp-wardens, ward-cutters, hedge-workers and the keepers of cleared dungeons, who pays them, and who can refuse.
  • On Walking Into the Current — Wellspring Encounter and Recovery What happens to a body that meets a Wellspring without meaning to.
  • Mechanica: Extraction Without Recognition The practice everything above defines itself against.
