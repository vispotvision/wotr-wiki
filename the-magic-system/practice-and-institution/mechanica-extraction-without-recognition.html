Mechanica: Extraction Without Recognition

Mechanica: Extraction Without Recognition

Nov 28, 202611 min read

Every other practice in the register asks. This one takes, and the difference is structural before anyone gets to the morals, and the Continuum (the whole lawful order of reality) records it in the ground.

January Street got its name honestly. For twenty-seven winters the roses along its gutters bloomed through the snow, fat and red and smelling of a June nobody else was having, and the whole tenement row sat with its windows open while the rest of the city froze, and strangers came from three towns to eat their dinner on its doorsteps. Then an Enforcement party in Division blue came down the cellar steps of the Ockley bakery with a warrant and two assay instruments, and found what the street had been keeping warm on: a thing of oak and copper the size of a mill wheel, going round and round in the dark, with one pipe of rose-colored light running out of the floor. The crowd was on them inside the hour, a thousand people with bakers’ peels and fire-irons. The serjeant lost an ear, and he stopped the wheel anyway. Every rose on January Street went black by morning, thirty-eight people froze before the thaw, and the old millwright who built it, Jonas Ockley, was found three towns away mending a sluice and could not see what the fuss was about. Walk there today. Nothing grows, your lamp burns the wrong way, and the roses’ smell comes up through the cobbles every January for no reason any Measurewright has been able to give.

The street never paid a copper for its roses. This page is about who did.

I. What It Actually Is

A lawful working imposes a boundary on a law that is already running, and the law does the work. The practitioner supplies the condition and pays for the privilege of setting it. Mechanica skips the condition.

An operator does not invoke a Wellspring (a governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether, in the scholars’ account one of sixty kinds of Aether with its own law), because invoking one requires Authorization (the glyph role naming a lawful channel) and Authorization is precisely what they do not have. They take finished output from a system that already did the conditioning, and route it. The law was conditioned by somebody else, or by a site, or by a thing that was alive at the time.

Which means Mechanica always has a donor. There is no such thing as an operator working alone, and every apparatus in the record has something on the other end of it.

II. The Three Practices

Extraction. Output taken from a Soul Crystal (the soul itself, which stores Essence and channels it outward) that is not the operator’s. The donor may be captive, purchased, dead, or unaware. This is the oldest form and the one the Statutes were written against, and its products are the most efficient in the register because a Crystal has already done the expensive part. It is also the plainest crime on the page. Essence is a soul’s own substance and the Core is the center of its identity, so what comes down the pipe is the donor, by the spoonful, and a donor who survives it is less of a person than went in.

Soul Splicing, Trait Collapse Engines, and the whole vocabulary the Accord uses in its prohibition schedules describe extraction and nothing else, which is why the schedules read as hysterical to anyone who has only encountered the second and third practices.

The butcher’s line. A monster’s core is in a way its Soul Crystal, and an alchemist who renders one into Essence Drafts is doing lawful work under the Standing Index, the Crystal Draw (draw being the bench’s old word for a lot taken off its source) every hunter’s hall lives on. A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner is the same procedure on a different table, the great crime, and it is why Grade IV on the Index has never balanced: every year more of it is sold than the lawful sources can yield, and the coil reads the two Drafts the same. The operator will tell you the donor was already dead. The law has never found that interesting.

Tapping. Output taken from a Wellspring site without the site’s participation. A lawful practitioner at a concentration harmonizes, which is a negotiation the current can decline. A tap does not ask. It opens a path from the vein into an apparatus and holds it open, and the site is pulled from without reciprocity until it stops giving. Nearly every tap on record sits on Wild ground, where there is heat and light to carry off. A Spirit-type site has no fire to give a pipe, and the few apparatus ever built to drain one were not built for anybody’s stair.

This is what produces a site running under Obsession Force (recognition curdled into taking). Priests call the corruption spiritual, and I will let them; a Measurewright calls it the physical consequence of sustained withdrawal against a current that was never consulted, and the site’s subsequent behavior toward visitors is the behavior of a system that has learned what people are for. Dungeons are alive and learning, whether or not they think, and scholars have stopped arguing about whether this one was taught.

Engine work. Built apparatus that performs extraction or tapping without a practitioner present. The operator’s skill is entirely in the construction, the device runs unattended, and this is the form that scales.

Engine work is why Mechanica is an economic problem as much as a criminal one. Extraction requires a monster. Tapping requires a monster with a workshop. An engine requires a competent millwright, a license he does not have, and a district that cannot afford a warden’s fee.

The Enforcement Division’s own figures, which it does not publish in this form, show that the overwhelming majority of Mechanica in the Concord is somebody’s heating.

III. Why It Leaves a Scar

The Four Glyph Roles are Authorization, Boundary, Direction and Sealing, and they are a sequence rather than a checklist. A working that never established Authorization cannot execute a lawful Sealing, because the Seal closes an operation against the warrant that opened it and there is no warrant to close against.

So a Mechanica working never ends. It is interrupted, abandoned, or exhausted, and the boundary it opened stays open.

Mechanica debt is the Continuum’s record of that unclosed operation. Nobody is punishing anybody and nothing is watching. An unsealed boundary is a persistent local condition, the field around it never returns to its prior state, and every subsequent working in that area is conditioned by a boundary nobody set deliberately and nobody can find.

Statute XXVI, Mechanica Irreversibility. The one damage in the register with no documented remediation. Concordia (the art of aligning many fields into one) can close a Hollowing (a region losing its capacity for lawful working) over years. Nothing closes an unsealed boundary, because closing requires a warrant and the whole point was that there was never one.

IV. What It Costs the Operator

Nothing, directly, and that is the entire problem.

Every lawful practice bills the practitioner. The speaking (a working cast live from the body) bills the body in the same scene. The cutting (a working inscribed in material) bills a season and a stone. The pouring (a working bottled as a Draft) bills provenance. Votia (working bought with a vow) bills the rest of a life. Mechanica bills the donor, the site, or the district, and the operator walks away with output they did not pay for.

This is the honest reason the Accord’s condemnation carries so little weight with the people who do it. A doctrine that says power must cost is being preached by the institution that sets the prices, and an operator heating a tenement off a tapped vein has a reply to that which nobody in the Division has ever answered in public.

The delayed bill. Operators are merely billed late. Sustained work in an unsealed field produces a documented syndrome: Shell clouding (the soul’s outer layer losing clarity) without corresponding output, Coherence (how closely a person’s stated Path matches how they live) falling with no change in conduct, and Traits that begin flickering in the absence of Essence Starvation (running below a tenth of reserve).

The Crystal is being conditioned by boundaries it did not set. The operator’s own architecture starts obeying the mess they are standing in. Renderers can identify a long-term engine man on the table by the state of the Shell alone, and several of them will not say so on a record.

V. Reading a Site That Has Been Worked

Residue that does not disperse. Ordinary Aetheric Residue thins on a schedule set by local density, toward a floor it never passes. Mechanica residue holds undiminished, because the operation that produced it never closed and is in some sense still producing it.

The pull that does not answer. A lawful practitioner at a tapped site finds Harmonics (the stat that governs pulling Aether from the environment) costs elevated for no visible reason. They are working against a gradient somebody else established and left running.

The wrong quiet. Dead ground would at least be honest. A worked site reads as ordinary ground with one thing missing, and most surveyors identify the absence about a minute after they should have.

Instruments disagree. The coil (the assay instrument a practitioner stands inside) reads a field; the assay fork reads a density; the two do not reconcile, and the discrepancy is the size of what is being taken. This is the only reliable detection method and it requires two instruments and someone who can be bothered to compare them.

VI. Enforcement, and Why It Fails

The Enforcement Division is real, funded, and armed, and it prosecutes extraction with genuine ferocity. It prosecutes tapping when the site belongs to somebody with standing. It does not meaningfully prosecute engine work, and corruption has little to do with the reasons.

An engine is a device in a cellar. Finding it requires the two-instrument comparison, performed on the right street, by a searcher who has a reason to be there. Removing it turns off a district’s heat in a month with a name. And the prosecution requires proving who built it, in a trade where the millwright is three towns away and the householder genuinely does not know what is under the floor.

The Division’s internal position, stated once in a report that was not circulated: enforcement against engine work is enforcement against poverty, performed with a warrant, by men who can see that perfectly well.

VII. The Part the Accord Does Not Discuss

The Concord’s chartered Cores, each of them a dungeon somebody cleared and now holds, are licensed, sealed and lawful, and their Authorization is held institutionally rather than personally. A householder warming her stair off a warden who fills at a chartered Core has never warranted anything in her life.

The Accord’s position is that the company holds the warrant and the work runs under it, which is coherent, and which is also the same structure as engine work with a license on top.

The distinction the Accord draws is that the chartered sites were harmonized with rather than tapped, at the founding, by practitioners named in the founding instruments. Several of those instruments are four hundred years old. Two are known to be forgeries. The Division that would investigate the third has been reorganized twice since the question was last asked in writing.

Nobody involved thinks they are damned. The householder is warm, and the neighbors call it “the warm” when they are cold and stolen work when they are angry. The millwright was paid. The Division man knows what turning it off does. All three have a reasonable position, and the ground goes on carrying the argument.
