From Omnara to a Blow

From Omnara to a Blow

Nov 17, 202623 min read

Account for this fist. Begin at the beginning.

The Guild of Measurewrights, second seal examination, the only question on the paper

On a hot blue Thursday in the eel season, at the crown of Ambrel Bridge, with the market in full cry and the river running gold under the arches, a toll collector named Fitch told an eel-seller named Tam Hobday that her barrow now counted as a wagon. Tam was fifty-one, wide as a door, in scarlet stockings and a leather apron, and she had been pushing that barrow over that bridge since before Fitch’s father met his mother. She set the handles down. She breathed in through her nose until the apron creaked, and an ember-orange coat came up off her shoulders, steady as a banked forge, and every eel in the barrow turned to face the other way. It ran down her right arm and stayed there. “Toll This!” said Tam Hobday, and she hit him on the point of the jaw. He left through the back wall of his painted booth with a good part of the booth, in a spray of green planks and copper pennies and three of his teeth, and he went into the river forty feet down with his hair on fire. The bridge cheered until the pigeons left.

An examiner of the Guild happened to be eating a pie at the next stall. He went back up to the hall that afternoon, wiped the board, and wrote the question at the head of this page, and it has been the whole of the second seal paper ever since. Candidates get three hours. Most of them start with the fist and work up, and they run out of time somewhere around the hips.

What follows is the model answer, with the order kept and my own remarks let in. It walks the whole chain from the first thing there was down to Fitch’s jaw, one step at a time, for anyone who has never opened these shelves. Where the world’s own word has a plainer cousin in foreign books I give both. Every step ends at the page that goes deeper, and the table at the foot has the lot on one sheet.

1. Omnara

Before anything there was everything, with no parts in it. Omnara is the old archivists’ name for that state, the Apeiron, the undivided source. It had no sequence or weight or awareness, because those are parts. Every law, every god and every soul that would ever be was in there as potential, with nothing yet to tell them apart.

For Tam: her fist has a knuckle end and a wrist end, and it happens before his jaw breaks. None of that was possible yet.

Go deeper: Omnara and the Division.

2. The Division and the three who rose

Omnara split, and no surviving record says why. Three currents came out of it, and three planes rose from the currents, and from each plane rose a being the registers call an Epoch. The nearest foreign words are primordial and god, and both undersell it.

PlaneWhat it isWho rose from it
The Physical PlanePhysical reality under Aegor: matter, body and the world’s settled habits, every event and mountain rangeAegor, the Stone-Father
The Soul PlaneSpiritual Reality: souls, the dead, what a thing meansAshura, the Breath Between Worlds
The Plane of FateEvery possibility, idea and concept, what has to be so before anything can happen: the law and construction of the worldBara, also written Bairos, the Weaver Eternal

The first two are the lower realities, and a person lives in both at once. Fate stands above them and is no reality at all. “Plane” is a courtesy, like calling a very old and dangerous man “sir”.

For Tam: her arm is on the Physical Plane, the woman deciding to use it is on the Soul Plane, and the fact that a blow can land at all is up above both of them.

Go deeper: The Three Epochs.

3. The Veil

Where one plane’s law meets another’s there has to be a border, and that border is the Veil. Think of a wineskin with a pleat stitched down the middle. The skin wraps both lower realities, and every realm anybody has ever named is somewhere inside it. The pleat, the inner fold, runs between Physical and Soul, thick in some places and thin in others, and it is what a crossing crosses. The skin’s outer face is where the lower realities meet Fate, so a thread pulled down from Fate comes in through there.

For Tam: the fold is close enough that one woman can be on both sides of it without noticing, which is the ordinary condition of being alive.

Go deeper: The Veil, which has the map of the realms.

4. The Works and the Archons

Inside the Plane of Fate, before there was a before, Bara wrote the seven Works: the first grammar, the bare functions by which anything ends, stands, passes, unfolds, is known, is balanced or is whole. Out of the tension among the three Epochs came the fourteen Archons, and an Archon is a Work crowned, a law with somebody on the throne. The closest foreign phrase is concept given a god: Order, Chaos, Form, Time, Memory, Death and the rest. A Hollow Throne is the same law with nobody home, and it runs on regardless.

Most if not all of the Archons are Sovereign on all three planes. Bara stands above them, so they are the most powerful things that still act up there, which is a smaller claim than the shrines make of it.

For Tam: a jaw can break because Ruin is a function, and the jaw stays broken in the right order because Time has an owner.

Go deeper: The Fourteen Archons, and Hataraki for the Works themselves.

5. The Titans

Titans descend from Archons. An Archon is a principle enthroned, and a Titan is the same principle bearing weight, come down out of the throne room and into the load, with an Epoch to forge it a back. There are fourteen. Atla’zon holds weight and the burden of order, which is which way is down, Gaia holds what things are made of, Valmir holds permission to end. Foreign books say world-bearer or primordial titan, and for once the foreign books are close.

A Titan has no Stage and no ladder and walks no Path, and it does not hear you. Its near opposite is a World Spirit, a soul whose claim on the world has grown into a place, and that started out as somebody.

All of this makes a tidy order of authority, and the Guild asks for it on the paper. Fate and the Works stand over everything. Under them run Titanic Law, Archonic Law and Wellspring Law, which are three domains of authority and not three ranks. Everything anyone has ever done with power takes its permission from one of the three, and all three rest on Fate.

For Tam: Prometheus, who holds destruction as transformation, has never heard of her, and she is about to use him.

Go deeper: The Fourteen Titans.

6. The Sixty Wellsprings

Titans are the Wellsprings themselves, and the Sixty are their manifestations: each is one way a Titan’s law shows up, small enough for a soul to live with. A burn, a lamp and a hot wind are all ways one fire shows up, and Prometheus shows up six ways on the register. Foreign books say affinity or element, and a Wellspring is bigger than either. It is a governing idea that changes how a soul uses its power, a lens laid over magic that was already potent.

Each has a kind, which names its home: Wild (thirty-three) to Aegor, Spirit (fifteen) to Ashura, Fate (six) to Bara, Medium (six) to the Veil, at home on all three. A kind is a home and never a lock. Places where one pools are Wellspring sites, or Cores in common speech, and people kill for them.

For Tam: she has lived for thirty years with Pyreveil, the Fire of Renewal, Wild, deep ember-orange. Whatever it touches it burns down so that something can stand up in its place. She came to it after the first barrow burned.

Go deeper: The Sixty Wellsprings.

7. Aether

The Division left offcuts. Aether is the world’s own substance, the part of Omnara nobody used, and every foreign book in the world calls it mana. Because it was never divided it still holds every possibility and has no nature of its own, so it will carry any idea: the same medium burns in one valley and grieves in the next. That is how a Wellspring reaches anybody.

Only a body can pull it in. The medium is endless, and any one stretch of ground’s supply is not, so where you stand matters. Fighters say fat ground and lean ground, and will walk you half a league to get onto the one they like.

For Tam: a stone bridge over a warm river at midday, with a market’s worth of lamps and frying pans on it, is decent ground for a fire-lens. She has been pulling from it for thirty years and it has never asked for a toll.

Go deeper: the end of Omnara and the Division, and Two Sets of Books.

8. The soul

All three Epochs had a hand in a soul, so it has three layers. The Soul Crystal is the soul, and the scholars mean that literally.

LayerGiven byWhere it sitsWhat it does
Aether ShellAegor’s groundThe Physical WorldKnows and works matter, force and heat
Essence CoreAshura’s breathThe Soul PlaneHolds identity, memory and Traits, and authors everything the soul does
Attraction LayerBara’s threadReaching toward FateA soul’s own share of what could be

Everyone has all three, the people who never wake included, and so do beasts, though a beast’s Shell stays sealed until evolution or a Level threshold cracks it. This Core has no relation to a Core on a map, whatever they tell you in the taverns. About one person in ten wakes their Crystal, nearly always as a child, and the rest live out their lives with a little sheet and a sleeping soul.

For Tam: she woke at eleven, gutting eels, and put the knife through the bench.

Go deeper: The Three Epochs and How Magic Works.

9. Essence, and the Charge

Here the chain forks for the first time, over what a soul does with Aether once it is in. Older model answers sent everybody down one road, and the Guild now fails the candidate who does.

Pull Aether into a woken Crystal and refine it, and it takes the stamp of whoever refined it. From then on it is Essence, which foreign books call spiritual energy. It is the soul’s stamped blood, a little more of you than there was, and it is held in the Core. That is the Spirit road, and spending Essence is spending yourself: below a tenth the Core itself begins to fray, names and memory first.

Pull Aether in and leave it raw, packed in the Shell as it came, and it is the Charge. That is the Body road. The Charge has nobody’s stamp on it, so spending it is spending the world, and what the spender pays is the body’s price for handling it: heat, torn fiber, recoil. Below a tenth the Shell starts taking its Charge out of the flesh.

The third road stores nothing. A Fate worker pulls a thread down for the one working and pays from the soul’s own share, and step 13 has the bill.

Whichever fills it, the store you hold is your reserve, one figure on the card, and it is measured, spent and watched like money. A soul on two roads holds a share of each, and the two never trade back. The unwoken hold a trickle of Essence they cannot spend and no Charge at all, since the Shell is sealed. A fight Draft follows the road of whoever drinks it: Core stock arrives unstamped and the crash that follows is the stamping, and a hitter’s Salt goes down like a swallowed burn and leaves cramps and fever.

For Tam: she refines next to nothing. The coat is Ambrel Bridge’s own Aether, packed raw and burning in Pyreveil’s shape. What anyone who has stood near her in a temper would know blind is the way she wears it.

Go deeper: The Essence Ledger.

10. The climb

A soul grows by being melted down and rebuilt. That is tempering, and each rebuilding toward the next of the sixteen Stages (a cultivation realm, in foreign books) needs its Catalyst, a crisis or a long seclusion or a bought Draft. A lived breakthrough passes the Great Work in the soul’s own Path: it is broken down, purified, woken and fixed, the nigredo, albedo, citrinitas and rubedo of the benches, and a Body soul goes through all four in the flesh. A person tempers on a Wellspring, and that lens shapes what they spend, reaching double its effect after years of mastery at home. The lens tempered on longest sets the color of their aura. Level is separate, and moves by danger overcome. Along the way a soul lays down Traits, standing laws formed by what it survived.

The harmonization that ties a soul to a lens happens in three places, said once. The lock happens at the Shell, where Aether enters. The tie is held on the Layer. The lens shapes what the lead layer works: raw Aether in the Shell for a Body fighter, Essence in the Core for a Spirit fighter, the claim on the Layer for a Fate fighter.

For Tam: she is at Stage VI, Glory, reached the hard way across two floods and a fire. At Glory the soul first offers a named class, and hers offered her Barrow Queen, and she told it what it could do with that and then took it.

Go deeper: The Sixteen Stages, and The Ladder and the Draft.

11. The Path

Every soul has all three layers, and a Path is the one it has learned to lean on, hardened by repetition until it stops being a choice. There are three roads, and each ends under the Epoch who gave that layer.

PathLeans onSpendsWorks onForeign books sayFar end
BodyAether ShellThe Charge: raw AetherMatter, force, heat, the blowPhysical enhancement, then matter manipulationMatla
SpiritEssence CoreEssenceSouls, the dead, what a thing meansSoul manipulation, conceptual attackNull-Light
FateAttraction LayerThread, pulled and never heldOdds, prerequisites, bonds, oathsFate and probability manipulationHataraki

Fighters call the three styles Aether Manipulation, Essence Manipulation and Thread Manipulation, and for once the common names are exact. No road is the better one, and each has a clear loss against the others. Each layer also has its own ladder, read from Glory up: Aether Class, Essence Class and Thread Class. A ladder rung sets the yield (η), how much of each spend reaches the world, as a distillation has its yield. Stage sets how high a ladder can go and practice moves you up it, except for Thread, which goes by grip and decades and leaves most souls at nothing for life.

For Tam: Body, Mastered (her one road, walked as far as her Stage goes), because she has never in her life wanted anything to happen from a distance. Aether I, Muridic, the first rung: she commands matter as an object, it takes a good deal of noise to do it, and something over a third of what she spends never reaches the fist.

12. The blow

Now we can hit him. Here is that half second, in order.

  1. She draws in. The breath is the visible charge, and anyone can read it. Aether comes up out of the bridge and the warm air into her body.
  2. It is packed. Hers is the Body road, so nothing is refined. The Aether goes into the Shell raw, as the Charge, and Pyreveil lays its idea over it right there. Her Core decides and her Shell does the work.
  3. It goes out through the Shell, the layer she leans on and the only door into matter anyone has, and shows as a coat of her own aura, the skin of power called reinforcement. She gathers the whole coat into her right arm, so one place goes up a Grade (a letter on the F to EX scale that stats are read in) and all the rest of her drops to bare skin. Fitch could have killed her with a bread knife in that moment, had it occurred to him.
  4. The call. “Toll This!” is a technique name, for her purposes, and shouting it buys a surge for the beat it takes. It is also the loudest tell in the Quarter.
  5. The body does its work. The left heel plants on the crown of the bridge, her hips turn, the shoulder comes through last, and all that weight goes along a straight line into the point of the jaw. On the Body Path the full force bonus lands at contact, which is why she had to walk up to him.
  6. Both things hit. The fist breaks the jawbone at the hinge, and that half is plain bone and plain leverage, the same as any drunk in any tavern. The other half is the lens. Pyreveil’s idea is the fire that burns a thing down so that something can stand up in its place, and thirty years of it have made her Shell a furnace that thinks that way. On this road the idea lives in the tissue, so the Charge goes in with the knuckles as burning, and the world gives way exactly as far as her soul has truly been changed, and not a hair further. Once the fire is in him the world’s old habits take over, because fire set in hair goes on eating hair after she has stopped thinking about it. That is why his hair was on fire, and why it was still on fire in the river for about as long as his hair lasted. A glyph, had she known one, would have set a boundary on that law and told it where to stop. She has never known one.
  7. The furnace. The third or more that never reached the fist was the Shell burning, because the Shell is a furnace that never goes cold, and it came off her as glare and roar and heat, as a Shell’s burning always does. Every eel turned away for the same reason. A hitter burns loud and hot, and mastery burns quiet. The Measurewrights book that third as waste heat, and on their ledger it is.
  8. The price. Some of her reserve is gone, the knuckles are split, and the breath is short. She has spent the bridge and not herself, and the bill is the body’s: a hitter who goes on like this runs red, wet and steaming long before she runs strange. Her window, which is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner and nobody else, has it all down:
[TOLL THIS: landed, jaw.]
[Reserve: 71% ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░]
[Hit: right knuckles split. Vitality 97%]

That is the whole of the machinery. Every other blow in these shelves is this one with different numbers, and One Exchange, Annotated takes a real bout through it line by line.

A fighter who keeps going climbs further: a Release is a full transformation of what the lead layer does, and a Domain (a reality marble, in foreign books) is a claim made on the Attraction Layer that makes a stretch of the world run on the fighter’s terms. Tam has a Release she does not know about, and the river towns would prefer that it stayed that way.

13. The same fist on the other two roads

The examiner gives a bonus mark for this. Everything from Omnara to step 8 is the same for all three roads, they part at step 9 over what is done with the Aether, and here is where the parting shows.

RoadThe routeThe lost share
BodyAether drawn in, packed raw in the Shell as the Charge, shaped there by the lens, driven out through the ShellHeat, the Shell’s furnace burning
SpiritAether drawn in, refined in the Crystal into Essence, stamped, spent through the CoreA hollowing in the Core, and never heat
FateA thread pulled down through the Attraction Layer, paid from the soul’s own shareA knot, which the world collects later

On the Spirit Path she does not need to walk up. The working is authored in the Core and spent there, in Essence, reads Essence Class, and carries the author’s say on what a thing is. A Spirit master at Stage VI standing at the far end of the bridge could write across the booth until the whole structure forgot what a toll was for, or throw the working straight at Fitch, and his jaw would be perfectly fine and he would be unable to remember why he was standing there. What that master could not do from the far end is put him through the wall. Thrown Spirit force lands on souls and marks no matter, and anyone who wants a booth moved moves it through their own Shell, at whatever their Aether Class will bear. The room would show almost nothing, and the master would not even be warm: the lost share is paid in the Core, in a color gone flat and a word that will not come. No armor covers a soul, and a hitter who reaches that master ends the discussion.

On the Fate Path nobody hits anyone. Fitch’s stool has a worn leg, the booth has an old nail, and the wind is off the river. A true pull moves what the thing depends on, comes down through the Veil’s outer face as Thread, and takes Thread Class on top of everything. Low down it is one lean on the odds, which may cost half the reserve and leaves the puller bleeding from the nose across the street, and it often fails. Landed or failed it leaves a knot, and the world collects within days: the puller’s own stool, or a friend’s. It also moves only what already has a way of happening, so a well-kept booth with a sound stool stands.

Go deeper: the routes and the round of who beats whom on The Three Paths, and The Tell and the Knot for what a pull leaves behind.

14. The two books

The Guild records every working twice. The physical account says what happened to matter in the room, and a clerk with no soul to speak of can take it with a fork and a water clock. The stratal account says which Wellspring’s idea governed the soul, what the Aether was doing, and what the Crystal paid. Both have to close.

Only the Shell’s price is heat, because only the Shell is a furnace. Loss through the Core or the Layer is paid in that layer, and the blood and the shaking are the report of that payment. A nosebleed is a receipt and not a leak, and that goes on the paper too.

For Tam: Fitch, the booth, the pennies and the river are the physical book. The reserve, the lens and the thirty years are the other one. Her lost third went through the Shell, so it is in the physical book as heat, and the eels will vouch for it. They balance.

Go deeper: Two Sets of Books.

15. And back again

What three hands put together goes back three ways. When a Crystal comes apart, the Core goes to Echo, which leaves for the Soul Plane within hours. The Shell goes to residue, which takes nine days to leave through the body and repays the Wellspring. The Layer goes to Name, filed in the Archive and never released. A person’s Path sets which of the three is heaviest: Body dead leave their Charge in the residue and are what the renderers race for, Spirit dead haunt, and Fate dead leave vows still binding. The unlensed feed the ground they lie in. Pattern may come round again in a bloodline, and a person never does. Every death stays permanent.

For Tam: she died in her bed at eighty-three with her boots on, against medical advice. The family sat with her until the room changed, as every family does, then took it in shifts with two hired spears and a very large dog for the nine days, because a Body master’s corpse is worth more than the street it lies on, and she had told them to. What she owed went back to Pyreveil, which is one way Prometheus shows up, whose law came down from a throne the registers still argue over, which is a Work that Bara wrote in the plane that rose from what Omnara stopped being. Fitch’s successor charges barrows at the handcart rate. There is a small painted sign to that effect at the crown of Ambrel Bridge, in orange, and a burnt place on the coping where nobody has ever got round to replacing the stone.
