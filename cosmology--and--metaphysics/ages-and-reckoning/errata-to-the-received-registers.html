The Conversion of Superseded Dates, with a Ruling on the Epochs

Over the gate of Hollingarth, in the northern quarter, the Thorsby family had cut THIRTY THOUSAND YEARS AND NEVER ONCE ASKED in letters of gilded oak as tall as a boy, and in the summer the Concordance was sealed Lord Piers Thorsby rode to the Guild’s survey camp with sixty lances in blue and butter-yellow to have the sum corrected in his favor. He let his Pressure (the weight a higher Stage lays on a room just by standing in it) roll across the camp table until the inkwells crawled. Measurewright Ingrith Sallow, who came up to about his sword belt, moved her inkwell back, handed him a shovel and walked him to the ash section, where she showed him one summer’s worth of the wall, four gray-and-rust bands where the old method had counted four years. Lord Piers hit the section with a two-handed maul to show what he thought of it.

Several of his ancestors’ centuries came down on his boots. He stood in them for a while, and then, to the great credit of the house, he began to laugh, and he was still laughing when the stonecutter’s bill arrived. Dividing by forty gave seven hundred and fifty years; the Guild’s audit of the charters gave six hundred; the stonecutter, who charged by the letter, gave a refund. Within the year the minstrels had him as Lord Six Hundred and the tenants of the north pasture had stopped paying rent on grounds of antiquity, and the instrument below is the Guild’s answer to everyone else who was about to ride out with a maul.

Issued by the Guild of Measurewrights under the Hexagonal Oath, as a companion instrument to the Concordance of Ages. Year 715 In Concordance, Withering Era, the twenty-fifth year of the Dark Ages.

This document leaves the Concordance as sealed and states how a reader holding an older register is to convert what is in front of them.

I. Why an Errata Rather Than a Recension

The Concordance withdrew the received chronology. It did not, and could not, withdraw the several hundred documents written against it.

The Archives requested a full recension, meaning that every affected register be reissued in corrected form. The Guild declined, for two reasons, only one of which is administrative convenience.

The first reason is that a recension destroys the evidence. A charter written in the old count is a record of what its signatories believed, and a bloodline claim written against a date that no longer exists is still a claim, still binding on its parties, and still the only surviving proof that the claim was made. Reissuing it silently corrected would leave the Accord holding a shelf of documents that all agree with each other and none of which anyone has read.

The second reason is that the conversion is mechanical. Where a superseded register gives a date in the old count, the correction is a substitution and calls for no judgment, and a reader who has this instrument in hand can perform it without consulting the Guild. Six substitutions cover the whole corpus.

II. The Ruling on Epochs

Before the substitutions, a distinction the Guild has been asked for since the Concordance was sealed.

The Concordance governs the Ages. It does not govern the Epochs, and no reader should attempt to convert one into the other.

The Ages are historical. They are counted, attested, and dated by physical evidence, and the whole of them runs from Year minus fourteen hundred to the present. The Epochs are cosmological. They concern the Division of Omnara, the three currents, the Plane of Fate, the Soul Plane, the Physical Plane, the Titans, and the Archons. Their durations are given in the surviving records as figures in the tens and hundreds of thousands, and the Guild has examined those figures and found that not one of them derives from a count.

No calendar existed. No observer stood outside the Epoch to mark its passage. The numbers are theological quantities in the shape of durations, and the Guild considers them accurate as statements about magnitude and meaningless as statements about years.

The ruling demotes nothing. A bloodline that traces itself to the Second Epoch is making a claim about origin and not about arithmetic, and the claim is unaffected by the Concordance. The Yukari registers stating that Senri Yukari perceived Ashura’s approach across a hundred and twenty thousand years remain as written. The Zettari registers claiming two hundred thousand years of continuous lineage remain as written, and the Guild notes that the Zettari’s own archive describes that figure as held out of conviction, which is the correct description and does the Guild’s work for it.

FindingStatement
The ruleAn Epoch figure is not to be placed on the Age count, subtracted from it, or reconciled with it. A document that gives both is giving a myth and a measurement and expects the reader to know which is which.
The consequenceNo bloodline codex, no Archonic register, no Path or Wellspring origin account requires amendment under the Concordance. The Guild has examined the principal registers of the Yukari, the Zettari, and the Moto and finds that not one of them cites an Age. They were never in conflict with the count, having been all along in a different conversation. The Alftian codices do cite one. They are stamped in the early years of the Dark Ages, and they stand on the count as sealed.

The same rule covers what the days of the Physical Epochs left lying about, when Bara, Ashura and Aegor walked in human form, and what the Great Spirit War left after them: dead and dangerous battlefields, relics people will kill for, old beings who remember. All of it is real, and much of it can be walked to and bled on. None of it can be given a year on this count, and the Guild has declined several generous offers to try.

III. The Six Substitutions

Where a superseded register uses the term at left, the reader substitutes the term at right. Where a date is attached, the reader converts it by the note given.

Beside the last row, in a later hand: the correspondence was early with both, by a generation in the one case and most of a century in the other. Nobody has sealed either name yet. See the Concordance, Section VI.

Below the table, in the same hand, a seventh that the Guild did not sign: where any document stamps a year from 690 IC onward as “the such-and-such year of the Imperial Age”, read “of the Dark Ages” and leave the number alone, so that Year 700 is the tenth year of the Dark Ages and Year 715 the twenty-fifth. The name Imperial Age belongs to what comes after the turn of the century, Year 800, the Renaissance of banks and universities, and to nothing earlier.

Under that, in a third hand, three names the Guild never got round to: where a register says “the Creation Age”, read the First Eon. Where it says “the Era of Myths”, read the days before the Great Spirit War, which Section II keeps off this count. “The Second Accord” stands in no sealed chronology, and a register citing it is giving its author’s own reckoning.

On the Withdrawn Figures

Every number in the old count above about two thousand derives from a single stratigraphic method that read seasonal ash layers as annual ones, at a site where the ash falls four times a year. The error compounds by a factor near forty across the depth of the section.

A reader encountering an old figure may convert it by dividing by forty and treating the result as an order of magnitude rather than a date. The Guild offers this reluctantly and only because it knows the alternative is that readers will invent a worse rule.

IV. Registers Requiring No Action

The Guild has surveyed the principal holdings and records the following as conforming already, so that no clerk spends a season correcting what is not wrong.

Documents stamped in the Voyager Era at low years of the count · The Concord Codex at Year 000 IC, the Heart Vault inner seal at Year 013 IC, the Hall of the Vanguard engraving at Year 014 IC, and the High Concordant’s Lattice writ at Year 027 IC are all correct as sealed. The Voyager Era is the opening era of the count and runs to Year 070. The apparent paradox in the first edition of the Four Ceilings arose from placing it five centuries late and has been withdrawn.

Bloodline and Path registers See Section II. None cite an Age.

Wellspring registers The sixty Wellsprings are described by law and behavior rather than by date. Where a Wellspring register gives an era of discovery, it is giving the era in which somebody wrote it down, which is a fact about scribes.

Technique and glyph registers Unaffected. A glyph combination has no date.

The Guild Accord instruments The Codex articles, the Divisions, the Tiered Path, and the Gate network all date to the Voyager Era and are correct as written.

V. What the Correction Costs

The Guild has been asked, twice in writing, to state plainly whether anybody is harmed by this instrument. It is, and the Guild would rather say so here than be asked again.

Every claim resting on antiquity is worth less this morning than it was worth before the Concordance was sealed. A house that held title by right of thirty thousand years now holds it by right of six hundred. The title stands as strong in law as it ever did. It has weakened in the only currency such claims actually trade in, which is the sense that a thing has always been so, and there are several holdings in the northern quarter where that sense was the whole of the security.

Four Archives careers rest on the withdrawn stratigraphy. The Guild names none of them and states that the method was standard when it was applied, that its authors were diligent, and that they were wrong about a rate of ash and about nothing else.

The Guild has no remedy to offer for either. It observes only that the count was wrong for as long as it went unexamined, that it was wrong the entire time those claims were being made, and that a claim which required the error to hold was never resting on the years.

Attested and sealed. Guild of Measurewrights, under the Hexagonal Oath, in the Archives Eternal beneath the Council Spire. Year 715 In Concordance, Withering Era, the twenty-fifth year of the Dark Ages.
