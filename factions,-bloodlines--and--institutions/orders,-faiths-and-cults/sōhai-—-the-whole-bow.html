Sōhai — The Whole Bow

Sōhai — The Whole Bow

Nov 28, 202616 min read

Sōhai · The True High Religion of the Okuchi

The Unsingled Crown · The Fourteen Labors · The Charge Against the Scale · What Crossed the Water

A doctrinal entry, compiled from house registers and from what the Church declines to file

Ice-out at Harlsey Landing is the morning the river breaks, and the whole north bank comes down to the pier in its best reds and blues to see the ration scale inspected in public, as it has been since anybody’s grandmother. In the spring of Year 688 an Ember Magistrate of Sancta Lux named Perrin Dunstable, riding his first circuit so far north, set his gold and azure lamps at the end of that pier, tried the scale, pronounced it true under Urion’s reference, and began writing the town’s custom into his circuit-roll, which is to say, in all innocence, claiming it. A salt-wife named Oddny Stackwell, four foot eleven and smelling powerfully of herring, who could not have named an Archon at knifepoint, asked him how his stack was. He asked her what she meant. She told him that a man who weighs and never squares a corner, says a dead name, feeds a dog or sings with anybody is a man with a job, and she invited him to do the other thirteen, there on the pier, since he had come all this way. To his great credit he tried. He fed the dog. He told a poor lie about a bishop, and they received it seriously. But by the time he reached the Moving, four hundred people had begun helping him with it, and his trestle, his roll-chest and both his lamps set off along the pier hand over hand, and at the Discard somebody burned the circuit-roll, and then the herring started. Seventeen noses were broken. The azure lamp went into the river, and a writ against Harlsey Landing still stands in the Magistrates’ books that no Magistrate has since volunteered to serve. Oddny Stackwell went home and salted pork. The Moto registers at Kharven-Seat enter that morning as the only successful public disputation on the Charge Against the Scale in the history of the question, and she would have been insulted to hear it.

What follows is the doctrine she held without knowing it had a name.

I. THE NAME AND THE GROUND

The land. The Moto call the body of the world as it stood before Malakar’s first blow broke the white shelf the Okuchi, the deep interior, the land behind. (Their registers call that blow the split and count the whole breaking as one event with a long middle; the Concordance’s later reckoning sets the blow near Year 310.) Every other people in the four quarters that troubles to name that country has named it after the disaster that ended it. The Zettari call it the First World, which is a forge-culture’s way of saying the one we can no longer reforge. The Marrowkin registers have no name for it at all and treat the question as unserious. The Moto name is the only one that does not describe an ending, and the reason is doctrinal. A thing that has been read correctly still stands in its place. It is behind you, and behind is a direction, and a direction can be faced.

The religion. Sōhai, the Whole Bow. Rendered in Concord common as the True High Religion, which is a translation the house tolerates and did not choose, because true and high are both comparative and the Sōhai’s entire position is that comparison is the error.

The endonym contains the doctrine. Sō, the whole, entire, without remainder. Hai, the bow, the acknowledgment made with the body. It is neither prayer nor petition. A bow is what you give a sovereign whose authority does not depend on your opinion of it, and you give it whether or not the sovereign is looking, and you give it to all fourteen or you have not given it.

II. WHAT THE SŌHAI HOLDS

The Fourteen are crowned. The Works are older. Both are true and the order matters.

Bara, whom the Archonic registers record as Bairos, authored the Works within the Plane of Fate before chronology: Ruin, Purity, Spirit, Temporality, Knowledge, Balance, Totality. The Works are the operative meanings at their barest. The Archons are those same meanings after they rose into sovereignty and were crowned. A Work is what a thing does. An Archon is that doing, enthroned, administered, and answerable.

From this the Sōhai derives its single load-bearing claim.

The Doctrine of the Unsingled Crown. No Archon may be addressed alone, because no Archon operates alone. Urion measures and Maelor records and Iesara reveals, and the Triad of Law functions only as three. Elyndra’s growth requires Auren’s endings or it becomes the Plague of the Living Flesh. Wyther’s motion without Maelor’s record is amnesia at civilizational scale. Uurgath’s entropy is the pressure that keeps the other thirteen honest, and a cosmos without him would freeze into an unchangeable perfection that is a subtler death than his. The lattice has no chief, as a pantheon has. It is fourteen sovereigns each of whom prevents the other thirteen from consuming reality, and the prevention runs in every direction at once.

Therefore: to petition one Archon is to assert that its principle can function without the others. That assertion is false about the lattice, and a false statement made in the posture of devotion is the specific thing the Sōhai calls blasphemy. The offense is inaccuracy, and the house has never dressed it up as anything grander.

The house arrived at this by four thousand years of reading rather than by piety, during which it observed what happens to a polity that elevates one meaning above the rest, and it observed this on itself first. (Four thousand is the house’s own figure. The Guild’s count of reckoned history is a good deal shorter, and the house’s registers, which cite no Age, have never troubled to argue with it.)

III. PRAISE IS LABOR

Hataraki means the Works, and it also means labor, and the Sōhai denies that this is a pun.

Fate, to the Sōhai, has nothing to do with prediction. Fate is work: the continuous assigning, preserving, undoing, measuring and completing of the roles of all things. If the Archons are the sovereigns of that labor, then the only coherent praise is to perform labor of the same kind. The Sōhai therefore recognizes two acts and no third.

The Bow. Acknowledgment. Performed with the body, silent, and directed at all fourteen in sequence. It contains no request. A Sōhai practitioner who asks an Archon for something has stopped practicing and started bargaining, and the house has a word for that.

The Work. A concrete act performed in the character of one Archon’s principle, performed daily, performed by hands. This is where the religion actually lives, and it is why a Kharven household can be observant without anyone in it being able to name a single Archon.

The Fourteen Labors

Assigned in the order of emergence. Every household is held to all fourteen. Most perform eleven without knowing they are performing anything.

ArchonPrincipleThe Labor
UrionBalanceThe Weighing. One thing weighed on a true balance each day. In Kharven this is the ration, and the scale is inspected publicly at Ice-out.
UurgathDisruptionThe Discard. One thing destroyed that could have been kept. The broken haft is burned instead of hoarded. Refusing to let a finished thing continue is the labor.
ThalenFormThe Squaring. A corner checked. A wall, a door, a stack. How’s your stack is Thalen’s labor worn down to a greeting, and no northern clansman alive knows that.
ValenTimeThe Order. Things done in their sequence and recorded in it. At the Reading, the names go in the order they died, and to read them alphabetically would be a desecration nobody could articulate.
MaelorMemoryThe Telling. One dead person’s name said aloud daily, in a room, to someone.
ZhaerenSpaceThe Walking. A boundary walked, a marker righted, a road kept passable one span further than your own need requires.
IrathDominionThe Command. One order given and one order taken, each day, both plainly. A man who only gives has failed the labor as completely as a man who only takes.
VeyraDesireThe Unnecessary. One thing made that serves no function. A carving on a bootjack. A dye nobody needed. The Sōhai holds a house with no ornament in it to be in a state of quiet sin.
IesaraInsightThe Looking. One assumption opened and checked. The barrel you believed was full. The granary floor you have never stood on.
KaetraDreamThe Telling of the False. A story told that everyone present knows is untrue, told well and received seriously. Children’s tales discharge the labor. So does a lie everybody has agreed to.
WytherMotionThe Moving. Nothing stands a full year where it stood. Furniture, herds, the position of the woodpile. Stagnation is the offense.
AurenDeathThe Waiting. Sitting with the dead. Kharven’s whole winter institution is Auren’s labor performed at national scale by a people who think they are being practical.
SelharHarmonyThe Tuning. Voices matched to another voice. Singing with, never at. A household that cannot sing together is diagnosed, never scolded.
ElyndraLifeThe Feeding. Something fed that is not yourself and cannot repay it.

The consequence for daily life and for governance: the Sōhai has no congregation, no clergy in the ordinary sense, and no building. It has households, and the household is the unit of observance, and a kingdom’s piety is measurable by walking into its kitchens. This is why the Moto have never built a temple and have never understood why anybody else does.

IV. THE RITE, THE HOUSE, AND THE HAND

The Bow. Fourteen inclinations, performed in emergence order, at the hinge of the day. In the Okuchi each was given its own station, its own hour, and its own resident house, and the full observance occupied a working day. In Kharven it is performed by one person in a cold room in under two minutes, and the fourteen stations are fourteen scratches cut into a doorpost, and everyone who cuts them believes they are counting something.

The Hand. Each Archon reads the Parunic lattice from a different station, and each station produces a different hand. Sōhai scribes write an Archon’s material in that Archon’s hand and in no other, and a document written in the wrong hand is void rather than incorrect. The fourteen daughter scripts are therefore liturgical objects as much as they are notation, and the surviving Moto material in Kharven is written across all fourteen, which is one of the reasons the Crown’s own archive is unreadable to Concord clerks.

The Reading. The house’s central sacrament and the origin of everything else. Determination of what a thing is, what it owes, and what it will become. In the Okuchi it was applied to persons at birth and, by way of the Covenant Scar, placed into the body. What the Sōhai teaches now is the doctrine’s stripped remainder: a thing read correctly has been given its Work, and a thing that has been given its Work has been praised.

The Queen’s Saishiki. The binding liturgy that made an oath structural instead of verbal. It attached to the Bow and the Reading both, and it is gone, and this is the practical condition of the religion in Kharven: the observance survives and the binding does not.

V. THE CHARGE AGAINST THE SCALE

This is the section every Sancta Lux Lector has read and every one of them has an answer to. Both parties are recorded here as they state themselves, because the argument is genuine and neither side is being stupid.

The Sōhai’s charge

Sancta Lux maintains one reference, one instrument, one consecrated ground, one Archonic magic in its charter, one seat, and thirteen appendices. It measures every act in four worlds against Urion and against nothing else. It calls the other thirteen Archons real, and it has never once weighed a man against Selhar, and it has no procedure by which it could.

The Sōhai’s word for this is Singling, and the charge is that the Scale is the largest and best-documented Singling in the history of the Realms. Idolatry requires an idol, and this is something colder. An institutional decision that one of fourteen sovereigns is sufficient, maintained across four thousand years, with paperwork.

The Sōhai grants freely that the order does not kneel. That is precisely the charge. A body that consults one reference exclusively for four millennia has performed a devotion and has kept better records of it than any shrine culture ever managed. Kneeling would at least have been honest about what was happening. (The order’s own rolls run eleven centuries, as its Lectors say loudly at this point in every disputation. The Sōhai answers that the singling of Urion is older than the order that does it now. The Guild’s count declines to sign either figure.)

And the doctrinal consequence, which is the part that closes the argument for the Moto: the Scale cannot weigh what it has no station for. Cozbi Mahuo’s numbered works return no reading at all. The order has known about that seam for a decade and cannot answer it without amending the reference. The Sōhai reads that seam, where the order sees a flaw in the instrument, as the shape of the thirteen Archons the instrument was never built to consult.

Sancta Lux’s answer

Recorded here in the order’s own register, because it is not a weak answer.

We do not worship, so we cannot be accused of worshipping one. The first clause of the Catechism of Equal Breath is a refusal and it precedes everything else we have ever written. An Archon is a crowned principle. A Titan is a load-bearing law. Neither wants anything, and prayer presumes wanting. Urion is not a listener, he is a reference, and a reference is chosen for its stability and not for its supremacy. We did not elevate Balance above Memory. We selected the only one of the fourteen against which a measurement does not drift, because a court that drifts is not a court.

The Moto have mistaken a scale for an altar. That is an understandable error made by a house whose entire art is recognition, and which therefore assumes that everything which is consulted is being revered. We consult. We do not revere. The distinction is the whole of our charter and the Moto have never accepted it, because accepting it would concede that a thing can be authoritative without being ancestral.

Where the argument actually sits

The Sōhai says the Scale worships in practice and denies it in doctrine. Sancta Lux says the Sōhai reveres in doctrine and cannot demonstrate it in practice, since fourteen labors performed by a woman salting pork are indistinguishable from a woman salting pork.

Both descriptions are accurate. Neither is complete. No arbitration exists, and none can, because the only body with standing to arbitrate is one of the two parties.

VI. THE TENANCY

The white shelf is Alabaster.

The Moto held the western reach of the one body and the seat of the Long Reading stood on white stone, and the Holy Sea of Alabaster stands on that stone now. Its Wellspring is testimony. Its whole apparatus is built to weigh a sworn account. The Sōhai’s position, stated flatly and never once withdrawn, is that testimony is a Moto Work, that the Wellspring site under Alabaster is a residue of four thousand years of Reading performed on that ground, and that Sancta Lux’s chapter hold there is a tenant occupying a sacrament it did not author, filing reports northward to a Grand Church at Altherion that has never held the deed.

The Sōhai does not press the claim. It has not pressed it in four hundred years. Six lines crossed the water and arrived with their registers and their retainers and no standing at all, and a claim you cannot enforce is a claim you are better off not making out loud.

The Seventh Objection is sealed at Meridian Hold. It has three living readers. One of them has stopped sleeping.

VII. WHAT CROSSED THE WATER

The six lines carried what could be carried, over the water that the first blow let in behind the white shelf. Registers, retainers, the population they could move, and the Bow, which weighs nothing.

What did not cross: the fourteen resident houses at the white shelf, one to each station, each with its own hours and its own hand and its own eleven-hundred-year continuity of practitioners. The Kagura Branch seasonal rites, held whole at Alabaster and held in fragments now. The full-day observance, which required a settled people with a surplus and has never once been performed in the north because the north has never once had an afternoon to spare. The Reading’s liturgy, which the Queen carried and which the Queen took with her.

There are households in the Greenhollow that perform all fourteen labors correctly, in order, every day of their lives, and could not tell you the name of a single Archon, and would be insulted by the suggestion that they were doing anything other than keeping a house properly.

The Sōhai holds that these households are the most observant people in the four quarters and that the loss of the names is a grief and not a failure.

VIII. HERESIES AND FAILURE MODES

Singling. Elevation of one Archon. The principal heresy and the one the house watches for in itself, since the Kōkan favor Totality and Totality is the Work most easily mistaken for a crown.

Petition. Asking. Converts the Bow into a transaction and the Archon into a merchant.

The Empty Bow. Observance performed without the labor. The Sōhai considers this worse than neglect, on the grounds that a man who has stopped bowing has at least stopped lying.

Devouring Certainty. The corruption vector of Knowledge, listed in the house’s own training register beside the other six, written by people who had watched it happen at Alabaster and declined to name the century.
