The Alchemical Index

Nov 28, 202644 min read

The sixty-one attested formulas · second edition, the Measurewrights’ physical reading

Corrin Sloe, called Featherback, told the whole Lantern Fair at Marl Harbor that he would step off the gilded bell tower nine times before supper and walk away from all nine, and he did it, in a goose-yellow coat, with half the harbor on the roofs and the children screaming his name. One dose of Grace Fall Draught, properly made by a properly sworn apothecary. Each time he hit the cobbles, rolled, stood and bowed, with a bruise and nothing worse, and each jump came from a higher window than the last, until he went off the very finial with a torch in each hand and the crowd threw so many flowers that the square smelled like a wedding. They carried him to the Guildhall supper on a door. Halfway through the goose, with a cup in one hand and a leg in the other, the dose’s window closed, and all nine falls arrived together. The bench went, and the floor under the bench, and the surgeon who set what could be set counted forty-four breaks and reported to the harbor-reeve that the man had plainly fallen from a great height, at dinner.

Sloe took six days to die and spent them dictating a complaint against the apothecary, who produced his batch notes and was acquitted, because the batch was excellent. It did everything its row below says it does, down to the last line of the row. That is why I love this index above every other book in the Guild’s keeping. Every failure in it comes out of the same place as the success, and whoever reads both columns may be sorry, but will never be surprised.

Every entry carries the Measurewrights’ account, an analogue and a mechanism stated in the analogue’s own terms, and a failure that falls out of the same place as the success. A formula works by its Wellspring’s idea, which the Sixty give with its Operation; the account beside it is the guild’s, often right about how much and never about why, and the names it gives the failures are theirs.

Nothing in an ingredient list is symbolic filler. Each component supplies a catalytic surface, a nucleation site, a stabilizing lattice, or a Wellspring-resonant trace, by the guild’s reading, and each material brings its virtue to the vessel. Where a step specifies darkness, silence or a season, it is specifying a condition the working needs and not a mood, and this edition says which.

Provenance Grade (the mark of where a stock came from, from Grade I, taken off a Wellspring site and carrying nothing, up to Class VI, found and unlocatable) is now stated on every row, because the Standing Index prices on it and this index has been silent on it since compilation. Twelve entries below are Class IV or above, and eight of them were carried for years without the column that would have said so. Four more reach Grade IV when their source bore a Crystal, and a source that bore a Crystal is lawful when it was a monster’s core and a hanging when it was a person’s.

The Essence Drafts rendered from monster cores for tempering, which pay for most of the benches in this book, are not among the sixty-one, since a lot follows its beast and has no fixed formula to enter. They are on The Common Drafts.

A word on the analogues. The Guild’s scholars reason from the law underneath a thing, which they read through the Aether, and they will cheerfully compare a salve to a device no living smith could build. Kindly do not write to me asking where to buy one.

The bench copy of every formula here, with its ingredients, method, effect, risk and its working by its Wellspring’s idea, is the Alchemical Formulary.
