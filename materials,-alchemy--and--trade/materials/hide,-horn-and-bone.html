Hide, Horn and Bone

Hide, Horn and Bone

Nov 28, 202615 min read

The creature-material register · hide, horn, claw, bone, sinew, gut, blood, organ, scale, quill and shell, and the core that pays for all of it.

Everything in this register was alive once. Some of it has not been told.

Chalked over the door of a Dorrosfenn rendering house, and repainted by the owners whenever it wears off

Bettany Quarrie’s wedding feast at Sarrow Cross ran three nights, and the wine town talked of nothing else for forty years. Her father, a vintner who had come into money and wanted the valley to know it, built a great hall for the purpose, hung its beams in saffron and kingfisher blue, set a whole ox turning, and hired the pipers of two parishes who detested each other. What he had mentioned to nobody was the loft, where he kept the most expensive thing he had ever bought: two hundred fathoms of Tidedrawn Cable, leviathan tendon a hand’s width across, taken on speculation against a siege that never came, and stored coiled, twelve days’ haul from salt water, by a man who had read the price and not the row. It had spent five weeks working its way across the floorboards toward the river. On the third night, halfway through the second pipers’ set, it reached the north gable and kept going.

The gable went out in one piece. The cable came down through the roof tree over the heads of the dancers like a black river with an appointment, its tail fouled around the king post, and a hand’s width of that cable holds a ship, so the hall went with it. The whole north end of the building walked eleven paces down the hill toward the Sarrow at about the pace of a plow ox, beams shrieking, carrying the high table along on its floor and leaving two pipers, an uncle and the ox underneath. Bettany Quarrie stayed in her chair with a cup in her fist and her yellow skirts full of roof, and shouted down the length of the wreck for somebody to bring more wine. The minstrels have her riding the cable to the river with a sword in her hand, and in the longer versions out to sea. It reached the water on the ninth day and has lain there since, by every account perfectly content, and the town charges to see it.

The trade’s word for what was in that loft is carry (what a creature’s life leaves in the material rendered out of it). It is graded on every row below, by Provenance Grade (the mark of where a stock came from), and any factor in the four quarters will sell it to you by the pound without once telling you what it is. I will.

What carry actually is, stated once. Every entry below is graded for it and the register has never said what it does.

A Threshold (the breakthrough that remakes a practitioner’s Soul Crystal) is a melt, and a melt cools into order only around structures that survived the heat. A lived one passes the Great Work in its Path’s own terms, nigredo, albedo, citrinitas and rubedo: broken down, purified, woken and fixed. In a Soul Crystal those structures are Traits, which are laws the bearer survived into existence. Carry is a seed: a law that something else survived into existence, still attached to the material it was rendered out of.

So a high-carry creature material used in Threshold work does more than work harder. It grows a region of the recipient’s new lattice around a law that was never theirs. The appetite in Ripper Talon is a hunting law a predator lived long enough to make true. The pull in Tidedrawn Cable is a law about deep water. Neither of them is a metaphor and neither of them stops.

Grade III carry is appetite-shaped and can be lived with. Grade IV is identity-shaped, and identity-shaped carry is what Identity Contamination is a description of: a Core (here the Essence Core inside the Crystal) that has absorbed enough borrowed law to stop being able to say cleanly which convictions are its own. This register is that supply chain. It has never been written down as one.

The Core

A monster’s core is in a way its Soul Crystal. Inside a creature’s chest the Essence Core (the seat of its nature) hardens over its life into a fist of glassy stuff in its own color, and it is worth more the stronger the monster. Monsters gain Levels by killing, each other included, evolve at their own thresholds, and are posted by Grade, E to EX, on the Commission’s board (see The Bestiary); the core table below is keyed to that Grade, and the materials further down carry the Accord ledger’s own material tier, T0 to T9, as every substance in The Master Material Ledger does. A core is the lawful Crystal Draw of The Standing Index, and an alchemist renders it into Essence Drafts used for tempering. A Well-spawn carries one under the mask, so a hunter may be paid twice on one kill: the hall on the mask, the rendering house on the core. Anything banished down its Well takes the core along.

A core keeps far longer than anything else on the carcass, which is why crews run for the hall with the mask and walk home with the core inside their shirts. What follows is the price at a rendering house’s door, for a clean core with a hall’s chit. A house pays between a third and a half of what the core will fetch poured.

Any core renders about a dozen doses at its own grade (What Things Cost carries the same rows, and Monster Cores the whole trade).

A wyrm core is the one the crews mean when they say a fortune. A good earth wyrm’s fetches 20,000 gold at the door of any rendering house with a crucible big enough, a good deal more for an old one with a name and a bounty, and the bill on the rim-town doors of the Scourge of Hell pays on top of that. Do not confuse any of this with a Core in the street’s sense, a place where a Wellspring pools, and be careful which one you promise a factor.

Swallowed raw, a core gives everything at once. Most die and a good share come out wrong. A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner reads the same on the coil, makes the strongest Draft of all, and is the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable. A rendering house can spot a human Crystal on the bench if it cares to look, and the houses that care are the ones still solvent after a hanging. Ask for the chit. Ask for the beast’s name. Then ask yourself why the price is good.

What a Kill Makes

Monster parts make gear, and most of what a fighter wears that is worth stealing was walking around once. A piece carries a Grade for power, on the same letters the Commission posts beasts by, and a Rarity for scarcity: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary, Mythic, Unique. Its material sets the most Grade it can hold, by the rows below and Grade and Rarity; the smith’s title caps the Grade she may make, and the merchant’s title caps the Rarity he may sell. The Accord’s old stamps (Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous, Proscribed) still go on the mark. An Appraisal skill and an assay instrument read a piece, and a good forger beats a weak appraiser. Only bonded gear grows.
