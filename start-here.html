On the last morning of the Salt Succession two armies stood across a field of green barley at the ford of Orrendale, scarlet shields on one side and gold-and-black on the other, and a cook’s daughter named Perrine Dorrow walked out alone between them in a butcher’s apron over borrowed mail. Her aura was the wet arterial red that makes a stranger’s pulse jump. She filled her chest and called the name of the only art she had across the whole valley, the Hunger of All Kings, and six thousand men laughed. A knight in the gold-and-black stood up in his stirrups to laugh better. Then the third day of a fast landed in every belly of the enemy front rank at once, and the line buckled, men sat down in the barley and wept for bread, warhorses dragged their heads down to tear at the crop while their riders slid off them, and the scarlet shields walked across and took the ford almost without a blow. Perrine’s ribs stood out through the apron by then, because that art eats the woman who works it, and she ate for nine days and never again kept down a whole meal. The minstrels had the song in every tavern by harvest. The queen who had hired her held a feast in her honor, since a fighter who can starve an army is a problem at table, and the third course was poisoned, and forty people in cloth of gold watched her die with their spoons in their hands. They buried her in the old barrow above the ford, on a battlefield of the Great Spirit War where nothing has grown straight since, and nobody who has dug there will say what the barrow was built to hold down.

What War of the Realms is

War of the Realms is mythic epic fantasy: Tolkien’s sense of age, Martin’s courts, Le Guin’s magic, and souls that climb. It is a medieval world that runs on old magic, with great houses at odds, lethal courts, deep and frightening powers, and a cultivation system running underneath all of it. The stories are set in the Dark Ages, the hundred and ten years from 690 IC to the turn of the century, on one great continent of four quarters, and the present year is 715 IC, the twenty-fifth of them. The name flatters nobody and misleads every visitor, because these are the loudest years on record: practitioners are common, schools run like football clubs with their colors, songs and grudges, heralds cry the news, minstrels get it wrong, and bookies take odds on all of it. Magic is done by living people and their crafts, and it runs on the alchemist’s lens rather than the natural philosopher’s: every working is a transformation the worker makes in themselves, an idea burned, dissolved or fixed in the soul, and the world gives way as far as that soul has truly changed. What burns in the crucible burns in the one who tends it. A city’s lamps burn the color of the warden who keeps them, and she can walk off the round, while the towns and the wilds still burn oil. There are no guns in any era, and a three-copper crossbow bolt can still kill a great fighter in a closed room. People who wake the power in their own souls climb sixteen Stages, and everyone says them as ranks: an Initiate, a Journeyman, an Expert, a Master. A Stage opens when the soul meets the one deed it asks, its Catalyst, and has lived enough, its Level, to hold it. Surviving what nearly breaks you meets the deed, and so does a bought Draft, which is why the rich are strong and why a lucky commoner with a monster core keeps great houses awake at night; a Stage bought ahead of the life to hold it sits loose and spilling in the soul until the life catches up. A soul comes to lean on one of three Paths: Body, which knows and works physical reality and has to close with you; Spirit, which works on souls, the dead and what a thing means, from across the field; and Fate, the hardest of them, which moves what has to be so before anything can happen, and at a ruinous price. None of them is better, and each has a way of killing the others. Each person’s soul reports their numbers to them alone, in a block of text called a window. Crews go down into dungeons, floors of living danger grown around a pool of power, to kill what guards the heart. Monsters level up, earn names and carry bounties. Guilds of every kind hand out the same nine words as titles that the fighters use as ranks, gear and beasts carry a Grade (a person never does), gear a Rarity besides, and blessings show on your sheet and sometimes lie. Each arc sets its own darkness: cruelty and loss land hard where the story turns, and then people fight back, and they win often, each win at a price you can see. Tolkien’s age lies under everything, and the land is its own thing: wildly fantastical and earthlike at once, grand and lived-in, and beautiful all the way through.

The climb in one sentence

Your soul climbs Stages, and each Stage asks one thing of you. Your life climbs Level, by everything hard and real you have done. Your hands climb skills, by use. Everything else is read from those three.

What climbsHow it climbsWhat people say
Stage, the soulIts Catalyst met, one deed per Stage, and Level enough to hold itThe rank: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon
Level, the lifeAnything hard and real: danger overcome above all, but also a flood night held or fifty years at a tradeA number, and from it how well the soul works its own road, its Mastery (Body IV, Spirit III)
Skills, the handsUseOne of the nine ranks: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon

Letters (the Grades, F up to EX) belong to things: gear, missiles, beasts and bounties. Nobody calls a person by a letter. How Power Grows walks the whole climb in one reading.

A short route before your first fight scene

When you want more than the primer and less than the whole section, these nine pages are the quickest way through. They cut across the shelves on purpose, and each one leans on the one before.

  1. The Gate: Who May Learn Magic. Who wakes their Crystal, who never does, and why that is no longer an accident.
  2. The Core Vocabulary. The basic words: the three planes, Aether (the medium the world is made of) and Essence (Aether a person has pulled in from the world around them and refined into their own), the Crystal, abilities and techniques, and the sheet and the climb.
  3. How Power Grows. The climb in one reading: Stage said as rank, the Catalyst each Stage asks and the Level that holds it, Mastery read from Level, skills on the nine ranks, and the Releases each Path reaches.
  4. The Sixteen Stages. The ladder of power, Stage (one of sixteen steps of the soul’s refinement) by Stage, told plainly: its rank, its Catalyst, what it feels like inside, what it looks like outside, what ordinary people call it, and what it costs to buy.
  5. The Trait System: Law, Function and the Forge. Where powers come from. A Trait (a standing law in the soul, formed when lived experience hardens into something the world accepts as true) is survived into being, never taught, and works as deep as its owner’s Mastery of the lead Path, so it grows as the life does.
  6. The Three Paths: Routing, Recognition and the Gate. The road a soul walks, Body, Spirit or Fate: what each one is, how each beats the others, what one, two or three of them cost, and what a Path (the layer of the soul it has learned to lean on) gates.
  7. The Working Trades. The ways a working (a deliberate act of magic) is set down: spoken in the moment, cut into stone, steel or paper, poured into a bottle, or sung by many voices together, each a skill and a trade, and all but the pouring want a woken Crystal. Also why the guilds look down on fighters who speak their workings, while the crowds cheer them anyway.
  8. The Sixty Wellsprings. The sixty Wellsprings (each an alchemical and spiritual transformation, the governing idea that changes how a soul uses Aether, a lens on magic already potent, each a transformation a person works on themselves that shows in the world, which the Measurewrights explain with one law of physics apiece as their own theory) sorted by kind, Wild, Spirit, Fate and Medium, by the plane each one calls home, each of them a way some Titan’s law shows up in the world.
  9. Counterplay: What Beats a Practitioner. How a practitioner (a person whose Crystal is awake) is actually beaten, in four routes. Read this before your first fight scene.
