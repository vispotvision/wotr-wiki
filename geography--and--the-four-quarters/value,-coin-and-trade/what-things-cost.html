What Things Cost

What Things Cost

Nov 28, 202613 min read

Everyday prices and wages in a kept Accord city of the Dark Ages (one whose lamps and wards are worked by hired hands on regular rounds), at the Accord rate, western standard

This list began as a lawsuit over a bowl of broth. In the spring of Year 702 Hob Ladleworth, who kept a cookshop in the Altherion saddlers’ market, put his broth up from four copper to five, and Scarlet Oriel Thrane, a Journeyman fighter who had eaten at his counter every morning for nine years, took it as an insult to her school, flared a coat of aura the red of a boiled crab and set fire to his awning in front of the whole market. Ladleworth sued for the awning and the copper. The magistrate asked what a bowl of broth ought to cost, forty witnesses gave him forty answers, and he ordered a true list of the city’s prices written out and cried in the square by a herald, which took three days and started two fights. The Arbitration Division has kept it current ever since. Thrane paid for the awning, and she paid five copper every morning for the rest of her life, loudly and under protest. Every figure below has been shouted at somebody in a public place.

One gold mark is twelve silver marks. One silver mark is twenty-four copper. A gold mark is therefore two hundred and eighty-eight copper, and almost nobody below a shop’s counting-room ever handles one.

A Nalūn salt bar passes for one silver mark, more in a hard winter. Imperial bronze goes by weight.

Prices are given as city prices inside a warden’s round. Past the last round, and everywhere north of the arrays (standing runed workings), most of this list is paid in kind: grain, salt, cloth, fuel, board, a day’s labor owed and settled at year’s end. Where coin changes hands out there, it is copper, and in a copper famine it is nothing at all.

The Bill

WorkPrice, monthlyNote
Sealed household, one room3 to 5 silverA warden’s lamp at the door and a cutter’s ward on the frame, by named hands under a lead seal, at a grade of work everyone knows runs below the fee. A poor stair splits one lamp and one seal among six doors and fights about it. Heat is your own hearth

The bill is what you pay a warden’s company for somebody’s hands on your door, and on a street that takes it, it is the largest line in a poor household after rent, and it comes whether the family has coin that month or not. The snuffers (the men who strip a defaulter’s lamps and scratch through his wards) come when it goes unpaid.

A guard company’s full quiver of enhanced bolts costs about what a clerk earns in a month. Ordinary infantry do not carry them. The rampart arbalest is on no price list; a city commissions one from a Guild master, in a decade with money in it, and names it.

Monster Cores

A monster’s core is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster. Priced by the monster’s Grade (the letter from E to EX that its bounty carries), raw, on the coil, at a renderer’s door. It renders to about a dozen doses of Essence Draft at its own grade.

GradeThe coreNote
E3 to 5 silverA week of a laborer’s wages, from something that had been at the henhouse
D12 to 18 silverAbout a laborer’s month
C3 to 6 goldA soldier’s half-year, and a crew’s first good night
B8 to 15 goldMost of a laborer’s year. A village will remember who brought it home
A30 to 300 goldThe bottom buys two horses and the top buys a clerk’s four years. A Grade A beast runs across three Stages and the coil tells you which
S800 to 8,000 goldMore than the crew’s home village will see in a generation
SS20,000 gold and upBy private treaty. It comes with an armed escort and several new friends
X and EXNot quotedUnder the Obsidian Seal, and never on a counter

A famous monster’s core carries a premium for the name, on top of any bounty, and the bounty is usually the smaller part. Hide, bone and scale sell separately, for about as much again while fresh, falling every day from the kill. Some of what is sold as monster core came out of people. A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner is the strongest stock of all and appears on no list, because the price includes the rope.

Essence Drafts and the Climb

An Essence Draft is rendered from a core and fed into tempering, and what a course of it buys is Level. Graded by the Stage it serves, about threefold a Stage, at a licensed house, where a house will sell it to you at all. On the street, three to ten times this.

The Stage bought outright is a course plus the Draft that supplies the Catalyst, drunk in a quiet room with a physician standing by. It opens the door and no more: the sheet shows the same Stage, the Crystal sits Overgrown until its owner’s Level grows to hold it, and Mastery and the Release still have to be lived and found by fighting. Commoners without the coin for a Waking Draft sell themselves into guild debt for it, and the term is generally the larger bill. A Fate Essence Draft is a Master Work and has never been quoted anywhere.

Titles

The same nine in every guild, grading trust in that trade, and earned by commissions done in it. The fee is what a hall charges to sit the examination and strike the silver token, and a hall that likes you may advance it against your term.

TitleThe feeWhat a stranger may hand you
Initiate2 silverCommon goods, Marked work, a Grade E commission
Apprentice6 silverCommon goods, Proofed work, a Grade D commission
Journeyman1½ goldUncommon goods, Tempered work, a Grade C commission
Adept5 goldRare goods, Instrument work, a Grade B commission
Expert15 goldRare goods, Hallowed work, a Grade A commission
Master50 goldVery Rare goods, Storied work, a Grade S commission
Grandmaster150 goldLegendary goods, Found work, a Grade SS commission
Archmaster500 goldMythic goods, Numinous work, a Grade X commission
ParagonNo fee. The halls come to youUnique goods, Proscribed work, a Grade EX commission

Goods for a merchant, work for a craftsman, commissions for an adventurer (Grade and Rarity has how the caps run). Fighting guilds ask one thing more, the title’s own rank in Stage, so a Journeyman’s token in a fighting hall wants Ascension at least, and only their tokens say how dangerous you are. A forged token runs about ten times the fee in any port that sells them, and it is hanged in every realm.
