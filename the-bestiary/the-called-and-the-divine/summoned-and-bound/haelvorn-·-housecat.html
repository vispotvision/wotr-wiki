Haelvorn · Housecat

Haelvorn · Housecat

Nov 17, 202641 min read

Native-Sovereign route form. Marrowkin, Passwarden branch, Waymark Guild. Reclassified as ordnance over the objection of the practitioner who holds it.

Every glyph in this entry’s chain is a fire glyph. Cold is not a substance and there is no Wellspring that makes it. There is only heat and where it is moved to, and this construct is a heat form running the current backwards.

The heat has to go somewhere. It goes up the tether. That sentence is the whole entry and the rest is arithmetic.

Serjeant Bette Quill of the Concord line named it from behind a cart. It was the fourth morning at Thinbreath Saddle, blue and blinding, and a column came up the pass at a run with its horns going, dyed felts and red banners and every blade it owned out in the sun, and in front of them, in the middle of the track, something white and enormous was standing about like a thing waiting to be let in. It had the head of a wolf with the lips frozen back off the teeth and a braid down its spine hung with iron rings. It opened its mouth. Along the first rank every lip split at once, and frost ran up the banner poles from the bottom, and for about four seconds nothing else appeared to happen, until the first man to parry felt his sword go off in his hand like a dropped pot. So did the next man’s, and then it was all of them, bright steel going through the air at the speed of the swing, and that morning’s filing is mostly a list of men killed by their own weapons and their neighbors’. Across the field a stocky Marrowkin woman had gone down on one knee in the snow with her collar torn open and her face the color of raw brick. The white thing shut its mouth, walked over to her through the middle of the rout, lowered its great head, and asked in a voice of perfect manners, “May I lie down?” She said yes. It lay down, and every fire on the saddle went out from the bottom up.

Bette Quill considered this for some time. “Housecat,” she said. “Wants in, wants your lap, and leaves dead things on the step.” It is the only name the line has ever used, and I have been within thirty paces of it myself, and I lost the skin off my lower lip and have no better word. For the newcomer: Vocatia is the art of calling something that already exists, and Native provenance means the thing owns its own Soul Crystal (the organ that refines Aether into Essence). Bond Class Sovereign means it sets the terms of its answering. Danger Tier is the Commission’s rating, given as though it were loose. A Draft is the four-glyph working that calls it, and Attraction Force and Obsession Force are the two ways a called thing can be reached, by recognition or by compulsion. The rest of the grammar is set out in the Summon Register.

I · Identity

Formal NameHaelvorn. High Parunic, from Hael (the verdict-flame, which burns only falsehood and imbalance) + Vorn (the forced termination, the collapse of an unstable state). The name predates the Guild chain by an unknown margin and does not correspond to the glyphs Rimsang actually drafts with, which is normal for a Native form and is noted here only because two archivists have filed it as an error
Field NameHousecat, universally, among Concord line-troops, and the Waymark Guild has stopped correcting them. Guild usage is the Waymark, on the reasoning that the thing is her certification wearing a body. Rimsang Halfyear has never called it anything at all in any record the Guild holds, including her own
SummonerRimsang Halfyear, Waymark Guild. Level 372 / 500, Band IV Warlord, Temperance Stage IX Invocation
Lineage · SchoolMarrowkin, Passwarden branch. Guild instrument rather than household pattern, which is the distinction that has caused every legal problem attached to this entry. A certified route belongs to the Guild. Nobody drafted the paperwork for a route that walks
LoanableNo, and the refusal is the construct’s rather than hers. The Guild has formally requested transfer twice, correctly, through its own procedure, and been answered both times with a question about whether the requesting party intended to travel anywhere. Neither request was granted and neither was technically denied
FormCall · Bind · Make, in that order of load. Call reaches it. Bind holds the circle. Make contributes almost nothing and is listed only because the Draft will not close without it
Summon TierIII · Greater
Danger TierT8, rated loose and hostile, which is two full steps above what its combat capability alone would earn. The rating is thermodynamic. A bound Haelvorn’s ceiling is set by how much heat one Marrowkin body can hold before it dies. A loose one has no such ceiling, and the exhaust still has to go somewhere, so it goes into the nearest Soul Crystal and keeps going until that Crystal fails, and then into the next
Accord ClassificationRegistered, Division of Concordia, then reclassified without her consent under the Arbitration Division’s ordnance schedule following the Thinbreath filings. Rimsang’s written objection is attached to the file and has never been answered. The Waymark Guild’s toll-right, which every Marrowkin branch honors without exception, has no provision covering a route the Accord has decided is a weapon

II · Provenance and Architecture

Crystal ProvenanceNative. Its own Crystal, held before the Guild existed and unaffected by anything the Guild has since decided about it
Essence CoreGenuine. It has never volunteered where it was before, and it has never been asked, because Rimsang’s position is that asking a thing where it came from is what you do to something you intend to own

A lossy Shell would waste most of the extracted enthalpy into the surrounding field and the thing would be a curiosity. This one conducts almost everything it takes, and everything it conducts arrives somewhere
Attraction LayerGenuine and bonded to the route, which is a category the Concordia readers do not have a box for and which two of them have privately said reads more like a bond to the idea of a route than to any particular stretch of ground. Rimsang is the current holder of the route. The bond is to the office
Crystal Developmental TierClear
Crystal StateRefined. No accretion whatsoever, which is worth stating plainly beside the Mule’s entry in the same register: whatever this construct takes, it does not keep, except in the rings, and the rings are not part of it
Aether ClassIII · Resonant
Essence ClassI · Listener. It knows who is on the road, and that is all it has ever wanted to know
Thread ClassII · Drawn. The route’s bond shows here, and a crossing it has certified holds a little better than it should
Summon Stability FacetB, at the ceiling of Tier III’s range. Native provenance again, and again nothing to do with the summoner’s competence, which Rimsang states in her own filings with what the Division has recorded as insufficient deference
TraitsDry Approach. It removes water vapor before it removes warmth, so the humidity crash always precedes the temperature drop by two or three seconds and is the only warning anyone gets. Load Certain. It knows the bearing capacity of any surface it stands on, to a tolerance the Guild has tested and failed to catch out. Scentless. It has no smell, produces none, and cannot be tracked by any animal yet tried, and the absence is itself detectable to anything with a nose that expects a body to have one
DomainNone registered. Three Concordia readers have filed a joint dissent arguing that the inversion layer described in Section VIII behaves as a Domain in every measurable respect and lacks only the registration, and the dissent has been received and shelved

III · Wellspring and Codex Assignment

Primary WellspringExuroth, the Trial Flame. Temperance through ordeal, hardship as the forge of will. Strengthens Ardency Overchannel, Tempering Overflow, and Vitality Threshold, which in plain terms means it raises what a body can be made to survive, and every single thing this construct costs its summoner runs through that clause
Secondary WellspringBasilithe, Passwarden branch primary, a Wild Wellspring of stone. It contributes nothing to the cold. It contributes the load rating, and without it the Span would be a sheet of ice rather than a certified crossing
Kind ResonanceExuroth is Wild, heat and its absence and the transfer between them, and that definition is what makes this entry legal rather than perverse. Everything here is a fire power running the current the other way, with no ice power anywhere in it (the Titan Vohrin’s frost is another matter and stands outside the Sixty), and the reason the construct is white is that white is what the far end of a fire looks like. Basilithe arrives underneath and makes the result bear weight
KindWild, primary and secondary both
Leans onThe Aether Shell. Body: it will not close on you unless you are on its road, and it takes heat, so bring no fire. Spirit: past the white to a Core that has never said where it came from. Fate: it holds to a route as an office, and the office can be contested by the person who holds it
OperationCalcination, Exuroth’s: the soul burned, plunged and burned again, until only the hardness that lives through the fire is left. Each ability below works by the idea its Function line states
The Measurewrights’ accountThermodynamics, with Material Science on the load-bearing side only. The guild reads every Mechanism field below as heat transport, phase change, or the mechanical consequences of both. Nothing in the kit is a projectile. Often right about how much, never about why
The DonationThe donation runs as thirst. Witnesses within the radius describe a sensation of being pulled from rather than pressed on, and several have reached for the word drinking independently and then apologized for it

IV · The Draft

Authorization Glyph[IrW] Will. Amplifies Essence through conviction, resists domination. Judicium primary, attested at Ignivale. Drafted on the Ignivale attestation. The Guild’s commentary on why an Authorization role for a route-instrument should be built out of a resistance-to-domination glyph runs to four pages and settles nothing
Boundary Glyph[Py] Pyre. Pins a combusting mark to a fixed point. Pyraeon primary, Thermodynamics. The spatial limit on this construct is a burn-mark, which is the single detail most Accord readers refuse to believe until they see the ground
Direction Glyph[Vt] Vector. Directs motion or force along a plotted line. Ignivale primary, Thermodynamics. The behavioral vector is route, and Vector is the only glyph in the index that means a plotted line rather than mere movement
Sealing Glyph[Sv] Sever. Cuts a rot-thread from a living host for burning. Catharsis primary, Thermodynamics. Sealing this form is drafted as excising something from a body, and the body in the grammar is hers
Invocation”Veni, non ad pugnam sed ad viam.” Come, not to the fight but to the road
Material AnchorFive iron rings, Guild-forged, four of them closed and one open. They ride in the construct’s mane and they are the only physical component of the working. Taking a closed ring off the braid is possible and has been done once. It discharges (see The Rings) and it does not transfer the naming-right, because the rings are storage and the authorization lives in her throat
Circle RequirementInternalized shorthand. She has not laid a formal Draft-circle in nine years. The four roles are spoken and the ground answers, which the Division has entered as an irregularity and the Guild has entered as competence
ReadinessTwo to three seconds if she is walking somewhere. Between forty seconds and never if she is not, and the variance is not hers to explain
DurationAs long as the route is unfinished. It has stood beside her for eleven days on the Thinbreath crossing and dissolved eight seconds after the last of the party stepped off her span, without being told
Call CostApproximately 6% of her reserve, which is low, and the lowness is a trap. The call is cheap. The Intake is not, and the Intake is not paid in Essence
Sustain CostNearly nil while it walks. She can hold it alongside two other constructs indefinitely and has. Nobody has ever seriously worried about her reserve and the Division’s entire threat assessment is built on the wrong resource

V · Bond and Standing

Bond ClassSovereign
Terms of AnsweringIt comes if she is going somewhere. The terms were set by the construct at the first calling and have never been renegotiated: it will make a way, it will hold that way open, and when the last body is across it will go. It asks to lie down and she has to say yes. That is the entire price as stated, and the Guild spent two years satisfied that the price was nothing. Every Accord deployment order since the reclassification has required her to name a destination she did not intend to reach, and it has come every time, and it has never once asked why the road ended in a battle. Whether it cannot tell or has chosen not to say is the open question of this entry and possibly of her life
Refusal ConditionsIt will not take heat from a body that is already failing to keep its own, which structurally forbids finishing wounded, freezing, or hypothermic targets and has cost two engagements outright. It will not lay a Span it would not certify, and its standard is the Guild’s standard, and it does not care that the pursuing force is thirty seconds behind. It will not remain after the crossing is complete, for any inducement, including hers. It will not discharge a ring into a person it has not first been shown
PrecedentThree recorded refusals. At the Kegshard relief it declined to close on a column already three days without fire and the column reformed and the relief failed. On the second Thinbreath contest it refused a Span across a saddle it judged unsafe and eleven Windward drovers went the long way and four did not arrive. At Halfyear’s own certification crossing, in the ninth year, it refused to leave when she told it to and stayed until the last two stragglers were across, which is the only occasion on record of the construct disobeying her and the only one she has ever refused to file

VI · Physical Description

It stands a little over two and a half meters at the shoulder on all fours and the proportions are wrong in a way that reads immediately even to people who could not say why. The forequarters carry perhaps two thirds of the mass. The shoulders and forelimbs are enormous, slabbed, built for pushing and for holding still against a load, and the hindquarters taper away behind them almost apologetically. Total mass runs near eleven hundred kilograms and almost none of it is in the back half. It is a body designed to arrive somewhere and stop.

The coat is white going to blue in the hollows, and the blue is depth rather than pigment. Light entering the outer guard hairs scatters short and comes back cold the way glacier ice does, and the only two likenesses the Accord has managed, both by sworn limners working from life, were sent back as embellished. Along the flanks, the shoulders and the outside of every limb run broad gray-blue bands, and the bands are frost-scar. Tissue that has been frozen and thawed and frozen again does not heal evenly, and the damage propagates along the fascia planes, which is why the banding is regular and why it looks deliberate and why three separate cultures have independently assumed it was applied by a hand. No hand touched it. A body that lives at the exhaust end of its own working scars this way.

The head is wolf and the snarl is permanent, because the lips have retracted from repeated freezing and no longer close over the teeth. The teeth themselves are the wrong color, closer to horn than enamel, and the two upper canines have hairline vertical cracks running from the gumline that have not propagated in nine years of observation and that Rimsang checks every time she braids. The eyes are pale, almost colorless, and the sclera shows all around them, which is why every witness account uses the word staring and why almost every witness account is wrong about what it was looking at.

Down the spine from the crown runs a single braid, thick as a forearm at the neck and tapering, laid in a five-strand round braid that the Waymark Guild does not teach and that Rimsang learned from a rope pattern. Bound into it at intervals are five iron rings, Guild-forged, blackened, each one closed around the braid so it cannot be drawn off without cutting. Four of them are shut. The fifth, nearest the shoulders, is open, its ends a finger’s width apart and never joined, and every Marrowkin who has ever seen the braid has understood exactly what an open ring in a sequence of closed ones means and not one of them has asked her about it.

The forepaws are broad enough to spread eleven hundred kilograms across snow that will not hold a Marrowkin, and the claws are black and glassy and refract at the tips. Where the coat breaks at the shoulders and the elbows, vapor comes off it and freezes as it goes, so that in cold weather the thing appears to be smoking upward and in warm weather the plume falls, sheets down the flanks and pools at its feet, and nothing about the sight is reassuring in either season.

The approach is dry before it is cold. Within four paces the dewpoint collapses and the water goes out of the air first, so lips split, eyes sting, and every exposed surface takes a coat of hoarfrost inside about ten seconds, and only after all of that does the temperature arrive. Sound sharpens instead of dulling, because cold dense air carries it better, so a shout at the far end of a field lands with a clarity that soldiers describe as being spoken to. It has no smell of any kind, and dogs put near it show none of the fear they keep for a predator and behave as they would at the edge of a hole. Where it has stood, frost flowers open in a radiating disc a pace wide, feathered and structural, and in high summer they last about twenty minutes.

Rimsang Halfyear is legible in every part of it. A guide’s construct: mass in the front for hauling and holding, feet for bad ground, a body that knows what a surface will bear, no offensive architecture anywhere on it, and a snarl it did not choose and cannot put away.

VII · Temperament and Voice

It speaks, and it asks questions. That is the whole of the register. They are honest questions, never rhetorical and never leading, and it waits for the answer every time however badly timed the asking is. The vocabulary runs to comfort, footing, and weather, and the subject it will not raise is what it is doing to her, which it has never mentioned and which she is certain it knows about to the tenth of a degree.

The questions are short and courteous to a degree that reads as parody until you have stood next to it. Is the ground good? Are they all across? Are you warm? That last one is the one the Guild has learned to dread hearing, because it only asks it when the answer has already gone wrong.

Its signature line, on every record where anyone was near enough to catch it, is “May I lie down?” It asks this before the Floor, every time, and it asks it in the middle of engagements, and it does not proceed until she says yes. Four separate Accord observers have described the effect of hearing a T8 entry request permission to lie down in the middle of a rout, and none of the four found a professional way to write it.

Under strain it stops asking. The register goes declarative and the first flat sentence out of it is the warning every Passwarden who has worked beside it has been told to listen for. What it says at that point is almost always a number, and the number is a distance, and it has never been wrong about the distance.

VIII · Abilities

  • THE INTAKE · Signature · Vocatia-Native · Extraction Glyph Basis: [Ra] Flame, igniting creative power and heating blood and Essence · [Wy] Motion, adding momentum and converting stasis into movement · [Ve] Desire, amplifying intent through longing

    “Calorem tuum mihi da.”

    Give me your heat.

    Trigger: It opens its mouth. There is no spoken component from Rimsang and no gesture, and the only tell is the humidity.

    Function: It removes heat from everything in front of it and does not put it back.

    Mechanism, the Measurewrights’ account: Thermodynamics, and the honest version rather than the flattering one. It generates no cold, because cold cannot be generated. It acts as a sink, and Exuroth opens the gradient, and enthalpy runs down that gradient into the construct by conduction, convection and radiation all at once and in that order of contribution. The clean Aether Shell conducts nearly all of it onward, and the great majority discharges into the Continuum. Roughly one part in three hundred does not. That part runs the tether and arrives in Rimsang’s body as heat, because there is nowhere else in the system for it to be.

    Numerical Effect: Cone of about thirty meters, twelve meters at the base. Ambient inside it drops on the order of forty-five degrees Celsius in four seconds. Water in any state present goes to ice, including standing water at depth and including water inside things. Convection stops, so open flame inside the cone will not burn and goes out without being extinguished. Somatic return to the summoner runs a core-temperature rise near three and a half degrees over a sustained pull, against a body that begins at thirty-seven and stops working somewhere past forty-two.

    Target Response: Nobody freezes to death. It is far too fast for that and the deaths are mechanical. Plain and low-alloy steel passes its ductile-to-brittle transition somewhere between twenty and sixty below, and a blade at fifty below fails when it is parried instead of bending, and it fails into fragments at the speed the swing was carrying. The first Thinbreath filing is mostly a list of men killed by their own weapons and their neighbors’. Mail behaves worse than harness and harness behaves worse than nothing. Exposed skin takes full-thickness cold injury in under ten seconds and the casualties who live lose the parts they were holding things with.

    Limitation: It is a cone in front of a mouth. It cannot be turned without turning the animal, it cannot be projected around a corner, and there is no version of it that reaches a target the construct is not facing.

    Weakness: For the whole pull, the exhaust is landing in Rimsang, and any competent Perception read finds her instantly because she is the hottest object on the field by a wide margin and getting hotter. She has been located this way three times and loosed at twice.

    Cost: Physical toll, escalating, and the least deniable cost in the register. She has taken her core past forty-one twice and lost consciousness both times. The permanent damage is on the left side, where the thermoregulatory failure settled: that half of her no longer sweats, and she carries it in warm weather the way other people carry a bad knee. The Guild’s physician’s note in the file says she has perhaps four more sustained pulls in her and has said so for two years.

    Vow: None sworn. The Guild has twice proposed one and she has declined on the grounds that a vow would make the arithmetic somebody else’s business.

    Counterplay: Do not stand in front of it, which is unusually achievable because the animal is slow to turn and the cone is visible from outside as a wall of hoarfrost. Beyond that: mass and moving air both defeat it, because a large enough thermal reservoir or a strong crosswind restores what the pull removes faster than the pull can take it. A burning building is functionally immune. So is a river in flood. So, awkwardly for the Accord, is a battle already on fire.

    Codex Assignment: [Ra] · [Wy] · [Ve] · Exuroth primary, Basilithe secondary · Calcination (the Measurewrights’ Thermodynamics).

    First Display: Not in a fight. A waystation hearth, a pot boiling over, the construct putting its head through the door and the pot going still and gray and solid between one word of an argument and the next. Everyone laughs. Rimsang leaves the room and does not come back for a while and nobody follows her.

    Sensory Description: The dry hits first and everyone’s lips split at once. Hoarfrost climbs whatever is nearest, upward, against sense. The fires bow down and go out without smoke. Rimsang’s face goes red and stays red, and she opens her collar, and in the worst of it she goes to one knee in snow with her palms flat on it and does not get up until it is over.

    Interactions: Cannot be run while the Span is setting, because the same gradient cannot do both. Fills the rings as a byproduct of every use, which is the only reason the rings ever have anything in them.

    Corruption Vector: An Obsession-Force Intake does not stop at ambient. It keeps opening the gradient after there is nothing left to take, and it takes from the summoner too, and the exhaust and the pull meet in the same body.

  • THE SPAN · Utility · Vocatia-Native · Phase Change Glyph Basis: [Grv] Gravity, a weight-calibrated span holding heavy transit · [Py] Pyre, pinning a mark to a fixed point · [Slg] Slag, melting and re-fusing a seam into a stronger dovetail

    “Transeant omnes, et pondus ferat.”

    Let them all cross, and let it bear the weight.

    Trigger: It walks into the water and stands. Rimsang speaks the certification aloud, in Guild form, with the load rating stated, and the rating she speaks is the rating she gets.

    Function: It freezes a crossing and Basilithe rates it, and the rating holds.

    Mechanism, the Measurewrights’ account: Thermodynamics for the phase change, Material Science for the result. Running water is the hard case, because convection keeps hauling fresh warmth into the gap faster than a static sink could clear it, and the construct answers by standing in the flow and taking the heat out of the water passing it rather than the water in front of it. Basilithe then does what Basilithe does to any structure and the ice sets with grain aligned to the load path instead of the freeze front, which is the entire difference between ice and a bridge.

    Numerical Effect: Up to about twenty-five meters of span across moving water, wider across still. Deck thickness comes in between forty and sixty centimeters, against the rough field standard where ten centimeters carries a person and thirty carries two tonnes. Rated spans have taken loaded carts. Setting time runs eleven to fourteen minutes on a river and under three on a lake. The Guild has load-tested nine of them and has yet to break one at the stated rating.

    Target Response: Nothing, because it does not have a target. It has a bank on the other side. The response worth recording is the pursuing force’s, and the record is consistent: they cross it too.

    Limitation: It cannot span anything the construct cannot stand in. No gorges, no chasms, no dry ground, and nothing over salt water, where the depression of the freezing point puts the required pull past what one Intake can deliver before the summoner cooks.

    Weakness: A Span is a permanent, mapped, physical fact for as long as the cold lasts, and it tells anyone who finds it exactly who was here, which direction they went, and how many of them there were, because the Guild certification is spoken into it and reads to any Judicium harmonization like a signature on a document.

    Cost: Social and legal, and it is the cost that has actually ruined her. Two of her spans have been walked by forces she did not lay them for, and one of those reached a Windward drover settlement it should not have been able to reach in that season. The Guild has not revoked her certification. The Guild has said nothing at all, which under Waymark procedure is worse than revocation and everyone involved knows it, and the toll-right she still technically holds is now honored in three holds out of eight.

    Vow: None.

    Counterplay: Wait. It melts. Beyond that, the Span is the single most exploitable thing in the entry, because it is a road and a road is neutral: a competent opponent does not deny the crossing, they let it be built and then use it, and the construct will not unmake a Span while anything is still on it, including them.

    Codex Assignment: [Grv] · [Py] · [Slg] · Exuroth primary, Basilithe secondary · Calcination (the Measurewrights’ Thermodynamics and Material Science, by declared crossover). [Grv] Gravity carries a Basilithe row in Material Science and is drafted here on the declared secondary rather than on the primary, which is legal and is noted so no reader files it as an error.

    First Display: A stream a child could jump. Rimsang certifies it anyway, aloud, in full Guild form, with a load rating, for a party of four who did not need it, because the form is the form. Somebody laughs at her. She finishes the recitation.

    Sensory Description: The water goes quiet from the middle outward. It does not stop moving all at once; it thickens, and the sound of the river drops out of the world in stages, and the last thing to go is the ripple noise at the banks. The construct stands in it to the chest and does not shiver. Rimsang says the rating with her hands behind her back and her weight even, which is the posture the Guild teaches for a certification and which she has never once broken.

    Interactions: Locks out the Intake while setting. Runs alongside the Floor without conflict, and the two together are how the Thinbreath crossing was held for eleven days.

    Corruption Vector: An Obsession-Force Span does not end at a bank. It keeps going, certifying ground nobody asked for a route across, and the routes it lays go to places that had been safe specifically because there was no way to reach them.

  • THE FLOOR · Control · Vocatia-Native · Stratification Glyph Basis: [Eith] Flux, controlled unpredictability breaking rigidity · [Py] Pyre, pinning a mark to a fixed point · [Sv] Sever, cutting a thread from a living host

    “Nihil hic ascendat.”

    Let nothing rise here.

    Trigger: It asks permission and lies down. Both halves are required and the asking is not ceremonial; the working does not initiate until she answers.

    Function: It builds a layer of cold dense air along the ground and holds it there, and inside that layer nothing rises.

    Mechanism, the Measurewrights’ account: Thermodynamics, stratification. Cold air is denser than warm air, so a sufficiently strong ground-level chill pools rather than mixing, and the Pyre glyph pins the pool to the mark instead of letting it drain downhill. What results is a stable inversion: a lid of warm air sitting on a floor of cold one, and the boundary between them does not turn over. Convection inside the layer stops, and every process that depends on convection stops with it.

    Numerical Effect: Radius forty to sixty paces from the animal, depth roughly to mid-thigh on a Concord human and to the chest on a Marrowkin, and the depth is the awkward part. Holds for as long as it stays down. Ambient inside the layer sits some twenty-five degrees under the air above it, which is not lethal and is not meant to be.

    Target Response: No open fire will burn inside the layer, at all, from any source, because a flame needs the hot gas above it to rise and take its place. Torches gutter, lamps die, fire-pots go dead in the hand, and every fire practitioner in the radius finds their ignition working correctly and producing nothing. Smoke does not rise; it lies down and spreads and blinds people at knee height. Feet and lower legs go numb in about a minute and clumsy in about three, and the casualty reports read as an unusual number of broken ankles. Scent pools, so hounds are useless. The layer kills almost nobody. It makes a room in which several things that were about to happen simply do not.

    Limitation: It is ankle-to-thigh deep. Anything above that height is entirely unaffected, which means archery is unaffected, cavalry above the layer is unaffected, and any working that does not require convection or a flame proceeds normally.

    Weakness: The animal is lying down, in the open, in the middle of it, and it will not get up until she tells it to, and everyone can see exactly where it is.

    Cost: Physical toll on whoever tends the rings afterward, which is the cost the Floor shares with everything else this construct does and which is treated in full below. The Floor’s own particular cost is that Rimsang must say yes. It asks, and she answers, and she has answered yes on two occasions where the layer went down over her own people’s fires on a night that was already cold, and both times she was correct, and both times four or five people who did not die in the fighting nearly died of the night instead. She does not discuss it and the file does not either.

    Vow: None.

    Counterplay: Get above it. Ladders, walls, saddles, a table. The layer has a top and the top is at thigh height, and a competent opponent stops fighting on the ground and starts fighting off it. Failing that, strong wind destroys the stratification outright in under a minute, and any sufficiently large heat source inside the radius punches a chimney through it that the layer cannot close.

    Codex Assignment: [Eith] · [Py] · [Sv] · Exuroth primary, Basilithe secondary · Calcination (the Measurewrights’ Thermodynamics).

    First Display: A camp. A bad night, a wet fire, a dozen tired people, and the construct lying down without being asked so that the smoke stops going up the valley where it would be seen. The fire dies too. Nobody connects the two things for another year.

    Sensory Description: The fires shrink and settle and go out from the base upward, which is backwards and is the thing witnesses fix on. Smoke rolls out flat and lies on the ground and moves like water. Breath fogs at the wrong height. The construct puts its chin on its forelegs and closes its eyes, and it looks, in the middle of all of it, exactly like an animal that has been walking all day.

    Interactions: Compatible with everything. The Intake fired from inside the Floor gets a measurable boost because the starting ambient is already down. The Span holds longer under a Floor and this is how an eleven-day crossing is possible at all.

    Corruption Vector: An Obsession-Force Floor does not stop at thigh height and does not stop at a radius. It keeps stratifying outward and downward until it reaches the point where nothing in it rises at all, including the people, who are still breathing and are no longer able to stand up out of it.

  • THE RINGS · Discharge · Vocatia-Native · Radiant Release Glyph Basis: [Fge] Forge, alchemically binding two materials at a soul-thread level · [Ig] Ignite, the spark occurring only when a chosen outcome opens · [Py] Pyre, pinning a combusting mark to a fixed point

    “Redde quod sumpsi.”

    Give back what I took.

    Trigger: She shows it a target. Physically, by looking at the thing and putting a hand out, and the construct will not discharge into anything it has not been shown first. Then one ring opens.

    Function: It gives back, all at once, a portion of what the Intake took.

    Mechanism, the Measurewrights’ account: Thermodynamics, storage and release. The fraction of extracted enthalpy that the Shell does not pass to the Continuum and does not pass to Rimsang is bound into the closed rings by the Forge glyph, held at soul-thread level rather than as any physical hot object, which is why the rings are cold to the touch and why nobody working it out from first principles believes the Guild’s account. Ignite opens the chosen ring only. Release is radiant rather than convective and therefore effectively instantaneous and effectively unblockable by anything that is not opaque.

    Numerical Effect: Each closed ring holds on the order of four megajoules, which the Guild’s own reckoning renders, for people who need it in terms they can picture, as a good ladle of lamp oil, a little over a hundred grams, burned in a single instant. Discharge goes out as a forward hemisphere of about five meters. Across that face the delivered flux runs near ten joules per square centimeter, which is the threshold for full-thickness burn at short exposure, and the calculation is the Guild’s and has been checked.

    Target Response: Everything facing it within five meters burns through the full thickness of the skin in the time it takes to register that something happened. There is no blast and no pressure wave and nothing is thrown, which is why survivors’ accounts are so consistently useless: they report a light and then the injury, with nothing in between. Behind cover is genuinely safe. Behind a person is genuinely safe. It is the most survivable thing in the entry provided there is anything at all between you and it.

    Limitation: Four discharges exist, total, and only four, and they refill only through Intake use. There is no version of this ability that works on a construct that has not first been made to take heat from somewhere.

    Weakness: An open ring is visible on the braid at a hundred paces and anyone who has read this entry can count how many she has left from outside bow range.

    Cost: Physical toll on the braider, which under Guild practice and construct preference means her and nobody else. A discharged ring must be re-closed and re-braided by hand into a mane that sits, at rest, some thirty degrees under ambient, and it takes between four and six hours with bare fingers because gloves cannot manage the strand work. She has lost three fingers on the left hand and the last joint of the right thumb. The fifth ring has been open for two years. The Guild’s file records it as an incomplete oath and the Guild is not wrong about what an open ring means, and she has never corrected the record, and the truth is that she can no longer close it because she does not have the hand for it any more.

    Vow: Not sworn and structurally unswearable, per her own filing, on the grounds that a Marrowkin cannot swear on a ring she cannot close.

    Counterplay: Cover, and almost anything counts. Beyond that: cutting a closed ring off the braid discharges it uncontrolled at the point of cutting, which has been done once, deliberately, by someone who knew exactly what it would do and did it anyway, and the Guild has that person’s name and has never released it.

    Codex Assignment: [Fge] · [Ig] · [Py] · Exuroth primary, Basilithe secondary · Calcination (the Measurewrights’ Thermodynamics).

    First Display: A frozen lock on a waystation door in a bad wind, four people outside it, one ring opened at a distance of half a meter onto an iron plate, the door open before anyone finishes complaining. Nobody present understands they have watched an incendiary.

    Sensory Description: The ring opens with a sound like a small bell and the sound arrives after the light. There is no heat felt at the construct’s end at all and the mane does not stir. Rimsang’s outstretched hand stays out until it is done, and afterwards she looks at the braid rather than at what she has just pointed at.

    Interactions: Depends entirely on the Intake for its charge. Cannot be used inside her own Floor without punching a chimney through it and collapsing the stratification, and she has done this once on purpose to open a way out.

    Corruption Vector: An Obsession-Force Rings does not require being shown a target and does not open one ring. It opens all of them, at whatever is nearest, and there is no procedure for closing rings that were emptied without a hand consenting to it.

IX · Dissolution and Failure

Clean SealingIt does not wait to be sealed. When the last body is across the crossing it goes, in about eight seconds, and the going looks like an animal walking out of sight behind something there is nothing behind. What remains is the Pyre mark: a scorched disc on the ground roughly a pace across, black, dry, and warm to the hand for about an hour, which is the detail that convinces heat diagnosticians and baffles everyone else
Severed TetherThe construct is unaffected and completes the crossing. What comes back up the line into Rimsang is the whole of the somatic return with the Continuum discharge closed off, which is the failure mode the Guild physician’s note is actually about. Modeled as fatal inside forty seconds of an active Intake. It has never happened during one. It has happened twice while the construct was merely walking, and both times she was fine, and both times she stopped walking
Behavior in SilenceIt holds and it keeps walking. Inside a Silence Realm the Intake, the Span, the Floor and the Rings are all unavailable and the construct continues along the route at the same pace, unhurried, taking nothing, asking nothing. Two Accord observations agree it does not appear to notice, and one dissenting note argues that a thing which stops asking questions has noticed a great deal
Resistance to DissolutionRecorded once, at her own ninth-year certification crossing, when it declined her order to leave and stayed until the stragglers were across. She has never filed it. The account in this register comes from two members of that party and is entered here without her confirmation and against her stated preference
Permanence HazardIt stops being a summon on the day there is no route left to make. The construct is bonded to the office and not the woman, and the Waymark Guild’s own procedure passes a certification to a successor on the holder’s death. Nobody has written down what happens to Haelvorn when Rimsang Halfyear dies, and the reason nobody has written it down is that the obvious answer is that it goes to whoever holds the route next, and the second most obvious answer is that it does not

X · Corruption Vector

Attraction Force answers by recognition. Haelvorn comes because someone is going somewhere and it makes a way and then it leaves, and the sum of its consent is that it asks before it lies down.

The Obsession-Force inversion does not need a road. It makes them anyway. It certifies routes nobody requested, to places that were safe because they could not be reached, and it does not ask before it lies down because there is no longer a party whose comfort is the point. The Intake stops distinguishing between the field and the summoner, and the exhaust and the pull arrive in the same body, and the arithmetic of that has one solution.

The compelled version also stops requiring a hand. The rings are the tell and they are the whole tell: a lawful Haelvorn cannot refill or reopen a ring without someone consenting to sit in the cold for six hours and pay for it in fingers, and that requirement does more than any mechanical limit could, being the last thing standing between the construct and unlimited discharge. An Obsession-Force Haelvorn closes its own rings.

Every Passwarden who has read this entry has arrived at the same objection and it is entered here because it is correct: the described inversion is a great deal easier to reach than the register’s phrasing implies. It requires only that a guide stop having anywhere in particular to go.

XI · Counterplay

On seeing it arrive, get above the ground and out of the cone, and understand that the animal is not the problem. Nothing in the kit tracks, nothing in it is fast, and the construct has no offensive architecture of any kind that is not borrowed from the Intake’s leftovers. It is a stretch of road with teeth it does not use.

Of the three recurring vulnerabilities, one applies hard, one is decisive, and one is nearly useless. Separation from the native Wellspring is decisive but rarely available, because Exuroth is an ordeal Wellspring and ordeal is not a place. Essence-dampening applies hard and is the correct standing answer, since dampening the tether does not stop the Intake but it does close the Continuum discharge, and closing the Continuum discharge routes the entire return into Rimsang, which ends the engagement in under a minute by ending her. The Arbitration Division’s field guidance recommends this. It is worth writing down plainly that the Accord’s standard answer to its own registered asset is to make its summoner die of the working, and that the guidance has been distributed to companies who have never been told what it does. Radiant current is nearly useless here and is the exception to the register’s general rule: a Luminalis working aimed at this entry supplies energy to a sink, and the sink takes it.

The real counter is arson. A large enough fire inside the radius defeats every ability in the kit at once. It outpaces the Intake’s pull, it punches a chimney through the Floor, it thaws a Span, and it puts the construct in the position of taking on more heat than the tether can move, which the tether resolves by moving it into her. Three separate forces have worked this out independently and the fact that it works is the reason the Kegshard filings exist.

Signature legibility applies and is unusual in shape. The construct itself is nearly silent to a resonance read and the summoner is not: during a pull she is the brightest thermal object on the field, and any competent Perception harmonization finds her without effort. Everything about this entry that can be exploited is exploited through her.

XII · Attested Encounters

The Halfyear Certification, Waymark Guild record, complete and uncontested. The original route: a saddle crossing that exists between the fourth and eighth months and does not exist otherwise, certified by Rimsang alone, per Guild requirement, with the construct present. The Guild’s own record is dry and administrative and runs to two pages of load ratings. The byname comes from this document and was not intended kindly.

The Thinbreath Saddle, Accord Archive and Guild record, and the two do not agree about anything except the dates. Eleven days, a Span held under a Floor, a crossing that the Windward and Tallow accounts each describe as having been laid for the other side. The Accord’s filings from this engagement are what triggered the ordnance reclassification. Norbu Waymark, senior guide of the waystation, is recorded as having certified the Guild’s version of the account, and nothing further about his role is entered here because nothing further is established.

The Kegshard Relief, Accord Archive, Arbitration Division report, dated. The refusal to close on a column three days without fire, and the failed relief that followed. The Division’s assessment is that the Refusal Conditions in Section V constitute an unacceptable operational limitation. The dissenting note appended by the Concordia reader present argues that the construct declined to take heat from people who had none, states that this is the same clause that has kept it from killing its own summoner, and asks the Division to consider whether it would like that clause removed.

The Cut Ring, no record anywhere. Field accounts, five of them, from two companies and one waystation, agreeing that a closed ring was cut off the braid at contact range by someone who knew what would happen. They disagree on who. The Guild holds the name and has released nothing, and the only reason the register is confident the name exists is that the Guild has twice declined to say it does not.
