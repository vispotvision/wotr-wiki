Vaelmorn · The Standing Grave

Vaelmorn · The Standing Grave

Nov 17, 202630 min read

Divine Beast · Vocatia · Native Provenance · Bond Class Consenting · Danger Tier T6, contested

He has never killed anyone. The tier is for what he does to a place, and the two Bench members who voted against it were both arguing for a higher number.

“Sustine quod cadit. Noli deponere.”

Hold up what falls. Do not set it down.

For fourteen years nobody died at Halloway Mire, and for the first eight they held a fair about it. The Long Fair filled the causeway every midsummer with marigold garlands and yellow bunting, eel pies, pilgrims from four counties in their good blue, a sexton who had gone into eels because his own trade had left him, and an undertaker suing the parish for loss of custom. Annis Reedy, the hedge-worker who started it, had knelt in the peat in the first spring and spoken the old words over her sister’s boy, crushed under a turf sled, and on the second day a stag came over the water as tall as a hayrick and walked as slow as a tide, furred in living moss with ferns along his spine and a dead forest of antler holding the mist above him, a small brown woman with her eyes shut pacing at his flank. The boy stayed about to die. After that the four villages carried their dying down the boards on doors and set them within his ninety meters, and there they stayed: a carter with a stove-in chest who was still finishing the same story six years later, a grandmother with the wasting who kept up with all the news, and a great many more who took cake when it was brought and did not get better, and who were, all of them, still there. Annis Reedy called him nine times, and paid a name for each, and in the third year, among people she loved and could not address, she walked over the ridge and out of the parish, since that was the one way left to die. No child was born in any of the four villages from the ninth year on. The parish register gives both facts in the same volume, and nobody has ever written a sentence connecting them, and I am not going to be the first. In the fourteenth year the abbey’s peat diggers drained the mire for peat, and he turned and walked north with the woman beside him. His field held eleven days more, going from the edge inward. Then four parishes rang their bells until the ringers fell over, and dug two hundred and six graves in a week, and that was the last of the Long Fair.

I have stood inside the ninety meters for an afternoon with a cut thumb, and it stayed exactly as wet as it was when I walked in, and began to itch again about a quarter of an hour after I walked out. He is one of the old ones, mythic by any reckoning, a thing the bog villages will kneel to and the Bench cannot find a statute for, and he also has a chest that hangs too low and a neck carrying four times what a neck should. For the newcomer: Vocatia is the art of calling something that already exists, and Mnemata (memorycraft) carries what he keeps. Native provenance means he owns his own Soul Crystal (the organ that refines Aether into Essence). Bond Class Consenting means he may refuse an order that contradicts his nature. A World-Tethered form borrows its persistence from the ground that holds it. Danger Tier is the Commission’s rating, as though he were loose. Shell, Layer, Draft and the rest of the grammar are set out in the Summon Register.

VI · Physical Description

He stands three point one meters at the shoulder and the proportions are wrong for the height, front-heavy and narrow through the hindquarters, with forelegs like fence posts and a chest that hangs low enough to drag through standing water. The hide is not visible anywhere. What covers him is moss, layered and matted into a pelt forty centimeters deep across the shoulders and thinning to a ragged fringe along the belly and the backs of the legs, and the color runs from the dull olive of old bottle glass at the crown to a sodden near-black where it trails in the water. It is alive. Small pale fruiting bodies stand up from it in patches and there are ferns rooted along his spine, and the whole surface moves faintly in still air with the slow settling motion of something that has been undisturbed for a long time and is not enjoying being disturbed now.

The rack spans four point six meters and masses somewhere above two hundred kilograms, which is four times what the neck of any living cervid could carry and is one of the reasons the assessors stopped calling him an animal. The tines are bare hardwood, gray and lichen-crusted, forking and reforking into a dead thicket that catches mist and holds it. The count of the points changes. Foresters who have watched him across seasons put the range between forty and something over ninety, and the two Archives entries that bothered to record a figure recorded different ones eight days apart. New tines come in wet and dark and stiffen over weeks. Old ones do not shed.

The face is the least altered part of him and the hardest to look at. A cervid skull’s architecture under the moss, long-nosed and heavy-browed, with the moss growing in a clean line that stops at the muzzle and leaves the gray hide of the nose and lips bare. The eyes are set deep and are entirely dark, without shine, and they do not appear to focus on anything nearer than the horizon. He blinks perhaps twice an hour. Around him the air is cold and smells of iron, wet bark, and the sweetish organic sourness of turned peat, and the temperature inside his ninety meters runs four to six degrees below the field outside it in every season measured.

The woman stands at his left flank and has never been observed anywhere else. She is a head shorter than an ordinary woman and narrower, with the compression through the ribs and hips that peat does to a body over centuries, and she is preserved rather than alive in a way that survives about four seconds of looking. Her hair is the color and texture of wet peat itself, dark to nearly black, hanging in matted ropes past her waist and threaded through with living sphagnum that appears to be rooted in it. Her skin is tanned to the deep russet-brown of oiled leather, taut over the cheekbones, the features intact and slightly flattened, the closed eyelids smooth and sunken. Her eyes have never been observed open. Her shoulders are narrow and sloped, her chest flat against the sternum, her abdominal wall drawn in hard against the spine, and her hands and feet are perfect: every knuckle, every nail, the nails slightly long. She wears a shift of undyed wool felted into the skin of her upper arms and back so thoroughly that the two are no longer separable, though the weave is still legible at the hem where it hangs to mid-calf. Around her throat, a ligature groove three fingers wide, with the braided pattern of the cord still impressed in it.

She walks when he walks. She stops when he stops. She has never spoken, has never been recorded touching him, and stands close enough to his flank that the moss has begun growing across the outside of her left arm.

VII · Temperament and Voice

Vaelmorn is gentle in a way that has ruined a great many people’s ability to think about him clearly. He does not startle, does not threaten, moves out of the path of anything smaller than himself, and has been observed standing motionless for two hours rather than disturb a nesting bird. Children in bog country get warned about the places he has been, and about him never.

He does not speak. Something near him does. Practitioners inside the ninety meters report hearing names, individual and unfamiliar, spoken once at the edge of audibility in voices that do not repeat. The Archives hold four hundred and nine transcribed names from six separate field records and have matched eleven of them to people in the death registers of the surrounding parishes. Nine of those eleven were recorded as drowned.

His one legible mood is reluctance, and it surfaces only when he is asked to move away from ground he has been standing on for a long time. He complies. He always complies. The complying takes a long time and something in the set of the head makes people stop asking twice.

The behavior every account agrees on: when a thing dies within his radius, he turns his head toward it before anyone else in the field has registered that it happened. He does not go to it. He just knows, and looks, and waits to be asked.

VIII · Abilities

  • THE CARRY · Signature · Preservation · Irreversible Glyph Basis: [Dr] Vitality · [Vael] Renewal · [Ma] Recall

    “Tolle et porta.”

    Take it up and carry it.

    Trigger: A dying thing inside the radius, and a request. He will not do it unasked, and the four centuries of record contain no exception.

    Function: The dying stops, and the thing stays short of living.

    Mechanism, the Measurewrights’ account: Biochemistry, and the reading is the peat bog. Vaelmorn’s Essence settles into the tissue and does to it what sphagnan does in acid anoxic water: it binds the free calcium the decay bacteria need, drops local pH toward the low fours, and strips the oxygen out of the interstitial fluid. Cellular metabolism falls to a rate at which nothing further happens, which is a long way short of stopping. The wound does not close, the blood does not clot, the heart does not restart, and the body does not rot. The Anamnesis component does the same operation on the identity, holding the pattern intact and unadvancing, which is the difference between a preserved corpse and a passenger.

    Numerical Effect: Local tissue pH driven from 7.4 to between 3.8 and 4.4 within ninety seconds of contact. Metabolic rate suppressed to roughly two percent of baseline, comparable to the deepest cold-water submersion recoveries and sustained without limit rather than for forty minutes. Attested passenger count is unknown and the lowest serious estimate is four hundred.

    Target Response: The subject remains aware. This is the finding that produced the second petition to restrict him. Passengers do not decay, do not age, do not heal, do not sleep and do not speak, and the eleven matched names in the parish registers suggest they retain enough of themselves to be heard saying who they are.

    Limitation: It cannot be reversed by him, by the summoner, or by any working the Bench has entered. It cannot be applied to something already dead. There is a window, and the window closes.

    Weakness: Every Carry adds mass. He is measurably slower than he was in the earliest reliable records and the rack is heavier, and both trends run in one direction.

    Cost: Attraction-category, carried by the summoner. One name per summoning, taken at the moment of the request and never at random. He takes the name of someone the summoner loves and has not asked him to carry.

    Vow: He must be asked. A request from anyone present is sufficient, including from the dying party, which is how most of the passengers arrived.

    Counterplay: Prevent the request, which means the counterplay is social rather than martial and is the reason this ability has never been answered on a battlefield. Failing that, finish the dying before he reaches them. Enforcement guidance says this in more words and the field has understood it perfectly since the guidance was issued.

    Codex Assignment: [Dr] · [Vael] · [Ma] · Primary Verdantia · Secondary Rebirthine · Fermentation (the Measurewrights’ Biochemistry). A crossing worth marking: [Ma] Recall is an Anamnesis assignment and is doing the identity work rather than the tissue work

    First Display: A deer with a broken back, and a forester who asks without thinking. The deer stands up. It has been standing since, and it does not eat, and the scene should end with somebody realizing that.

    Sensory Description: The moss on his flank rises and settles. A cold comes off him that people describe as a smell before they correct themselves. The subject’s blood stops moving in the wound and stays exactly where it is, wet and bright and going nowhere.

    Interactions: Any healer working inside his radius finds their work held rather than accelerated. Benediction and Anima Spirare techniques do not fail. They simply stop progressing at the moment they would have closed something

    Corruption Vector: An Obsession-Force Carry does not wait to be asked and does not wait for dying. It takes the living, and the taking is indistinguishable from the lawful version except that the subject is walking and talking one second and is a passenger the next.

  • HIC NEMO · Area · Passive · Ambulatory Domain Glyph Basis: [Th] Foundation · [El] Life · [Ur] Balance

    “Hic nemo moritur.”

    Here no one dies.

    Trigger: Permanent while he is present. It is his Domain and he does not seat it, raise it, or drop it.

    Function: Inside ninety meters, nothing finishes. Nothing heals either.

    Mechanism, the Measurewrights’ account: Biochemistry. The same suppression the Carry applies to one body, applied thin across a volume. Ambient temperature inside the radius falls four to six degrees, dissolved oxygen in standing water and in open wounds drops toward zero, and the local pH shifts acid. Clotting cascades stall at the fibrin stage. Sepsis does not advance. Blood loss continues at rate and consciousness does not. A man opened across the femoral artery inside the radius bleeds out onto the moss for as long as anyone is willing to watch and does not lose the thread of what he was saying.

    Numerical Effect: Radius 88 to 94 m, measured on eleven occasions. Onset within four minutes of his arrival, persistence eleven days after his departure, decaying from the edge inward. Wounds sustained inside show no granulation tissue at all, which is the diagnostic a Physician’s Eye can call from across a room.

    Target Response: Everyone in the radius survives and nobody recovers. Field surgeons hate him with a specificity they reserve for no other entry in the register, because triage (sorting the wounded by who can wait) stops functioning: there is no dying category, there is no stable category, and every wounded man is exactly as urgent as every other wounded man for as long as the field holds.

    Limitation: It is not selective and he cannot narrow it. The enemy’s wounded are held identically. Two engagements in bog country have ended in a truce for no reason more elevated than that both lines had a hundred men who would not stop being about to die.

    Weakness: The radius announces him from a kilometer out. Cold air, no insect noise, standing water gone flat and iron-smelling. Nothing about him is a surprise to anyone who has read the register.

    Cost: Environmental and permanent. Ground he has stood on for a season does not decompose afterward. Leaf fall accumulates, nothing breaks down, the soil stops feeding, and the conifer belt he has spent four centuries walking is thinning from underneath. The Withering correlation in section I is nobody’s proven case and it is also not nothing.

    Counterplay: Leave the radius, and take the wounded with you. That is the entire answer, it works completely, and it requires a commander willing to move casualties who are all screaming that they are fine.

    Codex Assignment: [Th] · [El] · [Ur] · Primary Verdantia · Secondary Fixatio · Fermentation (the Measurewrights’ Biochemistry). A crossing worth marking: [Th] Foundation is a Petralon assignment in Mechanics, and it is supplying the boundary geometry that a purely biological chain cannot

    First Display: A row of surgeons’ tents that stops losing anyone, and a surgeon who is delighted for eleven hours.

    Sensory Description: Breath fogs. The bog goes quiet in the specific way that has nothing to do with sound and everything to do with insects. Blood on the moss stays wet and stays red for days.

  • THE LENDING · Defensive · Interposition · The Ugly One Glyph Basis: [Lei] Binding · [Leth] Blood · [Th] Foundation

    “Commoda pondus unum.”

    Lend one weight.

    Trigger: Harm arriving at Vaelmorn or at something he has been asked to shield. He lowers the rack.

    Function: A passenger takes the injury.

    Mechanism, the Measurewrights’ account: Biochemistry through the bond structure rather than through tissue. Each tine on the rack is an anchor point for one carried identity, and the Attraction Layer’s knot permits the damage to be routed along the bond to the passenger instead of to the bearer. The passenger is already suppressed and cannot die of it. They can be broken, opened, burned and unmade, and every bit of it holds at the moment of infliction and does not progress, and they carry it from then on.

    Numerical Effect: One tine per lending, consumed. The tine goes gray-white, splits, and does not regrow. No upper limit on the severity absorbed and no attested failure to absorb. Between forty and ninety tines available at any time, which is a resource pool measured in people.

    Target Response: Whatever struck him has struck nothing. Witnesses report the sound of the impact arriving from somewhere off to the side, which is the passenger, and the sound is not always only an impact.

    Limitation: He cannot lend a weight to protect himself from being asked to Carry. He also cannot decline to lend, and the register is explicit that this is not a mercy: he interposes for anything he has been asked to shield, and he has been asked to shield some appalling things.

    Weakness: Somebody is paying. A practitioner who understands the mechanism can force a lending deliberately and repeatedly, and the Bench has heard one case in which a commander did exactly that for six hours to establish how many tines a Divine Beast has. He got to nineteen.

    Cost: Borne by the passengers. This is the only technique in the register where the cost falls on a third party who did not agree to it and cannot object, and the entry records that plainly because burying it would be worse.

    Vow: None, and the absence is the problem.

    Counterplay: Do not attack him. He is not a threat and has never been one, and the correct answer to Vaelmorn on a field is to walk around him. Every party that has attacked him has spent the engagement damaging strangers.

    Codex Assignment: [Lei] · [Leth] · [Th] · Primary Anamnesis · Secondary Anima Spirare · Separation (the Measurewrights’ Abstract and Entropic Systems). A deliberate departure from the entry’s Biochemistry spine: the bond does the work here, and the bond is Anamnesis’s

    First Display: Somebody throws a stone at him, badly, out of nerves. A tine splits. Off to the left, in the mist, something takes the hit and the sound is wrong.

    Sensory Description: The rack comes down and the thicket of it fills the whole of the forward view. A dry crack, and then a second sound from the wrong direction, and the mist over the water moves as though something in it has folded.

  • WHAT IS CARRIED · Passive · Vow · The Refusal Glyph Basis: [Vael] Renewal · [Va] Law

    “Quod portatur non ponitur.”

    What is carried is not set down.

    Trigger: Permanent, unconditional, and four centuries deep.

    Function: Nothing Vaelmorn has taken on can be released by him, by a summoner, by a seal, or by his death.

    Mechanism, the Measurewrights’ account: Biochemistry as the visible half and Verdantia’s Endless Spring as the whole of the rest. The Wellspring’s documented corruption vector is the inability to let anything end, and in a practitioner it is a degradation of Harmonics Stability that a Bench can treat. In Vaelmorn it is the load-bearing structure. Removing it would remove him rather than cure him, and everything he is holding goes wherever four hundred suspended identities go when the suspension stops, which nobody has established and two filings describe as the reason nobody has tried.

    Numerical Effect: No figure available and the register declines to estimate one.

    Target Response: Passengers do not petition. The eleven matched names are the closest thing to a statement anyone has recovered from them, and they were recovered by transcription rather than by conversation.

    Limitation: Absolute and symmetrical. He cannot set down and he cannot be made to. The Sealing glyph in section IV addresses the summoner’s obligation and has never once addressed his.

    Weakness: He is a fixed and predictable quantity. An enemy who knows he cannot release anything knows everything he will do for the rest of the engagement, and the only genuine uncertainty in a Vaelmorn engagement is what the people around him will ask for.

    Cost: Everything. Every gram of the moss, every one of the tines, the four to six degrees, the thinning belt, the fourteen years at Halloway Mire. All of it is this technique’s cost accumulating, and it has never been paid down.

    Vow: The technique is the vow and neither party entered it voluntarily.

    Counterplay: None entered. The Measurewrights’ standing dissent notes that a Bench that has struck a hundred and forty-one formulas has never opened a Threshold case on an entity whose entire function is a refusal to stop, and offers no explanation for that.

    Codex Assignment: [Vael] · [Va] · Primary Verdantia · Secondary Lethegraal · Fermentation (the Measurewrights’ Biochemistry). A crossing worth marking: [Va] Law is a Petralon assignment, entered here for the binding rather than the structure, and this is the entry’s loosest Codex fit

    First Display: The [Vael] seal, spoken correctly, by a summoner who has done everything right. He turns and walks north and the woman goes with him.

    Sensory Description: Nothing happens. That is the display.

X · Corruption Vector

The lawful Vaelmorn waits to be asked. The compelled one does not, and the difference is the only thing standing between the register’s gentlest entry and its worst.

Attraction Force answers by recognition. Obsession Force takes the same shape and compels, and an Obsession-Force Carry does not require dying, does not require consent, and does not require a request. A living body inside the radius is taken mid-sentence. The suppression lands, the pH drops, the metabolism falls to two percent, and the person continues at the exact state they occupied when it arrived, aware, unable to advance, and walking at his flank by the following morning.

The second inversion is the one that turns him from an event into a condition. A compelled Vaelmorn stops needing the radius. Hic Nemo is a Domain he carries because he is holding it and holding it is all he does, and a knot forced rather than tied does not stay ninety meters wide. The projections in the sealed filing run to a valley, then to a watershed, then stop, because the clerk who wrote them stopped.

And the third one, which the Measurewrights raised and nobody answered. Every entity in the register can be corrupted from outside. Vaelmorn’s corruption vector is a description of his own Wellspring’s documented failure mode, running unchecked, in a body designed for it. He may not require an external agent at all. He may simply be further along.

XI · Counterplay

Walk around him. He has no attested kill, no offensive technique, and no capacity to pursue anything faster than a walking man. This is the entire correct answer and every failure in the record is a failure to accept it.

Move the wounded out. Hic Nemo works completely and reverses completely, from the edge inward, once the casualties are outside the radius. It requires a commander willing to load men who are all insisting they feel fine, and two commanders in the record could not do it.

Do not attack him. The Lending routes every injury to a passenger. Six hours of deliberate attack at the Bench-heard case produced nineteen split tines and no progress, and the register’s flat position is that the commander in question spent an afternoon torturing strangers.

Deny the ground. Halloway Mire ended when the peat was cut. Drainage works, is slow, is the only method with a documented success, and it costs a wetland.

And the one nobody will write in a field guide. Nothing about him is dangerous until somebody asks. The counterplay to Vaelmorn is a line that will let its own men die, and the Accord’s guidance says this in eleven words of doctrine and has never once said it out loud.

XII · Attested Encounters

Halloway Mire. Fourteen years continuous, eleven of them after the summoner’s death. Ended by peat cutting. The parish registers for the surrounding four villages show a mortality rate of zero across the entire period and a birth rate that fell to zero in the ninth year. No document in the Archives connects the two figures and both are in the same volume.

The Truce at Corran Fen. Two lines, roughly a hundred wounded each, neither able to close and neither able to withdraw casualties. Terms were agreed by men shouting across ninety meters of bog at a stag who was not listening.

The Nineteen. Bench-heard. Six hours of deliberate attack conducted to establish tine count. The commander was censured on procedure. The passengers are not named in the finding and the Measurewrights’ request that they be named was refused as immaterial.

The Standing Deer. Folk account, no Archive entry, and the oldest story attached to him. A forester, a broken back, and a request made without thinking. Every version of the story ends the same way, with somebody working out that the deer has not eaten.
