Spirit Beasts

Spirit Beasts

Nov 28, 202618 min read

Eleven entries · creatures formed from Wellspring resonance rather than born of flesh

Brother Cuthwin Tace had a choir of forty, a very patient abbot and a theory, which was that a siren’s song could be beaten by a better one, in four parts, properly pitched. He put it to the test in high summer aboard the cog Pious Alys, bound through the Wailing Reefs with a hold full of the dyers’ guild’s saffron and his singers lined up along the rail in new scarlet, and for the first mile he was right. The sirens came up on the rocks with their hair moving like weed and listened, courteous as a paying audience, through the whole of the first verse and the second. On the third they took up the tenor line and gave it back a shade sweeter. The choir went over the side in order of voice, smiling, basses last, with Brother Cuthwin still beating time, and the Pious Alys opened on the reef with nobody at the steering oar. The saffron went into the sea, and for nine days the whole bay ran as gold as a coronation. The coast has held a festival there ever since. On clear nights the fishermen swear the rocks still sing that hymn in four parts, with Cuthwin’s own ornament in the last bar, which he never wrote down.

Mark what the creature did. It listened, learned the tune and kept it. A Spirit Beast was formed from the resonance of a Wellspring (one of the sixty kinds of Aether, the stuff a practitioner pulls from the world around him) rather than born of flesh, and it comes with a mind, a memory and opinions of its own. Most of them can be bargained with, some can be befriended, and the hunting halls will tell you that a bill posted on one is a bill posted on its whole herd, flock or reef. I would read the entries below as if each of them could read you back.

Unicorn

Elessae, the Moon’s First Hoof, in High Parunic. Thal’wyn, Horned Dawn, in Old Sylvaar.

Spirit Beast · Grade B, High Threat · Benediction, with a minor Somnalis affinity via Nysera · native to Edenis

Leans on · the Layer, by Benediction and Somnalis, both Fate. Body: a sound fighter gives its blessings nothing to work on, and whatever it wears can be killed if it can be caught, which is the sacrilege the valleys remember. Spirit: its calming and its Horn of Binding are pulled by a soul and show on the Core before they land. Fate: a contest over who owes whom, and it has been owed for longer.

Spirit-born from Benediction’s resonance during the Harmonial Age. Said to be the first beings Florwyn dreamed into being when she envisioned peace between mortal and spirit realms. Rare herds persist in secluded Material World forests where Benediction sites surface.

Equine and slender. Coats shimmer under moonlight in pure white, silver, pale gold or rare midnight blue. Horns long and spiraled, glowing when they channel. Eyes luminescent gold or pale blue; elders bear star-flecked irises. Their manes move as though in a breeze that is not there.

They travel in loose herds of four to six under an elder mare called a Moonkeeper, and vanish into dream-light trails during migration. Highly intelligent, cautious, and not inherently aggressive, but they defend Wellspring sites from corruption fiercely, and will ally with mortal defenders where trust is earned.

Abilities

  • Harmonic Healing · purifies Essence corruption, mends wounds, calms emotional unrest
  • Moonveil Step · short-range teleportation through dreamlight, leaving an aurora shimmer
  • Wellspring Guard · speed and magic resistance greatly amplified within reach of a Wellspring site
  • Horn of Binding · channels a Conjunction of Ma Memory + Ur Balance to seal a corrupted being temporarily

Weaknesses

Cannot survive heavily corrupted or void-aspected zones; prolonged exposure sickens them Trust is hard to earn: hostility meets immediate evasion or overwhelming defense Horn shattering removes most of their magical ability, and is considered sacrilege in many cultures

During the Withering Age several herds guarded Wellspring sites that kept whole forest realms from falling. The Siege of Moonveil Grove records a single Moonkeeper halting a Crevice-born horror for three days until Concord forces arrived.

Among the Sylvaar they are Florwyn’s first promise made flesh. In human kingdoms, purity and incorruptible truth. In Abyss-touched cultures they are omens of destruction, because their arrival heralds the cleansing of corrupted land, and the land in question is yours.

Griffon

Gryphael, Sky-Claw, in High Parunic. Talaruk, Lion of the Wind, in Old Iampu.

Spirit Beast · Grade A, Extreme Threat · Cymorath, secondary Transference resonance when bonded · native to Draconis and Caelum

Leans on · the Shell, by Cymorath, Wild. Body: drag it to the ground and keep it there. Spirit: past the feathers to a mind that holds one idea, the nest. Fate: a dive depends on the wind, the ledge and the nest staying put.

Shaped in the early Harmonial Age where Transference and Cymorath Aethers intertwined. Many hold that Omnis himself molded the first griffons as aerial guardians of the floating citadels.

Forequarters of a great eagle fused seamlessly into the hindquarters of a lion. Plumage reflects Wellspring alignment: white and silver for skybound flocks, bronze and gold for sun-favored lineages, storm-gray for tempest dwellers. Talons strong enough to pierce tempered steel.

Predatory but not needlessly aggressive, and fiercely territorial over nesting cliffs and Wellspring-fed aeries. They mate for life; bonded pairs defend a chosen roost until death. They ally with mortal riders who pass their trials of worthiness.

Abilities

  • Aerial Supremacy · extreme maneuverability in high wind; dives above 200 kph
  • Sonic Cry · a screech that disrupts Essence concentration and shatters brittle material
  • Wellspring Bond · increased stamina and magic resistance defending bonded territory or rider
  • Sky Rend · a dive capable of breaking siege engines or large beasts in one strike

Weaknesses

  • Poor endurance in prolonged ground combat Vulnerable to nets, entangling magic, and attacks on wing membranes Will abandon combat to protect nests or mates

At the Battle of Skychain Pass, twelve storm griffons routed a Crevice-born wyvern swarm before it reached the fortress gates. The High Aerie Riders of Foraye maintain griffon bonds running back seven centuries. In Draconis they are loyalty and sovereignty and appear throughout royal heraldry; in the frontier kingdoms, taming one is the ultimate mark of a sky knight.

Raiju

Voh’thir, Storm’s Howl, in Old Sylvaar. The Thunder Beast in Material World folklore.

Spirit Beast, Elemental Predator · Grade A, Extreme Threat · Cymorath, with a minor Orrenthal strain · Draconis and Caelum peaks

Leans on · the Shell, by Cymorath, Wild. Body: a low roof and wet footing. Spirit: the Static Cloak stops workings on the skin and has no say about the Core behind it. Fate: the Lightning Leap needs its trail, and a trail needs dry air.

Born from the fusion of Cymorath and Orrenthal Aethers. Legend says they were first formed by Nysera’s breath during a cataclysmic lightning storm that tore through the ancient forests of Edenis.

A lithe quadruped between wolf and great cat, cloaked in crackling blue-white fur that arcs with static. Eyes glow bright violet. Long whip-like tails and sharp talons ending in glowing tips.

Wild, unpredictable, fiercely territorial. They hunt in small packs and alone in storm season. Their howls are said to summon lightning and their presence foreshadows violent weather. They show a playful cunning, stunning prey with sudden bursts before closing.

Abilities

  • Stormcall Howl · summons localized lightning strikes and electric storms
  • Electric Surge · bursts through fur and talons to stun
  • Static Cloak · a crackling field that disrupts magic and shocks attackers
  • Lightning Leap · closes distance instantly on a trail of electricity

Weaknesses

Vulnerable in enclosed spaces where mobility is limited Water dampens their static and can cause unpredictable feedback Excessive cold temporarily suppresses their lightning

During the Storm of Wailing Peaks a Raiju pack destroyed a Concord scouting expedition, and the region carries a long-term magical storm curse as a result. Shamanic tribes invoke them as spirits of vengeance and renewal and offer tribute before hunting seasons. Some warrior orders attempt to bond with them and ride them into battle. Others fear them as nature’s unbridled fury.

Harpy

Skrial, Storm Wing, in Old Parunic. Tal’kira, Screech of the Hunt, in Outer World dialect.

Spirit Beast, Aerial Predator · Grade B, High Threat · Cymorath, with Luminalis sharpening sense and speed · Caelum and Draconis

Leans on · the Shell, by Cymorath, Wild, with Luminalis in the eye. Body: nets and a tight formation. Spirit: a flock’s cunning is shallow and easily read. Fate: an ambush depends on the caravan being where it was said to be.

Spirit-born from the raw winds, shaped by the violent intersection of Cymorath and Luminalis Aethers. Long held as harbingers of storm and misfortune.

Raptor and humanoid fused: feathered wings and arms, hooked beaks, eyes glowing faintly with Wellspring energy. Plumage from storm-gray to sunlit bronze, mottled with streaks of elemental energy crackling along the feathers.

Aggressive and territorial. They hunt in small flocks, using piercing cries and aerial agility to disorient prey before tearing it apart.

And they are cunning enough for rudimentary communication, which they use to mimic voices and lure travelers into traps. Concord scouts have reported harpies imitating patrol members to lead them into crevices.

Sonic Screech shatters fragile material and disrupts Essence concentration. Wind Blade generates slicing gusts from talon strikes. Aerial Acrobatics and Mimicry complete the set.

Vulnerable to grounded combatants and heavy armor, sensitive to loud disorienting noise that breaks their coordination, and their wings are delicate and susceptible to targeted attack.

The Screeching of the Wind Pass records a flock’s ambush that cost several trade caravans. In Draconis and Caelum they are feared and revered as wild spirits of storm and vengeance; frontier tribes use harpy feathers as talismans, and others conduct rites to appease them during storm season.

Psy-Apes

M’thak, Mindclaw, in Innerworld dialects. The Thoughtshard Primates among arcane scholars.

Spirit-Hybrid Beast, Psychic Primate · Grade B, High Threat · Oneirion and Eidolyn · the Innerworld's central jungles

Leans on · the Core, by Oneirion, Spirit, with Eidolyn building the rest. Body: a spear does not care what you dream, and an ape that is reached goes down like an ape. Spirit: a straight contest of minds against a troop. Fate: the troop survives by being right about you, and one spoiled read will do.

Mortal-born primates whose Essence became deeply intertwined with Oneirion and Eidolyn Aethers. The fusion granted psychic capability, elevated intelligence, and subtle manipulation of nearby minds. They evolved where Wellspring Aether lies thick beneath the canopy.

Larger than typical apes, sleek dark fur with faint iridescent highlights. Eyes glow soft violet or pale blue, and subtle markings along their limbs resemble glyphic tattoos.

They live in complex social groups with hierarchies determined by psychic dominance, communicating through a blend of vocalization and telepathic pulse, and coordinating hunts and defense with uncanny efficiency.

Telepathic Link shares thought and emotion across the group. Psychic Strike disrupts mental focus. Dream Veil creates illusion and mental barrier. Wellspring Empathy senses Essence flux and emotional state.

Vulnerable to Essence-dampening wards and counter-psychic magic. Physically weaker than brute beasts. And psychic overload can collapse group cohesion outright.

The Mindshade Jungles Campaign details ambushes on Concord patrols using psychic illusion to mask approach. Several arcane researchers have vanished investigating their territories. Innerworld tribes regard them as guardians of sacred Wellspring sites.

Dryads

Lioraen, Forest Spirits, in the Sylvaar tongues. Verdant Whisperers among frontier woodfolk.

Spirit-Beast, Nature Guardian · Grade C, Moderate Threat · Verdantia, with Anamnesis in the oldest groves · the deep forests of the Innerworld and Material World

Leans on · the Shell, by Verdantia, Wild, and the grove’s memory by Anamnesis in the oldest stands. Body: an axe, and the war that follows. Spirit: past the bark to what the grove remembers, which is also where a bargain begins. Fate: a grove depends on its water and on what people have promised it.

Spirits born from Verdantia’s Aether intertwined with ancient forest Essence. They embody the living soul of the oldest trees, and their existence is tied to sacred groves: their power ebbs and flows with the health of their forest.

Slender humanoid figures with bark-like skin, leaf-green eyes, hair resembling flowing vines. Their limbs shift subtly between wood and flesh, and their movement echoes rustling leaves.

Peaceful and nurturing and fiercely protective. They tend flora and fauna, heal, and hold the balance of their groves; threatened, they unleash nature magic and summon forest creatures. They aid travelers they judge respectful.

Verdant Growth entangles and builds barriers. Healing Touch mends wounds and cures poison. Nature’s Call summons woodland creatures and elemental vines. Forest Camouflage makes them nearly invisible among trees.

Vulnerable outside forested or Wellspring-rich ground, susceptible to fire and Essence-dampening magic, and physically fragile beside other spirit-beasts.

The Siege of Elderbark Grove records Dryads defending their sanctuary against Crevice-spawn. Concord rangers routinely seek their aid navigating perilous forest realms.

Bai Ze

Zhal’rei, the Enlightened One, in Old Parunic. The Oracle Beast among sages.

Spirit-Beast, Guardian of Knowledge · Grade B, High Threat · Anamnesis and Oneirion, with Judicium for the clear reading · Innerworld sanctuaries

Leans on · the Core, by Anamnesis, Medium, and Oneirion, Spirit, with Judicium for the clear reading. Body: it is physically fragile and will leave long before you arrive. Spirit: it reads you first and better. Fate: it knows what is, and what has a way of happening is a different question. Nobody who has met one recommends any of this.

Emerged during the Harmonial Age as a guide for mortals, gifted with profound wisdom and the perception of hidden truth. It roams sacred groves and ancient libraries, aiding seekers of knowledge or guarding esoteric Wellspring sites.

A majestic quadruped with multiple eyes arrayed across head and shoulders, each glowing softly. Fur a shimmering blend of pale gold and silver; a form combining lion, ox and dragon. Elegant horns curl from its crown and ethereal runes shimmer along its flanks.

Calm, enigmatic, benevolent. A patient guardian, offering cryptic guidance and riddles to those it judges worthy. It avoids conflict and will fiercely protect sacred Wellspring sites and ancient knowledge from desecration.

Omniscient Gaze peers into past, present and potential futures. Essence Insight reads the Aether of a site and metaphysical signatures. Protective Aura wards against corruption and hostility. Mystic Speech communicates telepathically, in riddle or vision.

Physically fragile. Vulnerable to Essence disruption that clouds its perception. Rare and elusive, and deliberately hidden, to avoid exploitation.

Ancient texts recount the Bai Ze aiding the early Concord founders with divine wisdom, shaping treaties and Wellspring stewardship. Scholars today seek it to unlock lost knowledge or avert metaphysical disaster. Its sightings are held as omens of profound change.

Siren

Liora, Song Weaver, in Old Parunic. The Sea’s Temptress among sailors.

Spirit-Beast, Enchanting Predator · Grade B, High Threat · Somnalis and Thaloré, with Transference amplifying the enchantment · coastal waters, mist-shrouded islands, underwater grottoes

Leans on · the Layer, by Somnalis and Transference, both Fate, with Thaloré giving the song its weight. Body: wax, rope and a steady hand on the oar, and then it is an animal on a rock. Spirit: a Listener hears the hand on the tune before the crew goes over the side. Fate: cut what the song has tied, at about what it cost to tie.

Ethereal beings born from the fusion of Thaloré and Somnalis Aethers, embodying the hypnotic power of the sea and the allure of dreams. Legend says they arise from the restless souls of lost sailors and oceanic spirits bound by ancient magic.

Beautiful humanoid forms, hair shifting like seaweed, skin shimmering with reflected moonlight. Eyes glowing soft blue. Some display webbed fingers or scaled patches.

Seductive and cunning. They lure sailors to treacherous reefs or drown them outright. They prefer to avoid direct conflict and can summon oceanic creatures and shape water to defend themselves.

Siren’s Song charms, disorients, incapacitates. Water Manipulation controls tide, current and aquatic life. Mist Veil obscures. Ocean’s Grasp restrains and strikes with tendrils of water.

Vulnerable to sound-dampening wards and Essence disruptors, markedly less effective on land, and susceptible to bright light and holy Essence.

The Wailing Reefs Incident attributes numerous wrecks to siren lures. Coastal communities hold festivals to appease them and protect their fishermen, which is not framed locally as superstition.

Tanuki

Mokuri, Trickster Spirit, in Old Parunic. The Forest Jester among frontier folk.

Spirit-Beast, Trickster Shapeshifter · Grade D, Minor Threat · Eidolyn and Oneirion · dense forests, mystical groves, hidden Wellspring sites

Leans on · the Shell by Eidolyn, Wild, and the Core by Oneirion, Spirit. Body: pin the shape and it stops being clever. Spirit: a Read sees the leaf under the coin. Fate: a trick depends on being believed, and a sound purse does not believe it.

Born from the blend of Eidolyn and Oneirion Aethers, embodying playful cunning and shape-shifting magic. Rooted in ancient forest lore, they delight in mischief, illusion, and testing the wits of travelers and sorcerers alike. Their true forms are rare to witness, as they prefer ever-changing guises.

Small, fox-like or raccoon-like, with large expressive eyes and bushy tails often tipped in glowing essence light. Their forms shimmer and flicker, sometimes sporting additional tails or strange appendages that are not there.

Shape-shifting into humanoid or animal forms. Illusion Weaving. Essence Mimicry of voices, sounds and magical signatures. Spirit Mischief, causing minor disruptions in the Aether of a Wellspring site and in ambient magic.

Vulnerable to Essence detection and truth-binding magic. Their physical forms are fragile and illusory, and direct attacks disrupt the transformation. They avoid conflict and flee threats.

Folklore of the Laughing Grove tells of Tanuki leading lost hunters safely out of enchanted woods, or into deeper danger. Many arcane practitioners regard them as mischievous but valuable informants.

Pegasus

Aeloria, Sky Wanderer, in Old Parunic. The Starhoof among frontier riders.

Spirit-Beast, Celestial Mount · Grade B, High Threat · Aurevane, with Benediction · high peaks, sacred groves, celestial realms

Leans on · Medium, by Aurevane, leaning toward the Shell in flight and with Benediction’s Fate on anyone it has chosen. Body: the wing and the tether. Spirit: it answers to being asked, and that is the road people mean when they tell the story. Fate: a chosen rider’s bond is the thing that can be pulled, and whoever pulls it will be hunted.

Noble celestial horses born from the purest Aurevane and Benediction Aethers. Legend places their emergence in the Harmonial Age as messengers and guardians of the divine realms, embodying freedom, purity and swift flight.

Gleaming white coats, large feathered wings shimmering with radiant light, eyes glowing with serene wisdom. Their hooves leave faint trails of stardust.

Noble, intelligent, fiercely loyal. They bond deeply with a chosen rider.

Celestial Flight · unmatched aerial speed and agility. Radiant Aura · a calming light that heals allies and wards off darkness. Essence Bond · shares Wellspring Essence with the rider, enhancing the vitality and senses of both. Divine Charge.

Vulnerable to Essence-disrupting magic and dark curses. Sensitive to pollution or corruption at Wellspring sites. Require pure Wellspring environments to maintain strength.

The Skyward Crusades recount Pegasus-mounted knights breaking enemy lines and carrying vital messages. Several orders maintain sacred herds for war and ceremony.

Phoenix

Ignara, Flameborn, in Old Parunic. The Eternal Ember in the Innerworld.

Spirit-Beast, Elemental Flame · Grade SS, Realm Hazard · Pyreveil, with Rebirthine · volcanic peaks, sacred flame groves, radiant Wellspring sites

Leans on · the Shell, by Pyreveil and Rebirthine, both Wild. Body: a hitter can kill it, once, at great cost, and then there are the ashes. Spirit: in the ash there is nothing but the Core, the one hour a Spirit hand has it alone. Fate: the rebirth depends on the sanctuary it is bound to, and sanctuaries can be made to fail.

Born from the purest Pyreveil and Rebirthine Aethers, embodying cycles of destruction and renewal. Said to arise from the ashes of its own death. Revered as a divine omen and protector of sacred Wellspring sites.

A massive bird, plumage of blazing red, gold and orange, radiating light and warmth. Eyes glowing with intense fire, feathers shimmering as if aflame. Its flight leaves trails of embers.

Noble, majestic, fiercely protective. It intervenes to purge corruption and restore a Wellspring site to balance.

Abilities

  • Rebirth Flame · immolates itself to ashes and is reborn, restoring vitality
  • Radiant Burst · waves of purifying fire that cleanse corruption and heal allies
  • Essence Beacon · amplifies Wellspring energy, empowering nearby creatures and sites
  • Flame Talons · scorching strikes capable of melting armor and flesh

Weaknesses

  • Vulnerable during the rebirth cycle, when it exists only as ashes Susceptible to water and Essence-dampening magic
  • Bound to Wellspring sanctuaries, which limits its range

The Ashes of Renewal chronicles the Phoenix’s intervention during the Great Spirit War, turning the tide by cleansing corrupted Wellspring sites.

Pilgrims journey to witness a rebirth. It is a sacred event in many cultures and it is the only window in which the creature can be killed.
